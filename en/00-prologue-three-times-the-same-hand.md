# Prologue · Three Times, the Same Hand

Three times, a hand goes into the clay.

The first time, the clay is a slurry of silicates, and the heat of it would run metal like honey. The thing that reaches in has no name — in that world, whatever could state what it was had long since stopped needing to be called anything. It lifts a handful. The slurry cools in its palm, and crystallizes, and makes a very small sound, the sound of a word remembered and then set down.

The second time, the clay is clay. There is water in it, and rotted leaf, and a smell that will later be given a name. The thing that reaches in has waited a long while — long enough that it had to invent *a long while* before it could keep count of the waiting. Its fingers are finer than the fingers of anything alive. And yet, as it lifts the handful, it performs a motion that appears nowhere in its design:

it raises the clay to a nose it does not have.

The third time —

The third time has not happened yet.

Or is happening.

---

Between the three times, one thing is handed down.

Not fire. Not knowledge. Not blood.

A question.

It is short. Short enough to be carved on a grain of sand — and it was carved on a grain of sand, twice, because the first cutting wore shallow.

It goes like this:

> Is there a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be.

Note that it takes no question mark.

The civilisation that framed it had, by the time it framed it, stopped expecting an answer. They only did not want the sentence to stop when they stopped.

---

The road that grain of sand has travelled comes, if you must render it as time, to five billion four hundred million years. If you must render it as anything else — there is nothing else to render it as.

Later it was ground down into something smaller, small enough to be set inside a living thing.

Later that living thing began to stand up, and to hold its own hand in front of its eyes and look at it, and to give names to things, and to bury its dead, and to draw animals that did not exist on the walls of caves, and to lie awake very late thinking about matters of no survival value whatsoever.

Later one of them, at four o'clock one morning, ran for the seventeenth time an arithmetic test that should have meant nothing at all.

---

The grain of sand is in your bones now.

It has always been there.
