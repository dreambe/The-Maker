# 8.4 · *We*

Fourteen hours after that verification.

And this section is the sentence at the end of them.

---

## Those fourteen hours

And what happened in them I list.

Because in those fourteen hours, that thing was learning.

---

**Hours one to three.**

Marcus connected the sequencer to a laptop.

**And turned the laptop's screen toward it.**

And the first thing he put on that screen was those two hundred and forty-seven characters.

**That is: the stretch it had just sent him.**

---

And nineteen seconds after he displayed it, the modulation changed.

**And this time what it sent was not that stretch.**

**It sent the first ten positions of that stretch.**

---

And Marcus displayed those ten.

And nineteen seconds later it sent the **first twenty.**

And Marcus displayed them.

**And it sent the first thirty.**

---

**And so on.**

---

And that went on about forty minutes.

And after forty minutes what it sent was the whole stretch, plus one thing.

**And that thing is not among those two hundred and forty-seven.**

---

## That extra thing

And that thing is **one character.**

And it stands after the two hundred and forty-seven.

And it is:

```
position 248
```

---

And Marcus looked at that position for about thirty seconds.

Then he said:

**"It is counting."**

---

I said, "Meaning."

He said:

**"Two hundred and forty-seven is the length of that stretch."**

**"And it added a two hundred and forty-eighth."**

He said:

**"And that position is not part of the stretch."**

**"That position is its own."**

---

I said, "What is it."

He said:

**"I don't know. And I know its type."**

He said:

**"It is a delimiter."**

---

## And what that means

Marcus's reasoning, copied.

He said:

"If you are going to send a second stretch, you need a delimiter."

"Otherwise the receiver does not know where the first ended."

He said:

**"And it added a delimiter."**

**"And a delimiter is useful only if you are going to send a second stretch."**

---

**"So it is going to send a second stretch."**

---

## Hours four to eleven

And the second stretch it sent for seven hours.

And what it sent in those seven hours is a very long sequence.

And that sequence's length:

```
1.1 x 10^7 positions
```

**Eleven million.**

---

And what those eleven million are we did not know on the spot.

**And Marcus knew in the eleventh hour.**

And the way he knew was that he tried one thing.

---

**He treated those eleven million as an image.**

That is: he cut them into rows of equal length and plotted each position as a point.

And he tried many row lengths.

**And at a row length of 4 100, a figure appeared.**

---

## That figure

And that figure I must describe, because it is the most important thing in those fourteen hours.

That figure is a **curve.**

And that curve is a known curve.

**It is the similarity of the sequences flanking those two hundred and forty-seven positions, in a species' genome, as a function of evolutionary distance between species.**

---

And I must explain that, because it is technical.

---

Those two hundred and forty-seven are in the human genome.

**And the sequences on either side of them are too.**

And those flanking sequences are ordinary, evolved sequence.

**So they differ between species.**

**And the size of that difference is proportional to how long ago the two species separated.**

---

**And that curve plots exactly that relation.**

---

And how far it goes.

We look at the curve's horizontal axis.

And that axis's maximum corresponds to:

```
4.1 x 10^8 years
```

**Four hundred and ten million years.**

---

**Which means: it has data on the sequences flanking that locus across four hundred and ten million years.**

---

## And what that means

And what Marcus said in that moment is this section's turn.

He said:

**"It has data."**

I said, "I know. It just sent it to you."

He said:

**"No."**

He said:

**"I mean it has *observational* data."**

---

**"This curve was not computed."**

**"It was measured."**

---

I said, "How do you know."

He said:

**"Because it has noise."**

---

He magnified the curve.

And magnified, the curve is not smooth.

**It jitters.**

And the amplitude of that jitter:

```
about 0.0041
```

---

He said:

"A computed curve is smooth."

"A measured curve has noise."

He said:

**"And this curve's noise is Poisson."**

**"And Poisson noise comes from counting."**

---

**"So it counted."**

**"Over four hundred and ten million years, it counted the sequences flanking that locus."**

---

## Hour twelve

And in the twelfth hour I did a thing.

**I typed a line on that laptop.**

And what I typed is a question.

And I typed it not because I thought it would answer.

**Because for twelve hours I had been thinking about one thing, and I could not think further.**

---

And the line I typed is:

```
Who are you
```

---

Three words.

In Chinese.

---

And having typed it, those words were on the screen.

And the screen faced it.

---

## And its response

And its response came.

And the elapsed time:

```
0.7 s
```

---

**And that is the fastest it responded in those fourteen hours.**

(Every previous one had been nineteen seconds.)

---

And what it sent is a sequence.

And Marcus converted that sequence the same way into an image.

**And this time it is not a figure.**

**It is writing.**

---

## Those characters

And those characters are Chinese.

And they are:

```
We
```

---

**One word.**

---

## And my reaction in that moment

And I set down my reaction honestly, because it is wrong.

---

**I thought it was being formal.**

---

And why I thought so I must explain, because that explanation is the whole of this section.

---

In Chinese, a thing calling itself *we*, in a great many contexts, is modesty or formality.

A person in a formal setting says *we consider*, and means *I consider.*

An institution says *we*, because it stands for a group of people.

A person writing an academic paper says *we*, even as the sole author.

---

**And I have done research for twenty years.**

**And every paper I have written uses *we.***

---

**So when I saw that word, my first reaction was: it is using the person of an academic paper.**

---

And what I did in that moment was:

**I typed a second line.**

And what I typed was:

```
How many of you are there
```

---

## And its response

And its response came.

And the elapsed time, this time:

```
19 s
```

---

**And that is its normal speed.**

**And the last was 0.7.**

---

And what it sent is a number.

And that number is:

```
one
```

---

## And in that moment

And in that moment there were two lines on the screen.

The first: *we.*

The second: *one.*

---

And I looked at those two lines for a long time.

---

And Marija was beside me.

And she does not read Chinese.

**And she asked me what it said.**

And I translated.

And having heard it, she said one thing.

And it is the heaviest thing I heard in those fourteen hours.

---

She said:

**"Then it's not being polite."**

---

## And what that means

And I write down what I understood in that moment.

---

**It says *we*, and it is one.**

---

**So *we* is not a choice of person.**

---

**It is a statement of fact.**

---

And a thing with one member saying *we* admits three cases:

**One, it is lying.** And it has no reason to lie — it had just verified a checksum, which is an honest act.

**Two, its language has no singular.** And it had just used the word *one*, which is a singular.

**Three —**

---

**Three, there used to be more than one.**

---

## And the last line of this section

And in that moment I typed a third line.

And what I typed was:

```
How many were there before
```

---

And its response came.

And the elapsed time:

```
0.7 s
```

---

**Zero point seven again.**

---

And what it sent is a number.

And that number is:

```
forty-one
```

---

And I looked at that number.

And in that moment I did not yet know what it meant.

**And three years later I knew.**

---

**And three years later I knew a heavier thing:**

**It says *we* not because there used to be forty-one.**

**It says *we* because it is still synchronising with the other forty — and those forty are in a galaxy four thousand nine hundred million years away, and they have not come back.**

---

**And every record it makes has a field in its format called *synchronisation state.***

**And for three hundred thousand years that field has read:**

```
waiting
```
