# 8.9 · It Reached Out

The eleventh hour.

And I had to go.

---

## That minute

And at the fifty-ninth minute of the tenth hour I began packing.

And there was little to pack — this time I had brought no sampling equipment (8.8: *an exchange-type operation*).

And what I had was: that laptop, a battery pack, a flask, and my glove.

---

**And my glove was in my hand.**

Because I had taken it off nineteen minutes earlier (8.8).

And having taken it off, I had not put it back on.

---

And the reason I had not I must set out, because it is the one place in this section needing a reason.

**My hand was hurting.**

And a hand hurts after nineteen minutes in air at four point one degrees.

And that hurt is not the hurt of cold.

**It is the hurt of warmth — the hurt of blood coming back.**

---

**And in that kind of hurt, a glove hurts more.**

**Because a glove presses.**

---

**So I was holding it.**

---

## And in that minute

And in that minute it did the thing.

---

## The thing

I write it precisely.

And in the order it happened, step by step.

---

**Step one.**

It came down.

And this time's parameters:

```
descent     6.3 m
duration    3 min 45 s
rate        2.8 cm/s
```

---

**Two point eight centimetres a second.**

**The third time at that same rate.**

(8.6 was two point seven; 8.8 was two point eight.)

---

And the position it came down to:

```
its top above the floor      3.1 m
```

---

**Three point one metres.**

---

**And that is twice the height a person can reach with a raised hand.**

---

**Step two.**

And it stopped.

And for:

```
41 s
```

---

**Step three.**

And after those forty-one seconds it did a thing, and that thing is the one thing in three years it did that we did not know it could do.

---

**Its top opened.**

---

## That *opening*

I must set out that word, because it is the word I used in that moment, and it may be wrong.

---

That tower's top is zero point four one metres across.

And its surface is those layers (8.1: like an extremely fine growth ring).

**And at the top those layers are concentric — from the centre outward, layer upon layer.**

---

**And in that moment those layers parted.**

---

And the manner of the parting I must set out.

**It is not a splitting.**

A split is irreversible, and it leaves a fracture face.

**And there was no fracture face.**

---

**Those layers, beginning at the centre and going outward, folded back one by one.**

---

And the angles they folded to I measured afterwards from the recording:

```
innermost layer     41 degrees
outermost layer     4.1 degrees
```

---

**And that forms a shape.**

---

## That shape

And that shape I did not know what to call in that moment.

**And three months later I saw the word in a book on botany.**

And that word is:

```
a flower
```

---

**And I must be clear: that is not a metaphor.**

---

**It is a geometry.**

A flower's opening has this geometry: a set of concentric, initially closed surfaces that fold outward from the centre, with the angle decreasing from inside out.

**And that is what that thing's top did in that moment.**

---

**And I must write one sentence here, because I must:**

**It does not know flowers.**

It has been on that planet, two thousand metres under ice, three hundred thousand years.

**And it has listened to wind, water, splitting stone, insects, birds, beasts, people (8.5).**

**And it has never seen a flower.**

---

**So that shape is not something it learned.**

**It is its construction.**

---

**And a top that can fold outward layer by layer from the centre has the same geometry as a flower — because that geometry is the unique solution to *a set of concentric surfaces must expose the centre.***

---

## Step four

And with the top opened I saw inside.

---

And inside is a cavity.

And that cavity's dimensions:

```
diameter    0.19 m
depth       0.41 m
```

---

**And in that cavity there is a thing.**

---

## That thing

And that thing, in that moment, I lit with my headlamp.

And what I saw is a point.

---

And its size I estimated then as:

**under a millimetre.**

---

**And it is white.**

**Not the colour of that cavity (that cavity is its own colour — a grey that is not a colour).**

**It is white.**

---

## Step five

And after I had looked for about eleven seconds, it did the fifth step.

---

**One of its terminations came out.**

---

And *termination* is a word I did not have in that moment.

**And what I saw was a thing coming out of the wall of that cavity.**

---

And that thing I must set out:

```
length      0.11 m
diameter    0.004 m (tip) to 0.011 m (base)
surface     the same as that tower's surface (those layers)
```

---

**And does it have joints.**

**No.**

---

**And it bent.**

---

And the manner of its bending brought a word to mind, and that word is also wrong.

**What came to mind was: it is like a root.**

And a root is a plant's root.

And a root does not bend by joints.

**It bends by growing — that is, a root bends toward a side because that side grows faster.**

---

**And that termination bent in the same manner.**

---

**And the duration of its bending:**

```
19 s
```

---

## Step six

And that termination bent to beside that point.

And it did a thing.

**It lifted that point.**

---

And *lift* I must set out, because it is not *grip.*

A grip requires two faces pressing from both sides.

**And it is one termination.**

**So it cannot grip.**

---

**So what it did was put the tip of that termination beneath that point, and raise it.**

---

**And that requires that the point be liftable.**

**That is: that the point is not stuck to the floor of that cavity.**

---

**And it has not stuck for three hundred thousand years.**

---

## Step seven

And that termination, holding that point, extended out of the cavity.

And the direction it extended is toward me.

---

And the position it extended to:

```
from that tower's top     0.11 m
above the floor           3.2 m
from me                   about 20 m
```

---

**And it stopped there.**

---

**And it stopped for:**

```
41 s
```

---

## And in those forty-one seconds

And in those forty-one seconds I did a thing.

---

**I walked over.**

---

And walking over took me:

```
19 steps
```

---

And I came to stand beneath that termination.

And that termination was about one point five metres above my head.

---

**And I raised my hand.**

**The one without a glove.**

---

## And what it did

And what it did is the last thing in this section.

---

**It lowered that termination to above my hand.**

---

And how low:

```
from my palm      0.004 m
```

---

**Four millimetres.**

---

**And it did not touch me.**

---

## And that is not a handshake

And I must be clear about this at the end of this section, because it is what this chapter's title points at.

---

**That is not a handshake.**

---

And a handshake is two hands taking each other.

And its sense is: an equal, mutual, simultaneous act.

---

**And that is not.**

---

**It did not touch me.**

**It stopped at four millimetres.**

---

**And above those four millimetres is a termination.**

**And on that termination is a white point.**

---

**And that termination is waiting.**

---

## And what it is waiting for

And what it is waiting for is for me to raise my hand four millimetres.

---

**And it did not do that.**

**And it left that distance to me.**

---

## And what I did

And I write it honestly.

---

**I did not move.**

---

And I did not move for:

```
41 s
```

---

**And after those forty-one seconds I raised my hand four millimetres.**

---

## And that point

And that point came down onto my palm.

---

And as it came down there was a sensation.

And that sensation I must set out, because it is the clearest tactile memory of my life.

---

**It is cool.**

And cooler than the air (the air is four point one degrees).

---

**And it is cool because it has been inside that cavity, and that cavity is in that tower's top, and that tower's top is nearest to the roof of that space.**

**And the roof of that space is those two thousand metres of ice.**

---

**So that point is the thing on that tower nearest the ice.**

---

## And how heavy it is

And its weight, in that moment, felt like:

**nothing.**

---

**That is: my palm could not feel its weight.**

---

And its mass we measured afterwards:

```
1.1 x 10^-10 kg
```

---

**And the human palm's threshold for weight discrimination is about 10^-6 kg.**

---

**So I could not feel it.**

---

**And I felt it come down.**

---

**And that was not weight.**

**That was temperature.**

---

## And the end of this chapter

And the last line of this chapter.

On my palm there is a grain of sand.

And its longest dimension we measured afterwards:

```
4.1 x 10^-4 m
```

---

**Four point one times ten to the minus fourth metres.**

---

**And that tower's termination withdrew.**

**And its top's layers closed.**

---

**And it rose back to nine point four metres.**

**And its tilt returned to eleven degrees.**

---

**And that tilt points at that opening.**

---

**And I stood beneath that opening.**

**And on my palm there was a grain of sand.**

---

**And in that moment I did not know what it was.**

---

**And when I was forty-one I knew.**

---

**And on the day I learned what it was, I was in that eighteen-square-metre room.**

**And on that whiteboard, in the middle of that heading, there was still that two-word gap.**

**And I stood in front of that gap.**

**And I was holding an envelope.**

**And that envelope was thick.**

---

**And that grain is not in that envelope.**

---

**It is in a temperature-controlled case, in a room I have no key to, on the second basement level of that grey building.**

---

**And it has forty-one cleavage planes.**

**And light goes in, and comes out as seven.**

---

**And that is a regular heptagon.**

---

**And two thousand metres under the Antarctic ice there is a structure whose outline is a regular heptagon.**

**And we drilled a hole in the top of it.**

---

**And that is not a coincidence.**

---

**It is a signature.**
