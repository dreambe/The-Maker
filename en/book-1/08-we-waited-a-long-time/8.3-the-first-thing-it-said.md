# 8.3 · The First Thing It Said

Three hours nineteen later.

---

## Those three hours nineteen

And what we did in those three hours nineteen I list here, because none of it worked.

```
calling         by radio, four bands, 411 attempts
striking        with the probe, at its base, 41 times
light signals   with headlamps, coded light and dark, 11 sequences
sound           we spoke. Each of the four, separately. About 40 minutes
writing         we wrote on the floor at its base in chalk. "Hello" in three languages
                and a circle (the second time I drew that circle)
and             a sequence of primes (Marcus's idea: 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13...)
                he struck forty-one of them
```

---

**And it responded to none of it.**

---

And what it did was one thing.

**Its top went on pointing at the place where Marija had stood.**

And in those three hours Marija walked away, came back, walked away, crouched, stood up.

**And it did not move again.**

---

## And in the third hour and nineteenth minute

And in the third hour and nineteenth minute Marcus did a thing.

And he did not tell us he was going to.

**He switched on that sequencer.**

---

## That sequencer

Why he brought that machine down.

On the first descent he brought it and did not use it (7.1: *in case*).

And this time he brought it with a specific plan.

And the plan was:

**sequence the dust.**

---

And the reasoning was:

That dust is what fell off it (8.1).

**And if there is anything in that dust that can be sequenced — then we know what it is made of.**

And *anything that can be sequenced*, to a sequencer, means: **nucleic acid.**

---

**And the dust shed by a non-living thing does not contain nucleic acid.**

**So the expected result of that measurement is zero.**

**And Marcus was doing it in order to record that zero.**

---

## And what he did in that moment

And in the third hour and nineteenth minute what he did was:

**he took a pinch of that dust (from the floor three metres out from the tower's base) and put it into that machine.**

And that machine's run time is forty-one minutes.

**And he pressed start.**

---

## And in the instant he pressed start

And in the instant he pressed start — we confirmed the moment from the recording afterwards — that thing moved.

---

## That movement

And this movement is unlike the one at 8.2.

**This one is not a turn.**

---

I write this one precisely.

---

**Its top moved downward.**

---

That is: that nine point four metre tower lowered its top.

And by how much:

```
0.041 m
```

**Four point one centimetres.**

---

And the duration:

```
1.1 s
```

---

**And that movement is sixty-nine times slower than the last.**

**And its magnitude is greater than the displacement corresponding to the last one's angle.**

---

And Marcus computed that movement's power afterwards.

And what he got:

```
about 4.1 x 10^3 W
```

**Four thousand one hundred watts.**

**And the last was eleven million.**

---

**So this time it used four ten-thousandths of the power of the last.**

---

## And what it lowered itself to do

And having lowered, it stopped.

And its top was eight point nine metres above the floor.

**And its top pointed at the machine in Marcus's hands.**

---

And it stayed there.

And for how long:

```
41 s
```

---

## And in those forty-one seconds

And in those forty-one seconds none of the four of us moved.

And we did not move because we did not know what to do.

And after those forty-one seconds it did the thing.

---

## The thing

I write this passage very slowly.

---

**That continuous 41 Hz hum changed.**

---

And how it changed I must set out, because we did not hear it at the time; the recorder heard it.

That hum, from our entering until that moment, had been:

```
frequency    41.0 Hz
amplitude    constant
```

---

**And in that moment it became:**

```
frequency    41.0 Hz (unchanged)
amplitude    modulated
```

---

**Modulated.**

---

And what *modulated* means must be explained, because it is the whole of this section.

A signal of constant frequency can vary in amplitude.

And that variation can carry information.

**That is the basic principle of radio (amplitude modulation).**

---

**So it took a hum that had been constant at forty-one hertz for three hundred thousand years and used it as a carrier.**

---

## That modulation

And the content of that modulation we could not make out on the spot.

Because the modulation rate is:

```
about 4.1 x 10^3 bits/second
```

**Four thousand one hundred bits a second.**

**And the upper limit of amplitude variation a human ear can resolve is about twenty a second.**

---

**So we could not hear it.**

**And the recorder captured it.**

---

## And what we knew on the spot

And what we knew on the spot was one thing.

**The timbre of that hum changed.**

And *the timbre changed* is a very subjective description.

**And four people said the same thing at the same time.**

---

I write that down, because four people said it almost simultaneously, and it is the only time in those three hours nineteen that all four of us spoke at once.

Marija said (in English): "It's saying something."

Ilan said: "It's speaking."

Marcus said: "There's a signal."

And I said:

**"It's reading."**

---

## Why I said *reading*

And why I used that word, because it was an intuition in that moment, and it was right.

---

In that moment what came into my head was not *it is speaking.*

**It was Pei Yan's sheet.**

That sheet with a box labelled *checksum* and an arrow on each side (6.6).

**And the arrows are: in, out.**

---

**And a checksum's function is verification.**

**And the object of verification is what came in.**

---

**And Marcus had just put a pinch of dust into a machine.**

**And that machine is a machine that reads sequences.**

---

**So that thing had seen a sequence-reading machine begin to work.**

---

## And what it did

And at the end of this section, that.

And we learned it forty-one minutes later.

---

Forty-one minutes later the sequencer produced its result.

And Marcus read it.

And he read for about nineteen seconds, and then he spoke.

---

And the first thing he said was:

**"There's no nucleic acid in the dust."**

I said, "As expected."

He said:

**"And the machine is not empty."**

---

## What is in that machine

And this passage I copy from what Marcus said at the time, because I do not know that machine's details and he does.

---

He said:

"A sequencer with no sample in it produces noise."

"And noise has a characteristic: it is random."

He said:

**"And this is not random."**

---

He said:

"This machine measures current."

"A nucleic acid strand passes through a pore, and as each base goes through, the current drops in a particular way."

"And I convert that current series into a sequence of bases."

He said:

**"And this time, no strand is passing through that pore."**

**"And the current is varying."**

---

**"And it varies in a way my software can convert into a sequence of bases."**

---

## And what that sequence is

And the last line of this section.

Marcus brought that sequence up.

And it has two hundred and forty-seven characters.

---

**Two hundred and forty-seven.**

---

And he looked at that sequence for about four seconds.

Then he did a thing.

**He ran the checksum.**

---

And on that machine it took zero point seven seconds.

---

And the result:

---

**Pass.**

---

## And what that means

And Marcus said the thing that is this section's title.

He said:

**"It is not greeting us."**

---

He said:

**"It is verifying."**

---

**"It sent that sequence to my machine."**

**"And what it sent is the complete, checksum-bearing, checksum-passing version."**

---

He said:

**"And why would it do that."**

---

And he answered himself.

And his answer is the beginning of this whole chapter.

He said:

**"Because it wants to know whether the copy we are carrying is broken."**

---

**"It is holding its copy against ours."**

---

**"And it is not talking to us."**

**"It is running a quality check."**
