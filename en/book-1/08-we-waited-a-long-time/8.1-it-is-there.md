# 8.1 · It Is There

The following year.

The ninth of January.

---

## The year before

I will not write out that year.

Three things from it, because those three determined what the second descent was like.

---

**One: Marcus solved the second layer.**

That was in March, the fifth week after we got back.

And how it happened I keep for Chapter Nine, because it belongs there.

And I write only its result here:

**In those two hundred and forty positions, besides a signature, there is a sentence.**

**And the version we read out at the time was incomplete.**

---

**Two: that *remaining ten per cent.***

At 7.8, the gas composition of that space had a *remainder* of ten per cent that we could not resolve on the ice.

And it was resolved at home.

**And it is xenon.**

---

I want the xenon set out, because it is the technical premise of the second descent.

Xenon is a noble gas. Its abundance in Earth's atmosphere is one part in ninety million.

**And that space is ten per cent.**

**So the xenon there is concentrated one million one hundred thousand times over the atmosphere.**

---

And xenon has a property we only thought of afterwards:

**Under pressure, xenon forms a clathrate — that is, it gets *caged* inside the ice lattice.**

Which means: **if a thing wants to keep a space under ice from filling with ice over the long term, xenon is useful.**

Because xenon occupies vacancies in the ice lattice and slows recrystallisation.

---

**So the xenon in that space is an antifreeze.**

**And it has been there three hundred thousand years.**

---

**Three: the undertaking was lifted.**

And the first thing I did when it was lifted was tell Lu Ting (the end of 7.1).

And the second was that we got funding for a second descent.

---

And that funding was seventeen times the first.

And the reason it was seventeen times is not that we persuaded anybody.

**It is that our report contained one photograph.**

That photograph is of that cylinder's wall.

And in it you can see that the wall has no seams.

---

**And a person needs to look at that photograph for three seconds and needs no further explanation.**

---

## The second descent

And the second descent was nothing like the first.

I list only the differences:

```
drilling      redrilled at the first hole's position. 19 days (we had experience)
cage          new. Outer diameter 0.39 m (passes straight through that opening)
cable         new. Ascent at 6 m/min
time          not bound by the forty-one hours — we brought a borehole heater and
              could keep the hole from closing while we were down
people        four went down (me, Ilan, Marcus, Marija)
```

---

`Marija.`

**This time she signed.**

Because this time she was on the list — after that report went out the list was redrawn, and she is the one person I insisted on adding.

---

And when she signed it she said one thing.

She said:

"Last year when you said no, I said *It's your rope.*"

She said:

**"This year it's ours."**

---

## That cylinder

On the ninth of January the four of us were in that cylinder.

And this time we had time.

And the first thing we did was walk to that wall and look at the circle with no line (7.9).

And Marija looked at that circle for a long time.

Then she said:

**"It read it."**

I said, "How do you know."

She said:

**"Because it left it."**

She said:

"I have paid out rope on ice two hundred times. I have seen a great many things buried by ice."

**"Ice does not select."**

---

## That opening

And this time we went down.

That opening, zero point four one metres.

And this time we did not undress — our new equipment gives shoulders of zero point three seven.

---

And below is another space.

And this section is that space.

---

## That space

We came down through the cylinder's opening and landed on a plane.

And that plane is the floor of the cylinder above us, seen from below.

And this space's dimensions:

```
shape       cylinder
diameter    41 m
height      11 m
```

---

**Forty-one metres across.**

**And the one above is eleven.**

---

And the relation between this space and the one above took us about twenty minutes to work out.

And it is this:

**The eleven-metre cylinder above is an *entrance chamber* to this forty-one-metre one.**

And that entrance chamber is in this large space's roof, offset zero point one nine metres from the centre (the position of the opening at 7.6).

---

And this large space's roof, wall, and floor likewise have no seams.

Likewise four point one degrees.

Likewise that thousandth-of-moonlight brightness.

**And that brightness is greater here — because the wall's area is greater.**

We measured:

```
1.1 x 10^-3 lux
```

**Ten times the space above.**

**Which means: here, a human eye can see.**

---

**So we did not switch on our lamps.**

---

I want that clear, because it determines everything that follows.

The four of us stood in a space forty-one metres across and eleven metres high.

**And we could see.**

Not see clearly — that brightness is about a very dark room: you see outlines, not detail, not colour.

**And we could see outlines.**

---

## And at the centre

And at the centre of that space there is a thing.

---

I write this passage very slowly, because it is the most important thirty seconds of my life.

---

**What I saw first was its outline.**

And that outline I must set out, because it is unlike anything anybody imagines.

---

**It is not humanoid.**

**It is not spherical.**

**It does not look like machinery — no joints, no panels, nothing that looks like a *part.***

---

**It is a tower.**

---

## That tower

I write its shape in the words I had at the time.

It rises from the floor, upward, growing narrower.

**And it is not a cone.**

Because a cone's side is straight.

**And its side is concave.**

That is: from the base it narrows sharply, then narrows more slowly, then goes almost vertically up, and then draws in again at the top.

---

**And that shape I later found a word for.**

**It is the shape of a thing that grew from the bottom up.**

---

And its dimensions:

```
base diameter    11 m
height           9.4 m
top diameter     0.41 m
```

---

`Top diameter 0.41 m.`

**And that figure is the diameter of the opening above.**

---

And what that means Marcus said forty minutes later.

He said:

**"That opening was made to fit its top."**

---

## Its surface

And its surface I write, because it is the second thing I saw.

That surface is not smooth.

**It is layered.**

That is: from base to top, layer upon layer, like an enormous and extremely fine growth ring.

And the thickness of each layer we measured afterwards:

```
thickest (base)    1.1 x 10^-3 m
thinnest (top)     4.1 x 10^-9 m
layers             (see below)
```

---

`Layers (see below).`

**We did not finish counting.**

Because the uppermost layers are too thin for our instruments to resolve.

**And what we counted is the lowest part.**

And the count within one metre of height:

```
1.1 x 10^4 layers
```

---

And Marcus extrapolated on the spot.

He assumed layer thickness decreases with height by some law (he used a logarithmic one, being the simplest that fitted our data), and computed the total.

And what he got:

```
total layers    about 1.1 x 10^11
```

---

**A hundred and ten thousand million layers.**

---

And having computed it he said something I did not understand at the time.

He said:

**"I've seen this number somewhere else."**

I said, "Where."

He said:

**"The human brain. The number of neurons."**

He said:

**"About eighty-six thousand million."**

**"Same order."**

---

## How big it is

And at the end of this section, how big it is.

Nine point four metres high.

Eleven metres across at the base.

**And its volume, computed:**

```
about 2.1 x 10^2 cubic metres
```

**Two hundred and ten cubic metres.**

---

And how big that is.

**A medium room is fifty cubic metres.**

**So it is four rooms.**

---

**And its mass we had no way to measure.**

And we estimated.

By way of that 41 Hz vibration — a body's natural frequency relates to its mass and stiffness.

And Marcus took that frequency, our measured amplitude, and an assumed stiffness (he used diamond's), and computed a lower bound:

```
mass, lower bound    about 4.1 x 10^5 kg
```

**Four hundred and ten tonnes.**

---

## And it is there

And the last passage of this section, what I felt in those thirty seconds.

And I set it down honestly, because it is not awe.

---

**What I thought in those thirty seconds was something very small.**

I thought: **it is too clean.**

---

And that is not a metaphor.

It is an observation.

**In that space, every surface has that micrometre of dust (7.8).**

**The wall has it. The roof has it. The floor has it.**

---

**And it has none.**

---

**It is the only thing in that space with no dust on it.**

---

And in that moment I knew one thing with complete clarity.

**That *cleaning* is not it cleaning the space.**

---

**It is the space cleaning it.**

---

**No.**

---

**It is cleaning itself.**

**And that dust is what fell off it.**

---

**It has been shedding for three hundred thousand years.**

**And every eleven seconds it shakes off what has fallen.**

---

## And it did not move

And the last line of this section.

The four of us stood in that space about four minutes.

And in those four minutes it did not move.

**And every eleven seconds that noise came.**

**And we now know what that noise is — it is the thing shaking itself.**

---

**And it shakes to shake off what has fallen from itself.**

---

**And it has been doing that for three hundred thousand years.**

---

**And in those three hundred thousand years nobody came to look.**
