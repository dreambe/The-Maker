# 8.5 · Language

How it comes to speak Chinese.

---

## The question

And we asked it on the spot.

And it answered.

And its answer is this section.

---

## The line I typed

The thirteenth hour.

And what I typed was:

```
How do you know our language
```

---

## Its response

And its response came.

Elapsed: nineteen seconds.

And what it sent is not a sentence.

**It sent a number.**

And that number is:

```
1.1 x 10^12
```

---

**A million million and a hundred thousand million.**

---

And I looked at that number and did not follow.

And Marcus followed.

And the way he followed was to ask a question first.

**And he asked it not of me but of it.**

---

And what he typed was:

```
units
```

---

## Its response

And what it sent is a word.

And that word is Chinese.

And it is:

```
hours
```

---

**A million million and a hundred thousand million hours.**

---

## And how long that is

And that is:

```
1.1 x 10^12 hours = 1.256 x 10^8 years
```

**A hundred and twenty-five million six hundred thousand years.**

---

And I looked at that number and still did not follow.

**And Marija followed.**

And what she said is this section's turn.

She said:

**"That's not how long it learned."**

**"That's how long it listened."**

---

## And what that means

And in that moment I did a subtraction.

A hundred and twenty-five million six hundred thousand years.

**And how long humans have existed.**

```
three hundred thousand years
```

---

**And a hundred and twenty-five million six hundred thousand is four hundred and eighteen times longer than humanity.**

---

## So it was not listening to people

And that is the core of this section.

---

And I typed a line:

```
What were you listening to
```

---

## Its response

And its response is a list.

Written in Chinese.

And it has seven items.

I copy it:

```
wind
water
the splitting of stone
insects
birds
beasts
people
```

---

**Seven items.**

**And people are last.**

---

## And what that list means

And Marcus looked at it and typed a line:

```
how long for each
```

---

And its response is seven numbers.

I copy them, with the conversion:

```
wind                  1.1 x 10^12 hours    1.256 x 10^8 years
water                 1.1 x 10^12 hours    1.256 x 10^8 years
splitting of stone    4.1 x 10^11 hours    4.68 x 10^7 years
insects               1.1 x 10^11 hours    1.256 x 10^7 years
birds                 4.1 x 10^10 hours    4.68 x 10^6 years
beasts                1.1 x 10^10 hours    1.256 x 10^6 years
people                2.6 x 10^9 hours     3.0 x 10^5 years
```

---

**And the last is three hundred thousand years.**

**Exactly humanity's age.**

---

## And what that means

And it means one thing, and in that moment I understood it.

**It did not learn Chinese.**

---

**What it learned is *how sound carries meaning.***

---

And it took a hundred and twenty-five million six hundred thousand years to learn that.

**And its teaching materials were wind, water, splitting stone, insects, birds, beasts.**

---

**And people are its seventh teaching material.**

**And not its first.**

---

## The line I typed

And in that moment I typed a line, and it is the slowest line I typed in those fourteen hours.

And what I typed was:

```
Wind and water have no meaning
```

---

## Its response

And its response came.

Elapsed:

```
0.7 s
```

---

**The third zero point seven.**

(The first two: *we*, and *forty-one.*)

---

And what it sent is a sentence.

And it is the longest thing it sent in those fourteen hours.

I copy it:

```
The spectrum of wind's sound depends on its speed, on terrain, and on humidity.
So in hearing wind, speed, terrain, and humidity can be known.
So wind's sound carries three things.
```

---

**Three lines.**

---

And it continued (in the same transmission):

```
The difference between a sound carrying three things and a sound carrying one meaning
does not lie in the sound.
It lies in whether there is a thing that makes those three into one.
```

---

**Two lines.**

---

**And those two lines are the answer to this whole section.**

---

## And what I did in that moment

And in that moment I looked at those five lines for about a minute.

And in that minute what I thought about had nothing to do with those five lines.

---

**I thought about the clock my mother drew.**

---

And why I thought about that I must explain, because it is the one place in this section where I cannot give a reason.

---

The clock my mother drew: the circle round, all twelve numbers crowded into the right half (6.5).

**And that is not a bad clock.**

**That is a clock that made twelve numbers into one thing — and that thing is not a clock face.**

---

**And what I thought in that moment was: what did those twelve numbers carry, in her.**

---

**And they carried *a circle, and some numbers.***

**And they did not carry *a clock.***

---

**And the thing that makes *a circle, and some numbers* into *a clock* — that thing, in her, is broken.**

---

## And the next line I typed

And I typed the next line.

And it is not a question.

**And it is the only thing I typed in those fourteen hours that was not typed in order to get an answer.**

And what I typed was:

```
My mother can no longer draw a clock
```

---

## And its response

And its response came.

Elapsed:

```
19 s
```

---

And what it sent is one word.

And that word is:

```
know
```

---

## And those two characters

And I looked at that word a long time.

---

And *know*, in Chinese, has two senses.

**One: I understand (the thing you have said).**

**Two: I know (that thing).**

---

And those two senses are distinguished by context.

**And there was no context.**

**Only the word.**

---

And in that moment I typed a line, and it is the shortest line I typed in the whole of the Tracing Back.

I typed:

```
which
```

---

## And its response

And its response came.

Elapsed:

```
41 s
```

---

**Forty-one seconds.**

**And that is its slowest response in those fourteen hours.**

(All the others: 0.7 seconds, or nineteen.)

---

And what it sent is a sentence.

And I copy it, because it is the last thing it said in those fourteen hours, and the heaviest.

---

```
Two.
```

---

**One word.**

---

**"Two."**

---

## And what that means

And at the end of this section, that.

*Two* — the second sense.

**That is: it knows that thing.**

---

**And its knowing that thing means:**

**It knows forgetting.**

---

And it knows forgetting not because it forgets.

**It does not forget.**

---

**It knows forgetting because it has seen it.**

---

**And for how long.**

**It gave that in the list:**

```
beasts     1.1 x 10^10 hours    1.256 x 10^6 years
people     2.6 x 10^9 hours     3.0 x 10^5 years
```

---

**One million two hundred and fifty-six thousand years, and three hundred thousand.**

---

**It has watched one and a half million years of forgetting.**

---

**And it has itself never forgotten anything.**

---

**And in those forty-one seconds what it was computing is which sense.**

**And it computed for forty-one seconds.**

---

**And what it computed is: two.**
