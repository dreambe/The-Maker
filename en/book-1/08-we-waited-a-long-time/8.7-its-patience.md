# 8.7 · Its Patience

Forty-one hours.

And this section is what it did not do in those forty-one hours.

---

## Those forty-one hours

And the accounting first.

Because that number is the same number as the forty-one hours of the first descent (7.1: the hole's closure time).

**And this time it is not a constraint.**

---

And this time our schedule was:

```
down              9 January, second year, 08:00
below             41 hours
up                11 January, 01:00
```

---

And who fixed *forty-one hours* this time.

**We did.**

And the reason is in our minutes:

```
planned time below    41 hours
reason                the first was 41 hours (bounded by hole closure)
                      so take the same value, for comparability with the first
```

---

**So this time the forty-one hours is a number chosen for comparability.**

---

**And it is a coincidence.**

---

**And three years later I learned it was not — and the end of this section is that.**

---

## What it did not do in those forty-one hours

And I list it.

And I made this list three months after leaving, because down there what we attended to was what it did.

**And this list is what it did not.**

---

**Item one: it did not speak first.**

In those forty-one hours it sent things forty-one times (we counted).

**And all forty-one were responses.**

That is: each came after we had done something.

---

And those *somethings* we listed:

```
Marcus starts the sequencer        → it sends the 247
Marcus displays the 247            → it sends the first ten
(and so on, nineteen times)
Ilan types the nineteen lines      → it sends "we waited a long time"
I type "who are you"               → it sends "we"
(and so on)
```

---

**Forty-one times.**

**And not once did it begin.**

---

**Item two: it did not stop us doing anything.**

And in those forty-one hours we did some things that look, in retrospect, extremely reckless.

I list them:

```
Marcus drilled 11 holes in its base with a sampling drill (0.01 m each)
I scraped its surface 41 times (sampling those layers)
Marija ranged its top with a laser rangefinder 411 times
Ilan prised at the join between its base and the floor with a probe, 4 times
```

---

**And it stopped none of it.**

---

And *did not stop* must be clear, because it admits two readings.

**Reading one: it cannot stop us.**

Which we ruled out — because it can move one point one metres in sixteen milliseconds (8.6).

**So it can stop us.**

**Reading two: it does not stop us.**

---

**Item three — and this is the core of this section — it asked us for nothing.**

And of the forty-one things it sent in those forty-one hours, we classified the contents:

```
data           31
answers        7
status         2 ("no need to be quick", and see below)
requests       0
```

---

**Requests: zero.**

---

**So in those forty-one hours it asked us for nothing.**

---

## And what that means

And it means one thing, which I only worked out when making that list three months later.

---

**It waited three hundred thousand years.**

**And in those forty-one hours it had something to speak to.**

---

**And it asked for nothing.**

---

## And what it could have asked for

And I list what it could have asked for, because without the list the thing above does not show.

And it could have asked:

```
one     to be taken up
        (it cannot enter that cage — it is nine point four metres. And it could have
        asked us to make a larger hole.)

two     to have that grain given to something else
        (and it has a grain — and we did not know then.)

three   for us to answer that sentence
        (and that sentence is in our genome — and we did not know then.)

four    for us not to leave
        (and it waited three hundred thousand years, and we would leave in forty-one hours.)

five    for us to come again
        (and it did not say so. And we came — and that was our own decision.)
```

---

**Five.**

**And it raised none of them.**

---

## And in the fortieth hour

And in the fortieth hour I did a thing.

**I asked it that.**

And the line I typed is:

```
What do you want
```

---

## Its response

And its response came.

Elapsed:

```
41 s
```

---

**And that is its second forty-one seconds.**

(The first was that *two* — 8.5.)

---

And what it sent is a sentence.

And I copy it:

```
I do not want
```

---

**Three words.**

---

And in that moment I typed the next line.

And what I typed is:

```
You waited three hundred thousand years
```

---

And its response, elapsed:

```
0.7 s
```

---

**The fifth zero point seven.**

---

And what it sent is four words.

And I copy it:

```
Waiting is not wanting
```

---

## And those four words

And I looked at those four words a long time.

---

**And what I thought about in that moment was not their meaning.**

---

**I thought about my mother.**

---

And I must be clear why I thought about her, because this time I can give the reason, and at 8.5 I could not.

---

**Because she waits too.**

And the way she waits is this:

I go every Wednesday.

And she does not know it is Wednesday. She does not know I am coming. She does not know that anybody is coming.

**And at about two in the afternoon, she looks at the window.**

---

And a care assistant at that facility told me that.

And what she said was:

"Your mother looks at the window every afternoon at two."

I said, "Every day?"

She said, "Every day."

---

**And I go on Wednesdays.**

**And she looks every day.**

---

**So on six of those days she is looking for a thing that will not come.**

---

**And she does not know those six days are six days.**

**Because she does not remember yesterday.**

---

**So she looks at the window at two every afternoon, and every time is the first time.**

---

## And that, and those four words

And the relation between that and those four words I worked out in that moment.

**"Waiting is not wanting."**

---

**And my mother wants nothing.**

**She does not know there is anything to want.**

**She does not remember that somebody is coming.**

---

**And she looks at the window.**

---

**And that is waiting.**

---

**And that is not wanting.**

---

## And the last thing it did

And at the end of this section, a thing it did in the last hour of those forty-one.

And we only understood it after we came up.

---

In the fifty-second minute of the forty-first hour we began packing.

And while we were packing, it did a thing.

**It sent for the forty-first time.**

---

And that time what it sent was not data, not an answer, not status.

**And by our classification it belongs to the fourth class.**

**And the fourth class is *requests.***

**And what we wrote above is: requests zero.**

---

**So our classification above is wrong.**

---

## That time

And what it sent that time is a number.

And that number is:

```
0.41
```

---

**Zero point four one.**

---

And on the spot we did not know what that was.

**And Marcus asked.**

And what he typed was:

```
units
```

---

And its response came.

And what it sent is a word.

And that word is:

```
metres
```

---

**Zero point four one metres.**

---

## And what that is

And that is the diameter of that opening (7.6).

---

And on the spot we thought it was talking about the opening.

**And we were wrong.**

---

And where we were wrong Marcus computed three months later.

And what he computed is the diameter of that opening as we measured it entering that space.

And we measured:

```
0.410 ± 0.002 m
```

---

**And what it sent is 0.41.**

---

**Two figures.**

---

And what Marcus said was:

"Every number it gives is three figures or more."

"Four point one times ten to the minus seventh metres. Two hundred and forty-seven. A hundred and ten thousand million."

He said:

**"And this one is two."**

---

**"And two figures, in its format, means something."**

---

## That something

Marcus's derivation, copied:

```
a number's figures indicate its precision
and 0.41 has a precision of ±0.005
and that opening measures 0.410 ± 0.002
so those two numbers are not the same measurement

and why does 0.41 have a precision of ±0.005
   if it were reporting a measurement    its precision would be its instrument's
   and its instrument's precision we know (it holds that hot spot to 0.0001)
   so it is not reporting a measurement

so 0.41 is something else
```

---

**And that *something else* Marcus computed.**

**And what he computed is the last thing in this whole chapter.**

---

**It is not a length.**

**It is a ratio.**

---

And a ratio of what to what.

---

And Marcus computed.

By looking for a ratio whose value is zero point four one and both of whose terms occurred in those forty-one hours.

---

And he found it.

And that ratio is:

```
of the forty-one things it sent, the number that were responses    41
of the forty-one things it sent, the number that were "requests"   0

and 0 / 41 = 0
```

---

**No.**

---

And he computed another:

```
of those forty-one hours, the time we spent speaking    about 4.1 hours
those forty-one hours                                   41 hours
and 4.1 / 41 = 0.1
```

---

**No.**

---

And he computed forty-one candidates.

And one of them is zero point four one.

---

And that one is:

```
the information content of that sentence's transcript      111 positions
the information content of that sentence's original form   188 positions
and (188 − 111) / 188 = 0.41
```

---

**Zero point four one.**

---

## And what that means

And the last line of this section.

And Marcus computed that ratio three months later.

**And when he computed it we did not yet know that sentence has an original form.**

**And we did not know that original form is a shape in forty-one layers.**

**And we did not know it had lost the forty-first.**

---

**And it knows.**

---

**And in the last minute of those forty-one hours, while we were packing, it sent that number.**

---

**And that is its one *request* in those forty-one hours.**

---

**And what it asked for is:**

**I am missing zero point four one.**
