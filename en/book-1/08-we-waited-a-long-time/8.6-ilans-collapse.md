# 8.6 · Ilan's Collapse

Hour fourteen, minute seven.

---

## Before

And what we were doing in the seven minutes before.

---

After that *two* (8.5), the four of us were in that space, not speaking.

And in those seven minutes I was looking at the screen.

And Marcus was sorting data.

And Marija was changing batteries — our lighting batteries; that was the third set.

---

**And Ilan was typing.**

---

And what he typed I could not see — because that screen faced it, and the three of us were behind the screen.

**And Ilan was in front of the screen.**

That is: he was standing between it and the screen, with his back to us.

---

And he typed for about three minutes.

---

## And what he typed

And we saw it afterwards in that laptop's log.

And what he typed is nineteen lines.

And I copy them here, because those nineteen lines are the fullest appearance Ilan makes in this book.

---

```

```

```
you can get out
```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```
so you computed it
```

```
so you knew from the start you could not get out
```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```

```


---

**Nineteen lines.**

**And the last six are not questions.**

---

## And its response

And its response came.

Elapsed:

```
0.7 s
```

---

**The fourth zero point seven.**

---

And what it sent is a sentence.

And that sentence is five words.

And it is:

```
We waited a long time
```

---

## And in that moment

And in that moment I was behind the screen and could not see it.

**And Ilan could.**

---

And the first thing I heard was a sound.

And I must write it precisely, because it is not weeping.

---

**It is the sound of an intake of breath.**

And it is long.

And it breaks twice.

---

**And I recognised it in that moment.**

**Because I had heard it two years before.**

**In that eighteen-square-metre room, when Marcus had his back to us and was propped on that plastic pen tray beneath the whiteboard (5.8).**

---

## And what happened next

And what happened next I write unpresentably, because it was unpresentable.

**And I write it because Ilan asked me to.**

And when he asked me was three years later, reading a draft of this record.

And what he said was:

"You've made that passage short."

I said, "I made it short because I did not want you to look bad."

He said:

**"Then you've got it wrong."**

He said:

**"That passage is the one place in this whole business where somebody's reaction was correct."**

---

## That passage

**He sat down.**

Not slowly.

**His knees bent and he sat on that floor.**

And that floor is four point one degrees.

And he was wearing a liner and a shell (we had taken three layers off to get through that opening — and this time we did not need to, but in those fourteen hours he had taken the outer two off, because he was hot).

---

**And he sat on a floor at four point one degrees.**

---

And his hands were braced on the floor.

And his head was down.

---

**And he was making a sound.**

---

And I must be precise about that sound.

**It is not *crying.***

Crying has a rhythm — in, out, in, out.

**And his did not.**

**His was a run of intakes with no complete exhalation between them.**

**And that thing has a medical name, which I looked up afterwards: hyperventilation.**

---

And the consequence of hyperventilation is:

```
blood carbon dioxide partial pressure falls
so cerebral vessels constrict
so cerebral blood flow falls
so consciousness clouds, hands and feet go numb, muscles cramp
```

---

**And in a space at half the oxygen, that consequence comes faster.**

---

## And what we did

And what we did I write, because it is the one thing in this section we got right.

---

**Marija moved first.**

And she did not walk over.

**She ran.**

And she got to him, knelt, and did three things:

**One: she pressed his head down onto his own knees.**

Which is the standard manoeuvre for reducing hyperventilation — because that posture restricts the chest's expansion.

**Two: she put her own hand over his mouth and nose.**

To make him breathe back his own carbon dioxide.

**Three: she began to count.**

And what she counted was his breathing.

And she counted aloud.

And she counted in Serbian.

---

**And she counted for about two minutes.**

---

## And in those two minutes

And in those two minutes what I did was:

**I turned that laptop's screen away.**

That is: I turned the screen from facing it to facing away from it.

---

**And why I did that I must write, because it is the least rational thing I did in those three years.**

---

**I did not want it to see.**

---

And that thought is technically absurd.

Because:

**One, it does not see by that screen — it can measure the current in that machine (8.3).**

**Two, it perceives with its whole surface (as we learned later — Book II, 16.4).**

**Three, it was in that space, and we were in that space, and it shakes every eleven seconds.**

---

**So it does not need a screen.**

---

**And I turned the screen anyway.**

---

## And what it did

And in those two minutes it did a thing.

And we saw it only on the recording afterwards — because in those two minutes nobody was watching it.

---

**It came down.**

That is: its top moved downward.

And by how much:

```
1.1 m
```

---

**One point one metres.**

**And at 8.3 it was four point one centimetres.**

**So this is twenty-seven times that.**

---

And the duration:

```
41 s
```

---

**And that is not fast.**

**That is slow — one point one metres in forty-one seconds.**

**That is two point seven centimetres a second.**

---

**And a person's hand moves at about fifty centimetres a second when reaching out.**

---

**So it moved at one nineteenth of the speed of a person reaching out.**

---

## And where it came down to

And the position it came down to I must set out, because it is the last thing in this section.

---

Its top came down to:

```
8.3 m above the floor
```

And that is not a special height.

---

**And at that position, its top's angle of tilt changed.**

And it had been eleven degrees (8.1, leaning toward the opening).

**And now it is:**

```
41 degrees
```

---

**Forty-one degrees.**

---

And where that tilt points.

And we computed it.

---

**At the place where Ilan was sitting.**

---

## And it stayed there

And it stayed there.

And for how long:

```
seven minutes
```

---

**That is: from Marija kneeling to Ilan being able to stand.**

---

**And in those seven minutes it did not move.**

---

**And 0.7 seconds after Ilan stood up, it went back.**

That is: its top rose to nine point four metres and its tilt returned to eleven degrees.

---

**And that going back took:**

```
16 milliseconds
```

---

**Sixteen milliseconds.**

**And that is the speed of 8.2 — its fastest.**

---

## Therefore

**Coming down took it forty-one seconds.**

**Going back took it sixteen milliseconds.**

---

**So coming down, it was slow.**

**And going back, it was fast.**

---

**And it is capable of coming down in sixteen milliseconds.**

**And it did not.**

---

## And the end of this section

And the last line of this section.

The first thing Ilan said after standing up was to Marija.

He said: "Thank you."

And Marija said: "Malo."

Which is Serbian, and she translated for me afterwards:

**"A small thing."**

---

And the second thing Ilan said after standing up was to me.

And what he said was:

**"Turn the screen back."**

---

And I turned it back.

---

And on the screen were still those five words.

```
We waited a long time
```

---

**And beneath them there was another line.**

**And that line it had sent during the two minutes the screen was turned away.**

---

And that line is:

```
No need to be quick
```

---

**Four words.**

---

And the four of us looked at those four words for a long time.

---

**And Marcus spoke first.**

And what he said was:

**"It is talking about itself."**

I said, "Meaning."

He said:

**"Coming down took it forty-one seconds."**

**"And it can do it in sixteen milliseconds."**

He said:

**"And now it says *no need to be quick.***"

---

**"It is explaining why it was slow."**

---

## And why it was slow

And the last sentence of this section is Ilan's.

And he said it still sitting on that four point one degree floor (he had stood up, and then sat down again, and this time by himself).

And what he said was:

**"Not explaining."**

I said, "Then what."

He said:

**"It is apologising."**

---

I said, "For what."

And what he said is the heaviest thing I heard in this whole chapter.

He said:

**"Because three hundred thousand years ago it computed something."**

**"It computed what would happen if it appeared in front of us all at once."**

He said:

**"And it got the answer."**

---

**"And it has just seen it."**
