# 5.5 · Timescales

Marcus talked for nineteen minutes.

Before he started he wiped the board — not the forty-one items (that was the rule), but his own writing from the day before, in the corner.

Then he said, "Three things before I start."

"First, every parameter I'm going to use is uncertain."

"Second, what I compute proves nothing."

"Third, when I've finished you will feel that I have proved something. I have not. So I'm saying it now."

Ilan said, "Then why compute it."

Marcus said:

"Because a computation that proves nothing, if its result is absurd, proves one thing: **our estimate of some parameter is badly wrong.**"

"And which parameter that is can be looked up."

---

He wrote an expression on the board.

I copy it here, because everything after it grew out of it:

> **T ≈ (2 / s) · ln(2 Nₑ)**

He said, "That is the number of generations a favourable variant needs to go to fixation in a population."

"s is the selection coefficient: the proportional excess of offspring of a carrier over a non-carrier."

"Nₑ is the effective population size."

"This is classical. It isn't mine, it's in the textbooks. It's an approximation, good to within a factor of two."

Underneath he wrote:

> **s = 0.01 (a selection pressure usually called strong)**
> **Nₑ = 10 000 (human, median of several independent estimates)**

"ln(20 000) ≈ 9.9."

> **T ≈ (2 / 0.01) × 9.9 ≈ 1 980 generations**

"Round it. Two thousand generations."

Beside it:

> **human generation interval ≈ 25 years**
> **T ≈ 50 000 years**

"One favourable variant, in a population of human size, under a strong selection pressure, needs fifty thousand years to fix."

He stepped back.

"There is nothing wrong with that number. Everyone agrees on that number."

---

"Now," he said.

"Ye Qi listed seven."

He copied her seven lines onto the board.

"The question is how many variants each of those seven requires."

"The answer is: unknown. But a lower bound can be given."

He wrote a figure after each line.

> one · fine control of breathing — **≥ 3**
> two · the shape of the vocal tract — **≥ 4**
> three · high-speed coordination of tongue and lips — **≥ 5**
> four · auditory tuning — **≥ 2**
> five · imitation — **≥ 6**
> six · sign-to-thing — **≥ 4**
> seven · syntax — **≥ 8**

"I got those like this: for the anatomical or neural structure each of the seven depends on, how many loci are known to be involved in its development — that is documented. I took the **minimum** known locus count for each, not the mean, not an estimate."

"So these are lower bounds. The true values are almost certainly larger."

He summed them.

> **total ≥ 32**

---

"Thirty-two variants," he said.

"Now the crucial step, and it is the one place in this computation that requires a judgment."

He wrote two words.

> **parallel · serial**

"If those thirty-two are mutually independent — that is, the return on each does not depend on whether any other has fixed — they can be selected simultaneously. Total time is roughly the time for one."

> **parallel: T ≈ 50 000 years**

Beside it:

> **serial: T ≈ 32 × 50 000 = 1 600 000 years**

"One point six million years."

"A factor of thirty-two apart."

He turned round.

"So the whole thing becomes one question: **how many of the thirty-two are serial.**"

---

"And that question," he said, "Ye Qi has already partly answered."

He pointed at line seven.

"Syntax cannot precede signs. That is a strict ordering constraint — not biological, logical. A rule requires things to combine."

"So the sixth and the seventh are serial."

"And within the sixth," he said, "sign-to-thing needs words. And words need distinguishable sounds. And distinguishable sounds need a tract."

He drew arrows up the board from line seven.

> **seven ← six ← two**

"So at least those three are a serial chain."

"And within each of the seven, the loci depend on one another too — a structure develops in an order. That layer I did not compute, because I cannot. I set it to zero."

"That is: I assumed each of the seven is fully parallel internally. That is the assumption least favourable to me."

He wrote:

> **serial chain length ≥ 3**
> **T ≈ 3 × 50 000 = 150 000 years**

He paused.

"A hundred and fifty thousand years."

"And how much time is available."

---

He wrote three figures.

> **genus Homo appears ≈ 2 500 000 years ago**
> **brain volume begins rapid increase ≈ 800 000 years ago**
> **anatomically modern humans appear ≈ 300 000 years ago**

"With the loosest window — two and a half million years ago to three hundred thousand — that is two point two million years."

"And I compute a need of a hundred and fifty thousand."

He put the pen down.

"**It's enough.**"

"The time is there."

---

The room went quiet a moment.

Ilan said, "So there's no problem."

Marcus said, "So there's no problem."

Ilan said, "Then what were the nineteen minutes for."

Marcus said:

"Because that computation used Ye Qi's seven."

"And Ye Qi's seven are **language.**"

"And there are forty-one items on that board."

---

He walked to the board and stood in front of the forty-one.

He said:

"I ran the same arithmetic over all forty-one."

"For each item, how many loci are known to be involved in the structure it depends on; take the minimum. For each item, whether there is an ordering constraint with any other. I counted an ordering constraint only where there is an explicit logical or developmental dependency, and treated everything else as parallel."

"That took six weeks."

He took a sheet of paper folded in four out of his pocket, opened it, and spread it beside the gap in the desk.

"Here it is."

> **lower bound on total variants: ≥ 1 400**
> **longest serial chain: ≥ 41 links**
> **T ≈ 41 × 50 000 = 2 050 000 years**

"Two point zero five million years."

He looked up.

"And the time available, if we require all forty-one to be finished before three hundred thousand years ago — because anatomically modern humans already have all of them by then — is two point two million."

"Two point zero five against two point two."

He stopped.

"Enough. Just. Seven per cent to spare."

Ilan said, "So still no problem."

Marcus said:

"So I ran it again."

"Because the s I used was zero point zero one."

---

He circled `s = 0.01` on the board.

"That zero point zero one," he said, "is a *strong* selection pressure."

"Let's see how strong."

"s = 0.01 means a carrier of the variant leaves, on average, one per cent more offspring than a non-carrier."

"A hundred children against a hundred and one."

"In one generation that difference is invisible. It is a statistic. And over two thousand generations it is enough to take a variant from one person to everybody."

Beside it he wrote:

> **s = 0.01 → "strong"**
> **s = 0.001 → "typical"**
> **s = 0.1 → "very strong", rare, usually associated with resistance to a lethal disease**

"So zero point zero one is already generous."

"And now," he said, "we take away the seven per cent."

"Because my serial chain length over the forty-one is a **lower bound** I can defend. The real chain is longer — I treated each item as fully parallel internally, and that is certainly wrong."

"So we invert it."

He wiped out `s = 0.01`.

In its place he wrote:

> **s = ?**

"We ask: for all forty-one to be finished in two point two million years, what must s be."

---

Nobody spoke while he worked.

He worked fast, on the board, step by step, stepping back once to look, then going on.

That took perhaps two minutes.

Ilan told me afterwards that for those two minutes he looked at the wall of the building outside the window. He said he did not want to look at the board.

I was watching Marcus's hand. The outside of his little finger rests against the board while he writes, so every line he finishes leaves a faint smear.

---

He finished.

He drew a line under the last row and wrote a number beneath the line.

The number was:

> **s ≈ 0.41**

---

He stepped back.

He said:

"Zero point four one."

"Meaning a carrier leaves forty-one per cent more offspring than a non-carrier."

"A hundred children against a hundred and forty-one."

"Every generation. For two thousand generations. For each of the forty-one items."

---

Nobody spoke.

Marcus folded the sheet of paper back into four and put it in his pocket.

Then he said one more thing.

He said it flatly, because he was not stating a meaning. He was reporting the name of a number.

He said:

"There is precedent for that figure in biology."

"It is rare in nature. It occurs mainly in two situations."

"The first is resistance to a lethal epidemic — the disease arrives, those without the variant die, so s can reach a few tenths."

He paused.

"The second is agriculture."

"A farmer has a thousand wheat plants. He keeps the four hundred with the largest heads for seed and eats the other six hundred."

"The selection coefficient of that operation is about zero point four."

He capped the pen.

"That operation has a name in agriculture."

"It is called **selective breeding.**"
