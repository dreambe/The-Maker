# 5.7 · The Word

The first thing Lu Ting did after standing up was go and turn the light on.

He walked to the door — Ilan was still there, and stepped half back to let him past — and pressed the switch.

The fluorescent tube came on. The old kind, which flickers three times before it holds.

First flicker: the room all white. Second: dark. Third: it settles.

He said, "Too dark. Can't read the board."

He went back and stood in front of it.

---

He looked at the forty-one items for a while, and then said:

"I'm going to say some words first."

"All of them wrong. I'm saying them in order to rule them out."

---

**"The first: *create.*"**

He said:

"That one can't be used."

"Not because it's wrong. Because it's too large."

"That word has been in use in human language for thousands of years and there is far too much hanging off it — temples, scriptures, a whole apparatus about purpose and meaning."

"Say that word and you are no longer talking about our forty-one items. You are talking about something else, and somebody else finished talking about that a long time ago."

He shook his head.

"And it isn't accurate. *Create* means from nothing to something."

"And our forty-one items are not from nothing to something."

"Our forty-one items say: **something was already there, and then somebody moved it.**"

---

**"The second: *design.*"**

"Better. More accurate. It says there were drawings, an intention, something thought out in advance."

"And it says only half."

"Design is a thing on paper. When the design is done, it still has to be built."

He pointed at the board.

"And our forty-one items are not forty-one ideas. They are forty-one things **already in people's bodies.**"

"Your wisdom teeth are not a design. Your wisdom teeth are in your mouth."

---

**"The third: *manufacture.*"**

"The trouble with that one is the raw material."

"Manufacture is making a product out of raw material. And in that word the raw material is passive, lifeless, at the disposal of whoever has it."

"And on this planet, before people, there were three thousand million years of living things."

He paused.

"People were not made out of clay."

"People came out of **other living things.**"

"And those living things had their own way of going, and had been going for three thousand million years."

"*Manufacture* does not acknowledge those three thousand million years."

---

**"The fourth: *modify.*"**

"A lot of people will like this one, because it sounds mild. It means: there was a thing, and it was altered."

He said, "And it does not hold."

"Because of the gap."

He went and stood in front of item 21 — his own, seven lines, an entire morning's work.

"Modification is gradual. Modification leaves intermediates."

"And the gap means exactly this: **there are no intermediates.**"

He touched the seven lines lightly with the back of his hand.

"Thirty years. I have been looking for intermediates. Everything I found is on this side of the gap or on that side."

"There is nothing in the gap."

"So it is not modification."

---

**"The fifth: *introduce.*"**

He said, "That one I used for thirty years."

"That is the word in my unpublished manuscript. I wrote *some unknown mechanism introduced a new form.*"

"I used it because it is safe. It does not say who introduced. It does not even say whether there was anyone. It can mean a migration, a climate event, a process we do not yet know."

He looked at us.

"And for thirty years that manuscript has been in a drawer."

"So that word's safety had a price."

"It is safe enough to say nothing."

---

He put his hand down.

He stood in front of the board with the fluorescent tube above him, and that light makes an old man's face look terrible — I remember noticing, and wondering whether he knew what he looked like.

He said:

"So there is one word left."

---

He did not say it at once.

He said something else first.

He said:

"I should account for where I got this word."

"From 1968 to 1972 I was in the army. Engineers."

"What I did for those four years was put things in places."

"Anything: bridges, pipe, generators, communication lines. We did not design them; design was somebody else's business. We did not build them; building was the factory's business."

"What we did was the last step: **take a thing that has already been made, move it to a predetermined position, and let it begin to function there.**"

He paused.

"There was a specific word for that step where I served."

"It is in the documents, in the orders, in the summary reports."

"The word is:"

---

**"Deployment."**

---

The tube was humming. Those old tubes have a faint continuous hum you do not normally hear.

I heard it then.

---

Lu Ting said the following in front of the whiteboard. Not fast, not slow. He did not sound like a man announcing anything. He sounded like a man explaining the definition of a technical term — which is what he was doing.

He said:

"That word has four components. I'll take them one at a time."

**"First, the thing deployed is already made."**

"Deployment does not include manufacture. You cannot deploy a thing that has not been built."

"So the word does not require us to answer *how were people made.* It leaves that question somewhere else."

He glanced at items 22 and 23.

"And it fits what we have in hand: that sequence did not grow. It was written in."

**"Second, deployment has a position."**

"Deployment is always putting a thing in **a place.** Not into the world — onto a coordinate."

"A position implies a choice of site. A choice of site implies a reason."

He said, "So the word forces a question we have not asked."

"Not *why make people.*"

"*Why here.*"

**"Third, deployment has a time."**

"Deployment has a moment. The order comes down, the thing arrives, it is installed, it is switched on. Before, not there. After, there."

He pointed at item 21.

"The gap."

"The gap is not a deficiency. The gap is a moment."

**"Fourth,"** he said, "the fourth component is the hard part of the word."

He was silent a long while.

In that silence Ilan finally came back from the doorway and sat down.

Lu Ting said:

"Deployment has a function."

"You deploy a thing because you want it **to do something** in that position."

"A bridge is for crossing. Pipe is for carrying. A generator is for supplying. A line is for transmitting."

"Nobody deploys a thing that does nothing."

He put his hand flat on the board.

"So the moment you say the word, it brings a question with it, and the question is not *who are we.*"

"It is:"

---

**"What are we for."**

---

Nobody spoke.

---

Then Lu Ting did a thing that I still think is the most decent thing I have seen.

He turned from the board to face the four of us and said:

"Now I'll say why it's me who says it."

"I know all four of you had the word. I could see it. In those three minutes I looked at each of you."

He said:

"I am not saying this word because I am brave."

"I am saying it because I am cheap."

---

He said:

"He. You are thirty-nine. You have just been buried once at a conference. If you say this word it goes into the minutes, the minutes go into the file, and in five years somebody digs it out and you are finished in this profession. You have thirty years left to live."

"Ilan. Forty-six. You have already paid seven years, a marriage, and a professional reputation for this. Say the word and what's left goes too."

"Ye Qi. Thirty-three. You have only just started. What you have on forgetting is real, and it is enough for a whole career on its own. Get yourself tied to this word and nobody will read that work seriously again."

"Deng. Twenty-nine. Three years without a job. Say this word and you will never get inside a mathematics department again."

He stopped.

Then he said:

"I am seventy-four."

"My wife died five years ago. I have no children. My students have their own laboratories now; they don't need me, and they will not be harmed by anyone laughing at me."

"I retire next year. Everything I was going to get in this life I have got, and what I did not get I am not going to."

"The only thing I have left is a manuscript that sat in a drawer for thirty years."

He knocked on item 21.

"And it is here now."

---

He put his hand down.

He said:

"So let me say it."

"It costs me nothing to say this word."

"It costs you."

"This is not a question of courage."

**"It is a question of cost."**

---

In that moment I understood something I had not understood in three months.

From the day Han Yin explained budget categories, every step of this had been an accounting.

**The money had to belong to a category, because the category decides whether money comes out.**

**My finding had to become a subordinate clause, because a clause is what passes review.**

**Nine of eleven refused, because each of them had done their own arithmetic.**

**Twenty-two of forty-one items have no revenue, because the cost was paid first.**

**And now a man of seventy-four stood at a whiteboard, priced the saying of one word across five people, and chose the cheapest.**

---

**The cheapest was himself.**

---

He put the pen in the tray under the board.

Then he said the last thing he said that day.

He said:

"Minute it."

He looked at me.

"He, you do it. You run this. What you write is what counts."

"One line will do."

---

I opened my laptop and made a new document.

I typed the line.

Then I looked at it for a long time.

It reads:

> 14 April, 17:41. Group discussion. A working term is proposed for the phenomenon under investigation: **deployment.**

> Proposed by: Lu Ting.
