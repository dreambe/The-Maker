# 5.9 · Somebody Erased *Evolution*

I got in at 07:20 the next morning.

I was first. The door was locked and I unlocked it.

I pressed the light on my way in.

The tube flickered three times. On the first, the room went all white.

---

In that first white I saw that a word was gone from the whiteboard.

---

I should say where it had been.

On the morning of the eleventh of April, when we began the forty-one items, Lu Ting wrote a heading across the top of the board.

I was beside him while he wrote it. He wrote it carelessly, one line, and when he had finished he thought it was crooked and smudged the edge of the first letter with his thumb.

The line was:

> **Phenomena that cannot be explained by evolution**

It stood at the top of the board, centre-left, and it hung there for four days.

---

And now the line reads:

> **Phenomena that cannot be explained by　　　　**

---

*Evolution* is not there.

Where it stood there is nothing.

**Nothing was written in its place.**

---

I stood in the doorway with my hand still on the switch.

The tube had settled and the hum had begun.

I stood there about a minute.

Then I did something: I counted.

**The word *evolution* had appeared five times on that board.**

The first, in the heading.

The second, next to item 1, wisdom teeth — Ilan wrote it on the first day: *(evolutionary lag)*, in brackets.

The third, in the *revenue* column of item 6, body hair, inside the sentence we filled in.

The fourth, in the sentence of Ye Qi's that Marcus copied into the bottom left of the board on the first afternoon: *evolution is a word that requires no reason.*

The fifth, beside Marcus's field of arithmetic from yesterday, in small letters: *all of the above computed within the evolutionary framework.*

**Five.**

**One had been erased.**

**The one in the heading.**

---

The other four were all still there.

The one in brackets was there. The one in the revenue column was there. Ye Qi's sentence in the bottom left was there. Marcus's small note was there.

Only the one in the heading was gone.

---

I want to set down what I thought then, because I have thought about it for years since, and I am still not certain I have it right.

**Erasing the word from the heading is not the same act as erasing those four.**

Those four are the word in use. To use a word is to employ it in a sentence — it is a component, it is explaining one specific thing.

**And the one in the heading was an authorisation.**

The *evolution* in the heading did not refer to any of the items. It said: **what these forty-one have in common is that this is what they stand against.**

It set a coordinate frame for the whole board.

**Erasing it is not a denial of the word.**

**It is the withdrawal of the frame.**

---

And whoever erased it wrote nothing in its place.

I want that clear, because it is the crucial part.

**That person could have written *deployment.***

The word had been said at 17:41 the previous afternoon, was already in my document, already had a proposer and a time.

**And that person did not write it.**

**That person left, where the word had been, a gap the width of the word.**

---

I went over.

I stood in front of the board, under the heading.

That patch is cleaner than the board around it.

Let me be exact about what that means. A whiteboard that has been used for years is not uniformly white. It carries a very thin grey that will not come off — the residue of hundreds of erasures, the corpses of handwriting. From across the room it is white. Close up it is pale grey.

And the place where those letters had stood had been **freshly** wiped.

Freshly wiped is whiter than old.

So that gap is not a hole.

**It is a patch whiter than everything around it.**

---

I put out my hand and touched it.

I do not know why I touched it. I touched it.

My fingers went across the line, over *by*, then over the empty space where the word had been, and then off the end.

That stretch is **smoother** than the rest.

The rest has a very slight resistance to it — that thin grey.

And that stretch has none.

It is smooth, clean, and there is nothing on it.

**It is the cleanest place on that board.**

---

I took my hand away and stepped back and looked at the heading.

> **Phenomena that cannot be explained by　　　　**

---

And in that moment I understood something.

Something I had come within an inch of understanding three months before, in a museum, in front of an empty glass case between two skeletons.

Lu Ting said a thing to me that day, and it is the first line of his thirty-year manuscript:

**"A gap is not insufficient data. A gap has a shape."**

---

I stood at the board looking at the space where the word had been.

It has a shape.

It is one word wide and one line high.

Its edges are clean — whoever erased it did it neatly, without catching the *by* on the left or the edge on the right.

**It is the position left behind after something has been precisely removed.**

**And the shape of that position is the shape of the thing removed.**

---

Three months earlier in the museum, the label on the empty case was blank.

I assumed at the time that the museum had not made the label yet.

I know now that was not it.

**An empty case has nothing in it, and it has dimensions.**

**Its glass was made to the size of something. Its plinth was made to the weight of something. Its position was fixed by where something ought to stand between those two skeletons.**

**It is a container built to order for a thing that does not exist.**

---

And that heading, from that morning, is the same thing.

> **Phenomena that cannot be explained by　　　　**

It has a gap.

The gap has a width, a height, edges, and a surface cleaner than anything around it.

**And it is empty.**

**And forty-one items hang beneath it.**

---

I stood there a long time.

Then I heard steps on the stairs — Ye Qi, who came in early that day because she was going to maternity.

Before she pushed the door open, I did one thing.

**I did not write the word back.**

**I did not write *deployment* either.**

I took a pen out of the plastic tray under the board — the one Marcus had put back the day before; the cap was still separate, at the other end of the tray.

I held it.

I stood in front of the gap for a moment.

Then I put the pen back.

---

That gap stayed on that board for three years.

In three years that room went through two air conditioners and one rewiring, and seventeen people came through it, of whom eleven stayed a day.

What was on that board changed many times. The forty-one items became sixty-three. The *revenue* column filled in nineteen, had seven struck out, and gained four more. Marcus's number was revised twice, and after the second revision it was zero point three eight.

And the heading did not change in three years.

**Nobody wrote *evolution* back.**

**And nobody wrote anything else.**

---

I still remember how that patch felt.

It is the one place on that board where your hand meets no resistance.
