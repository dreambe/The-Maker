# 5.8 · The Thirty-Seven Minutes After

I finished typing that line at 17:41.

We broke up at 18:18.

Thirty-seven minutes.

In those thirty-seven minutes nobody discussed anything.

---

**17:41 to 17:44.**

I was looking at the screen.

The cursor blinked at the end of the line.

I remember doing something stupid: I made the line bold, then not bold, then bold again, then not.

Four times.

I do not know why. I think I was waiting for a reason not to save it.

At 17:44 I pressed save.

The filename was a date I typed without thinking.

**It is the Tracing Back's first record.**

---

**17:45.**

Ilan stood up, took something out of his coat pocket, and went out.

He did not say where.

He has been sober eight years. He never gave up cigarettes.

I learned later that he went to the window in the third-floor stairwell. That is the only window in the building that opens all the way, and everyone who smokes in that building goes there.

He was there nineteen minutes.

That is not the length of one cigarette.

---

**17:46 to 17:52.**

Lu Ting was packing up.

He packed slowly, and in a strange order: he put his coat on first and then began clearing the desk. So he was packing bent over, in a coat, one item at a time.

His manuscript. The tacky rubber band. His reading glasses (he has two pairs, one worn, one in a case, and the one in the case is never used). A vacuum flask. A pill box.

His pill box has six compartments, not seven.

I looked at that pill box for a while.

I was thinking that my mother's has seven.

I was thinking about why anyone makes a six-compartment box.

I was thinking that a six-compartment box, once emptied, must be started again, and that the day it is started again is not the same weekday as the day it was started before.

I thought about that for about two minutes.

**I know what I was doing. I was thinking about something entirely unimportant in order not to think about the important thing.**

I still do it. It is the one useful skill I learned that day.

---

**17:53.**

Ye Qi spoke.

She said:

"I want to go somewhere tomorrow."

I said, "Where."

She said, "Maternity."

I waited, because I expected her to explain.

She did not.

She said, "I know somebody there. I want to spend a day."

I said, "Watching what."

She said:

"I want to spend a day watching newborns."

"I want to see the thing when it comes out and can do nothing at all."

She took the rucksack off her knees and put it on the desk, both hands still on it.

"I have worked on the clearance of memory for six years."

"This afternoon something occurred to me."

"A newborn can do nothing and remembers nothing."

"And its forgetting is already installed."

She looked up.

"It has not one memory yet, and the clearance pathways are already there, waiting."

She said:

"That is not degradation."

"That is **pre-installed.**"

Then she stopped.

She took the rucksack back onto her knees, put her hands on it, and looked down at them.

That was the last thing she said that day.

---

**17:58.**

Marcus was still in front of the board.

He had not come back and sat down since finishing that number. At one point Lu Ting went over to speak and he stepped aside; when Lu Ting had finished he stepped back.

His back was to us the whole time.

At 17:58 I noticed his shoulders moving.

---

I want this written clearly, because I have heard versions of it since, and all of them are more presentable than what happened.

**He was not weeping silently. There was no shaking of the shoulders and somebody laying a hand on him.**

**He was crying badly.**

It went like this: first a long intake of breath that broke twice. Then he raised a hand and wiped the back of it across his face, hard, the way you wipe off something dirty. Then another breath in. This one had a sound to it — a short sound, like a word swallowed back.

Then he bent forward and put both hands on the tray under the board.

The tray is plastic and shallow and holds pens. When he put his weight on it, it made a noise, because it cannot hold a man's weight.

He stayed like that, bent, head down, facing the board.

About forty seconds.

Nobody went over.

I want that clear too: **nobody went over.**

Not because we are cold. Because all four of us understood the same thing at the same time: he is twenty-nine, he is the youngest of us, he has just spent an afternoon being used by the four of us as an instrument that supplies numbers, and he is now doing the thing he least wants us to see.

**Going over would have meant he could never work in that room again.**

So we looked elsewhere.

Ilan was out. Lu Ting was closing his glasses case. Ye Qi was looking at her hands.

I was looking at the wall of the building outside the window, which by then was entirely dark.

---

**18:01.**

He straightened up.

He wiped his face with the back of his hand and spoke.

He said:

"Something's wrong with my eyes."

Nobody answered.

He said, "The light. The frequency of this light."

He pointed at the tube overhead.

"Fifty hertz. Some people are sensitive to it."

Lu Ting said, "Mm."

That one syllable.

---

Marcus went out to the sink at the end of the corridor to wash his face.

He came back with his face wet, and the front of his hair wet, and his eyes red.

**He did not explain. We did not ask.**

He went back to the board and stood in front of the number.

Then he said one thing, the last thing he said that day, and the only sentence in those thirty-seven minutes that had anything to do with the subject.

He said:

"The number I worked out this afternoon has nothing to do with item forty-one."

I said, "Meaning."

He said:

"Zero point four one is about how the forty-one items came to be. It is a technical parameter of **deployment.**"

"That business is finished."

He raised a hand and pointed at item 41 — his own, from three days earlier: **there is more information in there than a signature requires.**

He said:

"And this one has not been dealt with."

---

He said:

"I've been thinking about one thing all afternoon, and then I had a problem."

"What I was thinking was:"

"If in that sequence, besides the checksum, there is a further stretch."

"And if that stretch is long enough to hold a sentence."

He paused.

"Then," he said, "it is not a signature."

"A signature means *I made this.*"

"And a sentence means —"

He did not finish.

He put his hand down.

He said:

"A sentence means somebody spoke to us."

---

**18:09.**

Ilan came back from the stairwell.

He smelt strongly of smoke. He did not sit down; he stood in the doorway and said:

"Outside that window upstairs, there's a nest."

"Where the drainpipe turns."

"This year's. Three in it."

When he had said that, everybody in the room looked at him.

He said:

"I'm just saying."

Then he came in and put his laptop in his bag.

---

**18:18.**

We broke up.

We did nothing to the board that night — no wiping, no photograph, no copy.

The forty-one items were still up.

The *revenue* column was still twenty-two blank.

Marcus's number was still in the bottom right corner with a line under it.

The tube was still on, because the last one out was me, and I forgot the switch.

I remembered on the stairs and went back up.

When I pushed the door open the room was lit and empty.

The whiteboard faces the door.

Forty-one items, two columns, five hands, three colours.

Standing in the doorway, what came into my head was not those words.

**What came into my head was my mother's pill box.**

**Seven compartments. The Wednesday one.**

**Filled by me.**

**She does not know somebody arranged the order.**

I pressed the switch and the room went dark.

I shut the door and locked it.
