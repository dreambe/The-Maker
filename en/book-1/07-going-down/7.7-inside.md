# 7.7 · Inside

Ilan went first.

I second.

---

## How we went

That opening is zero point four one metres across.

And our six layers, worn, make our shoulders about zero point four eight metres.

**So we had to take them off.**

---

I write this because it is the most absurd thing in those forty-one hours, and it is true.

At two thousand and ninety-seven metres down, in a pressure vessel zero point four four metres wide, we took off the outer three layers.

And with them off, our shoulders are zero point three nine.

**And that opening is zero point four one.**

---

And with three layers off, what is the environment.

That vessel is at twenty-nine degrees (7.5).

**And below that opening we did not know.**

---

Before going down, Ilan did one thing.

He took something out of the tool bag — a thin line with a thermometer tied to it.

He lowered it through the opening.

Eleven metres (the length of the line).

**Then he drew it up and read it.**

And the reading:

```
four point one degrees
```

**The same as that surface.**

---

I said, "It's four degrees inside."

He said, "Mm."

I said, "Then we won't freeze to death."

He said:

**"We'll be cold. Four degrees will make us hypothermic in two hours."**

He said:

"And all we have is what is left of the forty-one hours."

He worked it out.

**He said: "Three hours twenty."**

---

Where that number comes from.

Forty-one hours, from twenty past one in the afternoon of day forty-one.

We entered the cage at eight on the morning of day forty-two (eighteen hours forty gone).

Three hours forty of descent (to eleven forty).

**And the ascent takes seven hours forty.**

**So we must begin the ascent seven hours forty before the forty-one hours end.**

**The forty-one hours end at twenty past six on the morning of day forty-three.**

**So we must begin the ascent at twenty to eleven on the night of day forty-two.**

**And it was eleven forty in the morning.**

---

**Eleven hours.**

And Ilan said three hours twenty because he had also counted two other things:

```
dressing and undressing          40 minutes both ways
through the opening and back      20 minutes both ways
safety margin                     Ilan's insistence, 1 hour
the rest                          reserved for "if something goes wrong"
```

**So usable: three hours twenty.**

---

## That opening

Ilan went first.

And how he went I write, because it is unpresentable and necessary.

That opening is zero point four one metres.

**And to pass through a zero point four one metre circular hole, a person must put one arm up and one arm down and take the shoulders through at an angle.**

**Which means that for those few seconds neither hand can hold on.**

And how deep it was below, we did not know.

---

So what Ilan did was:

**he tied a line round his waist and the other end to the cage structure.**

And while tying it he said:

"He."

I said, "Mm."

He said:

"If I don't speak after I'm down, don't follow."

He said:

"Go back up, and tell them what is down here."

---

I said, "What counts as speaking."

He paused.

Then he said:

**"I'll count."**

**"When I'm down I'll keep counting. You listen."**

**"When I stop, you go."**

---

## The numbers he counted

He went down.

And he counted.

And I listened above.

And he counted one every two seconds.

I remember the numbers, because I was listening.

**One. Two. Three. Four.**

And after *four* there was a soft noise — his feet touching.

**Five. Six. Seven.**

And after *seven*, he stopped.

---

**He stopped for about two seconds.**

And in those two seconds my hand was already on the rim of the opening.

**And after two seconds he spoke.**

And what he said was not a number.

He said:

**"Come down."**

---

I said, "You stopped."

He said:

**"I know."**

**"Come down."**

---

## Going down

I do not write my own passage through.

Only what I thought in the three seconds of it.

**I thought about my mother's pill box.**

Seven compartments.

And that opening is a circle.

**And I do not know why I thought about that in those three seconds.**

---

## Inside

I landed.

And the first thing I did after landing was stand up (the opening is two point one metres above the floor; Ilan caught me).

And the second was to switch on my headlamp.

---

And what I saw I write in the order I saw it, because the order matters.

---

**One: the shape of the space.**

It is not a cave.

It is a **cylinder.**

```
diameter    11 m
height      4.1 m
```

And its top is the surface we came down through (from below, it is the cylinder's roof).

And its floor is a plane.

**And its wall is curved, because it is a cylinder.**

---

And one thing about that cylinder, because it is the first impossible thing I saw.

**It has no seams.**

Between roof and wall, no seam.

Between wall and floor, no seam.

**On the wall there is no weld, no line of rivets, no boundary between one plate and another.**

---

**So it was not assembled.**

---

And in that moment my first thought — and I set it down honestly — was:

**this is natural.**

Because a thing without seams, in my experience, is only ever a natural thing.

**And I dismissed that thought zero point three seconds later.**

Because it is a cylinder.

**And natural things do not make cylinders.**

---

**Two: temperature.**

Four point one degrees.

And that temperature, on a body with three layers off, is a very specific sensation.

**It is not cold. It is a sensation of *being taken away from.***

And I was there three hours twenty.

**And in the last half hour my fingers began to disobey.**

---

**Three: light.**

Before I switched on the headlamp, it was dark.

**And not entirely dark.**

I want this clear, because it is among the most important things I noticed there.

Before switching the lamp on I kept my eyes open for about four seconds.

**And in those four seconds I saw a little.**

Not a shape. A very faint, uniform, colourless brightness.

And whence.

**From the wall.**

---

And we measured it afterwards. That brightness is:

```
1.1 x 10^-4 lux
```

**And moonlight is 0.1 lux.**

**So that brightness is a thousandth of moonlight.**

**And the lower limit of the fully dark-adapted human eye is about 10^-6 lux.**

**So it is within the visible range, and only just.**

---

And what it is.

We learned afterwards: it is not luminescence.

**It is that wall's material, at four point one degrees, radiating — and its thermal radiation has a very faint visible tail.**

**That is: it is warm, and warmth has a tail in the visible band.**

---

**So the light in that space is the thing's own heat.**

**Three hundred thousand years.**

---

**Four — and this is the core of this section — sound.**

I write this very carefully.

---

I looked at that cylinder for about forty seconds with the lamp on.

And in those forty seconds I took my hood off.

**I took it off because I needed to listen.**

And why I needed to listen — my thought at the time was simple:

**if there is a thing down here maintaining four degrees, then it is spending energy.**

**And a thing spending energy makes a noise.**

---

I took the hood off.

And in that first second what my ears heard was myself — my breathing, my heartbeat, the rub of clothing.

And after those.

---

**There is a sound.**

---

I write that sound precisely, because I have been asked about it a hundred times since, and I have given the same description every time.

**It has two components.**

---

**The first component: a continuous, low-frequency hum.**

The frequency, measured afterwards:

```
41 hertz
```

**Forty-one hertz.**

And forty-one hertz is above the lower limit of human hearing (about twenty) and below the speech band.

**It is a frequency you can hear and will not notice — unless you are somewhere entirely still.**

And its loudness:

```
21 decibels
```

**Twenty-one decibels, which is the background of a very quiet bedroom.**

---

**The second component — and this one is the whole reward of taking off the hood.**

Above that continuous hum there is something **discontinuous.**

A very soft, very short noise.

**And it has an interval.**

---

I listened about two minutes and counted.

The interval is:

**eleven seconds.**

---

**Every eleven seconds, one noise.**

---

## That noise

At the end of this section, that noise.

Because that noise is the first thing we found down there that is **not structure.**

The cylinder, the temperature, the light — those are properties of an object.

**And a noise that occurs every eleven seconds is not a property.**

**It is an act.**

---

I said, "Ilan."

He said, "I hear it."

I said, "Eleven seconds."

He said:

**"I've counted thirty-seven."**

That is: he took his hood off two minutes before I did.

---

I said, "What is it."

And what he said is the second thing he said down there (the first was *come down*).

He said:

**"He, don't take it for the sound of a machine."**

I said, "Meaning."

He said:

**"A machine's period is set by its design. It has no reason to be eleven seconds."**

**"It could be ten. It could be twelve. It could be zero point seven."**

He said:

**"And eleven seconds is a breathing interval."**

---

I said, "What."

He said:

**"Not a human's. A human's is four seconds."**

**"Eleven seconds is the breathing interval of something very large and very slow."**

**"If it breathed."**

---

I said, "It doesn't breathe. It doesn't need oxygen."

He said:

**"Right."**

**"So it is not breathing."**

**"So it is something else, and its period is eleven seconds."**

---

## And what I did in that moment

And in that moment I did a thing I discussed with nobody.

**I switched off my headlamp.**

And with it off, the space returned to that thousandth-of-moonlight brightness.

**And in that brightness I listened to that noise.**

Eleven seconds.

One.

Eleven seconds.

One.

---

And on the ninth I did a thing.

**I spoke.**

And what I said was not a question.

What I said was my name.

**I said: "My name is Shen He."**

---

And after that, eleven seconds.

**And after those eleven seconds the noise came.**

**On time.**

**The same as the nine before it.**

---

And I waited another eleven seconds.

**Another came.**

---

**It did not respond to me.**

---

And what I felt in that moment was not disappointment.

**It was a very clear, almost administrative recognition.**

**I recognised that what I had just done was to send a signal into a place where I did not know whether there was a receiver.**

**And that thing has been doing that for three hundred thousand years.**

**It wrote a sequence into a species' genome, and waited.**

---

**And I had just done the same thing.**

**And I waited eleven seconds.**

**And it waited three hundred thousand years.**

---

Depth reading (Ilan's wrist unit): two thousand and ninety-nine metres.

Time: eleven fifty-eight in the morning.

**Remaining: three hours and two minutes.**
