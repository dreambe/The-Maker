# 7.9 · A Line Wiped Clean

Three forty-one in the afternoon.

Time to go.

---

## The two hours before

I will not write out those two hours.

Because in them we did what we always do: measure, sample, photograph, record.

And their product is a list, and that list became the Tracing Back's core archive.

I give only its counts:

```
measurement points    411
samples               41 tubes
photographs           1 100
audio                 3 hours 20 (continuous)
handwritten notes     Ilan 19 pages, me 11
```

---

And one thing in them I write separately, because it bears on this section.

At ten past two we did something we had not intended to do.

**We looked for that noise.**

---

That is: where does the pulse every eleven seconds come from.

And we looked for forty minutes.

And the method was an accelerometer, taking the pulse's amplitude point by point on the wall, the roof, and the floor of that cylinder.

**Because a vibration source's amplitude falls with distance.**

**So where the amplitude is greatest is nearest the source.**

---

And we measured forty-one points.

And the result:

```
wall     amplitude 4.1 x 10^-7 m (uniform; the forty-one points differ by under 0.03)
roof     amplitude 4.1 x 10^-7 m
floor    amplitude 4.1 x 10^-7 m
```

---

**Forty-one points, the same amplitude.**

---

And what that means Ilan said when the measuring was done.

He said:

**"There is no source."**

I said, "Meaning."

He said:

**"A point source's amplitude falls with distance."**

**"A line source falls more slowly."**

**"A surface source barely falls at all near the surface."**

**"And an amplitude that is the same everywhere has one possible cause."**

He said:

**"The whole thing is vibrating."**

---

**"It is not that there is something inside making a noise."**

**"It is that this thing, as a whole, moves once every eleven seconds."**

---

## Three forty-one in the afternoon

The three hours twenty were up.

And Ilan said: "Go."

And we began packing.

---

And packing, in that environment, took nineteen minutes.

Because everything goes back: forty-one sample tubes, the accelerometer, the thermometers, the camera, the probe, the sampling knife, our three layers.

**And nothing may be left.**

That is the treaty requirement (full return, 7.3).

**And down there it has a second meaning.**

---

And that second meaning Ilan said when we were half packed.

He said:

**"He, we put this place back as it was."**

I said, "We're taking everything anyway."

He said:

**"That isn't what I mean."**

He said:

**"Those eleven lines you drew."**

---

## Those eleven lines

I write down the three minutes of that discussion, because it is the close of this whole chapter.

---

I said, "They can't be wiped."

He said, "Why can't they."

I said:

**"They are drawn in dust. The dust is one micrometre thick. Wipe it and that whole patch of dust is gone."**

I said:

**"And that would be more conspicuous than leaving the eleven lines."**

---

He said, "Then leave them."

I said, "Mm."

---

And after that he said something that stopped us both for about twenty seconds.

He said:

**"It will know."**

---

I said, "Know what."

He said:

**"It moves every eleven seconds. It is cleaning."**

**"And the next time it cleans, it will meet those eleven lines."**

He said:

**"And it will know somebody was here."**

---

## And what I did in that moment

And in that moment I did a thing, and I am still not sure it was right.

And I set down honestly what I did and what I thought.

---

**I went back to the wall, crouched, and drew a twelfth line in that dust.**

---

And I drew that one differently from the eleven.

The eleven were drawn offhand, at various lengths, to measure the blurring rate.

**And the twelfth I drew slowly.**

**And it is a shape.**

---

The shape I drew is:

**a circle.**

---

And that circle is about zero point zero four metres across.

And having drawn it, I drew a line across it.

And that line divides the circle into an upper and a lower half.

---

Why I drew it, because it is not a symbol; it is a **measurement.**

---

**A circle is a shape recognisable as non-natural.**

Because in a randomly disturbed dust layer every trace is irregular.

**And a circle does not occur naturally.**

---

**And that crossing line is a scale.**

Because it divides the circle in two, and if the dust re-accumulates, that line will vanish first (it is finer than the circle's edge) and the circle after.

**So a later observer, seeing the circle without the line, knows that a stretch of time has passed.**

**Seeing both, knows the time is short.**

---

**So it is not a message.**

**It is a clock.**

---

And having drawn it, I stood up.

And Ilan was beside me.

And he looked at that circle for a while.

Then he said:

**"You are leaving something for it."**

I said:

**"I'm leaving something for whoever comes next."**

He said:

**"Who comes next."**

---

And I did not answer.

**Because in that moment I did not know who comes next.**

**And when I was forty-one I knew.**

**And it is not a person.**

---

## Going up

We went up through that opening.

And going up is harder than coming down — the opening is two point one metres above the floor, and we had to climb.

And the way was: Ilan lifted me, and I pulled him from above.

**And before he came up, he did a thing.**

---

## What he did

And this one I saw on the camera's recording, because I was already above.

And that passage is eleven seconds long.

---

In those eleven seconds what he did was:

**he switched off his headlamp.**

And with it off, the space is that thousandth-of-moonlight.

**And he stood in that brightness for about eight seconds.**

**And did not move.**

---

And at the eighth second, the noise came.

**And after that noise, he raised his hand.**

**And put it on the wall.**

---

And his hand stayed on that wall about two seconds.

**Then he took it away, switched on the lamp, and came up.**

---

And having come up, he said nothing.

And I did not ask.

**And three years later, watching that recording, I asked him.**

And what he said was:

"I was saying thank you."

I said, "To what."

He said:

**"Don't know."**

He said:

**"That wall is four degrees. And my hands were cold by then."**

**"And it was warm."**

---

## That line wiped clean

And at the end of this section, that.

And we learned it the following year.

---

The following year we went down again.

(That descent was unlike this one, because that time we had a better cage, a faster cable, and time beyond the forty-one hours. And what happened is Chapter Eight.)

And on that second descent, the first thing I did was walk to that wall.

---

And I looked at the dust.

**And the dust is there.**

**Thickness: one micrometre.**

**Uniform.**

---

And I looked for the eleven lines.

**Gone.**

**All eleven, gone.**

---

And I looked for the circle.

---

**There.**

---

I write exactly what I saw.

The circle is there.

**And its edge is as sharp as when I drew it.**

And the line across it —

---

**Gone.**

---

## What that means

I crouched at that wall a long time.

And what I worked out I set down here.

---

**The eleven lines are gone. And the circle is there.**

And those two things cannot be explained by one mechanism.

**Because if dust re-accumulated and covered the lines — it would cover the circle too.**

**Because the circle is finer than the eleven lines (I drew it slowly, so shallow).**

---

**So it was not covered.**

---

**And if it was shaken apart — the circle would be shaken apart too.**

---

**So it was not shaken apart.**

---

**So those eleven lines were taken away.**

**And that circle was left.**

---

## And that crossing line

And that crossing line is gone.

And crouched at that wall I worked out why.

---

And that answer took me about two minutes, and it is the last thing in this chapter.

---

**That crossing line was my measure of time.**

**It is finer than the circle's edge, so it vanishes first.**

**So I used it to tell a later observer: this much time has passed.**

---

**And it is gone.**

**And the circle is there.**

---

**So a later observer sees: a circle, with no line.**

**So a later observer reads: a long time has passed.**

---

**And only a year had.**

---

## And this is that thing

**It read the circle.**

**It read that the circle is a non-natural shape.**

**It took away the eleven offhand lines.**

**It left the circle.**

---

**And it took away the crossing line.**

---

**And that crossing line is the clock.**

---

I crouched at that wall looking at a circle with no line.

And in that moment I understood something, and it is the second most important thing I understood in the whole of the Tracing Back.

---

**It is not cleaning.**

**It is answering.**

---

**And what it answered is:**

**"I do not use your clock."**
