# 6.7 · Ruling Out: A Hoax

The sixth line.

Nine days.

**And it is the strongest conclusion of the eight.**

**And the only one whose ruling-out made the whole thing worse.**

---

Why it is on the table.

Because it will certainly be asked.

In three years of the Tracing Back I briefed fourteen different bodies. In eleven of those fourteen, somebody asked this question.

Phrased differently. The polite version: *have you considered the possibility of artificial construction.*

I met the impolite version once: *who is playing you.*

**And the question is correct. It ought to be asked.**

So I did it.

---

My method was to **list what the hoax would require.**

Not to guess who. Not to reason about motive.

**To cost it.**

A hoax is a project. And a project has a bill of quantities, a schedule, a budget, required permits, and required points of control.

I made the list.

---

## What the hoax requires

---

**Item one: implanting the same sequence in the data of forty-one sequencing centres.**

Those one hundred and ninety-four thousand genomes come from forty-one different centres in nineteen countries, spanning twenty-three years (1998 to 2021), on seven different platforms.

Two roads to getting the sequence into all of that.

**Road A: contaminate the source — have the sequence carried in some reagent every laboratory uses.**

I looked into it. What would have to be controlled is:

```
global suppliers of library preparation reagents     4 (of which 2 hold 78% of the market)
providers of reference standards                     3
the reference genome in the bioinformatics pipeline  maintained by 1 international consortium
```

It looks feasible — only a handful of controls.

**And this road has a fatal problem:**

A contaminant carried in a reagent appears in **every** sample sequenced, including controls, including non-human samples.

And I checked those centres' non-human sequencing data (they sequence other species at the same time).

**The sequence is not there.**

**So it is not reagent contamination.**

**Road B: implant one by one — modify the raw files of every dataset.**

I costed this road.

One hundred and ninety-four thousand genomes, each with tens to hundreds of gigabytes of raw data. Implanting two hundred and forty-seven base pairs so that it behaves consistently in every downstream analysis (coverage, quality-score distribution, paired-end mates, insert-size distribution, error rate, and continuity with the surrounding region) requires a separate, delicate operation per sample.

**And those data sit in the independent storage systems of forty-one centres, of which seventeen have never had their data on a network.**

**So this road requires, at forty-one institutions in nineteen countries, one person each who can physically reach the storage and who knows how the sequence must be altered.**

Forty-one people. Twenty-three years. Zero leaks.

---

**Item two: implanting in one thousand four hundred and seven ancient samples, and forging the deamination gradient.**

This is the most expensive item on the list, and it is technically **impossible.**

I wrote at 6.4 why the gradient cannot be forged.

And here is a thing I did not write at 6.4:

**Even if you could forge the gradient, you would also have to forge its correlation with age.**

Those one thousand four hundred and seven samples range from four hundred to forty-five thousand years, and the correlation of their gradient slopes with age is 0.88.

**To forge that, you would have to compute, for each sample, the slope proper to its age, and realise that slope on the reads you implant.**

And those ages were measured by **other people** by other methods — carbon fourteen, stratigraphy, OSL, argon-argon.

**So you would have to know in advance what other people would measure, and forge to that.**

And some of those datings were performed after the samples were sequenced.

**So you would have to predict future measurements.**

---

**Item three: making those nine differences consistent with three hundred thousand years of mutation.**

Of one hundred and ninety-four thousand people, nine fail the checksum (6.3). Nine differing sites, all different, distributed as a neutral sequence's mutations over three hundred thousand years would be.

**To forge that, you would run a population-genetic simulation, compute the diversity proper to three hundred thousand years, and then, following that result, pick nine people out of one hundred and ninety-four thousand and alter one site in each.**

And the nine you picked would have to be statistically indistinguishable from random in geographic origin, in age, and in year of sequencing.

I tested that. They are.

---

**Item four: Antarctica.**

This item I do not cost.

Because it does not need costing.

**That structure under the ice has been holding a constant temperature for three hundred thousand years (4.6).**

To forge it you would build, beneath the Antarctic ice sheet, something capable of holding a constant temperature for three hundred thousand years, and bury it beneath a layer of ice formed three hundred thousand years ago.

**And the age of that ice layer was measured by other people from cores.**

---

**Item five: the gap in the fossil record.**

The most interesting item, because it is the only one where you need not **do** anything.

**What you need is to *not put in.***

The gap in the fossil record is the absence of transitional forms (3.6).

To forge an absence, you would have to guarantee that at every fossil site in the world, nobody has ever dug up a transitional form.

**And fossils are formed naturally, found at random, and dug by tens of thousands of people scattered across the globe, unknown to one another, over more than a century.**

**So you cannot guarantee it.**

**Unless the transitional forms were never there.**

---

**Item six — and this one is decisive.**

**1998.**

In 1998 a Russian Antarctic party recorded the thermal anomaly in their report, annotated *instrument calibration error* (4.1; and Han Yin's single pen mark on our material was there).

**1998.**

And in 1998:

```
human genome reference sequence        did not exist (draft 2001)
targeted nucleases                     zinc-fingers just demonstrated (1996), never used in humans
high-throughput sequencing             did not exist (post-2005)
whole-genome sequencing of ancient DNA  did not exist (post-2010)
```

**So whoever set this up had, in 1998, to know already where that sequence was, what it looked like, and that it carried a checksum — at a time when humanity could not read its own genome.**

**And in that same year he had to arrange a thermal anomaly in Antarctica, and arrange for a Russian party to measure it, and arrange for them to write it down as an instrument calibration error.**

---

I reached that item on the seventh day.

Alone in that room. Evening.

I wrote the six items on a sheet and, at the bottom, one line.

The line reads:

> **Whatever could bring off this hoax has capabilities not less than are required to have done the thing for real.**

---

I want the argument written out, because it is the step in this chapter I am most certain of, and it is a logical argument, not a technical one.

---

Occam's razor is usually used thus: between two hypotheses that explain the same phenomena, take the simpler.

And the two hypotheses are:

**A: humanity was designed and deployed by something not human.**

**B: there is a human hoax that has forged all the evidence supporting A.**

And usually *hoax* is simpler than *miracle.*

**So usually you take B.**

---

**And not this time.**

Because forging those six items requires:

```
controlling the physical storage of 41 institutions in 19 countries for 23 years
    — a penetration exceeding any existing organisation
forging a chemical gradient that is physically unforgeable
    — a capability exceeding known physical law
predicting other people's future measurements
    — a capability exceeding known physical law
burying under the Antarctic ice a device that holds temperature for 300 000 years
    — an energy system exceeding existing engineering
guaranteeing that no transitional form ever appears at any fossil site on Earth
    — control over five hundred million years of strata
knowing, in 1998, a thing that could not be measured until 2021
    — see above
```

---

**And hypothesis A requires:**

```
something able, three hundred thousand years ago, to deploy a species on Earth.
```

---

**And what hypothesis B requires is a proper superset of what hypothesis A requires.**

**Because anything that could forge those six items could certainly do A.**

**And something that could do A could not necessarily do B — because B requires predicting future measurements, and A does not.**

---

**So between these two hypotheses, the razor takes A.**

---

I want to write down the evening I finished that step, because something happened, and it has nothing to do with data.

I finished and pushed the sheet away.

**And then I laughed.**

Not a bitter laugh, not the *this is so absurd I can only laugh* laugh.

**I genuinely found it funny.**

Because I realised, in that moment, that I had just used an entirely rigorous method to prove that *somebody is playing us* is a more preposterous explanation than *we were made.*

And for the fourteen months before that, I had thought *we were made* was the preposterous explanation, and that I was the one who had to be careful.

**And now the positions had swapped.**

**From that moment, *we were made* is the conservative explanation.**

**It is the thriftier one, the one more consistent with known physics, the one with fewer assumptions, the one the razor points at.**

---

Alone in that room, a little after ten, I laughed at a sheet of paper for about ten seconds.

Then I stopped.

**Then I folded it and put it in the envelope.**

---

By then the envelope held:

**Lu Ting's page (1974, Shaanxi, a bone whose shape was wrong).**

**The table of eight lines.**

**The sheet with the nine identifiers.**

**Pei Yan's sheet (a box, two lines, three questions).**

**And this one (six items, and the line at the bottom).**

---

**Line six: does not hold.**

**Time: nine days.**

**Two remain.**

---

One last thing.

I used this section's material in the Tracing Back's twelfth external briefing.

The audience was seven people, three of them officials from outside our field.

I gave the six items, and then the razor argument.

When I had finished, somebody asked a question.

He was a man in his fifties, at the back, who had not spoken.

He asked:

**"Dr Shen. If this hoax is real — that is, if somebody actually did those six things."**

**"Then whoever did it is not human either."**

---

I said, "Correct."

He said, "Then we are still discussing the same thing."

I said, "Correct."

He nodded and asked nothing further.

**It is the most economical summary I heard in the whole of the Tracing Back.**
