# 4.2 · Who Ilan Is

Before replying, I looked him up.

I admit that, because it is not creditable: **before deciding whether to take a person seriously, I checked his CV.**

And I admit at the same time: had I not checked, I might not have replied.

---

His record is findable.

**Ilan Vosk.** Forty-six. Trained in glaciology, later moved to glacial archaeology — an interdisciplinary area studying what ice preserves: ancient air, ancient dust, occasionally biological remains, very occasionally human material.

Doctorate from a very good institution. The first seven years are handsome: steady output, two or three papers a year, two of them in very good journals. An independent position at thirty-one.

**And then a break.**

Thirty-four to thirty-nine. Five years. Zero output.

Not sparse. Zero. No papers, no conference presentations, no grants.

Then from forty, some things, all in marginal journals, all descriptive, all short.

**A record that breaks at thirty-four, barely resumes at forty, and stays marginal.**

---

My reaction on finding that break.

My first thought was: something happened.

In this field, a five-year zero makes people guess downward: illness, family, or — misconduct.

The third is the most guessed, because it best explains *why he has been marginal ever since.*

**I guessed the third.**

In that second, of a man who had written me an extremely restrained letter, attached seven years of data, and explicitly said *no need to reply*, I guessed misconduct.

---

I checked.

**Not misconduct.**

What happened was this.

At thirty-three he published a finding.

I will not write what it was; only its shape: in an ice core he identified a signal which, if it held, would push the date of a certain event back considerably.

The paper appeared in a very good journal. It drew considerable attention.

**Fourteen months later another group repeated the measurement and could not reproduce it.**

Seven months after that a third group identified the problem: his samples had undergone an unintended temperature excursion in transit. The excursion is in the record — he recorded it himself, meticulously — and the effect of that excursion on the signal he measured was not known to anyone at the time, including whoever wrote the handling protocol.

**Not fabrication. Not carelessness. An effect nobody then knew about.**

The effect was studied afterwards and is now in textbooks. **The first citation in that section is his retracted paper.**

---

He retracted.

I read the retraction notice. It is short. About a hundred words.

It reads:

> The signal reported in this paper could not be reproduced on review by three independent groups.
>
> On investigation, the samples used underwent a temperature excursion in transit, and that excursion has a systematic effect on the signal measured. The effect was not recognised at the time this work was carried out.
>
> The conclusion of this paper does not hold. The author requests retraction.
>
> The author apologises to the three groups who reviewed this result for the resources and attention expended.

---

That last line.

**Apologises to the three groups who reviewed this result.**

Not to the readers. Not to the journal. Not to the field.

**To the three sets of people who spent time redoing his experiment and could not.**

I sat in the office for a while after reading that line.

At the moment his career was destroyed, a man wrote a hundred words, and the last of them apologise for other people's wasted time.

---

Then the five years.

The record does not say what happened in them.

I learned part of it later, from him, in Antarctica, one night — where *night* is a convention, the sun not setting then.

He did not say much. I write only what he said.

He said: for those five years he went to the lab every day.

He said: he ran experiments every day and did not write them up.

He said: because he did not know whether what he was doing was right.

He said: **"It is not that I did not dare publish. It is that I did not know whether I could still do experiments."**

He said: every measurement he made in those five years he made at least three times, by at least two methods.

He said: they all agreed.

He said: he still did not publish.

I asked: why.

He said:

*Because agreeing is not enough.*

*The other time, mine agreed too. Three times, two methods, all of it.*

He said:

*A thing agreeing only shows you did not make the error you thought of.*

---

**The drinking began in the third year.**

That part he told me himself, quite flatly.

He said: from thirty-six to forty-two.

Six years.

I asked how he stopped.

He said: *I did not stop it.*

He said: that winter he was in the field alone, at a station, for nineteen days. What he had brought ran out, and resupply was six days late for weather.

He said: those six days were bad.

He said: **"And when the resupply came on the seventh day, I found I did not want it."**

He said: *Not strength. Six days I had no choice, and then it was over, and then I found out that it could be over.*

He said: *I know it is not like that for many people. I was lucky.*

---

Now the seven years.

He first measured that thermal anomaly at thirty-nine — the last of the five zero years, and while he was still drinking.

**He told nobody when he first measured it.**

He told me why later.

He said: *Because if I had said it then, people would have thought I was desperate.*

He said: *A man five years without a publication suddenly announcing he has found a hot spot that should not exist — that is not a discovery. That is a distress call.*

He said: *And they would have been right. I was desperate then.*

---

So he waited.

**He waited seven years.**

What he did in those seven years is the content of those sixty-seven pages:

Years one to three: repeat measurement. Same method, different times, different seasons. Four times. All consistent.

Year four: change method. He learned a technique he did not know, eight months of it, and then measured once. Consistent.

Year five: another method. This time he had somebody else do it — he gave the coordinates to a researcher in another country who did not know him and works in another area, asked them to take a reading while doing something else, and paid for it.

That person's result was consistent.

**And that person still does not know what they measured.**

Year six: he went through every possible natural explanation. Geothermal anomaly, radioactive vein, groundwater, subglacial lake, volcanism. Eliminated one at a time. That is pages twenty-three to fifty-one of the attachment.

Year seven: he wrote the sixty-seven pages.

Eleven months of writing.

**And then he sat on it for two months without sending it.**

---

I asked him about those two months.

He said: *I was working out who to send it to.*

He said: *I made a list. Nineteen people.*

He said: *I struck them off one at a time.*

I asked: *why?*

He said:

*Because I know what they would think.*

*They would look me up first.*

*They would find the retraction, and they would not read further.*

He said:

*That is not their fault. In their place I would do the same.*

He said:

*A man who has retracted saying he has found a thing that should not exist — the prior on that is two orders of magnitude below the same sentence from a man who has not.*

*That judgement is correct.*

*Bayes is not malicious.*

---

I asked: *Then why me?*

He said:

*Because on your last slide in that recording you wrote: this study offers no explanation of the phenomenon.*

He said:

*I watched the recording, got to that slide, and stopped.*

He said:

*If a person finds a thing and does not explain it, there are two possibilities: she does not know what it is, or she knows and dare not say.*

*Either is fine.*

He said:

*Either one, I can talk to.*

---

To end this section, the first thing I did after checking him.

I did not reply at once.

I read the sixty-seven pages through first.

Six hours.

Then I replied.

Two sentences.

**The first:**

*Dr Vosk, I have read it. On the figure on page 41 — I would like the coordinates of that point beneath the ice, if you can give them.*

**The second:**

*Also, I looked you up. I apologise for the first thing I thought while doing it.*

---

He replied quickly.

Two sentences as well.

**The first is the coordinates.**

**The second is:**

*No need. You were thinking in the right direction. You were only wrong this time.*
