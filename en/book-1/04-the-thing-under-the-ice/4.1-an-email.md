# 4.1 · An Email

Six words in the subject line:

```
Something down there is still warm
```

---

I nearly deleted it.

I want to be plain about why I nearly deleted it, because the reason has bothered me ever since.

---

Among the emails I get every day there is a category like this:

The sender is a stranger. The subject line is short and strange, sometimes in capitals, sometimes with an exclamation mark. The body is long — a thousand words to ten thousand. Inside is a grand theory, usually concerning the origin of life, the nature of consciousness, the true mechanism of evolution, or something the scientific mainstream dare not admit.

There is usually a great deal of bold.

There is usually a line saying *I am not from this field, but.*

And it usually ends: I know you are busy, but this matters, and I hope you will take a look.

**In my line of work we call these crank emails.**

I get three to five a month.

I used to read some. More in earlier years, fewer later.

Why fewer: not because they are all wrong — they are all wrong, and that is not the reason.

Because **replying is of no use.**

I replied seriously twice. I spent an afternoon each time identifying the step at which the reasoning broke, phrased as courteously as I could.

The first: six thousand words back, explaining that I had misunderstood the theory.
The second: a reply, then another, then one a week for seven months.

I stopped replying.

Having stopped replying, I stopped reading.

Having stopped reading, I began deleting on the subject line.

---

Ilan Vosk's email fell inside the arc of that delete.

Six words, strange.
Sender unknown.
An institutional suffix I had never heard of, with a long acronym.
An attachment — crank emails often have attachments, usually PDFs, usually self-typeset.

My finger was on the delete key.

---

I did not delete it because of something entirely accidental.

It was a Wednesday.

I was going to my mother's that afternoon and was tidying up. Clearing the inbox as part of it.

And in the fraction of a second before pressing delete, my eye caught the six words.

**Something down there is still warm.**

And for three months there had been a number in my head.

Not three hundred thousand years — the figure in my hands then was seven hundred thousand.

And *three hundred thousand years* is an order of magnitude unlike *ten thousand*, *a hundred*, or *a hundred million*. It falls **on the hominin timescale.**

A crank email's subject line says *the universe*, *consciousness*, *life*, *the truth*.

**It does not give a specific time with the right order of magnitude and a unit.**

What was wrong with those six words was not that they were strange.

**What was wrong is that they were too specific.**

---

I opened it.

The body reads as follows, verbatim, because it is short, and its shortness says a great deal.

---

> Dr Shen,
>
> I am Ilan Vosk, glacial archaeology. We have not met.
>
> Under the ice at a location in Antarctica I have measured a thermal anomaly.
>
> Its temperature is above the surrounding environment. The difference is small, about 0.4 K.
>
> I have measured it four times by different methods over seven years. The four values agree within error.
>
> The point is at about two thousand one hundred metres depth. From the local accumulation rate, ice at that depth is about three hundred thousand years old.
>
> If the point began producing heat after the ice formed, it must have a sustained energy source.
>
> If it was there before the ice formed, it has been there three hundred thousand years.
>
> I incline to the latter; my reasons are on page 11 of the attachment.
>
> I do not know what it is. I do glacial archaeology; my training does not cover this.
>
> I watched the recording of your talk at a conference (I was not present; I found it afterwards).
>
> On your final slide you wrote: *this study offers no explanation of the phenomenon.*
>
> Nor does mine.
>
> The attachment is all my data, including the raw records of the four measurements. Use it as you like.
>
> If you consider this unrelated to your work, there is no need to reply; I will not write again.
>
> Ilan Vosk

---

Now, one at a time, the things in that email that made me sit down, because they are all of it.

**One: *We have not met.***

A crank email does not open that way. It opens with *I have long admired*, or *I have read your work*, or straight into the theory.

*We have not met* is a statement of fact. It defines the relationship: I do not know you, you do not know me, we have no connection, and what I have to say does not depend on one.

**Two: the number carries an error.**

*About 0.4 K.*

Numbers in a crank email usually have no error, or an absurdly large one, or eight decimal places.

Nought point four kelvin is a small, specific, credible difference.

**Three: *four times by different methods over seven years.***

Four. Different methods. Seven years.

A person does not spend seven years on a hoax. And more importantly: **he knows the methods must differ.**

Four measurements by one method eliminate random error and not systematic error. Four by different methods eliminate the instrument itself.

**He knows that, and he does not explain how he knows it.**

An outsider does not know it. A person pretending to be an insider explains why he did it, to show that he knows.

**He does not explain.**

**Four: the two *ifs*.**

> If the point began producing heat after the ice formed, it must have a sustained energy source.
> If it was there before the ice formed, it has been there three hundred thousand years.

**He lays out both possibilities, and both are inconvenient for the conclusion he wants.**

The first implies a sustained energy source — which is stranger.
The second implies a thing sitting under ice for three hundred thousand years — which is also strange.

**He favours neither. He simply lists them.**

And he writes *I incline to the latter; my reasons are on page 11* — separating his inclination from his reasons, and giving the page.

**Five — this is the one that made me open the attachment.**

> I do not know what it is. I do glacial archaeology; my training does not cover this.

The core feature of a crank email is that **it knows what it is.**

It always knows. Its entire length is an explanation of what it is.

**And this one says: I do not know.**

**Six: *On your final slide you wrote: this study offers no explanation of the phenomenon.***

He had watched the recording.

There is a recording; the organisers filmed everything. It sits on the conference website, requires registration, requires paging through to the third afternoon's parallel session, and requires scrubbing to the last twenty-two minutes of a two-hour-forty-minute video.

**He found it, watched it, and remembered one sentence from my last slide.**

And the sentence he remembered is not my conclusion, not the checksum, not the four hundred billion controls.

**It is *this study offers no explanation of the phenomenon.***

That is the nearest thing to a distress call I could say in that room (3.1).

**He heard it.**

**Seven: *If you consider this unrelated to your work, there is no need to reply; I will not write again.***

---

Stop here.

That sentence I read three times.

*No need to reply; I will not write again.*

A man writes an email, attaches seven years of data, and voluntarily surrenders every right to follow up.

He does not say *I look forward to your reply.* Does not say *your response would be much appreciated.* Does not say *I will follow up.*

**He says: if you think it is nothing, pretend you never saw it, and I will not trouble you.**

The meaning of that sentence is: **I know this letter falls inside the arc of your delete. I do not intend to contest it.**

---

That afternoon I did not go to my mother's.

I telephoned her and said I had to work late.

That is the fourth of my four absences in three years (2.8).

**Yes. That one.**

At 2.8 I gave the reason for that absence, and I said I went to see Marcus Deng.

I correct it here: I misremembered.

Writing 2.8, I merged two things into one — the meeting with Marcus was a different Wednesday, about five months after this one.

**My fourth absence was because I was reading the attachment to an email.**

I correct it here, and I am not amending 2.8.

For the same reason as at 1.9: **in my memory, it was Marcus.**

And my memory carries no checksum.

---

I opened the attachment.

Sixty-seven pages.

Not a self-typeset PDF. A properly formatted technical report, with running heads, numbering, a list of figures, references.

Page one is an abstract.

Page eleven is the reasoning he mentioned.

And on page forty-one there is a figure.

The figure is a thermal map.
