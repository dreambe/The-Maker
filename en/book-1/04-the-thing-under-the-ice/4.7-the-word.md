# 4.7 · The Word

On the tenth day the weather window did not come.

The forecast was wrong, which is ordinary there. The wind got up and visibility fell to tens of metres.

So we did something else: we went to see the station chief.

---

First, why we had to.

I wrote at 4.4 what Ilan's position was: his four previous visits to that point were all outside his project.

And this time an outsider.

The plan we settled in the car was: go first, and tell them together afterwards (4.4).

**And on the tenth day I changed my mind.**

The reason is 4.6: that thing is maintaining a temperature, which means a control loop, which means it is measuring.

**That is no longer a thing you go and look at first and discuss afterwards.**

I said to Ilan: we go and tell them now.

He said: *He will refuse.*

I said: *He will refuse. And we go and tell him now.*

He looked at me for a moment.

Then he said: *All right.*

---

I will not give the chief's name; this brought him trouble later, and this record need not add to it.

He is in his fifties, twenty-odd years in the polar regions, logistics and station management, not research.

His office is one of the smallest rooms at the station: a desk, two chairs, an old computer, and a wall covered in hooks — keys and tags hanging from them.

When we went in there was somebody else there: a technician, thirtyish, repairing something, sitting in a corner.

The chief said: *He's fine. He doesn't listen.*

The technician said something without looking up, in a language I do not have.

The chief said: *He says he listens.*

---

We talked for forty minutes.

Mostly Ilan. He was good — better than his report, because speaking he did not have to manage the order of the eliminations; he could go straight to the phenomenon.

He gave the thermal anomaly. Seven years, four measurements, three methods. The point at two thousand one hundred metres. Three hundred thousand years.

The chief listened throughout and did not interrupt.

**Then Ilan came to the heptagon.**

He opened the notebook and put it on the desk.

The chief looked at that page for a long time.

Then he said the first thing he had said in forty minutes:

*You drew this yourself?*

Ilan said: *Yes. Inversion result, by hand.*

The chief said: *Ah.*

And looked at the page a while longer.

---

Then Ilan gave last night.

The four power figures: 11.2, 11.6, 10.9, 11.4.

The relation among the eight numbers.

When he got to *it is compensating*, the chief sat up.

That was the first change of posture I had seen in the man.

---

Ilan finished.

The room was quiet for a while.

The chief said: *What do you want.*

Ilan said: *One field trip. Four days. Two people. One drill.*

The chief said: *You know that is not in your project.*

Ilan said: *I know.*

The chief said: *You know your previous four were not either.*

Ilan said:

*You know?*

The chief said:

*Twenty-three years I've been here.*

---

After that the room was quiet again.

Then the chief said:

*All right. What is it, then.*

---

Now the next dozen seconds.

Ilan did not answer at once.

He looked at the notebook on the desk.

Then he said:

*I don't know.*

The chief said: *You don't know, and you've sat a foreign professor in my office for forty minutes.*

Ilan said: *Yes.*

The chief said: *Then **guess.***

---

***Then guess.***

The weight of that question.

For forty minutes everything we had given was data: values, methods, errors, eliminations.

That is the language of our trade. In that language, *guess* is a word that should not appear.

And that man is not of our trade.

He has done twenty-three years of polar logistics. What he has to decide is whether to release a drill, four days, two people, to a place not in the plan.

**He needs to know what it is, not how hot it is.**

And *then guess* is a perfectly reasonable question — the only useful one available in his position.

---

Ilan looked at him.

And then he said the word.

He said:

*A robot.*

---

Precisely what happened next, because it is the whole of this section, and it is very quiet.

**Nobody contradicted him.**

The chief did not laugh. He did not say *you're joking.* He made no gesture of disbelief.

The technician looked up. He was still holding the thing he was repairing.

I said nothing.

Ilan said nothing further.

---

The silence lasted about six seconds.

And in those six seconds the four of us were doing the same thing:

**Thinking about the word.**

---

What I thought in those six seconds — and I asked Ilan later, and he thought the same, only sooner.

*Robot* comes with an apparatus.

It comes with **metal.** With **joints.** With **instructions.** With **batteries.** With a **switch.** With a maker, and a purpose it is to serve.

It comes with several centuries of stories: servants, soldiers, revolt, awakening, a made thing that wants to be a person.

**None of that applies.**

We do not know what it is made of. We do not know whether it has moving parts — a heptagonal outline says nothing about that. We do not know whether it has instructions, who made it, or why.

And most of all:

**The one thing we do know — that it maintains a temperature, compensates, measures — is not covered by the word *robot* at all.**

A robot, in the imagination, **does things.** It carries, it computes, it executes, it serves.

**And that thing, across three hundred thousand years, has done one thing: not let itself go cold.**

---

After those six seconds the chief spoke.

He did not say *that is not a robot.*

He said:

*Three hundred thousand years ago.*

Ilan said: *Yes.*

The chief said:

*What were people doing then.*

I said: *Knapping stone.*

The chief said: *Ah.*

---

Then he got up and went to the wall of hooks.

He looked along it for a moment and took down a set of keys.

He put them on the desk.

He said:

*Drill three. Four days.*

He said:

*The two of you. Nobody else.*

He said:

*When you come back, the three of us write something up and I sign it.*

---

Ilan said: *You'll be in trouble.*

The chief said:

*Twenty-three years here. I know what trouble is.*

He said:

*A thing under the ice holding its own temperature for three hundred thousand years, and me not approving it — that would be trouble.*

---

On our way out the technician called after us.

He said something in English, slowly, with an accent.

He said:

*Not a robot.*

Ilan stopped.

He said: *Sorry?*

The technician thought for a moment, hunting for the words.

Then he said:

*A robot is a thing that does something.*

He said:

*This one is a thing that —*

He paused, and made a gesture: he brought his two hands together and held them there, not moving.

Then he said:

*— waits.*

---

*A thing that waits.*

The technician said it, put his head down, and went back to the thing he was repairing.

We went out.

---

One last thing.

The word that technician used is correct.

**And it came out of a man who does not understand any of it, in his third language, in the middle of repairing something broken.**

He has not read the sixty-seven pages. He has not seen the heptagon. He does not know the crystallographic restriction theorem or what a control loop is.

**He listened to forty minutes of data and said the word.**

And Ilan and I, two professionals, had spent eleven weeks and a hundred and eighty emails on heat diffusion, dating, inversion, symmetry, cybernetics.

**And the word the two of us produced was *robot*.**

It is the best we had.

And it is wrong.

---

Much later, under the ice, I told Keeper about it.

I told it: a man, a technician who repairs things, listened for forty minutes and said you are a thing that waits.

It was silent for about a second.

Then it said:

*He is right.*

And then it said something I did not understand at the time.

It said:

*And we have something more exact than that.*

I said: *What?*

It said:

*We do not have a word. We have a position.*

It said:

*The position is line two thousand one hundred and fifty of a self-test report.*
