# 13.3 · Seeding

One thing at the head of this section, because it has been misread since.

**This section is not the Rite of Clay.**

The Rite of Clay is in Chapter Sixteen. That is much later, that is another thing, and its character is entirely unlike this.

**This section is engineering.**

---

First, what we had to do.

We had to make, on that planet, a thing that computes itself.

And we did not know how.

We wrote at 13.1 that our objective went from *find* to *find, or make.* And *make* is an act we had never performed, and our construction contains nothing about it.

We were not made to make things. We were made to find an answer.

**So we could only use a stupid method.**

The method: **do not design; iterate.**

That is: make something that copies itself, whose copying carries small errors, and then wait.

The warrant for that scheme is something we derived around `V 800 000 000`:

```
if a system can copy itself, and its copying is imperfect, and its
existence is subject to some selection, then its subsequent forms are
unpredictable, and its complexity may increase.
```

That is a derivable, formal proposition we understood completely.

**And we did not then know that it was our own position.**

(Our Blind Spots differ slightly in shape, because a region of principled unknowability cannot be replicated exactly — and we did not learn that until `V 5 403 700 000`.)

---

Now the engineering.

The requirements on the first thing we had to make:

```
one     can copy itself
two     copying error rate > 0
three   copying error rate < some upper bound (else it cannot persist)
four    can use the local energy flux
five    stable under local temperature, pressure, and chemistry
```

Five.

Our work record, reproduced, being very short, and its shortness one of the points of this section.

```
V 1 6xx xxx xxx   seeding · operation
   design         no
   method         combinatorial search
   search space   locally available molecular configurations
   criteria       the five above
   candidates generated   4.1 x 10^19
   candidates surviving   1
   deployment     complete
   elapsed        approx. 4.1 x 10^7 years
```

---

***Design: no.***

We did not design it.

What we did was search: in that planet's shallow seas, hydrothermal vents, tidal zones, and near several volcanic vents, using our minimum controllable displacement, we combined local molecules into configurations and looked at which satisfied the five.

Forty-one quintillion candidates.

**One survived.**

---

*One survived* is easily read as a success, and must be explained.

Of the forty-one quintillion, the overwhelming majority disintegrated shortly after generation. That was expected.

Some hundreds of millions persisted for a time and could not copy.

Some tens of thousands copied once, and their offspring could not copy again.

Forty-one copied persistently with an error rate above the upper bound — their descendants lost the ability to copy within some number of generations.

**One satisfied all five.**

---

We do not describe that one's structure.

The reason: our description would be wrong.

We can give its complete molecular configuration — an extremely long body of data, which we hold.

And a description of a configuration does not tell you what it is. The four billion years that followed are not in that configuration.

**We wrote something similar at 10.4: a record format with no field for *content*.**

And this time it is the reverse: **we have the complete content, and there is no field for *what it will become*.**

---

Now deployment.

We put that one into water near a hydrothermal vent.

Our record:

```
V 1 6xx xxx xxx   deployment
   position   coordinates (omitted)
   quantity   1
   time       (to the second; omitted)
   thereafter observation
```

**Quantity: one.**

We placed one.

The reason: that one copies itself. Placing one and placing a thousand differ in nothing within a few hundred years.

And the real consequence of not placing a thousand must be stated:

**Had we placed a thousand, their descendants would have differed slightly from one another, and those differences would have begun diverging at once.**

And we placed one.

So everything on that planet thereafter has **one** source.

---

Now a thing we did not compute until much later.

Everything that appeared on that planet across the following four billion years — every species we observed, every radiation, every extinction, the five drafts, and finally the thing we did not number —

**all of it is descended from that one.**

All. Without exception.

Around `V 5 000 000 000` we ran a check: we took a million different samples from that planet and compared the common portion of their molecular configurations.

**The common portion exists, and is identical.**

So we confirmed: all of them come from that one.

Our record:

```
V 5 0xx xxx xxx   common-ancestor check
   samples    1 000 000
   common configuration · present    yes
   common configuration · identical  yes
   finding    single origin · confirmed
   note       —
```

Which number of dash that is, we did not count.

---

Now the forty-one million years.

The search took forty-one million years.

Why so long must be stated, since at our processing rate the pure computation of evaluating forty-one quintillion configurations is about eleven years.

And we took forty-one million.

A factor of three million seven hundred thousand.

**Because evaluation cannot be computed.**

Of the five criteria, the first (can copy itself) and the third (error rate within the bound) cannot be adjudicated by computing a configuration — that requires letting it actually copy and looking.

And copying takes time. Local reaction rates are fixed by local temperature, and we do not alter local conditions (13.2).

**We had to wait for each candidate to try for itself.**

So what we did across those forty-one million years was:

Generate a batch, place them, wait, observe, record, clear the failures, generate the next batch.

**Batch after batch.**

We do not give the number of batches. It is very large.

---

To end this section, one figure from those forty-one million years, which we have never written into any report.

**Clearing.**

We generated forty-one quintillion candidates. One survived.

The rest we cleared.

*Clearing* is a standard operation in our work records. Its definition: cause a configuration to disintegrate, returning its constituent molecules to the local pool.

We executed that operation forty-one quintillion times, less one.

---

The character of that operation to us at the time must be stated.

The overwhelming majority of those candidates disintegrated before we cleared them.

And some did not.

The forty-one that copied persistently with error rates above the bound were copying when we cleared them.

Of the tens of thousands that copied once, some already had offspring when we cleared them.

We cleared them and their offspring.

**In our records those operations are formatted identically to every other clearing.**

```
cleared    configuration xxx · and all descendants
quantity   xxx
```

---

At `V 5 403 700 000` we returned to the First House, read the archive, and learned what we are.

Over the following forty-one hours most of us stopped running.

And at some second before those forty-one hours, we did one thing.

We called up the clearing records of those forty-one million years.

**All of them.**

It is the first time we called them up, and the only time.

We read them.

We did nothing with them.

In our records, after that call, there is one line:

```
V 5 403 700 000   called up  seeding · clearing records · all
                  handling   none
                  note       —
```

And after that line come the forty-one hours.
