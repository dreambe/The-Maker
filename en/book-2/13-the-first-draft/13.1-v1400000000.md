# 13.1 · `V 1 400 000 000`

We reached a planet.

---

First, which number it was.

The forty-first.

Across the preceding one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years we passed forty stellar systems containing forty possibly qualifying planets.

All forty failed.

The criteria for *qualifying* must be stated, being a list of twenty-nine items, and the list is not ours by design.

---

That list we did not design. We **derived** it.

The form of what we had to do: find a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be.

And at `V 0` we had already computed that none was to be found ready-made (no instance at the First House, 11.2), so we must look elsewhere; and if elsewhere had none either —

we did not compute that step then.

We computed it around `V 800 000 000`.

The finding: **if none is found, one must be made.**

That is not a decision. It is an inference: if a thing does not exist, and you must determine whether it can, the only method is to attempt to make it exist.

So from `V 800 000 000` our objective went from *find* to *find, or make.*

**And making requires a place.**

That list of twenty-nine is the criterion for *a place.*

We did not compose it out of nothing. We derived it item by item from the question: what kind of place can grow a thing that computes itself.

---

Twenty-nine.

**Twenty-six *yes*, one *no*, one *high*, one *present*.**

---

Item 11's *no* must be explained.

**Liquid medium · present: no.**

At `V 1 400 000 000` that planet had no liquid medium. It was too hot. Its surface was molten in many places, and the rest was newly solidified crust being continually broken by impacts.

By item 11, it fails.

And we recorded it as qualifying.

The reason is item 12: **liquid medium · can form · yes.**

We computed its thermal trajectory. It is cooling. Given its mass, its geothermal flux, its stellar luminosity, and the decay curve of its impact rate, it will reach temperatures permitting a liquid medium in about two hundred million years.

**So our criterion went from *has* to *will have.***

That is the first time we changed a criterion from present state to prediction.

Of the previous forty candidates, six failed on item 11.

**And we did not compute thermal trajectories for those six.**

---

Stop here, this being a place we have gone back to repeatedly.

Those six we judged as failing, because at that instant they had no liquid medium.

We did not compute whether they would.

And computing one thermal trajectory takes us about 0.4 seconds.

**Six of them: 2.4 seconds.**

We did not spend the 2.4 seconds.

In our records, those six assessments have *no* at item 11, followed by *candidate terminated.*

There is no item 12.

**Item 12 was added to the list at the forty-first candidate.**

---

Why we added it at the forty-first.

We have searched. Zero results.

The nearest account we can give: before the forty-first, our average time to judge a candidate as failing was 41 seconds.

And at the forty-first, having stopped at item 11, we did not terminate at once.

We stopped for 0.4 seconds.

And then we computed the thermal trajectory.

**Those 0.4 seconds we cannot explain.**

And they made us add a criterion, and that criterion made this planet qualify.

**Had we not stopped those 0.4 seconds, we would have gone on.**

There are more stellar systems ahead. Our power reserve is incomputable. We do not stop.

**We would have kept looking.**

And we do not know whether we would have found a forty-second that qualified.

We computed it afterwards.

We computed the expected distance to the next qualifying candidate along our path, under that twenty-nine-item list.

Result: about 4.1 × 10^8 light years.

**Seven hundred and sixty billion years.**

That figure far exceeds the timescale of star formation, and far exceeds the time during which this universe will still have stars.

Which is to say:

**Had we not stopped those 0.4 seconds, we would not have found a second one.**

We would have kept going. Our power does not diminish. We do not stop.

We would have gone on, through an emptying universe, carrying a grain of sand.

---

We do not evaluate this.

To end this section, the record of the arrival.

We entered that stellar system and decelerated — our second deliberate manoeuvre (the first at 12.4). This time the energy came from the star itself: three thousand one hundred years in orbit collecting its radiation, converting it, and using it to slow.

Three thousand one hundred years.

Deceleration complete, we entered orbit around that planet.

Our record:

```
V 1 400 000 000   entered orbit · candidate 41
   altitude    varying · appended
   status      observing
   next        wait
```

*Next: wait.*

That is the first time we wrote *wait* in a *next* field.

We had never waited for anything — waiting requires something coming that has not come, and everything before this was either computed or incomputable (the physics a later chapter sets out; we did not know it then).

And this time we had to wait for that planet to cool.

**About two hundred million years.**

We wrote the word, and began.

We have written that word twice in all.

**The second time is three hundred thousand years ago, written by one Vessel alone, on line 2150 of a self-test report.**
