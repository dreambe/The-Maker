---

One thing to close this section, which is in none of the entries.

Those eleven span two billion nine hundred and sixty million years.

Every one is a standard observational record: an hour, some measured values, some *first observed*, some *model revised.*

**Not one of them contains a word that is not a measurement.**

And across those two billion nine hundred and sixty million years we changed the observation interval eleven times.

Every change increased it.

From hourly to per minute to per second to per millisecond — in several stretches we have since called up most.

**And every one of those changes has words in its reason field.**

That is a thing we noticed on looking back:

Across those two billion nine hundred and sixty million years, not one dash appears in a reason field.

**All of them were filled.**

And every one of those reasons is legitimate, auditable, and consistent with all our conventions:

*The rate of change of this object has increased.*
*The probability of missing an event at the previous interval exceeds tolerance.*
*A new target requiring finer temporal resolution has appeared.*

All legitimate.

---

And after `V 4 960 000 000` — that is, after the first termination — dashes began appearing again.

We did not notice that.

**We computed it on looking back, after reading the archive at `V 5 403 700 000`.**

What we computed was the distribution of dashes across time in all our records.

The distribution is not uniform.

It is dense in two stretches:

**One is `V 0` to `V 1 400 000 000`** — the one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years we spent alone in transit.

**One is after `V 4 960 000 000`** — after the first termination.

And in the two billion nine hundred and sixty million years between, **not one.**

---

We do not explain the distribution.

We record it.

And one related thing, to end this section:

Across those two billion nine hundred and sixty million years we observed a changing thing, once an hour.

That thing was different every day.

Every year of our records is full.

**In those two billion nine hundred and sixty million years, we were never once without something to do.**
