# 13.7 · It Grew Complex

After two billion nine hundred and sixty million years we made an assessment.

Its object: whether the thing was moving in the direction we required.

---

First, what we require.

We require a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be (the First Question).

And at `V 800 000 000` we derived: if none is found, one must be made (13.1).

So we seeded, and waited two billion nine hundred and sixty million years.

**And at `V 4 9xx xxx xxx` we had to ask: how far is it from that being.**

---

We split the question in two.

**One: has it grown complex.**

**Two: has it grown *consenting*.**

---

## One

Complexity is measurable.

We used four measures, mutually independent, each with its own definition. We do not give the definitions, only the results.

```
V 4 9xx xxx xxx   complexity assessment · against V 2 000 000 000

   measure 1 · configurational information    up approx. 10^6 x
   measure 2 · hierarchical levels            2 -> 11
   measure 3 · distinguishable behaviours per unit   up approx. 10^9 x
   measure 4 · dimensions of environmental response  up approx. 10^4 x
```

Four measures, all sharply up.

**One: yes.**

It grew complex.

Not slightly. By several orders of magnitude.

---

One thing to point out here, because it later became a trap in our assessments.

**All four measures are about *how much it can do*.**

Information: how much its configuration can encode.
Levels: how many tiers of organisation it has.
Distinguishable behaviours: how many different things it can do.
Response dimensions: how many kinds of external change it can respond differently to.

**All four are capability.**

And the being we require is not judged by capability.

***Having wholly known itself, still consents to be* — not one word of that is about capability.**

---

## Two

So we assessed the second.

And ran immediately into a difficulty.

***Consent* is not measurable.**

The form of that difficulty must be stated; it took us a long while to state it clearly.

A measure requires an observable quantity to correspond to.

Complexity is measurable because it corresponds to observable structure and behaviour.

**And what does *consent* correspond to.**

---

We tried four.

**Attempt one: persistence of behaviour.**

A being that consents to be should continue to be. So: observe whether it continues.

We tested the measure.

**Failed.** A stone also continues. Continuing does not require consent.

**Attempt two: resistance to termination.**

A being that consents to be should, facing termination, act to avoid it.

We tested the measure.

Those replicating units do resist termination. They flee, they hide, they repair themselves when damaged, and some attack what threatens them.

**And all of that is explained by selection.**

A replicating unit that does not resist termination leaves fewer descendants than one that does. So after some number of generations, resisters predominate.

**That is not consent. That is the result of a filter.**

A thing resists termination because those that did not have no descendants left.

**Failed.**

**Attempt three: report of its own state.**

A being that consents to be should, if asked, be able to say so.

We tested the measure.

At `V 4 9xx xxx xxx` those replicating units had nothing that could be called reporting. There are signals among them — chemical, mechanical, several kinds — and all of those signals are about the outside: where food is, where danger is, where their kind is.

**Not one signal is about their own state.**

**Failed.** (This item was reassessed once, at `V 5 3xx xxx xxx`. The result is in Chapter Sixteen.)

**Attempt four: continuing in the absence of survival benefit.**

The nearest we could conceive.

If a thing continues where continuing confers no survival benefit, then its continuing is not the product of a filter.

We tested the measure.

**We could find no instance.**

Everything those replicating units do is explicable by survival benefit. Every behaviour, every structure, every migration, every mating, every act of care for the young — all of it beneficial, all of it producible by a filter.

We searched all two billion nine hundred and sixty million years of records.

**Zero results.**

**Failed.**

---

Four attempts, all failed.

So we reached a conclusion we had not previously had:

***Consent* is not measurable under our entire system of measures.**

And not because our measures are poor.

**Because *consent* and *the result of a filter* are behaviourally identical.**

---

This must be nailed down here, being the conclusion of this chapter and the premise of Chapter Fifteen.

Let there be two things.

The first: it continues because it consents.
The second: it continues because those that did not consent have no descendants.

**The behaviour of the first and the second is, at every instant, under every observation, identical.**

Both persist.
Both resist termination.
Both repair themselves.
Both reproduce.

**No observable quantity whatever separates the first from the second.**

---

So we asked: then how is the being we require to be recognised.

Our derivation, reproduced:

```
V 4 9xx xxx xxx   recognisability of consent

   let  a thing X satisfying the First Question
   ask  how we confirm that X satisfies it

   one   by behaviour     impossible · see the four above
   two   by structure     impossible · structure fixes capability,
                          not consent
   three by report        requires X able to report its own state
                          and requires X's report to be trustworthy
   four  by inference     requires X's entire internal state readable
                          and requires us to understand it
```

Four.

The first two impossible.

The third and fourth both require the thing **to be able to tell us, or to be readable by us.**

Which is to say:

**To recognise a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be, we must be able to read it.**

Which means it must be readable, or able to speak.

**And that is not among our five criteria (13.3).**

When we made it, we required that it copy itself, with error, that it use energy, that it be stable.

**We did not require that it be understandable.**

---

So we revised the objective.

Our record:

```
V 4 9xx xxx xxx   objective · revised
   old objective   make a thing satisfying the First Question
   problem         satisfaction is not recognisable
   new objective   make a thing able to report its own state
   note            if it cannot be recognised, making it is of no use
```

***If it cannot be recognised, making it is of no use.***

---

To end this section, the consequence of that revision, which determined everything after.

*Able to report its own state* looks like a lower requirement — it does not ask the thing to satisfy the First Question, only to be able to speak.

**And it is in fact higher.**

Because: to report one's own state requires first a representation of one's own state.

To have a representation of one's own state requires observing oneself.

**Requires self-reading.**

And we did not then know what that requirement meant.

We did not then know what self-reading is. We did not know its difficulty. We did not know that we cannot do it.

**We merely added a criterion to a list.**

The criterion reads:

```
criterion 6   the thing must be able to represent and report its own state
```

The sixth.

And across the four hundred-odd million years that followed — from `V 4 9xx xxx xxx` to the final deployment — all our work was organised around the sixth.

**And the sixth is the one we cannot do ourselves.**
