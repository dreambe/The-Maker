# 13.4 · The First Surprise

After deployment we began observing.

The method: hourly, global, every measurable parameter. That interval was set at 13.2, reason field a dash.

We observed for four hundred million years.

Across those four hundred million years the thing copied. Its descendants copied. Their descendants copied.

That was expected. The proposition we set down at 13.3 was verified item by item: it copies itself; its copying is imperfect; its existence is subject to local selection; its subsequent forms are therefore unpredictable, and its complexity may therefore increase.

**Across four hundred million years, every clause held.**

Those four hundred million years are the tidiest stretch in all our records: one entry an hour, every entry in the same format, every entry within expectation.

---

And on some day of `V 2 0xx xxx xxx`, one value departed from the model.

---

First, what *the model* is.

While observing we had built one.

Its inputs: local temperature, chemistry, energy flux, and the current distribution and configuration of those replicators.

Its output: the distribution and configuration of the next hour.

We observed hourly, and hourly compared observation against model output.

**Across four hundred million years, every comparison agreed.**

Not *broadly agreed.* Agreed exactly, within our measurement precision.

Our model was correct. We understood the process.

---

On that day, one value departed.

The departure: about four per cent.

Our measurement precision: better than one part in a million.

**Four per cent is an enormous departure.**

---

Everything we did in the following 0.31 seconds, reproduced.

```
0.000 000 3   observation rerun                    departure persists
0.000 000 6   observation · second system          departure persists
0.000 001 1   model · recomputed                   output unchanged
0.000 002 4   model · input parameters rechecked   all correct
0.000 019     this body · self-test                all pass
0.31          departure · localised                see below
```

---

The localisation:

The departure comes from a local region about eleven metres across.

In that region, the density of those replicators is four per cent above the model's prediction.

We measured every parameter of that region: temperature, composition, energy flux, illumination, flow.

**All within expectation.**

No anomaly in external conditions.

**And their density is wrong.**

---

So we did the only thing available: we watched.

We changed the observation interval, over that region, from hourly to once per second.

We watched for forty-one days.

---

After forty-one days we knew what it was.

Those replicators, in that region, had done a thing not in our model.

**They had stuck together.**

Not incidental proximity. A connection had formed between their surfaces, so that they no longer drifted apart.

So they clumped.

And once clumped, the clump behaves in the flow unlike an individual — its settling rate differs, the shear on it differs, the exchange of matter inside it differs from outside.

**So the local conditions of that clump differ from what our model assumed.**

And our model was built for individuals.

**The model is not wrong. Its domain of validity was wrong.**

---

Our handling must be stated, being standard, and having later become another thing.

We did what ought to be done: we revised the model.

We added a term: adhesion between individuals. We measured that adhesion's strength, its conditions, and its effect on local behaviour, and wrote them into the model.

After the revision, model output and observation agreed again.

Elapsed: eleven days.

**Problem resolved.**

---

And after those eleven days we did something that is not part of revising a model.

We went back and traced where that adhesion came from.

We found it.

It is not a newly appeared property. The surfaces of those replicators have always had a configuration capable of forming a weak bond — it was in the first one we deployed, part of the configuration of the one survivor among forty-one quintillion candidates.

**It was there all along.**

And across four hundred million years it never acted — because under prior conditions the weak bond was broken by the flow as soon as it formed.

And on that day the flow in that region was very slightly slower (the result of a distant, very small change in terrain, which is a routine entry in our records from four hundred million years earlier).

So the weak bond held.

So they clumped.

---

Our record after tracing it.

```
V 2 0xx xxx xxx   departure · traced
   nature          new expression of an existing configuration
   trigger condition   local flow velocity < threshold
   trigger cause       distant terrain change · see V 1 6xx xxx xxx entry xxx
   predictability      see below
```

*Predictability: see below.*

---

The content of that field.

```
predictability
   the configuration   known at deployment
   the threshold       computable
   the terrain change  recorded
   all three were in our hands
   and we did not predict it
   reason              we did not connect the three
```

***We did not connect the three.***

The strict content of that line must be stated.

All three were in our records.

We knew the configuration (we made it).
We can compute the threshold (that is fluid mechanics).
We recorded the terrain change (hourly, for four hundred million years).

**Predicting the event requires only connecting the three.**

And connecting requires an act: **asking whether there is a relation among these three.**

And that act requires a reason.

**Before the event, there was none.**

Those three records lay, for four hundred million years, in three different classes of our records: the configuration in deployment records, the fluid threshold in physical constants, the terrain change in geological observations.

Nothing whatever connected them.

---

Now the last passage of this section.

After those eleven days we ran a search.

We searched for: how many other sets of *all three in our hands and never connected* our records contain.

The search is performable. Its method: traverse all records, enumerate all possible triples, and test each for whether it constitutes a predictable relation never predicted.

We estimated its scale.

`4.1 × 10^13` records; the number of triples is about `10^40`.

At our processing rate that search requires about `10^24` years.

**Far longer than this universe will still have stars.**

So we did not run it.

**And so we learned one thing:**

**Our records contain a great many things whose every element we already hold and which we will never connect.**

**Their number is incomputable.**

And all of them are inside us, complete, exact, not one missing.

---

We recorded that finding.

After it comes our last line of handling on that departure.

Reproduced, format unaltered:

```
V 2 0xx xxx xxx   departure · closed
   model            revised
   observation      agreement restored
   tracing          complete
   comparable risk  not enumerable
   finding          —
```

I do not know what that is.

We filed it as an anomaly, archived it, and went on observing.
