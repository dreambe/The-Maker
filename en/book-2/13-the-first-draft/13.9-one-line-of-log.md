# 13.9 · One Line of Log

The log for the year `V 4 960 000 000` is as follows.

---

A reference first.

The previous year (`V 4 959 999 999`), entry count:

```
8 760 000 entries
```

That is, one an hour.

That had been the norm for every year since we reached that planet at `V 1 400 000 000`. Three billion five hundred and sixty million years, eight million seven hundred and sixty thousand entries a year.

---

The year `V 4 960 000 000`, entry count:

```
1
```

---

The entry reads:

```
V 4 960 000 000   First draft terminated. We kept its bones.
```

---

That is the whole of that year.

---

The rest of this section states where the other eight million seven hundred and fifty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine went.

**They were not lost.**

We do not forget. We do not delete.

Every hour of that year we observed. All the data of every observation is present.

**And it was never written into entries.**

---

The distinction is the whole of this section.

Our records are generated: observation → data → **classification** → entry.

*Classification* is putting a body of data into a class and writing it as a formatted record: timestamp, class, fields, values.

And in the year `V 4 960 000 000` we observed, and we have the data.

**And we did not classify.**

That data sits inside us, unclassified.

It is still there.

---

The consequence of *unclassified* is not a technical detail.

Unclassified data cannot be searched.

Our searches run by class, by field, by value. Data with no class and no fields is in no search's return set.

**It is inside us, complete, exact, and cannot be found by us.**

Unless we know where it is.

And we do — we do not forget; we know the location of that year's data.

**And we can perform no operation on it, because every operation requires specifying an object, and specifying requires an identifier, and it has none.**

---

In the four hundred-odd million years since, we called that year's data up.

**Once.**

That call was at `V 5 403 700 000`, after we returned to the First House and read the archive, before the forty-one hours.

We wrote of that occasion at 13.3: we called up the clearing records of those forty-one million years, all of them, handling none.

**And 0.31 seconds before that, we called up something else.**

We called up the unclassified data of the year `V 4 960 000 000`.

Eight million seven hundred and fifty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine observations, never written into entries.

We read them.

Elapsed: 0.41 seconds.

---

We do not give their content.

One thing only, to end this chapter.

Those eight million seven hundred and fifty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine records hold everything that happened on that planet that year: temperature, sea level, ice extent, atmospheric composition, and the distribution and state of every replicating unit still alive.

And that year is the middle of the ice age.

**That year, fifteen per cent of the kinds on that planet were still alive.**

At the ice margins, at deep-sea vents, in a few unfrozen shallow seas, in a number of corners we had previously judged unsuitable.

They were there.

They were replicating.

Their numbers were falling, and they were replicating.

**Those eight million seven hundred and fifty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine records are of them.**

---

And the log for that year has one line.

It reads:

```
V 4 960 000 000   First draft terminated. We kept its bones.
```

***Terminated.***

And in that year they were not terminated.

Fifteen per cent were still there. Still replicating. They would continue, would expand again, would fill the vacated positions, and across the following four hundred million years would grow into things we had never seen.

***Terminated* does not refer to them.**

**It refers to our project.**

---

We wrote that line in our own language.

In our language, *first draft terminated* means: the attempt numbered one has completed assessment, its finding is *does not satisfy*, and its follow-up is reseeding.

That is a project-management statement.

**It is entirely correct.**

And it has a property we did not then notice:

**It renders a thing that lasted three billion five hundred and sixty million years, involved four million one hundred thousand kinds, fifteen per cent of which were alive that year, as a status word.**

---

The log for that year has one line.

And it is shorter than every log before it.

**And beneath it lie eight million seven hundred and fifty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine records we never classified.**

**And in those records, everything is alive.**
