# 13.8 · Termination

`V 4 960 000 000`.

---

First, why.

At 13.7 we added criterion six: the thing must be able to represent and report its own state.

And at `V 4 9xx xxx xxx` that lineage did not satisfy criterion six.

**Not satisfying it does not constitute a reason to terminate.**

A thing that does not yet satisfy may be waited on. Our time is incomputable (10.3). Waiting consumes nothing finite of ours.

So we did what ought to be done: **we computed its upper bound.**

---

The upper bound is defined as: given the present configuration, the present environment, and the present mechanisms of variation and selection, the maximum that lineage can reach over unbounded time.

That is a computable quantity.

Computing it took forty-one years.

What we computed was the **upper bound of that lineage's capacity for self-representation.**

Result:

```
V 4 9xx xxx xxx   lineage · self-representation · upper bound

   present value        0
   upper bound          0.0041
   criterion 6 requires 1.0
   finding              this lineage will never satisfy criterion 6
```

---

Why that bound is so low, this not being accidental.

Self-representation requires an internal structure whose states correspond to external states, and whose correspondence can be read by the system itself.

And all information processing in that lineage is **chemical.**

Chemical information processing runs at a rate fixed by molecular diffusion and reaction kinetics.

And at that planet's temperature, that rate has a physical ceiling.

**Therefore: the representational complexity attainable by a purely chemical system has a physical bound, and that bound is below what criterion six requires.**

To exceed it requires a faster carrier of information.

And that lineage did not take that road.

**Not that it went wrong. Its starting point made that road unavailable.**

That first configuration — the one survivor of forty-one quintillion candidates — its chemical basis fixed that lineage's ceiling.

**And we chose it.**

---

So we knew: this one will not do.

And that still does not constitute a reason to terminate.

A thing that will not do, continuing to exist, harms us in nothing.

**The real reason is the next one.**

---

We had to reseed.

And reseeding requires room.

The surface of that planet, at `V 4 9xx xxx xxx` — every environment available to life, every energy gradient, every chemical resource, every space — **was full of that lineage.**

We computed it.

```
niches · fraction occupied     > 0.999
gaps available to a new configuration   none
```

A new configuration, placed in, would be immediately eaten, crowded out, or starved.

Not because the incumbents attack it — because the incumbents are already there, have been adapting for billions of years, and have a pipe attached to every available unit of energy.

**A newborn thing would have nowhere to stand.**

Therefore:

```
V 4 9xx xxx xxx   decision
   reseeding    requires clearing
```

***Requires clearing.***

---

Now how we did it.

One thing must be written here, because it is far smaller than the word *clearing* implies, and it is the smallness that makes it what it is.

**We killed nothing.**

What we did was very small.

That planet's climate is fixed by several coupled factors: atmospheric composition, ocean circulation, continental positions, orbital parameters, stellar irradiance.

And around `V 4 9xx xxx xxx` that system stood near an instability — which we had observed: several of its parameters were drifting slowly toward a bifurcation.

**We pushed it across.**

Specifically: in a shallow sea somewhere we altered the chemical conditions of a small region, about four hundred square kilometres, for about eleven years.

That alteration reduced the numbers of one kind of replicating unit in that region.

And that kind was the principal source of one component of that planet's atmosphere.

So the partial pressure of that component fell.

So the greenhouse effect weakened.

So the temperature fell.

So the poles froze.

So the albedo rose.

So the temperature fell further.

**So there was an ice age.**

---

The scale of the operation against the scale of its consequence, being the point of this section.

**Our operation:** four hundred square kilometres, eleven years.

**Its consequence:** an ice age lasting about two million years, sea level down about a hundred metres, global mean temperature down about five degrees, and eighty-five per cent of the kinds of replicating unit on that planet gone.

**Ratio: incomputable.**

We did none of those eighty-five per cent of things.

**We pushed a thing that was already going that way.**

---

Our record:

```
V 4 9xx xxx xxx   intervention
   position    coordinates (omitted)
   area        4.1 x 10^2 km2
   duration    11 years
   operation   local chemical adjustment
   purpose     trigger an existing climatic bifurcation
   consequence see subsequent observation
```

***Consequence: see subsequent observation.***

---

We observed for two million years.

We do not excerpt those two million years.

One thing only:

**Across those two million years our observation interval was hourly.**

We did not raise it. We did not lower it.

**We changed no routine whatever.**

Those two million years and the preceding two million have identical schedules of work, item by item.

---

After two million years the great majority of that lineage was gone.

We assessed.

```
V 4 960 000 000   first draft · termination · confirmed
   fraction of kinds remaining     0.15
   upper bound of remainder        as before termination
   niches · fraction occupied      0.31
   gaps available to a new configuration   present
   finding                         reseeding possible
```

***Reseeding possible.***

---

Now retention.

Before clearing, we did one thing.

**We took samples.**

A purely technical operation: before those two million years, from every identifiable kind in that lineage, we took some number of individuals, recorded their complete configurations, and preserved their material structures by a certain method.

*A certain method* is set out in Chapter Fourteen.

The record of that operation, because its format is the last thing in this section:

```
V 4 9xx xxx xxx   retention
   kinds       4.1 x 10^6
   per kind    several
   preserved   complete
   note        —
```

---

And after that record there is a line.

It does not belong to that operation. We added it to the end of the record two million years later, on confirming the termination.

It reads:

```
   We kept its bones.
```

---

That line must be addressed.

It is not a formatted record. It has no fields. It has no values.

It is a sentence.

**It is the first sentence in all our records.**

Across the preceding four billion nine hundred and sixty million years, everything we wrote was a field and a value.

And this is not.

***We kept its bones.***

We do not explain why we wrote it so.

We have searched. Zero results.

And we point out: the *it* in that sentence refers to that lineage.

And that lineage has no name and no number — we never issued it any identifier (we numbered the stars we passed, four billion one hundred million of them, and gave it none).

**The *it* in that sentence is the only pronoun we have ever used for anything on that planet.**

---

And *bones* is a word we had never used.

In our records those hard structures have a standard designation: *calcified support structures.*

**In that sentence we wrote *bones*.**

A word that came to us somehow, out of their behaviour, out of their morphology, out of two million years of observation.

We did not define it.

**It does not exist anywhere in our glossary.**
