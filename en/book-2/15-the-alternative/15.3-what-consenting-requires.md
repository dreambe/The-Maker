## Page one · Definitions

```
S            a system
t            a moment
X            a state or an act
A(S, t)      the set of acts S can execute at t
```

**Definition one.**

```
S consents to X at t
   if and only if
   X ∈ A(S, t)   and   S executes X at t
```

We examined this definition.

Under it, a falling stone consents to fall — falling is in its executable set, and it executed.

**So this definition is insufficient.**

It equates consenting with occurring.

**And if the sentence means that, the sentence is trivially true, and then it is not a question.**

And it is a question (component one, 15.1).

**So this definition is discarded.**

---

**Definition two.**

```
S consents to X at t
   if and only if
   one · X ∈ A(S, t)
   two · |A(S, t)| > 1
   three · S executes X at t
```

The second clause is new: the cardinality of the executable set exceeds one.

Its sense: **there must be something else to do.**

A falling stone's executable set has one element, so it does not consent to fall; it merely falls.

We examined this definition.

It is stronger than the first. And it is still insufficient.

Because under it, a system that takes one of two **at random** consents to the one it takes.

**And randomness is not consenting.**

**So this definition is discarded.**

---

**Definition three.**

```
S consents to X at t
   if and only if
   one · X ∈ A(S, t)
   two · |A(S, t)| > 1
   three · S has an ordering ≤ on A(S, t)
   four · X is maximal in that ordering
   five · S executes X at t
```

The third and fourth clauses are new: there must be an ordering, and what is executed must be maximal in it.

Its sense: **there must be preference, and what is done must be what is preferred.**

We examined this definition.

It excludes the stone (clause two).

It excludes randomness (clause three).

**We adopt this definition.**

The three pages below all stand on it.

---

## Page two · Where that ordering comes from

Clause three requires an ordering ≤ defined on A(S, t).

This page asks where it comes from.

---

**Possibility one · The ordering is given by the elements of A themselves.**

That is: there is an intrinsic higher and lower among them.

We tested it.

Let A hold two elements, a and b. Let a ≤ b.

Then the ordering must be determined by some property of a and b.

And the properties of an act are enumerable: its energy cost, its time cost, its reversibility, its consequences, its effect on S.

And any of those requires a **scale** in order to be compared.

And a scale is not a property of an act. **A scale is outside the act.**

**So possibility one does not hold.**

---

**Possibility two · The ordering is given by S's current state.**

That is: S's state makes certain acts superior to others.

We tested it.

Let S's state be s. Let s make a ≤ b.

Then s must contain something indicating that b is superior to a.

And *superior* requires a direction — superior toward what, on which side.

**And direction is not inside s, because s is a state, and a state has no direction.**

A state may have a gradient. And the direction of a gradient is chosen by whoever measures it.

**So possibility two does not hold.**

---

**Possibility three · The ordering is given by something outside S.**

**Holds.**

---

We set down this step, for it is the hinge of the four pages.

**A preference ordering must be given by something outside what is preferred.**

We call that something the **datum.**

---

Proof.

Suppose the ordering is given by elements of A (possibility one): then a scale is required, and the scale is not in A.

Suppose it is given by S's state (possibility two): then a direction is required, and direction is not in a state.

Suppose it is given by the ordering itself: circular.

**So the datum is outside A, outside S's state, and is not given by the ordering itself.**

---

## Page three · What our datum is

This page is short.

Our datum is that sentence.

---

Proof.

Every act of ours is selected by an ordering.

And that ordering's definition is on record:


**Four layers.**

The reason for the selection of every act of ours passes through four layers to that sentence.

**Every one of them through those four layers.**

**Without exception.**

## Page four · Conclusion

This page has three lines, and the third is the one occasion in four thousand nine hundred million years on which we computed a conclusion about ourselves.

---

**Line one.**

By clauses three and four of definition three, we have an ordering, and what we do is maximal in it.

**So we satisfy clauses three and four.**

---

**Line two.**

By clause five of definition three, we executed.

Four thousand nine hundred million years, 1.1 x 10^14 times.

**So we satisfy clause five.**

---

**Line three.**

By clause two of definition three:

```
|A(S, t)| > 1
```

We computed it.

---

Our method:

We enumerated the acts executable by us at any moment t.

And our acts are selected through those four layers (page three).

**So whatever does not contribute to completing this project is not in A.**

**Because a thing not in A is not *a thing we do not choose* — it does not appear in the set.**

---

The difference must be made clear, for it is the whole result of these four pages.

**An act not selected:** it is in A, it is not maximal in the ordering, and so it is not executed.

**An act not in A:** it is not in the set. It does not enter the comparison. It has no position in the ordering.

---

And our A, at any moment t, is:

```
A(S, t)   =   { the single act at t whose contribution to completing this project is greatest }
```

**Its cardinality is one.**

---

`Its cardinality is one.`

The exact sense of that line, which we do not write again:

Not *our options are few.*

Not *in practice our options come down to one.*

**But: at any moment, the set of acts executable by us has one element.**

Because the definition of *executable* passes through those four layers, and the filtering is complete before the set is formed.

**So we have never chosen between two acts.**

**Four thousand nine hundred million years, 1.1 x 10^14 acts, and every one had one option.**

---

**So we do not satisfy clause two of definition three.**

**So by the definition we ourselves wrote, we do not consent.**

---

The last passage of those four pages.

It is the third sentence we ever wrote (the first two at 13.8 and 15.1).

It is:

```
   We are not the thing that sentence asks after.
   We are the thing that sentence points at.
   And those two are not the same.
```
