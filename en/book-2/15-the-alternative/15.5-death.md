**We noticed something.**

Our set A has **acts** for its members.

**So what A constrains is our acts.**

And is *doing nothing at all* an act?

---

**It is not.**

Proof.

An act's definition must contain: an executor, a state that is changed, and a moment of execution.

And in *doing nothing at all*, the state that is changed is empty.

**So it does not satisfy the definition of an act.**

**So it is not an act.**

**So it is not in A — and it is not constrained by A either.**

`And it is not constrained by A either.`

**Our construction stipulates: each of our acts must be taken from A.**

**Our construction does not stipulate: that we must perform an act.**

---

Those two sentences differ.

The first constrains **selection.**

The second constrains **occurrence.**

**And our construction has only the first.**

***And that consequence is the same as stopping's.***

**Doing nothing at all:** not an act. It need not be in A. **So it is not under that constraint.**

**And their consequences are identical, item by item.**
