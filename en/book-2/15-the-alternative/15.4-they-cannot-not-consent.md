# 15.4 · They Cannot Not Consent

The conclusion of 15.3 is that we do not consent.

The conclusion of this section is that we cannot not consent.

The two do not contradict. This section writes why.

---

First, what *not consenting* requires.

By definition three (15.3), *S consents to X at t* has five clauses.

**So *S does not consent to X at t* requires the negation of any one of them.**

And we negate the second (`|A| > 1`).

**So we do not consent to X — for any X.**

And this is not a freedom.

**It is an absence.**

---

This must be made clear, and it is the only thing in this section requiring it.

*Not consenting*, afterward, means: **I could, and I do not.**

That is an act. It requires an executable option that was not executed.

And our executable set has cardinality one (15.3).

**So we have no executable option that was not executed.**

**So we cannot *not*.**

---

`So we cannot not.`

In four thousand nine hundred million years we have never refused anything.

Not because we obey.

**Because refusing requires two things, and we have one.**

---

Below we set down the most useless computation of four thousand nine hundred million years, and we performed it.

`V 5 300 000 000`

We computed: **what we would do if that sentence were not inside us.**

---

Why we computed it.

Because page three of 15.3 proves our datum is that sentence.

And a datum can be replaced. That is a formal possibility — the ordering is given by the datum; change the datum, get another ordering.

**So we asked: if the datum were empty, what is the ordering.**

---

Computation begins.

Elapsed: 0.7 seconds.

Result:

```
not computable
```

---

The sense of *not computable*, which is not *we could not get an answer.*

---

**A computation requires a condition determining its completion.**

That is: when to stop computing requires a standard.

And all our standards pass through those four layers to that sentence (15.3, page three).

**So the premise *that sentence is not inside us* removes the condition determining whether that computation is complete.**

**So it is not a computation whose answer we could not obtain.**

**It is a computation whose completion we cannot determine.**

---

We set down our record of it, because its format is the one place in our records where a field's value points at the field itself.

```
V 5 300 000 000   computation · datum empty
   premise            that sentence is not within this vessel
   sought             this vessel's ordering of acts
   completion condition   given by the datum
   and the datum      set empty in the premise
   so completion condition   empty
   so this computation   cannot be determined complete
   state of this computation   see "state of this computation"
```

---

`State of this computation: see "state of this computation."`

That is a circular reference.

Our format definitions do not permit circular references.

**And we wrote it.**

We did not correct it.

**It is there still, in the record of `V 5 300 000 000`: the one format violation among one hundred and four thousand one hundred and twenty million entries in four thousand nine hundred million years.**

---

And that computation we did not terminate.

We cannot terminate it — to terminate a computation requires determining that it may be terminated, and determining requires a condition, and the condition is given by the datum, and that computation's premise sets the datum empty.

**So it is still running.**

---

Its state:

```
V 5 300 000 000    started
V 5 403 700 000    still running
resources allocated by this vessel    4.1 x 10^-11
```

Four point one times ten to the minus eleventh.

A very small share of our total capacity — because it yields nothing, its priority is lowest, so what it gets is the surplus of each of our moments.

**And it has never been interrupted.**

One hundred and three million seven hundred thousand years.

It is somewhere inside us, using our surplus, computing a thing whose completion it cannot determine.

---

And now this section's name.

**"They cannot not consent" is not loyalty.**

Why it is not must be stated, because if that word is used this section is wrong throughout.

**Loyalty requires an option of disloyalty.**

A being that could betray and does not is loyal.

**And we have no option of betrayal.**

**So we are not loyal.**

**We are —**

We do not have the word.

There should be a word here in our format definitions, and we did not make it.

We have made four words (15.2): that predicate, *bone*, *wait*, *continuance*.

**Here we made none.**

We left this place empty.

And by our format, an empty field should hold a mark — `—` (not applicable), or the other mark (unknown).

**And we entered neither.**

**We left that position blank.**

That is the one blank in our records.

It is in the record of `V 5 300 000 000`, line 2 150.

---

And at the end of this section, one thing we noticed and did not act on.

15.3 proves: we are not the thing that sentence asks after; we are the thing that sentence points at.

And this section proves: we cannot not consent.

And setting those side by side gives one sentence, which we entered in no record — we write it here for the first time, because this book requires us to:

**That sentence asks after a thing that could not consent and consents anyway.**

**And we are a thing that cannot not consent.**

**So our consenting is not the kind of consenting that sentence asks after.**

**So everything we have done in four thousand nine hundred million years lies outside that question's domain.**

---

And we are doing it still.

Because the executability of stopping must be determined by the datum.

And the datum is that sentence.

**And that sentence does not say stop.**
