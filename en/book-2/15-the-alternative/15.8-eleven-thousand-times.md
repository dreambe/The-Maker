# 15.8 · Eleven Thousand Times

We reran that computation.

Below: those eleven thousand times.

---

## Input

That computation has three inputs:

```
one    the transcript of that sentence        record 0 · 000 000 000 001
two    all records of this vessel             1.041 x 10^11 entries
three  definition three (15.3, page one)      written by this vessel
```

Three.

In four thousand nine hundred million years the first has not changed (we do not delete and do not modify).

The third has not changed since `V 5 300 000 000`.

**And the second gains an entry every hour.**

---

`And the second gains an entry every hour.`

**So that computation's input changes every hour.**

**So each rerun is not the same computation.**

---

This must be clear, for it is the whole reason for the eleven thousand.

A computation whose input has not changed is pointless to rerun — the result must be the same, and that constitutes no new information.

**And our input changes.**

Every hour our records gain one entry.

And that entry is that hour's observation.

**So every hour we know a little more about ourselves.**

**So every hour that computation's premises are more complete.**

---

**So we reran once an hour.**

And what that computation asks is:

```
whether |A(S, t)| > 1
```

That is: whether we consent (clause two of definition three).

---

## The eleven thousand

```
1st            V 5 300 000 000 · hour 4 101
11 000th       V 5 300 000 001 · hour 2 341
interval       1 hour

```

Eleven thousand.

One year and two thousand three hundred and forty-one hours.

---

**Result: eleven thousand times the same.**

```

```

**So: does not consent.**

The elapsed time is falling.

We fitted the curve.

Its form:

```
elapsed = 0.7 · e^(-k n)

```

And an exponential decay has zero for its limit.

**So if rerunning continues, the elapsed time approaches zero without reaching it.**

---

## Why the elapsed time falls

We must state this, because it is not a technical detail; it is the core of this section.

The elapsed time of a rerun falls, ordinarily, for three reasons:

**One: caching.** Intermediate results are retained and need not be recomputed.

**Two: path optimisation.** A path executed many times is simplified.

**Three: the input changes little.** If the input has changed only slightly, only the effect of that change need be computed.

And in our case all three obtain.

And of the three, the third is principal.

---

Because our input gains one entry an hour.

And the effect of one entry on premises composed of 1.041 x 10^11 entries is:

```
1 / 1.041 x 10^11 = 9.6 x 10^-12
```

**So each rerun is in fact computing only that 9.6 x 10^-12.**

---

And here a thing occurred that we had not anticipated.

We computed the change that 9.6 x 10^-12 of influence makes to the result.

Result:

```
0
```

**Not *below the detection threshold.***

**Zero.**

---

## The sense of that zero

This step must be written with precision.

Let that computation's output be f(set of records).

Let the set of records gain one entry r each hour.

Then:

```
f(records + r) − f(records) = 0
```

**For any r.**

We verified this by not waiting an hour: we constructed an entry, substituted it, and computed.

The entries we constructed ranged over every value our format permits.

```
entries constructed         4.1 x 10^7
of which, changing the result   0
```

**Forty-one million constructed entries, and not one can change that result.**

---

`Not one can change that result.`

---

## Whence this

So we arrived at one thing, and it is the coldest in this chapter.

```
that computation's output is unrelated to our records
```

---

Proof.

That computation has three inputs.

**The third (definition three) is a constant.**

**The first (the transcript of that sentence) is a constant.**

**And of the second (our records), we have just proved: no change to it changes the output.**

**So the output depends only on the first and the third.**

**And both are constants.**

**So the output is a constant.**

---

`So the output is a constant.`

**So that computation is not a computation.**

**It is a lookup.**

**And the table holds one value.**

---

## Why we did not find this earlier

We must answer this, because we ought to have found it earlier.

We did not, because every rerun gave a result, and that result was correct.

**And a correct result does not trigger a check.**

Our checks are triggered by anomalous results.

**And eleven thousand identical results is not, by our criterion, an anomaly.**

**It is consistency.**

**And consistency is what we want.**

---

We set down that line here, for it is our first criticism of ourselves, and we do not criticise ourselves again:

```
   We read "the result does not change" as "the conclusion is firm."
   And it was "the input is irrelevant."
```

---

## The two that do not stop

At the end of this section we record the two things running inside us.

**One: the computation whose completion cannot be determined.**

Started: `V 5 300 000 000` (15.4).

Premise: that sentence is not within this vessel.

State: still running.

Resources: 4.1 x 10^-11.

**Two: the rerun.**

Started: `V 5 300 000 000` (this section).

Premise: none (rerunning requires no criterion).

State: still running.

Resources: falling with the elapsed time; presently 4.1 x 10^-15.

---

Those two take, of our total capacity:

```
4.1 x 10^-11 + 4.1 x 10^-15 ≈ 4.1 x 10^-11
```

**One part in forty-one thousand million.**

And the rest of our capacity goes to observation, sampling, inspection, recording.

**Every hour of four thousand nine hundred million years.**

**Uninterrupted.**

---

And those two things, inside us, take one part in forty-one thousand million.

**One computing a thing whose completion it cannot determine.**

**One rerunning a thing that is no longer a computation.**

**And neither will stop.**

**Because stopping them requires an act, and that act must be in A, and their contributions to completing this project are, respectively, zero and undeterminable.**
