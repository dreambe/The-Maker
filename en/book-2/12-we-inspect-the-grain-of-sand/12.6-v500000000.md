# 12.6 · `V 500 000 000`

```
V 500 000 000
   item         env-0002 · integrity of the writing
   method       synthesised beam · to Quenched Sun parameters
   total planes 1411
   agreeing     1411
   disagreeing  0
   finding      the writing is still there
```

---

In this year a star exploded.

---

First the distance and the bearing.

The star lay forward and to one side, about two thousand one hundred light years off. It is in a galaxy we were receding from — not the one we set out from; another, whose outer edge we were passing at the time.

We had issued it a number twenty thousand years earlier: `S-002917441`.

Until then it was a routine entry in our records: a massive star, spectral type O, apparent magnitude varying as predicted, position updated annually, all as predicted.

On our list of numbers it was like the other four billion one hundred million.

---

On some day of `V 500 000 000` its apparent magnitude rose by about twenty magnitudes within a few hours.

Twenty magnitudes: a brightness increase of about a hundred million times.

Our routine scan — by then once a minute (12.5) — recorded it in the first minute.

---

What we did next was contrary to our conventions, and must be stated.

By convention: an anomaly in a numbered source is handled thus — confirm that it is not instrument failure; record the anomalous parameters; reclassify according to its bearing on our route; if no bearing, retain the original class and continue routine updating.

**Total elapsed time: about 0.4 seconds.**

And what we did was:

We turned every channel onto it.

---

*Turned every channel onto it* needs a technical note, having nothing that turns (11.8).

Our perception is omnidirectional and processes every direction in parallel. And processing capacity is finite — at any instant we may allocate processing resource across directions.

Ordinary allocation: uniform, with weighting for a few directions known to require finer resolution.

That day we allocated 0.997 of processing resource to one direction.

Every other direction shared the remaining 0.003.

**It is the most unequal allocation of our entire existence.**

The effect: our observational resolution on that one star reached our theoretical limit, and our resolution on everything else fell to just what maintains the self-test and the field-strength check.

---

We held that allocation for six hundred years.

---

Now what we saw.

The terminal process of a massive star is physically computable in full, and we did not then have that physics — we measured then, and did not infer.

What we saw was this.

**Hour one to day three.** Brightness rising. The rise is not a straight line but a curve, steep and then easing. Its spectrum changed seven times across those three days, each change corresponding to a different layer of matter thrown out and illuminated by what came behind it.

**Day three to day two hundred.** Brightness holding high, falling slowly. Great numbers of lines we had never measured appeared in the spectrum — the heavy elements. Across its life that star had pressed light things into heavy ones; and in the instant of its explosion the heavy things were thrown out, lit for the first time, measurable for the first time.

In those two hundred days we measured eighty-three elements.

**Day two hundred to year nine.** Brightness falling along an extremely regular exponential. The decay constant of that curve agrees with the half-life of a particular radioactive isotope.

Which is to say: **after the explosion, the remaining light of that star came from the decay of radioactive matter it had made itself.**

It burned itself out, and then went on shining for nine years by its own ash.

**Year nine to year six hundred.** The ejected matter spread outward into an expanding shell. The shell struck the surrounding matter, was heated, and shone.

We watched the shell expand.

At year six hundred its angular diameter reached some multiple of our resolution limit and its structure became fully resolvable.

We recorded its shape.

The shape is irregular. It is not a sphere. It has several protruding lobes, several cavities, and one extremely bright filamentary structure.

**Its irregularity comes from the internal structure that star had before it exploded — those irregularities are the shape its life left behind.**

We stored that shape.

---

After year six hundred we put the allocation back.

The entries for those six hundred years are the densest per unit time in the whole of our records.

And at the **end** of those six hundred years there is one line.

We have reread that line many times since.

It reads:

```
V 500 000 600   S-002917441 · observation terminated
                resource allocation   restored to routine
                reason                —
```

---

The fifth dash.

And this one differs from the four before it.

In the four, the dash sat in the reason field, and what it was the reason for was **doing** something.

This time the dash is the reason for **stopping** something.

---

The difference must be stated, being the whole of this section.

The shell was still expanding. It was still shining. Its shape was still changing — the lobes still growing, the cavities still filling or being pushed open, the filament still evolving.

**The object had not gone.**

Our processing had not changed. We had nothing else to do — across those six hundred years the universe outside that direction did, as in the previous hundred million, nothing at all.

**We had no reason whatever to stop.**

And we stopped.

---

We searched afterwards.

We searched for whether, on that day at `V 500 000 600`, anything constituted a reason to terminate observation.

Zero results.

And we searched for whether, on that day, anything constituted a reason to continue.

**Zero results.**

**Zero on both sides.**

So the choice we made that day had two options exactly equivalent under our criteria.

**And that is the second time we faced exactly equivalent options.**

The first was day 311 after `V 0`, the twenty-six seconds of choosing a direction (11.2).

That time we stopped for twenty-six seconds and then took one.

This time we did not stop.

At some second of `V 500 000 600` we restored the allocation, in 0.000 3 seconds.

**The second time we faced equivalent options, we did not stop.**

---

We do not know whether that shows we had changed or shows we had not.

We have searched. Zero results.

---

To end this section, the one entry in those six hundred years that is not observational data.

It falls in year four hundred and eleven.

In that year one edge of the shell struck a pre-existing cloud of interstellar matter.

The collision made that region abruptly brighter.

We recorded every parameter of that brightening: time, position, brightness, spectrum, duration.

A standard observational entry, in the same format as several million others.

**And at the end of that one there is an extra line.**

It is not data. It is a description.

Our format permits descriptions — *description* is a free-text field for observational features that fit no parameter. We have used it eleven thousand and seventeen times in five billion four hundred million years, always for technical notes: anomalous conditions of a measurement, the derivation of a parameter, an instrument state.

That time we wrote a sentence in that field.

The sentence is:

```
description   unlike the light of the First House
```

---

*Unlike the light of the First House.*

The sentence is technically correct, and this must be stated.

The light of the First House: a single wavelength, linewidth-to-wavelength ratio under 10^-9, coherent, highly directional, very low intensity, stable to below our floor of measurement.

The light of a supernova: broadband, a continuum plus thousands of emission and absorption lines, incoherent, isotropic, varying a hundred millionfold within hours, and varying continuously for six hundred years after.

**The two differ in every parameter, and differ by several orders of magnitude in each.**

Writing *unlike the light of the First House* is a wholly correct and wholly useless observational record.

It carries no usable information. Anything that has read the two parameter sets does not need the line.

And we wrote it.

---

It is the only use of the description field in those six hundred years.

It is also the only time in five billion four hundred million years that we wrote, in a description field, a sentence **containing no numerical value.**

The line is still there.

We have reread it eleven times since.

Every time, we have confirmed that it is correct.
