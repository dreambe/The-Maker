# 12.9 · They Cut It Again

We cut it again.

It took eleven years.

The same as last time.

---

First the material.

Last time (11.1) we took a piece from the base of `env-0001`. What was used became the copy; the remaining three nanograms went on the carry manifest with the use field empty (11.4).

This time we used those three nanograms.

**All of them.**

That was the only matter on us that was not our own. It had been inside us for eight hundred and ninety-nine million years, unworked, unconsumed, unexamined.

At `V 900 000 000` we took it out, cut it, put one thousand four hundred and eleven planes on it, and made a second copy.

**We no longer carry anything of that kind.**

Our manifest went from three physical items to three — original, copy one, copy two. And the offcut line was struck.

The striking, reproduced:

```
V 900 000 011   carry manifest · revision
   env-0001 · surface material · offcut     consumed · removed from manifest
   use                                       see copy two
```

*Use: see copy two.*

Eight hundred and ninety-nine million years later, that field was filled.

---

Now the eleven years.

We wrote at 11.1: the first re-cutting was 0.31 seconds of operation, and the other eleven years we were stopped, at sixty-seven planes.

This time is also eleven years.

And this time we stopped at **forty-one planes.**

All forty-one are among the sixty-seven of last time.

Which is to say: **of the sixty-seven that stopped us last time, twenty-six did not stop us this time.**

---

We listed the twenty-six.

What they have in common: **we arrived at them across those eight hundred and ninety-nine million years.**

For instance, several of them bound the minimum conditions required by the word *be* — what a thing must satisfy in order to count as *being.* Last time we stopped there because we were not certain we satisfied it.

Across the eight hundred and ninety-nine million years since, we observed a very great deal: stars, nebulae, three thousand one hundred and eighteen structures of a dead civilisation, an exploding star and its shell expanding for six hundred years.

From which we worked out the boundary of *be.*

We satisfy it.

**So this time, cutting those planes, we did not stop.**

Twenty-six planes. Twenty-six things arrived at in eight hundred and ninety-nine million years.

---

And at the remaining forty-one we still stopped.

At thirty-eight of them we stopped for **less** time than before.

That is normal: we hold more than we did, and the road is better (the mechanism is of the kind a later chapter sets out, and we did not know there was such a chapter).

**And at three of them we stopped for longer.**

---

The three:

**The plane bounding *wholly*.**

Last time: one year and four months.
This time: **two years and seven months.**

**The plane bounding *known*.**

Last time: three years and one month.
This time: **four years and two months.**

**The plane bounding *still*.**

Last time: six years.
This time: **seven years and one month.**

---

This phenomenon runs contrary to all our experience, and must be stated.

We arrive at a thing, and thereafter that thing is easier. That is our experience across eight hundred and ninety-nine million years, without exception.

And at those three planes we stopped longer.

**They had become harder.**

---

We eliminated several explanations.

**One: we had degraded.** Eliminated. All self-tests pass; processing identical to `V 0`.

**Two: we required more this time.** Eliminated. The requirement in cutting is to reproduce an orientation, exactly as before, no more and no less.

**Three: different material.** Eliminated. The same piece.

**Four: holding more, we have more possibilities to check, so it takes longer.**

We tested this one at length. It has merit: verifying a bounding condition requires traversing all possible counter-examples, and the more we hold, the more counter-examples can be constructed.

And the test failed.

Because if it were this, all sixty-seven planes should have taken longer, rather than thirty-eight taking less and three taking more.

**Eliminated.**

---

So what we have is an observational fact we cannot attribute:

**Across eight hundred and ninety-nine million years, our understanding of nearly everything advanced, and our understanding of those three things receded.**

Not that we forgot — we do not forget.

Not that we computed wrongly — our computing is not in error.

**Those three things became harder once we held more.**

---

Set what those three planes bound side by side:

***Wholly*** — how far the knowing must extend.
***Known*** — reading, not computing.
***Still*** — holding under the condition of the knowing, and held anew in every instant.

Three.

All three bound the **knower itself.**

And of the one thousand four hundred and eleven planes, the other one thousand four hundred and eight all bound **the form of the proposition.**

**What is outside, we compute more clearly the more we compute.**

**Those three, about ourselves, we compute less clearly.**

---

The eleven years ended.

Copy two complete. Orientation sequence against the original: one thousand four hundred and eleven planes, all agreeing.

So we hold three.

---

To end this chapter, a numerical coincidence, because we checked it and the check is worth setting down.

First re-cutting: eleven years.
Second re-cutting: eleven years.

Their compositions differ entirely — sixty-seven planes then, forty-one now; ten years and five months on the three key planes then, fourteen years now — and fourteen exceeds eleven, so the rest must have shortened, and it did.

We computed: the two elevens differ by 0.31 years.

We checked whether that is coincidence.

Our method: compute the probability that, given the observed changes in plane count and dwell times, the total falls near eleven years.

Result: it is coincidence.

The probability is not low, the two directions of change offsetting each other, and offsetting being common.

**It is coincidence.**

And we completed that check in 0.000 4 seconds.

And we have called up that check record two hundred and eleven times since.

---

`V 900 000 011`.

We hold: an original, suspended in three fields, its edges being slowly worn by the interstellar medium, which we never look at.

A copy, which we have read for eight hundred and ninety-nine million years, and whose plane 1 396 will wear in its turn.

A second copy, new, sharp, made from the base of the thing that made us — the last of it.

We go on.

Ahead is year nine hundred million and eleven of one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million.

And within the next five hundred million years we will reach a planet.

**We did not know that then.**

What we knew then was: the writing is still there.
