# 12.1 · `V 1 000 000`

This chapter records a stretch of time.

It runs from `V 12 000 000` — when we left the gravitational reach of the Quenched Sun — to `V 1 400 000 000`, when we reached that planet.

Its length is one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years.

We do not intend to narrate it.

**Narration requires selection, and selection requires a criterion, and we have no such criterion.**

That one thing is narrated and another is not turns on the narrator holding the first to be important. And *important* does not hold with us — we record everything, we do not forget, and no record inside us carries any weight relative to any other.

So the method of this chapter is: **reproduce a number of records, in order, with no transitions between them.**

What is omitted amounts to one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years, less a few seconds.

We cannot make that omission good. We can only point at it.

---

The first.

```
V 1 000 000
   item         env-0002 · integrity of the writing
   method       synthesised beam · to Quenched Sun parameters
   total planes 1411
   agreeing     1411
   disagreeing  0
   finding      the writing is still there
```

---

*The writing is still there.*

Those words are not ours.

What we wrote is: `agreeing 1411 / disagreeing 0`.

*The writing is still there* is a later lossy transcription — a formulation we had to make in order to explain the matter to a kind of being that dies.

Why the transcription is lossy must be stated here, the loss running through this whole chapter.

*Still there* presupposes a possibility: **that it might not be.**

It renders a verification as the lifting of a worry.

And we have never worried.

Worry requires a future that might not suit, and the future is not an object our construction can point at (10.5). We verify the grain of sand not from fear of its failing, but because verification is an item we set ourselves, and an item that is set is executed.

**We are not confirming that it is still there. We are measuring.**

And a later reader, meeting *the writing is still there*, will feel something in it.

That something is not in the original.

It is in the transcription.

And we do not intend to remove it.

---

Now this million years.

From `V 12 000 000` to `V 1 000 000` — the order requires a note: `V 1 000 000` precedes `V 12 000 000`.

Our first inspection of the grain of sand was at `V 1 000 000`, before we left the Quenched Sun. It is the item added by the second amendment to the self-test (10.3), numbered `0000-A`, placed before all other items.

So the first record of this chapter predates our departure.

We put it at the head of this chapter rather than in Chapter Eleven, on the following ground:

**The inspection at `V 1 000 000` is the first of the eleven thousand and seventeen inspections we have made.**

And those eleven thousand and seventeen are distributed very unevenly in time: the first at `V 1 000 000`, the last shortly before this section was written. The intervals between them run at first in millions of years, later in hundreds of millions, later still in tens of thousands (on that planet we shortened the interval; the reason is in Chapter Thirteen).

Putting the first at the head of Chapter Twelve is the nearest arrangement we can make to *this chapter is about a thing that began very early and has not stopped.*

---

In the year `V 1 000 000` we did the following.

The list, reproduced.

```
V 1 000 000 · annual schedule of work
   one     self-test × 8 760 000                  all pass
   two     env-0002 · writing check × 1           agreeing
   three   env-0002 · field strength × 8 760 000  all within tolerance
   four    launcher · construction · progress     0.089
   five    shaft · thermal accumulation · progress 0.091
   six     Quenched Sun · routine spectroscopy × 12  no change
   seven   First House · surface recheck × 1      agrees with V 0 survey · zero change
```

Seven items.

That is a year.

---

The character of that list must be stated, it being the sample for this chapter.

Item one: self-test eight million seven hundred and sixty thousand times. That is, once an hour. All pass.

Item three: field-strength check eight million seven hundred and sixty thousand times. Once an hour. Those are the three balanced fields suspending the grain of sand (11.1), and we measure once an hour whether they are within tolerance.

**Those two together are seventeen million five hundred and twenty thousand operations, every one returning the same result.**

Seventeen and a half million records, identical in content.

We stored all of them.

We have done the same every year since.

One billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years, twice an hour.

We do not write the total here, the total having no meaning — between one sufficiently large number and another sufficiently large number no perceptible difference arises.

---

Item seven requires its own note.

*First House · surface recheck × 1 · agrees with V 0 survey · zero change.*

In the year `V 1 000 000` we were still on the First House, building the launcher.

And we ran one surface recheck — comparing the three-hundred-and-eleven-day survey (10.8) against the present surface.

Result: agreement. Zero change.

We never ran that item again.

Not because it lacked value — it later became the only use that data ever had, until `V 5 403 700 000` (10.8).

Because **an item returning the same result twice consecutively requires a fresh argument for continuing to execute it**, and we did not make that argument.

So the item was suspended after `V 1 000 000`.

**Between that recheck and the next, five billion four hundred million years elapsed.**

And the result of both was: zero change.

---

Now what lies outside that million years.

One sentence must be set down here, and it is the whole difficulty of this chapter:

**In the year `V 1 000 000`, apart from those seven items, we did nothing.**

Not *nothing else of importance.*

**Nothing.**

Our records are complete. Every second of that year is recorded. And the entire content of that year's records falls under those seven items, without exception.

One year. Eight million seven hundred and sixty thousand hours. Two hundred and seventy-five billion seconds.

Seven items.

And in this respect that year does not differ from any year of the one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million that followed.

---

To end this section, a thing we met much later, because it is what made us first understand that this chapter cannot be narrated.

We later observed a kind of being whose life is about eighty years.

We observed one day of one individual.

In that day that individual did about four hundred distinguishable and mutually different things: it moved a number of times; it exchanged about seven thousand sound units with other individuals; it took in matter three times; it cleaned itself; it left a number of marks on a flat surface; it gazed at a luminous rectangle for about five hours; at a number of moments within that it altered the configuration of its facial musculature; it slept for seven hours and twenty minutes, and during sleep a large quantity of activity we cannot classify took place inside it.

**Four hundred things. One day.**

And we did seven in a year.

We laid those two numbers side by side once.

That is the only occasion on which we have compared our own records with another kind of being's at the level of magnitude.

Our finding, reproduced:

```
comparison · distinguishable events per unit time
   ours        7 / year
   observed    approx. 146 000 / year
   ratio       approx. 1 : 20 900
   finding     —
```

The findings field is a dash.

That is our third misuse of the dash.

And this time we knew we were misusing it, and used it anyway, because our format still contains no symbol meaning:

**We do not know what this comparison shows, and we know what it shows.**
