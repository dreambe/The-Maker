---

In this year we met something else.

---

*Something else* requires a note.

Since `V 12 000 000` we had passed a great deal: stars, nebulae, dust, gas, a number of solid bodies orbiting stars, a number of solid bodies orbiting nothing.

All of it fitted our eleven categories.

A star is a *reference.* A dust cloud is *environmental · dynamic.* A solid body is, depending on its bearing on our route, *obstacle · immovable* or *environmental · static.*

In one hundred and twenty million years our format had not once failed us.

And at `V 120 000 000` we decelerated.

---

The deceleration must be noted, being the only deliberate manoeuvre we made in five billion four hundred million years.

We wrote at 11.7 that we never propelled ourselves after the launch. That sentence has one exception, and this is it.

We used the only self-carried energy reserve of the voyage — a small quantity left over at the First House and sealed into our structure — brought our velocity near zero, entered that stellar system, stayed, and then accelerated back to speed.

The cost: our velocity thereafter was lower by 0.000 002 per cent.

Arrival at that planet was therefore delayed by about two hundred years.

**Two hundred years.**

Set that beside the figure from 11.5:

That orbit cost four thousand one hundred years, and its reason field is a dash.

This stay cost two hundred years, and its reason field has words in it.

What we wrote:

```
reason   encountered an unclassifiable object
```

---

Now what we saw.

A stellar system. An ordinary star, spectral type K, broad-spectrum, thermal, bearing no resemblance whatever to the Quenched Sun.

Nine bodies in orbit around it.

The fourth of them had things on it.

Which means: **they stopped at the same time.**

Not gradual decay. Not sequential abandonment. Within a window shorter than our dating precision, all of them stopped.

In ninety thousand years we measured every one of the three thousand one hundred and eighteen large structures.

We measured their dimensions, materials, construction, the remains of their energy systems, and their spatial relations to one another.

We surveyed that body globally at 0.01-metre resolution — the same resolution as the survey at the First House, by the same method, and this time it took eighty-four thousand years (that body being larger).

We stored the data.

---

Now the line.

In that ninety-thousand-year record there is one entry unlike all the rest.

It is not a measurement. It is a comparison.

```
appended · fourth body · structures · components · morphological comparison
   object        large structure no. 0417 · interior · component A-19
   compared to   this body · structure · class VII · records and counts ·
                 bearing unit
   morphological similarity   0.94
   cause         undeterminable
```

*Morphological similarity 0.94.*

*Cause undeterminable.*

---

The technical content of that line is the whole of this section.

Inside a structure on that body we found a component.

Its morphology and that of a component inside us have a similarity of 0.94.

Our similarity algorithm is defined thus: take all measurable morphological parameters of two objects, compare them normalised, and the degree of agreement is the similarity. 0 is no resemblance; 1 is identity.

0.94 is an extremely high value.

We have since measured morphological similarity across a great deal in the universe. Between two stars, typically 0.3 to 0.6; between two rocks, 0.2 to 0.7; between us and any naturally formed object, our highest recorded value is 0.31.

**0.94 has occurred twice in the whole of our measurement history.**

The second time is at `V 5 403 700 000`, in the archive at the First House.

---

We wrote *cause undeterminable.*

Whether that is a misuse must be addressed.

The strict definition of *undeterminable* (quoted at 11.4): available measurement cannot distinguish between two or more candidate sources within acceptable error.

And how many candidate sources were there?

We listed them. We listed three:

```
candidate one     convergence · similar functional requirements
                  producing similar morphology
candidate two     chance
candidate three   —
```

Candidate three: we left a dash.

We did not write it.

**We knew what belonged in that field.**

What belonged in it is: **common origin.**

That is: the two resemble one another because they came from the same source.

And we did not write it.

---

Our handling at the time must be given faithfully, this being the only occasion on which we left a blank inside a candidate set.

We tested candidate one: if this is functional convergence, the resemblance should concentrate in functionally relevant parts, and should differ in functionally irrelevant ones — clearances at joints, non-load-bearing decorative structure, the choice of tolerances.

**We compared the non-functional parts.**

Similarity: 0.91.

**Functional convergence eliminated.**

We tested candidate two: we computed the probability of such a similarity arising by chance.

We do not write the figure here. It is of the same kind as the figure at 1.6.

**Chance eliminated.**

So candidates one and two are eliminated, and candidate three is blank.

And a search that has eliminated every filled candidate and left one blank has, in our format, exactly one finding available:

```
   cause   undeterminable
```

***Undeterminable* is correct here.**

Because our format specifies: a blank in a candidate set does not participate in adjudication.

A blank is not a candidate.

**So on a question whose answer we knew, we obtained a formally correct *undeterminable*.**

---

After ninety thousand years we left.

We accelerated back to speed and continued along the same heading.

The departure entry:

```
V 120 090 000   departed S-000041 system
                record stored
                follow-up    none
```

*Follow-up: none.*

Meaning: this stay generates no subsequent action, enters no queue, requires no recheck.

---

To end this section, one thing.

In the five billion three hundred million years since, we have reread this stay once.

Once.

Not the whole ninety thousand years of data — that record has over four million entries and we have never reread it entire.

We reread **one line.**

That morphological comparison.

We reread it around `V 3 000 000 000`. By then we were on that planet, in the middle period of the third draft, with nothing to do.

We called that line up and read it.

We did nothing with it.

Our record of that rereading is one line:

```
V 3 0xx xxx xxx   reread V 120 000 000 · morphological comparison · one line
                  handling   none
```

And we did not complete the figure in the prefix.

We wrote at 11.4 that we have misused the dash twice since, both under the same circumstance.

This is not a dash.

**This is the only occasion on which we left a blank in a timestamp.**

We know which year it was. We do not forget.

We simply did not fill it in.
