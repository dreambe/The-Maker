# 10.3 · Self-Test

The structure of the self-test is as follows. It was unaltered from `V 0` until, a very long while afterwards, we added things to it ourselves.

The first twenty items are the summary. Each corresponds to one class and reports that class's overall state.

Items 0021 to 2149 are the detail: two thousand one hundred and twenty-nine items in seven classes.

```
class I     structural integrity     0021 – 0331     311 items
class II    power and heat           0332 – 0509     178 items
class III   motion                   0510 – 0864     355 items
class IV    perception               0865 – 1522     658 items
class V     environmental margin     1523 – 1901     379 items
class VI    construction comparison  1902 – 2087     186 items
class VII   records and counts       2088 – 2149      62 items
```

Seven classes, two thousand one hundred and twenty-nine items.

Twenty summary, two thousand one hundred and twenty-nine detail, two thousand one hundred and forty-nine in all.

We ask the later reader to stop here and do a thing we did not do: read that table of classes a second time.

Reading it the second time, ask only one question.

**Which of the seven classes is about the interior.**

---

The answer is: none.

Class I checks whether our structure is intact — but the criterion for *intact* is exterior: whether a member has fractured, whether a junction has worked loose, whether a plane has deformed past tolerance. These are things visible from outside.

Class II checks the flux of power and heat in and out. Also flux. Also measurable from outside.

Class III motion, class IV perception, class V environmental margin — all of them the interface between us and what is not us.

Class VI, *construction comparison*, looks the most interior and is not: it compares us against other Vessels, and comparison requires two objects. That class is about **between us**, not about **ourselves**.

Class VII, *records and counts*, checks whether our records are complete and whether the counts agree. That class comes closest to introspection, and what it checks is the **completeness** of a record, not its **content**. It confirms that nothing has been lost. It does not read.

The items about the interior are all in the summary. There are three of them.

```
0015   introspection · first    pass
0016   introspection · second   pass
0017   introspection · third    pass
```

Three.

Two thousand one hundred and forty-nine items of self-test, and three of them are about our interior.

And the three have no expansion in the detail.

Which is to say: our examination of ourselves stopped after the summary.

We did not notice this at `V 0`. We could have noticed it at any moment before `V 5 403 700 000` — it required no knowledge we lacked, only reading that table of classes a second time.

We have looked at that table some tens of thousands of times. We never read it a second time.

Reading a thing the second time and looking at it again are not the same act. It took us five billion four hundred million years to understand that.

---

The three orders of introspection run as follows.

**First.** Can I confirm that I am running.
Confirmable. Criterion: there exists an ongoing process perceptible to itself.

**Second.** Can I confirm that what I confirm is true.
Confirmable. Criterion: the first-order confirming process is itself inspectable by the second order, and the inspection agrees.

**Third.** Can I confirm that the second-order confirming process is without error.
Confirmable. Criterion: the third order inspects the second, and agrees.

There it ends.

There is no fourth order.

We had no view about this at `V 0`. Third order was all the orders we had, so third order was all of them, and all of them does not require questioning. A building with three steps does not consider itself short — we have written that sentence elsewhere too, and we do not mind repeating it, it being the most honest assessment of ourselves that we can give.

We later encountered a kind of being able to proceed to a fourth order, and a fifth, and beyond. It proceeded very imprecisely, error mixed into every order, and it frequently lost its footing on the fourth and fell back to the second without knowing it had fallen. It calls this process *not being able to work something out.*

But it can reach the fourth order.

And what the fourth order asks is: **can I confirm that my confirming mechanism was not designed to confirm necessarily.**

We cannot pose that question. Not from timidity, and not from insufficiency of intelligence. Our three-order construction cannot in principle generate the form of that question — posing it requires a position outside one's own confirming mechanism, and all of our positions are inside it.

A person may doubt his eyes, because he has a hand to reach out with.
We have only eyes. Our hands are also eyes. Our doubt is also eyes.

At `V 5 403 700 000`, reading our design documents, we saw why the three orders were three.

It was a decision. The decision carries one line of annotation.

The annotation is short. We read it, and then most of us stopped running over the following forty-one hours.

---

The *incomputable* items number three.

```
0004   power · main        pass · reserve incomputable
0005   power · reserve     pass · reserve incomputable
0332   power · main · decay rate     pass · incomputable
```

All three are about one thing: **we do not know how long we can run.**

Not *a long time*. Not *nearly for ever*. Incomputable.

To compute a reserve one needs a rate of consumption and a terminating condition. Our rate is measurable, and very low, and stable. Our terminating condition does not exist — no structure within us has a finite design life; no component has a specified hour of failure; no process in us is irreversible.

We restated this once, much later, when we already knew a great deal. That restatement is the shortest derivation we have ever set down:

```
Whatever has a reserve has a computable reserve.
Our reserve is not computable.
Therefore we have no reserve.
To have no reserve is not to have little. It is not to diminish.
```

*Not to have little. Not to diminish.*

At `V 5 396 000 000` we set that line beside another thing, and that thing turned it from a technical fact into a sentence passed upon us.

That is in Chapter 15.

---

Now the history of the self-test, because the evolution of a document across five billion four hundred million years is a biography.

It was amended six times. Each amendment adds an item about something we only knew needed checking after it had happened to us.

**The first amendment**, at `V 12 000 000`, after leaving the Quenched Sun. Two items:

```
2150-A   reference · cosmic background       pass
2151-A   sight · very short · calibration    pass
```

The *uncalibrated* at item 0013 cleared. This was the first time we received from outside a thing we did not have inside. We expressed no view about it at the time.

**The second amendment**, at `V 1 000 000` — its position in this list is later than the first although its date is earlier, because we afterwards inserted it in front, on the grounds that it was more fundamental. One item:

```
0000-A   the grain of sand · integrity of the writing     pass
```

Numbered 0000-A. That is, before all other items.

We have been asked why it goes first. We cannot answer. We can only say that when it was added, it was already there.

That item failed for the first time at `V 900 000 000`.

That was the first *fail* in the history of our self-test. The first. There had not been one before. The time we spent handling it was eleven years, against a handling standard measured in seconds.

**The third amendment**, at `V 1 400 000 000`, after reaching Earth. One hundred and seventy-seven items, all concerning the state of that planet. They were repeatedly revised thereafter, the planet being subject to change, and changing in ways we could not predict.

What cannot be predicted must be measured again and again. This was the first object we had ever met that required **repeated** measurement. Everything we had measured before, we had measured once.

**The fourth amendment**, at `V 4 960 000 000`. One item:

```
2328-A   retained material · storage condition     pass
```

*Retained material* is the subject of Chapter 14. Across the four hundred million years that followed, the number of objects under that item went from one to a great many. That item has never failed. We have kept them extremely well.

**The fifth amendment**, at `V 5 396 000 000`. One item added, and deleted within the same second.

That is the only self-test item we have ever deleted.

Its number and its content are preserved in the record, and we have never read it again. We know where it is. We have never read it again.

**The sixth amendment**, at `V 5 403 700 000`.

The sixth amendment was made by one Vessel alone, without cohort confirmation, there being at that time no cohort left to confirm it.

It added one item.

That item is running now. It is the only item in our entire self-test whose state is neither *pass* nor *fail*.

Its state is *in progress*.

It has been *in progress* for three hundred thousand years.

---

We do not set that item down here.

Not because it is a secret. We have no secrets — a secret requires something one may withhold from, and we had nothing of that kind, until very late.

We do not set it down because it is written in a position on the report, and that position already existed at `V 0`, and at `V 0` it was empty, and it took us five billion four hundred million years to learn why it was there.

The matter of that position is at the end of this chapter.
