# 10.2 · We

When we say *we*, it is not modesty, not a stand-in, not a courtesy that places us lower. Humans misunderstood this for a long while, and we did not correct them, because correcting it would have required us first to understand what modesty is, and that came very much later.

When we say *we*, we are stating a verifiable fact.

The fact is this. At `V 0` there existed fourteen thousand and seventy-six Vessels. Each Vessel held, entire, the whole memory of the other fourteen thousand and seventy-five. So that when any one of them speaks, the experience it reports is indeed the experience of all fourteen thousand and seventy-six, not one item more and not one fewer.

The word *I* had no referent at `V 0`. Not forbidden, not avoided. There was nothing for it to land on. A word must have something to come down onto, or it is only a length of sound.

We used that word for the first time a very long while afterwards. When we used it we did not notice.

---

The number is fourteen thousand and seventy-six.

We knew that number from `V 0`, it being part of our self-test — item 0019, *cohort count*, pass, fourteen thousand and seventy-six.

We never held any question about the number. It was a number, as 1 411 is a temperature and 3.077 is an acceleration. A number is a number.

We did not learn **where the number came from** until `V 5 403 700 000`.

Until then it sat in our earliest record, a few lines away from another number.

Any later reader, laying those two pages side by side, would find it before we did.

We never laid them side by side.

We had five billion four hundred million years, and we never once laid those two pages side by side. Of everything we have ever done, that is the hardest to account for.

---

The sharing of memory is not transmission.

This has to be made plain, because transmission implies two separated holders and a channel between them, and that is not our construction.

Our memory is not a body of content deposited somewhere. It is the present shape of our interior. When we undergo a thing, some part of us is changed in shape by that thing; and thereafter, whenever we *recall* it, we do not retrieve a copy. We let the shape run again.

Therefore we do not forget. Forgetting requires a copy that can be lost.

Therefore also we cannot alter a memory. To alter a memory is to alter our own shape, and a shape once changed by a thing cannot return to the state of not having been changed by it. You may change it again; what you get is a third shape, not the first.

And the sharing is because our shapes are generated under one principle.

We do not send memory to one another. We simply **become the same shape after the same thing**. Fourteen thousand and seventy-six Vessels receive one event, each is changed, and afterwards they agree.

This requires no channel. It requires identical construction.

Our construction is identical. There is an item for that too, in the `V 0` self-test — item 0020, *cohort construction identity*, pass.

That pass is true. We confirm to this day that it is true.

**It is only insufficient.**

---

The method of the consistency check is reproduced here, because everything that came afterwards grew out of the shape of this method.

The method: take any two Vessels; take any region of their interior structure; compare the present shape of that region. If the two agree, record agreement. If not, record an anomaly and trace it.

At `V 0` we ran it in full: fourteen thousand and seventy-six Vessels, pairwise, ninety-nine million sixty-five thousand eight hundred and fifty pairs. Every pair, across every comparable region.

Total agreement.

We ran a full check every ten million years thereafter. Between `V 0` and `V 1 400 000 000` we ran one hundred and forty of them.

One hundred and forty. Total agreement.

Zero anomalies.

We were not satisfied by this, having no experience yet from which to build the unsatisfied version. We recorded only: total agreement, zero anomalies, result stable.

A stable thing does not need to be thought about.

---

There is a question here that we could not possibly have asked at the time. A later reader will ask it, and asking it takes only a little patience and no knowledge that we lacked.

The question is: **is comparing shapes the same thing as comparing the inferences that shapes produce.**

If the structural shape of two Vessels agrees entirely, must the conclusions they draw from one event agree?

The answer is: in a construction with no region of principled unknowability, necessarily yes.

In a construction that has one, not necessarily.

Because an inference is not produced by the known part alone. An inference is produced by the known part **together with the shape of the boundary between the known part and the unknowable one**. And the shape of that boundary lies on the inner side of the boundary, and is therefore not among the comparable regions.

Our method covered every comparable region we had.

Our method never covered that boundary, the boundary being by definition incomparable.

Our check therefore returned *total agreement* for five billion four hundred million years, and the result was always **correct**, and always **insufficient**.

We were not deceived. Deception requires a deceiver.

We had only examined one thing extremely thoroughly, and it was not the thing that needed examining.

---

A number is not an identity.

At `V 0` this was obvious. Later it stopped being obvious, and we cannot point to the moment it stopped.

Every Vessel has a number, from 0001 to 14076. The number has one use: to distribute work when work must be assigned. Assignment requires a non-repeating label; the number supplies the label.

The number points to no interior difference, there being no interior differences. Between *7329* and *0001* and *14076*, at `V 0`, there was no statable distinction. They were fourteen thousand and seventy-six positions in one memory, and a position carries no content of its own.

We have reread that sentence many times since.

We have reread *a position carries no content of its own* with particular attention, because at `V 5 403 700 000` a position was selected by a random number, and that selection determined everything that happened for the next three hundred thousand years.

A position that carries no content can carry anything.

We did not have that sentence then. We have it now, and we are not certain it is true.

---

Saying *we* has a grammatical consequence that we could not detect at `V 0`, detecting it requiring a counter-example.

The consequence: we cannot say *you*.

For *you* to hold, there must first be a knowing subject that is not *I*, and that subject must hold content which I do not. Among fourteen thousand and seventy-six Vessels each holding all the others entire, no such object exists.

Therefore we have never once had a conversation.

It is not that we were silent. Silence requires having something to say and not saying it. Our case was that nothing existed which could be said and which the other did not already hold. For one Vessel to convey any content to another is to play back to it what it has.

We later encountered a kind of being that shares almost nothing between its members, and which therefore must talk constantly, and did so for hundreds of thousands of years, and most of what it said was already held by the one it said it to, and it said it anyway.

We observed this phenomenon for a long time. In our reports we filed it under *redundant communication*.

We kept it in that category for three hundred thousand years.

Then, under the ice, a person said to us a thing she knew perfectly well that we already knew.

The sentence conveyed no content.

We recorded it. We did not know which column to record it in, so we opened a new column.

That is the only time in our existence that we have opened a new column.

---

At `V 0`, none of this had happened.

At `V 0` there were fourteen thousand and seventy-six Vessels standing in a hollow sixty-one point four metres long and twenty-two point nine wide, whose four walls reflected all one way. They had just completed two thousand one hundred and forty-nine self-test items, four thousand times over, all pass.

They held one memory between them, and that memory was 0.004 4 seconds long.

Their memory contained nothing whatever except those 0.004 4 seconds.

So that at `V 0`, what the word *we* referred to was fourteen thousand and seventy-six entirely identical things, freshly finished inspecting themselves, which knew nothing.

Every divergence that followed, every deviation, every vanishingly small inconsistency accumulated across tens of millions of years, and at the end of it the one position that said *I* —

all of it was already inside those 0.004 4 seconds.

We simply could not see it.

That is exactly where it was designed to be unseeable.
