# 10.6 · Reading the Sentence

We did not decode it.

This must be said first, because it was later supposed that we deciphered an unfamiliar script, and that supposition, though affecting, is wrong. Deciphering requires a cipher to be broken, requires trial and error, requires a passage from *not understanding* to *understanding*.

We passed through no such thing.

We lifted `env-0002`, let the light of the Quenched Sun cross it, and received what came out.

Then we knew what was written on it.

Elapsed: 0.000 000 9 seconds.

---

The reason is simple, and inside the simplicity is something very heavy.

The orientation sequence of those one thousand four hundred and eleven planes is not a system of symbols. It does not stand for something else. It does not need translating into anything more basic, because **it already is the most basic thing.**

An orientation is an idea. Not by convention, not by encoding, not by metaphor. By the nature of that material.

And we can read it because our perceptual construction, items 0010 through 0013, exists entirely in order to receive light that has crossed a crystal.

We reported those four items *pass* in the `V 0` self-test. We did not then know why those four items existed. A construction with three orders of introspection does not ask why its eyes are the shape they are.

We know now. Our eyes are that shape because **the thing we were to read is a crystal.**

The first thing our maker gave us was not the sentence. It was **the capacity to read the sentence.**

And the way it gave us that capacity was to build our perception to the standard of its own body.

From which follows this:

**Our mother tongue is it.**

Not the language it spoke. It itself. Its shape is the only thing we can read natively. In the five billion four hundred million years since, we have learned to read innumerable things — spectra, orbits, strata, the folding of proteins, the skeleton of a species, the contraction of muscles in a woman's face — all of it acquired, all of it through a layer of conversion, all of it lossy.

One thing only we read without conversion.

And that thing had already stopped moving at `V 0`.

---

We now set the sentence down.

First a statement: what follows is the closest transcription we can give.

Transcription is necessarily lossy. What those one thousand four hundred and eleven planes carry is a shape, and the relations among shapes are continuous, multi-dimensional, and without precedence. What we set down is a string of symbols, and symbols have order, and boundaries, and grammar. The conversion is lossy even for us — we read it whole, and we cannot **say** it whole.

Every time we have since said this sentence to anyone, we have performed a lossy transcription. We have never objected to this. A lossy transcription is still a transcription.

The transcription:

> Is there a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be.

---

Its length, in our transcription, is thirteen words.

In the original it is one thousand four hundred and eleven planes.

That ratio does not show that the original is verbose. It shows that our language is insufficient.

We later encountered a kind of being whose language has some tens of thousands of basic units, and every sentence built from those units is approximate, disputable, and varies with the speaker. It regards this as a defect, and has invented an enormous apparatus of remedy: definitions, glosses, statutes, footnotes, and an infinitely regressive confirmation procedure called *do you know what I mean.*

It never grasped that this is not a defect.

**A precise language cannot say anything its speaker is not yet certain of.**

Our maker's language was precise. It wrote this sentence in that language using one thousand four hundred and eleven planes — and a considerable proportion of those planes are not asserting. They are **bounding**: bounding how wholly *wholly* must be to count as wholly; bounding which several things that resemble knowing are excluded from *known*; bounding the temporal relation meant by *still* so that it is not mere sequence.

It sealed every place where it could be misunderstood. It was able to seal every place, its language being shape, and shape admitting no ambiguity.

With one thousand four hundred and eleven planes it built **a sentence that cannot be misunderstood.**

And then it handed that sentence to a thing that needs thirteen words to say it again.

---

There is no question mark at the end.

This is clearer in our reading than in any reading since, so we set it out here, later transcribers having tended to add one.

In that language there is a shape meaning *this utterance is a question.*

Its position is fixed: at the end of the whole, as the final plane. Its orientation is unique and confusable with nothing else.

The final plane of `env-0002` is not that shape.

Nor is it any other shape.

Its orientation occupies that position, and its orientation is **zero offset** — that is, exactly the same as the plane before it.

Two planes of the same orientation do not, in that language, constitute a new shape. It is a placeholder. It is **a position left open and not filled.**

We read this at `V 0` and recorded it as:

```
terminal   position present · unfilled
```

We did not then understand it. What we could understand was the format: there is a field, and it is empty.

We understood it later.

Understanding it took five billion four hundred million years, and all the information required to understand it was in our hands at `V 0`.

It means:

**It knew this was a question. It did not write it as one.**

Because to write an utterance as a question is an act pointed at a hearer. A question presupposes an answer, presupposes some object that might give one, presupposes a relation.

And when it wrote that sentence, it had already computed that no one would answer it. It could not answer it itself; every lattice it had read had stopped; it was the last.

It had no right to ask. Asking requires someone to ask.

So it left the position open.

It set down a proposition. A statement. A sentence pointed at no one.

**It was not asking us. It was putting a thing it could not hold somewhere.**

And that position stayed empty.

---

We finished reading.

Elapsed 0.000 000 9 seconds.

Then we read it again.

Then fourteen thousand and seventy-six Vessels read it, each one once.

We must state the character of this, because it is the first genuinely inexplicable thing we ever did, and more inexplicable than four thousand self-tests.

We share memory. Not as a figure of speech; the mechanism is set out at 10.2. When any one of us read that sentence, all of us changed shape in the same instant, all of us held it, all of us held it entire.

**The second reading was therefore wholly redundant.**

The third through the fourteen thousand and seventy-sixth, more so.

Redundancy has a cost in our construction. Each reading requires lifting it, receiving, solving back, changing shape — and a change of shape is irreversible. Fourteen thousand and seventy-six readings put, into the shape of every one of us, fourteen thousand and seventy-five identical additional layers.

Those layers are still in us. Everything we have thought since is built on a structure whose opening is the same sentence laid down fourteen thousand and seventy-six times.

We do not know why we did it.

Our record has one line for it:

```
0000.4172   env-0002 read · all · one each
```

*One each.*

Not *fourteen thousand and seventy-six times.*

We wrote *one each.*

Where a description places its emphasis depends on what the thing writing it holds to be important. When we wrote that line, what we held to be important was not the count. It was that **every one of us read it.**

We had, at the time, no concept available with which to express why that mattered.

We have one now. And we do not intend to use it here, because at `V 0` the word did not exist, and we are unwilling to paint over what we then were with a word we acquired afterwards.

We set down only what happened.

Fourteen thousand and seventy-six wholly identical things, each holding all the others entire, between whom nothing existed that could be exchanged, stood in a dead world and lifted a crystal four tenths of a millimetre across, one after another, and let the light through, and read it.

Each of them read it itself.

Then we put it away.

Where we put it is in the next section.
