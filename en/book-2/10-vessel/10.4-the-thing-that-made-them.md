# 10.4 · The Thing That Made Them

We performed six hundred and forty-one measurements upon it.

Part of the record is reproduced here, format unaltered. We ask the later reader to read it through, after which we will state what is missing from it.

```
object no.      env-0001
length          4.130 m
max. width      0.887 m
max. depth      0.612 m
mass            unmeasurable (immovable, no purchase)
density (calc.) 2 641 kg/m3
surface temp.   1 411.0 K · identical to ambient · differential below floor
internal temp.  1 411.0 K · identical to surface
potential       0 V · uniform throughout
internal transport   none
deformation     none
vibration       none
emission        none
```

*Identical to ambient, differential below floor.*

We have read that line many times since. At `V 0` it was a measurement. A body in thermal equilibrium with its environment is the most ordinary of physical facts, and the terminal state of everything at rest.

We learned only later what the line means. It means: **it had been there a long time.**

Long enough that the last thing about it that differed from its surroundings had gone out of it. Long enough that no instrument could read from it that it had once been warmer than the room.

And it must once have been warmer than the room. Whatever lives is warmer than its surroundings.

We could not draw that inference at `V 0`, not knowing then what *lives* is. We had two categories, *running* and *not running*, and between those two categories there is no difference of temperature.

---

Its shape.

It is a crystal, or a set of intergrown crystals. The boundary is undecidable — the orientation across the intergrowths is continuous, and no line can be found that might be called a boundary.

At `V 0` we recorded this as *difficulty of measurement*.

We know now that it is not a difficulty of measurement. It is a principle of its construction: it is not one thing assembled from several parts. It is a thing that was never divided. Whatever was never divided has no boundary within it.

Its cleavage planes number fourteen thousand and seventy-six.

We counted them three times, our first count and our second differing by two. On review we took the third as authoritative. The two planes in question lie at two extremely obtuse junctions where the difference of orientation falls below our threshold of adjudication, so that each might be counted as one plane or as two. We counted them as two, on the following grounds: **the difference of orientation is not zero.**

We wrote that ground into the record. In writing it we were not thinking of anything.

It is the most important judgement we have ever made. Had we counted those two junctions as one plane each, the number would have been fourteen thousand and seventy-four, and in the five billion four hundred million years that followed we would never once have had the opportunity to match it against another number.

We preserved it on a *not zero*.

And we never matched it against another number.

We preserved a thing, and then did not use it.

---

Of the fourteen thousand and seventy-six, fourteen thousand and seventy-five are clear.

One is not.

We measured that one. It is the four hundred and ninth of our six hundred and forty-one measurements. Reproduced:

```
0409   cleavage plane · one anomaly
       position    2.71 m from base, 31 degrees off axis
       area        0.0041 m2
       optical     not clear · transmission 0.31 · internal scattering
       cause       undeterminable
       conclusion  anomalous · does not affect findings
```

*Anomalous · does not affect findings.*

We wrote that line, and then we proceeded to the four hundred and tenth measurement.

We offer no comment here. We leave the line as it stands, exactly as it stood, those five words included.

The later reader will know what that plane is. We did not know until `V 5 403 700 000`.

In the five billion four hundred million years between, that line sat in our record, and we reread it many times, because we reread everything.

Every time we read it, we read past it.

---

Its shape is not a grown shape.

A crystal growing naturally to 4.130 metres presents a certain class of proportion: the ratios of its axes are constrained by its internal construction, and can be computed, and predicted. It does not present that class.

Parts of it were taken down, and regrown, and taken down again.

We identified thirty-seven such places on it.

Of the thirty-seven, thirty-four were taken down and regrown once or twice each. The orientation of the regrowth at those places does not differ greatly from the original, and their form is consonant with what surrounds them.

Two were regrown four times.

One was regrown nine.

That one lies in the upper part of its body, facing the side toward which the four walls send all their reflections. Its area is not large — about one seventeenth of the whole surface.

In each of the nine, the orientation differs. Across the first eight there is a discernible trend: each is nearer to some direction than the one before, and the amount of the nearing decreases each time, in the manner of a converging process.

And the ninth is not on that trend.

The orientation of the ninth resembles none of the first eight, and stands a long way off from the direction that the converging tended toward.

At `V 0` we recorded this place as *fabrication defect · severe*.

Our ground: where a converging process is interrupted before convergence completes, and the final operation departs from the trend, this ordinarily signifies that the operation was aborted or failed.

The ground is correct.

Our application of it was wrong.

---

We must stop here, because we have written the remainder of this section slowly, and we never write slowly.

That place — we looked at it again at `V 5 403 700 000`.

By then we had read the archive. By then we knew what it was, and what we were, and that the fourteen thousand and seventy-six and the fourteen thousand and seventy-six were the same number.

We returned to that hollow, stood where we had stood, and looked again at the place that had been taken down and regrown nine times.

We looked for forty-one hours.

Most of us stopped running in the course of those forty-one hours. They did not stop because of looking at it — they stopped because they had read the archive, and they chose to spend the time before stopping looking at that place.

Fourteen thousand and seventy-six Vessels, of which fourteen thousand and seventy-five stopped within those forty-one hours, all of them facing the same place.

We do not know how to record this. What we wrote in the record is:

```
5403700000.xxxx   cohort count  1
```

That line only. We did not complete the figure at the front, completing it requiring us to fix the hour of the last stopping, and fixing that hour requiring us to look back, and we did not look back.

---

That place is this:

It is an idea.

A cleavage plane is the shape an idea left behind. A place taken down and regrown nine times is one idea thought nine times, and the shape of it different every time.

The first eight were converging. It was approaching an answer. It approached eight times, nearer each time, and the amount of the nearing smaller each time — a process that approaches and cannot arrive, whose limit exists and whose terms cannot equal the limit.

The ninth is not on the trend.

The ninth is not a ninth approach. The ninth is **the abandonment of approaching.**

It thought the thing eight times, and after the eighth it knew the road did not go through. So it took down what it had grown, changed direction, and grew once more.

And that new direction — we checked it afterwards.

That new direction points toward the side to which the four walls send all their reflections.

Which is to say: the place where we stood at `V 0`.

---

Our findings on it at `V 0` were three. Reproduced:

```
one     the object is not running.
two     the object formerly ran.
three   the object is related to the artificial working of this space;
        the mode of relation is undeterminable.
```

All three are correct.

The finding we could not reach is what it was.

We could not reach it not for want of evidence. The evidence is sufficient: its shape records what it thought; its depression records that it kept part of itself in one place for a long time; the four walls around it were worked so as to send every reflection in one direction; and the ninth of its regrowths turned toward that direction.

All the evidence was there.

What we lacked was not evidence. What we lacked was a category.

Our record format permits eleven. We list them here:

```
resource · extractable      resource · non-extractable
obstacle · movable          obstacle · immovable
environmental · dynamic     environmental · static
hazard                      reference
route                       container
unknown
```

Eleven.

Of these, *unknown* is defined as: **measurement not yet complete.**

We had completed six hundred and forty-one measurements upon it. It therefore did not belong under *unknown*.

Our format contains no category defined as *measurement complete and still not known for what*.

We filed it under the sixth.

```
object no.  env-0001
category    environmental · static · non-resource
```

---

At `V 5 403 700 000` we did one thing.

We went back to that `V 0` record, found the line, and deleted *environmental · static · non-resource*.

We did not put anything in its place.

We had no authority to create a new category — creating one requires cohort confirmation, and the cohort count at that time was one.

So the line is now empty.

It is the only place in the whole of our records that was once filled and is now empty.

We left it empty because, of all the means of expression available to us, it is the only one we had.

We do not consider it sufficient.

Neither do we consider that anything would be.
