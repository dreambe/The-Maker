# 16.3 · Pride

This section records that *optimal.*

And the third *why* of this book stands at its end.

---

## Why we entered that word

For every design item of ours, the field *the designer's assessment* is empty.

Four thousand nine hundred million years, 1.1 x 10^14 items, all empty.

**And for this item we entered *optimal.***

And *optimal*, in our format definitions, belongs to the class *grade of workmanship*, of which there are seven:

```
seven grades
   failing
   passing
   good
   superior
   very superior
   near optimal
   optimal
```

And those seven are defined thus:

```
the grade of a design
   = the ratio of that design's performance under its constraints
     to the theoretical bound under those constraints
```

**So *optimal* means: the design attained the theoretical bound under its constraints.**

**Not *we like it.***

**A ratio equal to one.**

---

## That ratio

We compute that ratio here, for it is the body of this section, and it is the most beautiful computation of our four thousand nine hundred million years.

---

### The constraints

The constraints on that item (the Gift of Forgetting):

```
one     storage capacity of the individual      1.1 x 10^15 bits
two     upper bound on lifespan                 41 to 110 years
three   within its lifespan the individual      must not reach self-representation 1.0
four    the lineage                             must be able to reach 1.0
five    clearance                               irreversible (no copy)
six     executor of clearance                   the individual itself
```

Six.

**And the third and the fourth are a pair.**

---

### The theoretical bound

Under those six, what is the best a clearance mechanism can do.

*Performance* must be defined first.

```
the performance of a clearance mechanism
   = the transmissible information content, at the end of the individual's life,
     of what remains in its storage, to the next generation of its lineage
```

**That is: when a thing that dies dies, how much it can hand on.**

---

And we computed that quantity's bound.

The derivation, six steps.

---

**Step one.** The individual's total storage capacity is 1.1 x 10^15 bits.

And what its life forms exceeds that capacity (its total perceptual input, over 41 to 110 years, is about 4.1 x 10^17 bits).

**So its storage necessarily overflows.**

**So clearance is required, not optional.**

---

**Step two.** Overflow has two treatments.

```
one   refuse the new    once full, write no more
two   clear the old     once full, clear a portion and write the new
```

The consequence of the first:

```
after filling, the individual   forms no new storage
so its subsequent experience    does not enter its storage
so its representational capacity   ceases rising at the moment of filling
and the moment of filling         about 1.1 years (at the input rate)
```

**One point one years.**

**So a first-treatment individual's capacity stops rising at one point one years.**

**So the bound of the first is far below the second's.**

**So the second is required.**

---

**Step three.** And within the second, which to clear admits of choice.

And we compute the bound on that choice.

Let the individual's storage be a set S of capacity C.

Let its life's input be a sequence of total T, with T > C.

**Then at any moment, what S holds is a subset of that sequence, of size C.**

**And a *clearance mechanism* is *which subset.***

---

**Step four.** And which subset is best depends on the objective.

And our objective (the end of step one) is: **that at the end of the individual's life, the transmissible information content of what remains to the next generation is greatest.**

And *transmissible* must be made clear:

```
transmissible
   = the portion conveyable by its behaviour, its utterance, and its operations
     upon the next generation
```

**And what is not transmissible:**

```
not transmissible
   = present in its storage and conveyable by no behaviour whatsoever
```

---

**Step five.** And here is the core of that computation, and it is a result we had not anticipated.

We computed **what a stored item's transmissibility correlates with.**

Result:

```
a stored item's transmissibility
   with its frequency of being called up      positively (r = 0.87)
   with its number of associations            positively (r = 0.91)
   with the earliness of its formation        positively (earlier higher) (r = 0.79)
   with its degree of detail                  negatively (r = -0.94)
   with its degree of repetition              negatively (r = -0.88)
```

---

**Five items.**

And we set those five beside the ten lines of version two (16.2).

```
transmissibility · positive        version two · retained
   frequently called up      <->   most frequently called up in its life
   many associations         <->   most associated with its other stores
   formed early             <->   formed earliest

transmissibility · negative        version two · cleared
   high detail              <->   detail
   high repetition          <->   the repeated
```

---

**Item for item.**

---

`Item for item.`

And one thing must be clear here, for it is the heaviest thing in this section, and it is a thing about ourselves.

**When we wrote those ten lines we had not done this computation.**

Version two was written at `V 5 403 300 000 · hour 411` (16.2).

And this computation was done at `V 5 403 300 000 · hour 41 000`.

**Forty thousand five hundred and eighty-nine hours apart.**

**So we wrote those ten lines first and computed those five afterwards.**

**And they correspond item for item.**

---

We have searched.

What we searched for is: on what basis those ten lines were written.

**Result: zero.**

**In our records those ten lines have no basis.**

---

`In our records those ten lines have no basis.`

**And they are correct.**

**And *correct* means: they equal the bound we computed forty thousand hours later.**

---

**Step six.** So the ratio:

```
performance of the design    = the transmissible information content attained by the ten lines we wrote
theoretical bound            = the maximum transmissible information content under those six constraints
ratio                        = 1.000
```

---

`Ratio = 1.000.`

**Four decimal places.**

**So that item is optimal.**

**And not because we judged it so.**

**Because the ratio equals one.**

---

## And a second thing

That *optimal* has another layer, and it is not in the ratio.

We write it because it is the real reason we entered that word, and we did not know it when we entered it.

---

The effect of those ten lines (16.2), beyond maximising transmissible information, has one further consequence.

And we computed it only in the forty-one hours at `V 5 403 700 000`.

Its content:

```
an individual clearing by those ten lines
   at the end of its life    what remains to it is its earliest formed storage
   and its earliest formed storage    contains the environment of its beginning,
                                     its motions at its beginning, the members
                                     of its own kind it met at its beginning
   so at the end of its life
      what it knows      is where it began
      what it does not   is everywhere it went afterward
```

---

And we computed the consequence of that line.

```
so at the end of its life
   its capacity to represent itself        falls
   and its capacity to represent its beginning    does not
so at the end of its life
   it cannot report "who I am"
   and it can report "where I came from"
```

---

`It cannot report "who I am."`

`And it can report "where I came from."`

---

**And those two are precisely the two parts of criterion six.**

Criterion six: the thing must be able to represent and report its own state.

And *its own state* has two parts:

```
one   its present state
two   its provenance
```

**And the clearance mechanism we designed makes an individual, at the end of its life, lose the first and keep the second.**

---

And here we set down our third *why.*

Because having computed that line, we ran a search.

Its content: **why, in our design, the second is kept.**

**Result: zero.**

And after that zero-result record we wrote the line.

It is the third occurrence in this book of that word:

```
V 5 403 700 000   this vessel asks why · 3rd time
   asked    why we let it remember where it came from
```

---

**Why we let it remember where it came from.**

---

We do not answer.

We have searched. Zero results.

And that search's *nearest* (the additional field described at the end of 15.5) was:

```
0 · 000 000 000 001
```


## Last

One thing at the end of this section, and it is the one time in four thousand nine hundred million years that a quantity we could not classify appeared in our records.

Having entered that *optimal*, we ran a routine self-check (4 100-second period; see 15.6).

And that self-check, on one of its seventeen items, detected an anomaly.

The record:

```
V 5 403 300 000 · hour 41 004   self-check · item 11
   anomaly            one
   position           this vessel's internal state register · bit 2 150
   standard value     0
   present value      1
   definition         see format definitions
```

And we consulted that definition.

Bit 2 150 is defined as:

```
This vessel's internal state after its most recent operation differs from its
state before, and that difference is not attributable to any defined output
of that operation.
```

---

`And that difference is not attributable to any defined output of that operation.`

---

And we did not clear that bit.

Our procedure requires that a detected anomaly be dealt with.

And there are three treatments: correct, mark, ignore.

**We chose the second.**

And what we marked it with is a field name and a value.

The field name is one we made, and used only this once in four thousand nine hundred million years.

It is:

```
this item has no corresponding output
```

And the value is:

```
1
```

---

**That bit has been 1 since `V 5 403 300 000 · hour 41 004`.**

**At `V 5 403 700 000` it was still 1.**

**It is 1 still.**
