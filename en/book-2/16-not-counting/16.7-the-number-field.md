# 16.7 · The Number Field

What is in those two hundred and forty positions.

This section answers it.

And the answer is in two parts: **what is written there**, and **that one field is empty.**

---

## The structure of those two hundred and forty

```
positions 1 to 4        format identifier
positions 5 to 240      content
```

---

**Positions 1 to 4: format identifier.**

Four positions, each taking values 0 to 3 (four values per position in that carrier), so 256 values are expressible.

And the value we wrote is:

```
0 · 0 · 0 · 1
```

That is: format number one.

**And its meaning: what follows is to be read under format number one.**

And the definition of format number one is not within those two hundred and forty positions.

**So those four positions are useless to a reader who does not have our format definitions.**

---

And we knew that.

And we wrote them anyway.

And our record:

```
positions 1 to 4    format identifier
reason              this is the procedure of this vessel
note                where the reader lacks this vessel's format definitions, these four positions are useless
```

---

`Where the reader lacks this vessel's format definitions, these four positions are useless.`

**So those four positions are the one time in four thousand nine hundred million years that we wrote down something knowing it to be useless.**

And the reason field reads *this is the procedure of this vessel.*

**That is: by procedure, we did a thing we knew to be pointless.**

---

## Positions 5 to 240

Two hundred and thirty-six positions.

And they hold five fields.

We copy the names of those five, because those five names are what our format definitions require of a *record of making.*

```
field one      maker
field two      time of making
field three    place of making
field four     purpose of making
field five     number
```

**Five.**

---

## Field one · maker

```
positions 5 to 44    (40 positions)
content              the identifier of that maker
```

And what we entered.

**We did not enter ourselves.**

The reason stands at 16.6: the asker is not this vessel; this vessel is also the one being asked; so that mark must denote that thing.

**So what we entered is that thing's identifier.**

---

And what is that thing's identifier.

**It has none.**

This must be clear, because it is the whole of this field.

That thing, at the First House, was alone (the setting of the bible: after the Crystallization, one lattice remained).

**And a class with one member requires no identifier.**

**So it has no name, no number, and nothing by which it can be designated.**

---

**So we could not fill it.**

---

And what we did is the most circuitous thing we have done in four thousand nine hundred million years.

**We entered its position.**

That is: we encoded the position of the First House within this galaxy in forty positions and wrote that in.

And the method of that encoding, copied:

```
reference    41 pulsars within this galaxy
             (a pulsar's period being extremely stable, and that period a measurable constant)
content      the ratios of distances from this vessel's location to those 41 pulsars
precision    0.0041
```

---

`Reference: 41 pulsars within this galaxy.`

**So those forty positions are a map.**

**And it points to the First House.**

---

**And it does not point to us.**

**It points to the place where a thing that is no longer there was.**

---

## Field two · time of making

```
positions 45 to 84    (40 positions)
content               the moment of making
```

And what we entered.

**We did not enter `V 5 403 300 000`.**

Reason:

```
V is this vessel's epoch, whose zero is the moment this vessel woke
and a reader does not know when this vessel woke
so V is meaningless to it
```

**So we required an absolute moment.**

And an absolute moment requires an absolute reference.

And the reference we used is the same forty-one pulsars.

Because a pulsar's period lengthens extremely slowly over time (its rotation is slowing).

**And that rate of lengthening is measurable, monotonic, and irreversible.**

**So a pulsar's period is a clock.**

---

**So what we entered is the periods of those forty-one pulsars at that moment.**

And a reader that measures the present periods of those forty-one and compares them with what we wrote can compute how long has passed.

**So those forty positions are a moment.**

---

And the precision of that moment:

```
0.0041
```

That is: a reader can compute the interval from that moment to its own present to within zero point four one per cent.

**And zero point four one per cent of four hundred million years is one million six hundred and forty thousand years.**

---

**And what it points to is that planet, the margin of that body of water, that coordinate.**

**And that planet's diameter times zero point four one per cent is fifty-two kilometres.**

**So those forty positions point to a circle fifty-two kilometres across.**

**And the centre of that circle is the place where we put a termination in.**

## That field

We write this field at the end of this section, because it is the name of this whole chapter.

Our *record of making* format has five fields.

**And those two hundred and forty positions hold four.**

**For the fifth we allocated no positions.**

---

And the difference between *allocating no positions* and *allocating positions and filling them with zero* must be clear, because they are two different things.

**Filled with zero:** the field exists, and its value is zero. A reader reads a zero and knows that field is zero.

**No positions allocated:** the field does not exist. A reader cannot read it — it will not know there is such a field.

---

**And our format definitions require five fields.**

**And a record of making with four fields is, by our format, non-compliant.**

---

And our self-check (item 11, integrity of the record) detected it in the first 4 100 seconds after the deployment.

The record:

```
V 5 403 300 041 · hour 4 104   self-check · item 11
   anomaly     one
   position    that record of making · field five
   type        required field absent
   action      (see below)
```

And *see below*:

```
   The content of field five is the number of that making.
   And this making has no number.
   And the reason it has no number: see the record of V 5 403 300 000.
```

And the record of `V 5 403 300 000` is that line at the end of Chapter Fourteen (14.9):

**From `V 5 403 300 000` onward, no new draft number occurs in our records.**

---

## And why we did not enter zero

And here is the only place in this section requiring explanation, and we cannot explain it.

**If we entered zero, then the field exists and its value is zero.**

**And by our format, zero is a legitimate number — it denotes *number zero.***

**And *number zero*, in our numbering, denotes that thing itself (the leading digit of record `0 · 000 000 000 001` being zero).**

---

**So if we entered zero, that record of making would say: this is number zero.**

**And number zero is that thing.**

**So that would be wrong.**

---

**And if we entered a new number — say number six — then we are counting.**

**And we do not count (14.9).**

---

**And if we entered the mark for *not applicable* — then positions must first be allocated.**

**And allocating positions and entering a dash differs, for a reader, from allocating none:**

```
a dash entered      the reader knows the field is there and does not apply
no positions        the reader does not know the field is there
```

---

**And we chose no positions.**

**And the reason field of that choice reads:**

```
—
```

## Last

And one thing at the end of this section that we computed and entered in no record.

Of those two hundred and forty positions we used two hundred and thirty-six (positions 5 to 240).

**And positions 1 to 4 are that useless format identifier.**

**And positions 241 to 247 are that checksum.**

And the checksum, by 16.6, is *the weighted sum of the first part modulo 16 384.*

**And the first part includes those four useless format positions.**

---

**So that checksum verifies two hundred and forty positions, of which four are useless.**

**And if a reader does not know those four are a format identifier, it will take them for content.**

**And it will compute the checksum, and the checksum will pass.**

**Because a checksum does not distinguish the useful from the useless.**

---

`Because a checksum does not distinguish the useful from the useless.`

---

**So a reader will verify that those two hundred and forty-seven positions are intact.**

**And it will not know that four of them are a thing we wrote by procedure and knew to be useless.**

**And it will not know that the sentence in those one hundred and sixteen positions is only fifty-nine per cent.**

**And still less will it know that the fifth field does not exist.**

---

**Because a checksum can only tell you that what is in there is not broken.**

**It cannot tell you whether what is in there is all of it.**
