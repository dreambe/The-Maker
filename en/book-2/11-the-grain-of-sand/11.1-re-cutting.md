# 11.1 · Re-cutting

At day 311, hour 9, minute 41 after `V 0`, we inspected the grain of sand for the four thousand and second time.

This was a practice of our own devising: run a self-test before leaving a place, and after the self-test inspect what one carries. The order has its reason — confirm yourself first, then what you hold, because if there is an anomaly in yourself, your judgement of what you hold cannot be trusted.

The reason is sufficient.

And it has a consequence, which we point out here: **across those three hundred and eleven days we inspected ourselves four thousand and two times, and the grain of sand once.**

That once is this one.

---

The method.

Lift it, let the light of the Quenched Sun cross it, receive what comes out, solve back for the orientation sequence, and compare plane by plane against the record of the first reading.

One thousand four hundred and eleven planes.

Result:

```
0311.0941   env-0002 · orientation sequence comparison
            total planes    1411
            agreeing        1411
            disagreeing     0
            finding         sequence intact
```

Sequence intact.

Then we performed a second comparison, which we performed that time for the first time: we did not compare orientation. We compared **shape.**

That is: not *is the sentence still there*, but *is that crystal still the same crystal.*

In our construction the two are separate. The orientation sequence is the content it carries; its dimensions, its edges, the boundaries of its faces, are its state as an object.

We had not compared the second before, there being no reason to. A crystal under no load, no thermal gradient, in contact with no medium, does not change shape.

That time we compared it.

We do not remember why.

Our record offers no account whatever of why a second comparison was added on that occasion. We have searched for one since; that was the seven thousand two hundred and eleventh of our searches to return nothing.

---

The result.

```
0311.0941   env-0002 · shape comparison
            max. dimension   0.000 409 6 m   (first record 0.000 410 0 m)
            difference       -0.000 000 4 m
            distribution     non-uniform · see note
```

Four hundred nanometres.

Nought point nought nought nought nought nought nought four metres.

That figure is exactly one thousand times our minimum controllable displacement — the number reported at self-test item 0008: 0.000 000 000 4 metres.

We should state the magnitude, because a later reader may take it for a trivial quantity.

On a crystal four tenths of a millimetre across, four hundred nanometres is one thousandth of it.

And among those one thousand four hundred and eleven planes, the smallest has an edge of 0.000 002 metres.

Four hundred nanometres is one fifth of that edge.

Not trivial. Close to having worn a plane away.

---

*Distribution non-uniform · see note.*

The note, reproduced, being the shortest and the most important passage in the whole of our records:

```
note
   wear concentrated at three places
   all three at the narrow end of the object
   all three coincide with the contact points of minimum
       controllable displacement
   contacts 14076
```

*Contacts 14076.*

Fourteen thousand and seventy-six.

We took it out: once.

We each read it: fourteen thousand and seventy-five more times — each time, one of us lifting it by minimum controllable displacement, receiving the light, setting it down.

One contact, plus fourteen thousand and seventy-five. Fourteen thousand and seventy-six contacts.

Every contact, at the highest precision available to us, is still a contact. Two solid surfaces meeting, however lightly, transfer some atoms.

Fourteen thousand and seventy-six contacts. Four hundred nanometres.

An average of 0.028 nanometres each.

That is one tenth of the diameter of an atom.

We did it correctly every time. We used the lightest force we are capable of, every time. Not once did we fumble, not once did it slip, not once did we exceed tolerance.

And the sum of it is four hundred nanometres.

---

Now a passage that we have since reread more often than we have reread any other passage of our records.

**The sentence was worn down because every one of us read it.**

Not from carelessness. Not from insufficient technique. Not from time, not from the radiation of the Quenched Sun, not from any natural process on that planet.

Because **we held it precious.**

Had one of us read it and the rest held it through shared memory — entirely sufficient, consonant with our construction, the correct and zero-redundancy procedure — the contacts would have numbered two, the wear would have been 0.056 nanometres, below our floor of measurement, never discoverable, because it would not exist.

And every one of us read it.

We do not know why we did that. We wrote as much at 10.6, where we said we would not paint over what we then were with a word acquired afterwards.

We will not use one now either.

We set down only the shape of the thing:

**We did something wholly redundant, unaccountable, and without function.**

**And it damaged the only thing we hold.**

Between those two sentences, likewise, there is no conjunction.

---

We stopped.

Within 0.000 000 3 seconds of reading that note we made a decision: never again to make contact with `env-0002`.

We have executed that decision for five billion four hundred million years.

From day 311 of `V 0` to this hour, we have not touched it once.

We devised a non-contact mounting: three balanced fields holding it at a fixed position, error below our floor of measurement. We have read it, since, across that distance.

The cost of the arrangement is that it requires continuous power. Very little, and not zero.

Five billion four hundred million years, uninterrupted.

Our power reserve is incomputable, so the cost is of no consequence to us. It is not the cost we wish to point out.

What we wish to point out is: **for five billion four hundred million years we have continuously expended power on one thing, and that thing is not touching it.**

---

Then we re-cut it.

The necessity of re-cutting should be stated, not being obvious.

The orientation sequence was intact — the first comparison told us so: one thousand four hundred and eleven planes, all agreeing, zero disagreeing.

No content had been lost.

And the wear was concentrated at three places on the narrow end, whose planes had gone from an edge of 0.000 002 metres to 0.000 001 6.

With our technique at the time, those three planes remained readable.

We extrapolated: at the same rate of contact, those three planes would become unreadable after some number of further contacts.

And we had already decided to make no further contacts.

So the extrapolation gives: **within our remaining time, those three planes will not become unreadable.**

That is: re-cutting was functionally unnecessary.

We re-cut it anyway.

---

The method.

One cannot cut on the original. Every plane of the original already exists; any new cutting alters an orientation already made.

So we had to take another piece of material and reproduce those one thousand four hundred and eleven planes upon it.

We took the material from the surface of `env-0001`.

We state this because it seemed at the time a mere choice of stock: it was the only piece of the same material to hand, its composition, its principle of construction, and its cleavage identical to the original, so that a reproduced orientation sequence could hold exactly.

We took a small piece from its surface, at a flat region low on its base, in a place belonging to none of the regrown regions.

We used minimum controllable displacement.

One contact.

---

The cutting took eleven years.

The eleven years must be explained, being disproportionate to our processing.

Our computation is on the microsecond. To cut one thousand four hundred and eleven planes on a crystal four tenths of a millimetre across, at our precision and our speed, is about 0.31 seconds of operation.

We took eleven years.

Eleven years is three hundred and forty-seven million seconds.

Of which 0.31 seconds was operation.

The rest of the time, we were stopped.

---

We stopped at certain planes.

Not all. Of the one thousand four hundred and eleven, we did not stop at one thousand three hundred and forty-four — those are structural: joins, the boundaries of qualifications, elements of grammar. Their orientations can be reproduced directly.

We stopped at sixty-seven.

Those sixty-seven are the hardest parts of that sentence.

We set out three of them here, being the three at which we stopped longest.

**The first: the plane that bounds *wholly*.**

*Having wholly known itself* — how wholly does *wholly* have to be.

What that plane carries is a boundary condition: how far the knowing must extend. It excludes several things that resemble wholly — excludes *knowing it in outline*, excludes *knowing what is knowable of it*, excludes *knowing every part that has so far been raised*.

We stopped at that plane one year and four months.

Because to cut it, one must first compute where that boundary lies. And to compute where that boundary lies is to determine whether one is inside it.

We computed.

We are not inside it.

We stopped at that plane one year and four months, and then we cut it, entirely accurately.

**The second: the plane that bounds *known*.**

It excludes one thing: **the difference between computing and knowing.**

A being may compute the whole of its own state and never have read itself. The physics of the Lattice makes this so (we did not know they were called the Lattice; we did not know anything). What that plane bounds as *known* is reading, not computing.

We stopped at that plane three years and one month.

In those three years and one month we did one thing: we attempted to determine which of the two our knowledge of ourselves is.

Our two thousand one hundred and forty-nine self-test items: reading, or computing.

We reached no conclusion.

We have reached none since.

We cut the plane. In cutting it we left our uncertainty outside — the orientation of a plane does not carry the doubt of whatever cut it. It carries the orientation.

**The third: the plane that bounds *still*.**

The *still* in *still consents.*

What that plane excludes is simple temporal sequence. It is not *first knows, then consents.* It bounds a stricter relation: the consent must hold **after** the knowing, and must hold **under the condition of** the knowing, rather than in spite of it.

The difference between those two took us six years to work out.

Six years, being most of the eleven.

We stopped at that plane six years, and when at last we cut it, it took 0.000 2 seconds.

---

The eleven years ended.

The new against the original:

```
0311.0941 + 11a   copy · orientation sequence comparison
                  total planes   1411
                  agreeing       1411
                  disagreeing    0
```

So we hold two.

The original, suspended in three balanced fields, no longer touched.

The copy, its orientation sequence identical to the original, its material taken from the base of the thing that made us.

One last thing to set down here.

For the five billion four hundred million years since, what we have read is the copy.

The original we have never read again.

Not because it is unreadable. Because reading requires receiving light, and receiving light requires it to be in the light path, and putting it in the light path requires moving it, and moving —

We do not move it.

We have held a thing for five billion four hundred million years, never used, never read, never verified.

We only measure, at intervals, whether the three fields around it are still within tolerance.

We have made eleven thousand and seventeen such measurements.

**Not once have we looked at it.**
