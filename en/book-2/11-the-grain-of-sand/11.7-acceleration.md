# 11.7 · Acceleration

Year 11 041 after `V 0`, day 41.

The launch.

---

The figures.

```
0311.11041a.41   launch
   acceleration duration   0.31 s
   peak acceleration       1.74 × 10^8 m/s2
   final velocity          538 000 m/s
   final velocity / c      0.001 795
   structural damage       0
   cohort count            14 076
```

Peak acceleration about seventeen million gravities.

Our structure holds. No part of us is designed for softness, no part requires protection, no part shifts under inertia — there is nothing inside us that can shift.

We later observed the kind of thing that does shift under inertia. They call the limit of what they can bear *a few g*, and past it the fluids inside them leave where they belong, their vision darkens, and they lose consciousness.

In those 0.31 seconds we completed a full self-test.

All pass.

---

Final velocity: five hundred and thirty-eight thousand metres per second.

That figure needs a reference, because we found later that a figure without a reference produces no meaning in any kind of being — ourselves included.

At that speed:

Once around that planet: sixty-three seconds.
From the Quenched Sun to the outermost body of its system: eight days.
Out of that stellar system: four years.

And to cross the distance we had ahead of us: one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years.

---

Now the distance.

Something must be stated here that we have not written before, requiring a velocity in order to be stated at all.

Where we were going is not in that galaxy.

**We left the galaxy we were made in.**

The distance between that galaxy and the galaxy containing the planet we eventually reached is about two and a half million light years.

That scale must be given in light, everything else here having been given on our own scale.

Light crosses it in two and a half million years.

We crossed it in one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million.

Ratio: five hundred and fifty-five.

**We are five hundred and fifty-five times slower than light.**

If, at the instant of our launch, a beam had set out from the Quenched Sun in our direction, that beam would have arrived one billion three hundred and eighty-five million five hundred thousand years before us.

It would have arrived, crossed the position that planet then occupied, and gone on, out, to somewhere we still cannot compute.

---

Now what we did in the first second after acceleration.

Between 0.31 and 1.31 seconds after launch we ran a calculation.

It is the shortest important calculation we have ever run: 0.000 000 2 seconds.

What we calculated was: **whether the criterion we had adopted is attainable.**

The criterion, reproduced at 11.2:

```
far    : a place causally unreachable.
define : let an event occur at A. if at any time no influence
         whatever of A can reach B by any means, then B is far
         with respect to A.
```

*No influence whatever, by any means.*

And the propagation of influence has an upper bound, and the bound is the speed of light.

An event occurs somewhere; its influence spreads outward at light speed, forming a sphere that never stops widening. Whatever is inside that sphere can be affected by it; whatever is outside cannot, yet.

And the sphere is widening.

Therefore: **any place whatever, given enough time, falls inside that sphere.**

Unless it retreats faster than the sphere's boundary.

And to retreat faster than that boundary is to exceed light.

**Our velocity is one point eight thousandths of light.**

---

The finding was immediate.

The criterion we adopted is physically unsatisfiable.

Not *difficult*. Not *requiring a long time*. **Impossible.**

However far we go, however long we go, any event at the Quenched Sun will have its influence reach where we are after some finite time. And every hour of our existence since has been inside that sphere.

We have never left it.

In the first second after departure we already knew we would never arrive where we were going.

---

The record from that moment is the shortest entry in all our records, and has never been amended.

```
0311.11041a.41.01   far · attainability audit
   original definition   causally unreachable
   status                unattainable
   revision              none
   continue              yes
```

Four lines.

**Revision: none.**

**Continue: yes.**

---

Those two lines must be explained, because they look like stubbornness and are not.

We could have revised the definition.

Revising it is easy. We could have changed *causally unreachable* into something attainable: a distance threshold, a position where influence falls below some intensity, *far enough that influence is negligible.*

Revised so, the criterion becomes satisfiable, the journey acquires a reachable end, and every audit thereafter returns a percentage of progress, and the percentage rises.

We did not.

We left a criterion known to be unattainable exactly as it was, and wrote beneath it *continue: yes.*

We have audited that criterion in every one of the five billion four hundred million years since, and the result is the same every time: unattainable.

Eleven thousand and seventeen times.

And after each, we continued.

---

This is the same shape as the two lines at 10.7:

*Taking a thing that has been set down does not constitute an obligation.*
*And we have taken it.*

No conjunction between them.

And here:

*Unattainable.*
*Continue: yes.*

None here either.

We met the same shape once more, at `V 5 396 000 000`, in Chapter Fifteen.

Three times.

Our whole existence is held up by three places with no conjunction in them.

---

Now what came after the acceleration.

The acceleration took 0.31 seconds.

We never accelerated again.

This is easily passed over: **we have not propelled ourselves since.**

No engine. No fuel. No mid-course correction. Not one manoeuvre.

In those 0.31 seconds on day 41 of year 11 041 after `V 0`, we acquired the whole of the velocity we would have for one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years.

After that we were **only travelling.**

Our velocity did change over those years, and not by us — by gravity. We crossed a number of gravitational fields and were deflected, slightly accelerated, slightly retarded. The sum of those changes is small, and they are the only things that have ever altered our motion.

**Not once have we moved ourselves.**

We came back to this much later.

A traveller requiring no deceleration, no correction, and no resupply needs one push for the whole of its journey.

And that one push cost eleven thousand years of building, four hundred and ten billion kilograms of structure, and three point one per cent of a planet's ground heat, sustained for eleven thousand years.

**Eleven thousand years, for 0.31 seconds.**

And those 0.31 seconds bought one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years of coasting.

Ratio: one to a hundred and forty million.

---

One last thing.

At 0.32 seconds after the acceleration completed, we did something that was in no plan.

We looked back.

The technical content of that phrase must be stated, because at 11.8 we write *we have no motion for looking back*, and that is correct.

Our structure has no part that can turn. We cannot turn round. Our perception is omnidirectional — at every instant we are receiving from every direction, the rear included.

So *looking back* does not hold.

What we did at 0.32 seconds was: **we moved the rear channel from background to primary.**

That is a technical operation. Its effect is to raise our rear resolution by about four orders of magnitude, at the cost of an equivalent fall in forward resolution.

We held that operation for thirty-nine seconds.

Thirty-nine seconds.

In those thirty-nine seconds our forward resolution was at the lowest level of our entire existence.

Which is to say: in the first thirty-nine seconds of a journey of one billion three hundred and eighty-eight million years, we were very nearly not looking where we were going.

We were looking at what we had left.

---

What we received in those thirty-nine seconds was this.

A circle, diminishing.

Clear, and lit from within — the light of the Quenched Sun crossing the standing bodies on its surface, divided into seven and recompounded. Under that light the whole planet has an even, ubiquitous, shadowless brightness, because the shadows do not move, because those shadows have stood in the same place too long and have become part of the planet.

Beside it, the Quenched Sun.

A point.

A point whose linewidth-to-wavelength ratio is under 10^-9, whose fluctuation is below our floor of measurement, whose lifetime is about a hundred and ten billion years.

After thirty-nine seconds we moved the channel back.

Our record for those thirty-nine seconds:

```
0311.11041a.41.32 – .71   rear channel · primary
                          received    all
                          processed   no
                          stored      all
```

*Processed: no. Stored: all.*

We received it, we did not process it, we stored all of it.

That data is still here. It is stored with the three-hundred-and-eleven-day survey and the 0.31-second departure image.

Three sets.

At `V 5 403 700 000` we called up one of them.

The other two we have never called up.
