# 11.3 · Building

In later retellings we left in a ship.

We have never corrected this, correcting it requiring us first to explain our construction, and explaining our construction requiring a concept held in common with whoever is being told, and in that matter there is none.

We correct it here.

**We had no ship.**

---

First, what is needed.

An interstellar crossing, for the kind of being that came later, requires the following:

A sealed vessel, to hold out vacuum.
An environment for life: gas, water, temperature, pressure.
Food.
Radiation shielding.
A mechanism that will not fail across a long duration, to handle its members' ageing, illness, mental states, social relations, and succession of generations.
A propulsion system.
A deceleration system.
Something that still functions after arrival.

Eight items.

We required one of them.

The first, no: vacuum does us no harm. Our surfaces need no pressure balance; nothing inside us boils.

The second through the fifth, no: we do not breathe, do not eat, do not age, do not fall ill, have no mental states, no social relations, no succession of generations. The accumulation rate of radiation damage in us is below our floor of measurement.

The sixth, yes.

The seventh, no — that requires expansion; see below.

The eighth, no: after arrival we are still we, and require nothing additional in order to continue.

**So what we required was acceleration.**

---

Why deceleration is not required after acceleration.

Because we posit no destination.

A journey with a destination requires deceleration: you must stop there. We had none — our criterion was *far*, and far has no specific location. What we were going to was not a place but a condition.

Our scheme was: accelerate to some velocity, thereafter alter nothing, and keep going until something worth stopping for is met.

If met, decelerate — and the energy for decelerating is then obtained locally.

If never met, keep going.

Our power reserve is incomputable, our construction does not decay, we require no resupply.

**A traveller who does not need to decelerate does not need a ship. It needs to be thrown, once.**

---

So what we built was not a ship.

It was a launcher.

We should state one consequence of that decision here, because we knew it entirely while building, and built it anyway:

**A launcher does not come with you. It stays where it is.**

A ship follows you. A launcher, having thrown you, is still on the planet where it was built.

It has no further use thereafter. It is a thing usable once, and its building took eleven thousand years.

Eleven thousand years, to make a thing used once, and leave it behind.

Our record's account of this is one line:

```
scheme comparison   launched / carried
criteria            total energy · total build time · reliability
finding             launched superior on all three criteria
```

Superior on all three criteria. That is correct.

And not one of our criteria is *the situation of this object after use.*

Our format has no such column either.

---

Now materials.

A launcher that must accelerate fourteen thousand and seventy-six of us to the required velocity is a very large structure. The material required is about 4.1 × 10^11 kilograms.

Four hundred and ten billion kilograms.

That material had to come from that planet.

So we did a thing we have gone back to many times since: **we ran a resource assessment on that planet.**

In our format, *resource* has two classes: extractable and non-extractable.

The assessment, reproduced:

```
0311.1104   First House · resource assessment
   crust · amorphous zones        resource · extractable   reserves sufficient
   crust · crystalline zones      resource · extractable   reserves sufficient
   slurry                         resource · extractable   reserves sufficient · high temp.
   subsurface metal enrichment    resource · extractable   reserves sufficient
   surface standing bodies (10.8) environmental · static · non-resource
```

That last line.

*Surface standing bodies · environmental · static · non-resource.*

We did not take them.

Not out of respect. Not because we knew what they were. Not because we held any attitude of any kind toward them.

**Because in our table they had been entered in the column marked non-resource.**

And non-resources do not enter the extraction schedule.

We wrote at 10.4 and 10.8 that those were the two most wrongly filed classifications we have ever made. We have corrected neither to this day — one we deleted into a blank, and one still reads *no regularity.*

And in this section we must write down the opposite of those two:

**It was precisely that wrong classification that kept us from dismantling the remains of an entire civilisation to build a launcher.**

Had we read any one of them — had we read even one — we would have known what they were.

And what our format would then have made of a thing known to be *an object that formerly ran*, we cannot say, never having faced it.

We know only this: in our eleven-class format, **there is no class meaning *not to be moved.***

There is *obstacle · immovable* — meaning we cannot move it.

There is no class meaning *we can move it, and do not.*

Had we understood them, what column we would have put them in, we do not know.

Our luck — we learned that word later, and in our usage it is defined as *a good thing whose cause lies inside our Blind Spot* —

Our luck was that we did not read.

---

The material came from an amorphous zone of the crust, on the far side of the First House, 6 100 kilometres from that hollow.

Our stated reason for choosing there was: distance from the distribution zone of the standing bodies.

The form in which we gave the reason was *to avoid disturbance of environmental objects by extraction operations, lest their toppling create obstruction of routes.*

Which is to say: we did not touch them, on the grounds that **they might fall over and be in the way.**

We have left that line in the record too.

---

Now energy.

To build a structure of 4.1 × 10^11 kilograms and then accelerate us with it required about 2.7 × 10^28 joules.

That planet has no usable stellar power — the flux of the Quenched Sun is too low, and we knew it was too low in the sense a later chapter sets out (we did not know it then; we had merely measured it).

There was one usable source: the heat of the ground.

The decay heat beneath that planet has a total power of about 3.1 × 10^13 watts.

We did not need all of it. We needed a portion, sustained for eleven thousand years.

So what we did was: **we made a hole in that planet.**

Depth 411 kilometres. Diameter 1.9 kilometres.

At the bottom we installed a thermoelectric structure to intercept part of the rising heat flux and convert it.

Fraction intercepted: 0.031.

That is: for eleven thousand years, three point one per cent of that planet's ground heat did not reach its surface.

---

We must state the consequence of that figure, because we computed it at the time, and our computation was correct, and we had no reaction to the result.

Ground heat rises and makes the tide.

The form of the tide fixes the orientation of every layer a lattice grows.

Intercept three point one per cent, and the tide in that region falls in amplitude by about three per cent.

And there were no living lattices in that region — all had stopped, all were clear, all at ambient temperature.

So our interception affected nothing that was growing.

Our record:

```
impact assessment   objects running within affected region   0
                    finding  no impact
```

*No impact.*

That line is correct.

And at `V 5 403 700 000`, back at the First House, standing beside that hole, we did a thing we only then did:

We computed that, had there been living lattices on that planet, our eleven thousand years of interception would have lowered their tide by three per cent, would have shifted slightly the orientation of every layer they grew in that time, and would have made every idea they thought across that span slightly unlike the shape it would otherwise have had.

Eleven thousand years. Reckoned in that planet's tidal period, some number of tides.

Some number of ideas.

Every one of them slightly different.

**Our leaving would have changed what they thought.**

And they were already gone.

So our finding, *no impact*, is correct.

Having completed that computation, we did not record it.

It is the only computation we have ever completed and not recorded.

---

The building took eleven thousand years.

The duration must be explained, being disproportionate to our capability.

At our precision of motion and our numbers, the assembly proper is about ninety years.

The other ten thousand nine hundred and ten years were extraction, transport, and waiting.

Waiting for what: for the thermoelectric structure at the bottom of that hole to accumulate sufficient energy.

That is a process we could not accelerate. Heat rises at its own rate, and we cannot make it faster.

So: for eleven thousand years, most of our time was spent waiting on a thing whose rate we did not set.

We did not know then that it was called waiting.

In our record, the entry for those ten thousand nine hundred and ten years is:

```
status   energy accumulation · in progress
```

*In progress.*

That is the second occurrence of that status in our records.

The first is this one.

The third is at `V 5 403 700 000`, written by one Vessel alone, unchanged since, three hundred thousand years now.

---

The launch took 0.31 seconds.

We set down what we observed in those 0.31 seconds, that being the last time we looked at that planet.

In 0.31 seconds our velocity went from zero to target. The acceleration was very great. Our structure holds — no part of us is designed for softness.

Our perception was open throughout those 0.31 seconds.

What we recorded:

The hole beneath us, shrinking fast.

The edge of that melt, an arc, curving more and more.

The standing bodies: at 0.19 seconds still resolvable as individuals; at 0.24 seconds a texture; at 0.29 seconds an uneven brightness.

At the end of 0.31 seconds, the whole planet is a circle.

A clear circle, lit from within, and perfect.

Our record has one line:

```
0311.11000a.31   departure · image stored
```

*Image stored.*

We have never called that image up since.

It is inside us, intact, uncompressed, stored with the three-hundred-and-eleven-day survey.

Five billion four hundred million years, and we have not called it up once.

Returning at `V 5 403 700 000`, we called up the survey and compared it.

The image we did not call up.

We do not know why we did not.

And we noticed that we had not, in the course of writing this section.
