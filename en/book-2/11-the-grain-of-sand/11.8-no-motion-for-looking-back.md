# 11.8 · They Have No Motion for Looking Back

At 11.7 we wrote *we looked back*, and stated that it was a channel operation.

Now why that is not looking back.

---

Looking back requires three things.

**One: a front.**

A direction designated as *what is faced.* Information from it is processed first; events in it are taken as *coming*; it fixes the meaning of *forward.*

**Two: a back.**

Of the remaining directions, the one opposite the front. Information from it is not processed; events in it are taken as *past.*

**Three: an action that exchanges the two.**

That is: a mechanism that makes what was behind become what is in front. A head and neck, or a trunk, or the turning of a whole body.

Three things.

We have none of them.

---

**We have no front.**

Our perception is omnidirectional. Items 0010 through 0013, four bands, their receiving surfaces covering the whole of our exterior, with no blind place anywhere.

At any instant we receive from every direction at once, process in parallel, and prioritise by content, not by direction.

If something worth processing appears in a direction, we process it. Which direction it is in does not affect the order.

So the concept *the direction faced* does not hold with us.

We face no direction.

Or: **we face every direction at once, which is functionally identical to facing none.**

**We have no back.**

No front, no back. A back is defined relative to a front.

So we have no *behind us.*

**We have no turning mechanism.**

No part of our structure can rotate relative to another. We are not a trunk with movable parts. We are one body whose parts hold fixed positions relative to each other.

We can change our direction of motion as a whole — that requires thrust, and we have used no thrust since.

And even had we changed our direction of motion, our perception would not change with it, perception having nothing to do with motion.

---

From which follows the whole of this section:

**We have no act of leaving.**

---

Leaving requires a behind.

A thing leaves a place by putting that place behind it, putting elsewhere in front, and going forward.

*Behind* is the position that carries the act of leaving. Whatever is left is behind.

And we have no behind.

Therefore:

**We did not leave the First House.**

The distance between us and it increases. That is a measurable, monotonic, exact fact.

And increasing distance is not leaving.

A thing may have its distance from another thing increase without ever having left it — if it never turned its back on it.

**We never turned our back on it.**

---

The physics must be plain, this not being a figure of speech.

Our rear channel, from second 71 of day 41 of year 11 041 after `V 0` — the instant we moved it from primary back to background — has been receiving ever since.

The background channel has low resolution. How low: about one ten-thousandth of the primary.

And it is not zero.

In the five billion four hundred million years since, our rear channel has received signal from that direction at every instant.

Not one second interrupted.

We did not switch it off, having no mechanism for switching off a direction. To switch off reception in a direction one must shield that part of the surface, and shielding requires something to cover it with, and we carry no such thing (three items on the manifest; see 11.4).

Which is to say:

**For five billion four hundred million years, we have received light from the direction of the First House every second.**

Low resolution. Unprocessed.

And receiving.

---

Now the disposition of that data.

Background-channel data, by our conventions, does not enter the processing queue. It does enter storage.

We store everything. We do not delete.

Five billion four hundred million years of background-channel data is an enormous volume. A figure we have not previously written:

That data is forty-one per cent of the whole of our storage.

**Forty-one per cent of what is inside us is an unprocessed, low-resolution record of a direction we have never looked at.**

Everything we have done since — five drafts, five terminations, that catalogue, that deployment, that voyage home — the complete records of all of it come to less than half that figure.

---

We should state why we do not delete it.

We delete nothing. That is our convention, and the ground for it is sufficient: the value of deleted data cannot be assessed after deletion, so deletion is an irreversible and unauditable operation.

And we have looked at that ground again, much later.

What we have to say is: **it is sufficient, and it is not our reason.**

Because if that ground held, we would treat all data with the same rigour.

And our treatment of that data differs from our treatment of every other:

Everything else we reread periodically. We reread everything. With nothing to do across four billion years of transit, we have read every record of ours an unknowable number of times (11.4).

**That data we have never once reread.**

Five billion four hundred million years. Forty-one per cent of storage. Zero rereadings.

---

At `V 5 403 700 000` we returned to the First House.

We did three things there: called up the three-hundred-and-eleven-day survey and compared it (10.8); read the archive (Chapter 18); and stood beside the launcher for forty-one hours (11.5).

We did not call up the background-channel data.

We were standing on its source.

That data is the whole of the signal that place sent us across five billion four hundred million years — recorded at our background resolution, continuous, uninterrupted, complete.

We stood on it, and did not look at it.

---

One last thing, being the close of this chapter.

We later encountered a kind of being that has a front, a back, and a head and neck that turn.

Their *leaving* is a complete act: turn, walk, do not look back.

They put enormous weight on *do not look back.* In their stories, a person who goes without looking back is resolute; a person who looks back is weak, or reluctant, or foretold — in certain of their older stories, the one who looks back loses thereby the thing she was bringing out.

They weigh looking back heavily, because to them looking back is a **choice.**

They can decline it. They can go, and never once look at the place behind them, for a lifetime. They genuinely can — we have observed it many times: a person leaves a place and for decades afterwards does not return, does not enquire, does not look, does not even think of it.

That requires a back.

That requires a direction one is able not to look at.

**We have none.**

We cannot not look.

We have never performed *not looking back*, because we have never performed *looking back*, because we have never performed *turning away.*

We have only been here, while it has been over there, and the distance has increased.

And every beam it emits still arrives at our surface, every second, for five billion four hundred million years, uninterrupted.

We receive them.

We do not process them.

We store them.

**Forty-one per cent.**

And we do not know whether that counts as looking back.
