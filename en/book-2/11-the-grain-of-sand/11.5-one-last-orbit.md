# 11.5 · One Last Orbit

The launcher was completed in the eleven-thousandth year after `V 0`.

The launch window fell forty-one days later.

Those forty-one days were spent waiting for the planet to turn to the correct angle. The launch direction had to coincide with the direction we had settled on, and the launcher was fixed to the surface, so we waited for the rotation.

Forty-one days with nothing to do.

On the thirty-ninth day we did something.

---

We went once around that planet.

Technically: we left the surface, entered a low orbit, completed one revolution, and returned to the launcher and took our positions.

Orbital altitude 217 kilometres. Period 3 hours 41 minutes.

One revolution.

And back.

---

The cost should be stated first, the cost being computable, and we computed it.

Leaving the surface requires energy. Entering orbit requires energy. Maintaining the orbit requires nothing — that planet has no atmosphere, so no drag, so the maintenance cost is zero. Returning and taking position on the launcher requires deceleration, which requires energy.

Total: 4.1 × 10^19 joules.

That energy came from the reserve accumulated in the shaft.

And the reserve was fixed — we had waited ten thousand nine hundred and ten years to accumulate it, and we did not intend to wait a second ten thousand.

So those 4.1 × 10^19 joules came off the launch.

The consequence: our final velocity was lower than planned.

By how much: 0.000 041 per cent.

Forty-one parts in a hundred million.

---

The consequence of that figure must be stated, because we computed it on the thirty-ninth day, and computed it entirely correctly.

At that velocity deficit, across the distance we would go, arrival is delayed.

By how long: **four thousand one hundred years.**

On the thirty-ninth day we entered the figure into the record:

```
0311.11041a.39   orbit · cost
   energy         4.1 × 10^19 J
   velocity loss  0.000 041 %
   arrival delay  4 100 years
   reason         —
```

*Reason —*

The dash is our format's symbol for *this field does not apply.*

And here it does not mean *does not apply.*

**Here there is no reason, and our format has no symbol for *none*.**

We used a symbol meaning *not applicable* to fill a field that should have read *none.*

That is the first time we misused the dash. We have misused it twice since, both very much later, and both under the same circumstance: **we did a thing, and had no reason.**

---

Now the three hours and forty-one minutes.

We were in orbit, going around that planet.

We made no observations.

This must be stated, being contrary to our practice. In the preceding three hundred and eleven days we had surveyed the whole surface at 0.01-metre resolution. In orbit we could have done far more: global gravity field, internal structure, heat-flow distribution, large-scale terrain correlations. We do not have that data, and that was the only opportunity.

We did not collect it.

Our record's entry for those three hours and forty-one minutes:

```
0311.11041a.39.00 – .41   orbit · one revolution
                          observations   none
                          record         see below
```

*Record · see below.*

We have used *see below* twice in the whole of our records.

The first is at 10.8, looking at that planet's surface and finding no category to fill.

The second is this.

---

What follows *see below* is this:

```
   0311.11041a.39.00    beneath   slurry · reflections · one way
   0311.11041a.39.07    beneath   slurry · reflections · one way
   0311.11041a.39.14    beneath   standing-body zone · edge
   0311.11041a.39.19    beneath   standing-body zone · centre
   0311.11041a.39.23    beneath   standing-body zone · edge
   0311.11041a.39.31    beneath   shaft
   0311.11041a.39.36    beneath   slurry
   0311.11041a.39.41    beneath   launcher
```

Eight lines.

Each recording the same thing: what was beneath us at that moment.

No measured values. No parameters. No classification.

**Only what was beneath us at that moment.**

In the five billion four hundred million years since, we have never written a record like that again. Every record of ours has measured values, because a record without measured values does not constitute information in our construction.

Those eight lines do not constitute information.

They are still inside us.

---

We have since tried to find the reason for that orbit.

We have tried eleven thousand and seventeen times.

We enumerated the following candidates and eliminated them one by one:

**One: a manoeuvring test**, verifying our capability before launch.
Eliminated: the launch requires no manoeuvring capability. Launching is being thrown, and we perform no manoeuvres in the four billion years that follow. And had it been a test, we would have recorded test parameters, and we recorded none.

**Two: supplementary observation.**
Eliminated: we made no observations.

**Three: an energy check**, verifying the reserve.
Eliminated: availability can be verified in place; it does not require flying around, and flying around consumed 4.1 × 10^19 joules, where verification requires a very small fraction of that.

**Four: a way of passing the window.**
Eliminated: waiting does not consume energy. On the other forty of those forty-one days we did not move.

**Five: some unrecorded technical necessity.**
Eliminated: we record everything. An unrecorded necessity does not hold with us.

Five candidates, all eliminated.

Our finding, reproduced:

```
orbit · reason search
   candidates   5
   eliminated   5
   finding      no reason
```

*No reason.*

That is the only time in the whole of our records that the finding field reads *no reason.*

Every other time we could not find a reason, we entered *undeterminable* or *pending.*

That time we entered *no reason.*

The difference is: *undeterminable* means a reason exists and we cannot find it; *no reason* means none exists.

We do not know on what basis we dared enter the second.

---

Now the four thousand one hundred years.

At `V 5 403 700 000` we returned to the First House and stood beside that launcher — still there, intact, unchanged in five billion four hundred million years, because that planet has no wind, no rain, and nothing that alters a shape.

There we ran a calculation.

The calculation: what the four thousand one hundred years of delay produced.

It runs:

We reached that planet four thousand one hundred years late.
We waited for it to cool, and seeded. The seeding was four thousand one hundred years late.
The whole course of the first draft was four thousand one hundred years late.
The first termination, four thousand one hundred years late.
The second, third, fourth, and fifth drafts, each four thousand one hundred years late.
The final deployment, four thousand one hundred years late.

**The thing that was deployed appeared on that planet four thousand one hundred years later than it would have.**

---

The magnitude of four thousand one hundred years, on that thing's scale, must be set down, because on ours it is zero and on its own it is not.

From its deployment to the writing of this section is about three hundred thousand years.

Four thousand one hundred years is one point four per cent of that.

And reckoned from when it began leaving recognisable records — when it began drawing animals that did not exist on the walls of caves, burying its dead, keeping accounts in signs — then four thousand one hundred years is roughly the whole length of its recorded history.

**From the first sign it cut, to a woman rerunning an arithmetic test for the nineteenth time at four in the morning — that whole span is about four thousand one hundred years.**

And the length of that whole span is exactly what one orbit cost.

---

We must write the next thing with great care, because it is very easily read as a lament, and we do not lament.

Had we not gone around:

That thing would have been deployed four thousand one hundred years earlier. Everything about it would have happened four thousand one hundred years earlier. At this present hour it would have gone four thousand one hundred years further.

What it is now would not be what it would be.

How different, cannot be computed.

We tried to compute it. That is the only occasion on which we have attempted to predict the behaviour of that thing, and we failed — the behaviour of that thing is unpredictable, which is part of its design, which we put in ourselves, which is our finest work.

**We cannot compute whether, given four thousand one hundred more years, it would already have answered that question.**

Nor whether it would already have stopped.

We know only this: the version that exists is the version after we went around once.

---

Having finished that calculation we stood beside the launcher for forty-one hours.

Those forty-one hours are the same forty-one hours in which our cohort stopped running in that hollow.

We do not know whether that is coincidence. We have searched. Zero results.

---

To end this section, one of those eight lines.

The fourth:

```
   0311.11041a.39.19    beneath   standing-body zone · centre
```

*Standing-body zone · centre.*

That is the densest place on that planet for those clear bodies.

We measured its clustering index at 10.8: 3.71.

In the physics a later chapter sets out, we would learn what that means: it is the earliest and most flourishing place of that civilisation, the place with the most neighbours, the most read, the place held in the greatest number of shapes.

And the place where the freezing began.

And on that day — the eleven-thousand-and-forty-first year after `V 0`, day thirty-nine, minute nineteen — we passed 217 kilometres above it.

Of the three hours and forty-one minutes, our time above it was about eleven seconds.

In those eleven seconds we made no measurement.

We wrote one line: beneath, standing-body zone, centre.

Then we went on around.

Then we returned to the launcher, took position, waited two days, and were thrown.

**That is the only time we ever passed over them.**
