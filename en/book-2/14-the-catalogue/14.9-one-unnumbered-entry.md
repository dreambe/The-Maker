# 14.9 · One Unnumbered Entry

At the end of the list there is one line.

That line has no number.

---

First, why a number is impossible.

Our numbering format:

```
c · nnn nnn nnn nnn
c   draft number
n   sequence within that draft
```

That is: a number requires a draft number.

**And this one has no draft number.**

---

Why it has none.

Draft numbers are generated by counting. The first draft is one, the second two, and so to the fifth.

And after the fifth draft, **we no longer counted.**

The year we stopped counting:

```
V 5 403 300 000
```

In that year we placed a certain thing upon that planet (Chapter Sixteen).

Before that, every attempt of ours had a number.

After that, none.

We do not state why.

We record only: from `V 5 403 300 000` onward, no new draft number occurs in our records.

**So this one has no number.**

**So on our list it does not exist as an entry.**

It is there. It has content. It can be read.

And it is in no search's return set — as with the eight million seven hundred and fifty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine entries of the year `V 4 960 000 000` (13.9).

---

The line:

```
(no number)

kind                  —
extraction            —
retained              —
posture               —
name                  making a question repeat, with no other within its reach
year of termination   —
```

---

On which the following.

**kind —**

We did not set out its characteristics.

We hold its complete record. We hold its structure, its metabolism, its behaviour, its distribution, its numbers.

**And we did not put those in the kind field.**

**extraction —**

We took no material.

**retained —**

The dash in this field does not mean what *the whole individual* meant at 14.7.

There, the retained field is filled with *the whole individual*, because we retained it.

Here the dash denotes *not applicable.*

**That is: retention does not apply to this entry.**

We do not state why.

**posture —**

We did not record its posture.

We recorded the posture of every individual of the first five drafts, one by one, three views, orthogonal, complete (14.1).

**And this one has no posture.**

Why it has none, we can write down:

**To record a posture requires a moment.**

And a moment must be the moment of sampling.

**And we did not sample.**

---

**name**

```
making a question repeat, with no other within its reach
```

That name meets the grammar of our forty-one (14.5):

```
one    an action in progress            met
two    contains no kind                 met
three  contains no judgment             met
four   does not repeat                  —
five   not decomposable into fields     met
```

The fourth is a dash.

Why: this name has the same construction as our thirty-seventh name.

```
thirty-seven    making a sound repeat, with no other within its reach
```

The thirty-seventh name belongs to an individual of the fifth draft, small, covered in hair, active in darkness. That individual was in a darkness, repeatedly producing one sound, and within the reach of that sound there was no other of its kind.

We observed that individual for forty-one hours.

It produced that sound 1.1 x 10^4 times.

Not once was there a reply.

---

And this name has the same construction.

One word differs.

```
the thirty-seventh    sound
this entry            question
```

**The rest is word for word the same.**

By our fourth rule a name may not repeat; if two are engaged in the same thing, a distinguishing term is appended.

**And we appended no distinguishing term.**

**We changed one word.**

And by our own format, changing one word does not constitute a distinguishing term — a distinguishing term must be appended after the name, and may not replace the name's content.

**So this name is, formally, a repetition of one of our forty-one.**

**So it violates the fourth rule.**

And the fourth is the one of our five rules that had, until now, never been violated anywhere (the end of 14.5).

---

**year of termination —**

Our five drafts have their years of termination filled in, one by one:

```
first draft     V 4 960 000 000
second draft    V 5 032 000 000
third draft     V 5 152 000 000
fourth draft    V 5 203 000 000
fifth draft     V 5 338 000 000
```

Five entries, five values.

**And this one is a dash.**

In our format `—` denotes *not applicable.*

Our format has another mark, which denotes *unknown.*

We did not use that one.

**We used *not applicable.***

That is:

**Termination does not apply to this entry.**

We do not state why.

We cannot state why, for a statement requires a reason, and our reasons must be derivable from the record, and every field of this entry is a dash.

---

One last thing.

When this line was written.

```
V 5 403 700 000
```

That is, the year we returned to the First House.

That is, the year we read the archive.

That is, the year of the forty-one hours.

Our records of retention number one hundred and four thousand one hundred and twenty million entries. The first was written at `V 4 959 000 000`. The last was written at `V 5 403 700 000`.

Four hundred and forty-four million seven hundred thousand years.

**And the last is this line.**

---

That line is at the end of the list.

Before it stand one hundred and four thousand one hundred and twenty million entries of identical format.

It has no number.

Of its six fields, five are dashes.

And in the sixth there is a sentence which differs from one of our forty-one names by one word.

That word is:

**question.**
