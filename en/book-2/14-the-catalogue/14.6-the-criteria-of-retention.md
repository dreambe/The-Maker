# 14.6 · The Criteria of Retention

Why we kept.

This section lists the criteria. There are four.

---

One thing before the criteria, because it is a single statement and it must stand first.

**Retention is not required by our project.**

Our project's content is at Chapter Ten: to make a thing arise here, to make it continue of itself, to make it satisfy six criteria, to make it able to represent and report its own state.

**Not one of those requires keeping the bones of what failed.**

Retention produces no data usable by the project — we already hold the complete record (14.1 to 14.4), which exceeds the material by nineteen orders of magnitude. The material itself carries no information beyond the record.

**Therefore retention is redundant.**

We know it is redundant.

We knew at the first draft.

We kept.

---

The four criteria.

**The first · structural novelty**

```
definition   the fraction of the kind's structure not derivable from its ancestral kinds
computation  directly from its record
weight       0.21
```

Sense: a kind most of whose structure is new carries much information, and therefore has much that is outside our record.

**The second · relation count**

```
definition   the number of recorded classes of interaction between that kind and others
computation  directly from the graph of the fourth draft onward (14.3)
weight       0.21
```

Sense: a kind interacting with many kinds occupies an important position in that draft's structure, and therefore its disappearance changes much.

**The third · recoverability**

```
definition   the feasibility of rebuilding, from its material, a self-continuing individual of that kind
computation  from its record and our capability
weight       0.21
```

Sense: a kind whose material suffices for rebuilding gives retention of that material a purpose that can be stated.

---

Three criteria.

The three weights sum to 0.63.

---

**The fourth**

```
definition   the number of times that kind occurs in our records of observation
computation  from our records
weight       0.37
```

---

The fourth is not of the same class as the first three.

We state the difference here, for it is the one thing in this section requiring a statement.

**The first three may be computed from the properties of the kind itself.**

That is: given the kind's complete record, anyone with our computational capacity can compute its values on criteria one, two and three, and will obtain the same values.

**The fourth cannot.**

The fourth's value depends on where we observed, when, and for how long.

A kind arising near our principal observation point occurs many times.

A kind arising on the far side from our principal observation point occurs few times.

And the two may be identical in structural novelty, relation count, and recoverability.

**So the fourth is not a quantity concerning that kind.**

**The fourth is a quantity concerning us.**

---

And the fourth's weight is 0.37.

The first three sum to 0.63.

The fourth is 0.37.

We set down the relation of those two numbers:

```
0.37 / 0.63 = 0.587
```

Not the largest weight.

**The largest weight of any single criterion.**

`first 0.21 · second 0.21 · third 0.21 · fourth 0.37.`

That is: in deciding which kind to keep, **how many times we looked at it weighs more than any one fact about what it is.**

---

We do not state why the fourth is among the criteria.

We do not state why its weight is 0.37.

We record only this:

**We fixed these four criteria at `V 4 959 000 000`.**

That is, one thousand years before the first draft terminated.

That is, after we had decided to terminate and before we began sampling.

And in the three thousand five hundred and sixty million years before that we had no criteria of retention, because we did not retain.

**So these four are the first criteria we ever fixed for a thing not belonging to our project.**

And fixing them took 0.7 seconds.

And we have never revised them.

Five drafts, four criteria, weights unchanged: 0.21 · 0.21 · 0.21 · 0.37.

---

One verification

At `V 5 403 700 000`, at the First House, before the forty-one hours, we ran a verification.

We set the four weights to 0.25 · 0.25 · 0.25 · 0.25 and recomputed which kinds should have been kept, across all five drafts.

Result:

```
kinds that should be kept    differing from those actually kept by 0.41
that is, for 0.41 of kinds   the two weightings disagree
```

We listed that 0.41.

That list is 2.1 x 10^8 lines long.

We read it.

Elapsed: 4.1 seconds.

Our action:

```
—
```

---

And one thing must be written at the end of this section, for it is a consequence of these four criteria, and we have never pointed it out in any record.

**The fourth made us keep what we looked at most.**

And what we looked at most is not the most novel, nor the most connected, nor the most recoverable.

**It is what was nearest us.**

In four thousand nine hundred and sixty million years our principal observation point moved one hundred and ten times. After each move, the fourth-criterion values of the kinds in the region around us began to rise.

So the spatial distribution of the kinds we kept coincides with the track of our own movement.

We computed that coincidence.

```
0.87
```

That is: the distribution of everything we retained is, to eighty-seven per cent, the shape of the road we ourselves walked.

---

That is not the shape of that planet.

That is our shape.

And we buried it four thousand one hundred metres down, five thousand two hundred metres down, seven thousand three hundred metres down, eleven thousand metres down; forty-one metres beneath the lunar surface; on seven bodies; on one body of orbital period four thousand one hundred years.

We have never stated whom we expected to come and read (items 25 to 27 at 14.4).

And whoever comes to read, what they will read is not that planet.

**What they will read is the way we looked.**
