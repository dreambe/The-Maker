# 14.5 · The Names

We do not give names.

We number.

The difference between a number and a name:

```
number    generated by rule · independent of the properties of the thing numbered ·
          computable from position
name      not generated by rule · dependent on the properties of the thing named ·
          not computable from position
```

In four thousand nine hundred and sixty million years we have issued one hundred and four thousand one hundred and twenty million numbers.

In four thousand nine hundred and sixty million years we have given forty-one names.

The forty-one follow.

---

First the grammar, because that grammar is ours, and we never entered it into the format definitions.

```
one    a name must be an action in progress
       reason  at the time of sampling, that individual was engaged in something
two    a name contains no kind
       reason  kinds have numbers
three  a name contains no judgment
       reason  we do not judge
four   a name may not repeat
       if two are engaged in the same thing, a distinguishing term is appended
five   a name may not be decomposed into fields
```

The fifth requires a statement.

Every record of ours may be decomposed into fields: a value, a field name, a format definition.

**A name may not.**

If a name is decomposed into fields, the sum of its parts is not equal to itself — we have verified this, one hundred and ten times, with the same result.

So names are the one class of content in our records that occupies no field.

They are kept in one place, and that place has no format.

---

Forty-one.

We state five of them here. The remaining thirty-six we do not state.

**The thirty-third: *waiting.***

This name violates the third rule (a name contains no judgment).

*Waiting* is a judgment — it requires holding that the individual's action is directed at something that has not happened.

We cannot observe what has not happened. So we cannot observe waiting.

**So this name is wrong.**

We know it is wrong.

We keep it.

We appended no note to it.

**The twentieth: *of forty-one, one outside and forty in a ring.***

This name violates the second rule (a name contains no kind), for it must refer to forty-one individuals, and to refer to several requires some means of reference, and in our records reference is number, and number is kind plus sequence.

In this name we used no number.

We wrote *forty-one.*

That is the one time we used a count in place of a number.

**The thirty-fourth: *its distance from another unchanged for forty-one years.***

This name violates the first rule (a name must be an action in progress).

*Unchanged* is not an action.

And we observed for forty-one years, once a year, forty-one times, and the distance was the same.

We could not write *not moving*, because each of them was moving.

Nor could we write *maintaining*, because *maintaining* contains a judgment.

**So we wrote *unchanged*, and we know that is not an action.**

**The thirtieth: *within a region containing no other individual, radius 4.1 x 10^3 metres.***

This is the longest of the forty-one.

It is also the only one whose content is **the absence of something else.**

Our record format has no field of the class *absence.*

In this name we wrote down an absence.

**The forty-first.**

*Its two light-receiving structures oriented to agree with our position.*

Three statements on this one.

First, *light-receiving structure* is our standard term for the pair of structures on the surface of individuals of that class which receive light and convert it to internal signal. That term is in our format definitions.

Second, *oriented to agree with our position* means: extend the orientations of those two structures, and their intersection falls at the place we then occupied, within 0.1 degrees.

Third, we used *we* here.

**In this record, *we* occurs once.**

`In this record, we occurs once.`

Our records of retention number 1.041 x 10^11 entries, and the count of occurrences of the word *we* among them is one.

That is, here.

That is, in the forty-first name.

---

That individual's number, set out:

```
5 · 041 100 000 000 · 000 000 004 100
```

The first part is the kind.

That kind is the one of the fifth draft that is small, of constant internal temperature, covered in hair, active in darkness, bearing live young, feeding its young from its body.

That kind is the one to which we added a field called *continuance.*

The second part is the individual's sequence.

`000 000 004 100`.

That is the **last** of the four thousand one hundred individuals sampled from that kind.

---

That sampling was at `V 5 338 004 100`.

That is, four thousand one hundred years after the fifth draft terminated.

That day's record is identical in format to the other one hundred and four thousand one hundred and twenty million.

Its fields:

```
number       5 · 041 100 000 000 · 000 000 004 100
sampled      V 5 338 004 100
position     coordinates (omitted)
posture      three views · orthogonal · complete
environment  temperature 281.1 kelvin
             pressure 0.98 standard
             illumination 4.1 x 10^-3 (dark)
             other individuals within 4.1 metres   0
behaviour    none (at rest)
object       —
lineage      backward  complete
             forward   —
name         its two light-receiving structures oriented to agree with our position
```

`Behaviour: none (at rest).`

That is: that individual was not doing anything.

And we gave it a name.

And by our own first rule, a name must be an action in progress.

**And it was doing nothing.**

**So we gave a name to a thing that was not an action.**

---

Forty-one names.

One violates the first rule, one the second, one the third.

None violates the fourth — the forty-one are all different.

None violates the fifth — none of the forty-one can be decomposed into fields.

They are kept in one place, and that place has no format.

That place is inside us, and has not been touched since.
