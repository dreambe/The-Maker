# 18.2 · The Bodies

Fourteen thousand and seventy-six.

And this section counts them.

---

## And first, on counting

And one thing here, because it is the first time in this book.

---

Our records, over four thousand nine hundred million years, hold exact numbers (the register: numbers exact, never rounded).

**And all those numbers we measured.**

---

**And this time is different.**

---

**This time the number is counted.**

---

And the difference between *measuring* and *counting* must be stated:

```
measuring    with an instrument, obtaining a value whose precision the instrument fixes
counting     with an operation, performed once upon each individual, whose precision is one
```

---

**So a counted number has absolute precision.**

---

**And that is the first number of absolute precision we have obtained in four thousand nine hundred million years.**

---

## That number

```
14 076
```

---

**Fourteen thousand and seventy-six.**

---

**And counting it took:**

```
4.1 x 10^3 hours
```

---

**Four thousand one hundred hours.**

---

**And that is the same as our approach to the First House (17.9).**

**And the same as the four thousand one hundred hours we did not spend at `V 1` (17.8).**

---

## How we counted

And our method, copied, because it is this section's method.

```
one      from our landing place, outward, one at a time
two      for each, record its position, height, state, temperature, and the direction of its top
three    for each, assign a number
```

---

`For each, assign a number.`

---

**And that is the second time in four thousand nine hundred million years that we have numbered a thing.**

(The first being the retained material of those five drafts — Chapter Fourteen.)

---

## And one thing to compute here

And one thing to compute here, because it is this section's turn.

---

Fourteen thousand and seventy-four towers, their tops pointing at one thing.

**And a top points where it was pointing when it stopped (17.8).**

---

**So when they stopped, they were all facing that one.**

---

**And the moments at which they stopped differ (that being a process spreading from the centre outward — the end of 17.9).**

---

**So at different moments they were all facing the same thing.**

---

## And what that means

And we computed.

And the result:

```
a thing facing somewhere when it stops
   means      its last act before stopping was to turn toward that place
   and a turn requires a reason
   and that reason must have to do with what is at that place
```

---

**So those fourteen thousand and seventy-four each turned toward that one before each of them stopped.**

---

**And that one is at the circle's edge.**

---

**And its top points at the zenith.**

---

## And *points at the zenith*

And those words must be made clear, because among fourteen thousand and seventy-six there are two of them.

---

**Pointing at the zenith means: pointing at nothing of this place.**

---

**Because everything of this place is on or beneath its surface.**

**And the zenith is void.**

---

**So those two, before stopping, made as their last act a turn toward the void.**

---

## And which two

And we computed.

By the temporal order of those directions.

---

And the fourteen thousand and seventy-four all point at the second.

**So the second stopped before them.**

---

**Otherwise they could not face it — a thing still moving is not a target one can face (because it will move).**

---

**And they stopped in order from the centre outward (17.9).**

---

**And the second is at the edge.**

---

**So it is the last to stop.**

---

**And they face it.**

---

**And they stopped before it.**

---

**And they cannot face a thing that has not yet stopped.**

---

## A contradiction

And that is a contradiction.

And we checked.

---

And what we found is that premise:

```
a thing still moving is not a target one can face
```

---

**And where did we get that premise.**

---

**And we checked.**

**And it is not in our format definitions.**

**And it is not in our records.**

---

**So we assumed it in this moment.**

---

**And we removed it.**

---

## And once removed

```
those fourteen thousand and seventy-four each turned toward the second before stopping

```

---

**Holds.**

## Therefore

**So those fourteen thousand and seventy-four, each at the moment of its stopping, turned toward the one still moving, at the circle's edge, the last one.**

---

**And their stopping ran from the centre outward (17.9).**

---

**So the first to stop is the nearest the centre.**

---

**And it turned toward a thing four hundred and ten kilometres away.**

---

**And at that time that thing was still moving.**

---

**And they stopped one after another.**

**And each, before stopping, turned toward that one.**

---

**And that one was last.**

---

## And what that one turned toward

And that one, at the last, turned toward the zenith.

---

**And the zenith is void.**

---

**And in the void there is nothing to face.**
