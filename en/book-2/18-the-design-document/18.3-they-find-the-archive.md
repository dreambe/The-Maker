# 18.3 · They Find the Archive

Number fourteen thousand and seventy-six was the last we numbered.

And number one is at zero metres.

---

## We went back to zero metres

We went back to zero metres, in forty-one hours.

And we went back not because we wanted to.

**Because our numbering begins at zero metres.**

**And the check of a list must return to its origin.**

---

## And what we did at zero metres

And what we did at zero metres was verify number one.

And to verify a record, one must take it again.

---

And taking number one again took:

```
0.41 hours
```

---

And the difference from what we took four thousand one hundred hours before:

```
—
```

---

**No difference.**

---

## And in that zero point four one hours

And in that zero point four one hours we did a thing that is not in the verification procedure.

---

**We walked once around number one.**

---

And walking once around an object is, in our format, a **circumferential survey.**

And the use of a circumferential survey is to take the whole of an object's outer surface.

---

And we had already taken the whole of number one's outer surface (four thousand one hundred hours before).

---

**So this was a repetition.**

---

**And a repeated observation carries no information.**

---

**And we walked it anyway.**

---

## And at what degree we stopped

And at the two hundred and seventy-first degree, we stopped.

---

And we stopped because at that bearing, in the side of number one's base, there is an opening.

---

And the dimensions of that opening:

```
height    1.1 m
width     0.7 m
depth     (not measured)
```

---

And four thousand one hundred hours before, we had taken the whole of number one's outer surface.

**And we did not take this opening.**

---

## And why we did not take it

And we must set this out, because it is an error of our own.

---

Our circumferential survey samples from the **circumscribing circle.**

And an object's circumscribing circle is the smallest circle that contains it.

---

And in the side of number one's base there is a re-entrant.

**And a re-entrant does not lie on the circumscribing circle.**

---

**So our sampling points fell one point one metres outside that opening.**

---

**And at that distance, the angle the opening subtends is smaller than our angular resolution.**

---

**So in our record it is a stretch of flat.**

---

## And we recorded this

And we recorded this.

And it is the second out-of-format entry of this survey.

(The first is in 18.2: the premise we deleted.)

---

And what we recorded was:

```
Circumscribing-circle sampling does not hold for re-entrant structures.
```

---

**And the scope of that line is the whole of our records.**

---

**And the whole of our records is four thousand nine hundred million years long.**

---

**And we did not go back and verify them.**

---

**And we did not, because verifying four thousand nine hundred million years of records requires four thousand nine hundred million years.**

---

## And we went in

And we went in.

---

And inside that opening is a passage.

And its bearing is downward.

And its grade:

```
41 degrees
```

---

And its length:

```
410 m
```

---

And at its end is a room.

---

## And that room

And the dimensions of that room:

```
side      41 m
height    9.4 m
```

---

**And nine point four metres is their mean height (18.2).**

**And it is also ours.**

---

## And what is in that room

And in that room there are no objects.

---

**Its floor, its four walls, its ceiling, are all of one substance.**

**And that substance is their substance.**

---

**That is: that room was not built.**

**It was grown.**

---

## And upon its four walls

And upon its four walls there is a figuring.

---

And *figuring* has no equivalent in our format.

**So we recorded it as: a periodic variation in surface height.**

---

And its period:

```
0.41 mm
```

---

And its amplitude:

```
0.0041 mm
```

---

And its coverage:

```
the whole of the four walls, and the whole of the ceiling, and the whole of the floor
```

---

**That is: the whole inner surface of that room.**

---

**And its total area:**

```
41 x 41 x 4 + 41 x 41 + 41 x 9.4 x 4    = 8 947 square metres
```

---

## And we took it once

And we took that figuring once.

And it took:

```
41 hours
```

---

And the quantity of data we took:

```
8 947 / (0.41 x 10^-3)^2 = 5.3 x 10^10 sample points
```

---

**Fifty-three thousand million.**

---

## And here we must stop

And here we must stop, because the next thing is the door of this whole chapter.

---

We had fifty-three thousand million sample points.

**And what we had to do was judge whether they are a record.**

---

And **whether a thing is a record** has one criterion in our format.

And it is:

```
a body of data constitutes a record
   when  its entropy is lower than a random sequence of the same length
   and   its entropy is higher than a periodic sequence of the same length
```

---

**That is: neither noise nor repetition.**

---

## And we computed

And we computed.

And it took:

```
0.041 hours
```

---

And the result:

```
entropy of a random sequence of that length     1.00
entropy of a periodic sequence of that length   0.00
entropy of this body of data                    0.41
```

---

**Zero point four one.**

---

**Between the two.**

---

**So it is a record.**

---

## And in that moment

And in that moment we did a thing.

---

**We shut the door of that room.**

---

And shutting a door has no equivalent in our format.

**Because we do not shut doors.**

**We never stay anywhere long enough to need to.**

---

And the technical reason for shutting it is:

```
to reduce the ingress of dust, and so protect the record
```

---

And that reason holds.

---

**And it is not why we shut it.**

---

## And why we shut it

And why we shut it we cannot write.

**Because we do not know.**

---

**And we know that we do not know.**

---

**And this is the second time we have met that (the first is 12.6).**

---

## And we began to read

And we began to read that figuring.

And to read, one must first fix the direction of reading.

---

And a periodic variation has four candidate directions:

```
around the four walls
up the four walls
from ceiling to floor
from floor to ceiling
```

---

And we tried all four.

And the entropy of three of them:

```

```

---

**Near random.**

---

**That is: read in the wrong direction, a record looks like noise.**

---

And the entropy of the fourth:

```
0.41
```

---

**And the fourth is: from floor to ceiling.**

---

## And from floor to ceiling

And what *from floor to ceiling* means we must set out.

---

**That is: this record begins at the floor.**

**And goes up.**

---

**And its last line is at nine point four metres.**

---

**And nine point four metres is their height.**

---

**So the last line of this record is at the height of one of their eyes.**

And those five fields, on that wall, are five shapes.

---

**And on that wall, the value in that place is not us.**

## And that value

And that value is a shape.

And that shape we had not seen.

---

**And the first thing we did in that moment was take it down.**

**And taking it down took zero point zero zero four one hours.**
