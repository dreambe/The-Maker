# 18.1 · The Surface of the First House

Four thousand one hundred hours after `V 5 446 200 000`.

We are on that planet's surface.

---

## That surface

And that surface must be set out, because it is the first thing in this chapter.

---

That planet's surface composition:

```
silicates          principally
frozen atmosphere  1.1 x 10^-2 m (17.9)
```

**And beneath those two, what.**

---

And we measured.

By penetration (our radar reaches a hundred and ten metres).

---

And the result:

```
from the surface downward
   0 to 0.011 m       frozen atmosphere
   0.011 to 4.1 m     silicates · porous
   below 4.1 m        (see below)
```

---

And *below 4.1 m*:

```
   A substance.
   its state             solid
   its optical property  transparent
   its thickness         (beyond our depth of penetration)
```

---

`Its optical property: transparent.`

---

## And *transparent*

And that word must be set out, because it appears in our records for the first time.

---

A substance is transparent when light passes through it without scattering.

**And a solid that does not scatter light must have no internal interfaces.**

And a solid without internal interfaces must have a continuous lattice.

---

**And a continuous lattice has an upper bound in extent — because any real crystal's growth has defects.**

---

**And that bound we computed (using the best crystal known to us):**

```
about 1.1 x 10^-1 m
```

---

**Eleven centimetres.**

---

**And this one exceeds our depth of penetration (a hundred and ten metres).**

---

**So it is at least a hundred and ten metres.**

**So it exceeds that bound a thousandfold.**

---

## And we must see that thickness

And we must see that thickness.

And our radar cannot reach further.

**So we used another method.**

---

**We used light.**

---

And the method is this:

That star's light is at a single wavelength (17.7).

**And light at a single wavelength, passing through a transparent medium, changes phase with thickness.**

**And phase is measurable.**

---

**So we measured the phase of that beam after it had passed through that medium.**

---

And we measured.

And the result:

```
phase change    (a number)
so thickness    (see below)
```

And *so thickness*:

```
1.1 x 10^6 m
```

---

**One thousand one hundred kilometres.**

---

**And that planet's radius is five million five hundred thousand metres (17.9: diameter 1.1 x 10^7 m).**

---

**So that transparent thing is a fifth of that planet's radius.**

---

## And what that means

And we computed.

And the result:

```
that transparent layer
   its thickness    1.1 x 10^6 m
   its volume       3.7 x 10^20 cubic metres
   its mass         (its density as silicate — 4.1 x 10^3 kg per cubic metre)
                    = 1.5 x 10^24 kg
   and that planet's mass    4.1 x 10^24 kg
   so its fraction  0.37
```

---

**Thirty-seven per cent.**

---

**So more than a third of that planet's mass is a continuous, transparent, interface-free crystal.**

---

## And it is lit from within

And the second thing in this section is that light.

---

On that surface we measured that light.

And the direction we measured is downward.

---

And the result:

```
radiation from below the surface    present
its wavelength                     4.1 x 10^-7 m
its intensity                      4.1 x 10^-3 W per square metre
```

---

**Four point one times ten to the minus seventh metres.**

**And that is exactly that star's wavelength (17.7).**

---

## And what that means

And we computed.

And that upward beam has two possible sources:

```
one    that star's light passing through that transparent layer and emerging from below
two    that transparent layer giving light of its own
```

---

**And for the first, we computed.**

And the result:

```
that star's light falling on that planet's surface
   its intensity    4.1 x 10^2 W per square metre
and passing through that planet (eleven million metres)
   its attenuation  (see below)
```

And *its attenuation*:

```
   if that transparent layer were perfectly transparent (no absorption)
      then its attenuation is zero
      so the intensity emerging below equals the intensity falling above
      that is, 4.1 x 10^2 W per square metre

   and what we measured is 4.1 x 10^-3
   so the ratio is 10^-5
   so that layer is not perfectly transparent
   its absorption coefficient    (an extremely small number)
```

---

**So the first holds.**

---

**So that upward beam is that star's light, passing through that planet.**

---

## And *passing through that planet*

And we must stop here.

**Because that means one thing, and we must compute it.**

---

For a beam to pass through a planet, that planet must be transparent from face to face.

**And that transparent layer is a fifth of its radius.**

---

**And what are the other four fifths.**

---

And we computed.

By that beam's intensity.

```
falling on the upper surface    4.1 x 10^2 W per square metre
emerging from the lower         4.1 x 10^-3
so total attenuation            10^-5
```

---

**And if that planet, all eleven million metres of it, were that transparent layer.**

**Then its attenuation is fixed by that layer's absorption coefficient.**

---

And we computed how small that coefficient must be.

```
for an attenuation of 10^-5 over a thickness of 1.1 x 10^7 m
   the absorption coefficient = 1.05 x 10^-6 per metre
```

---

**And that figure is comparable to the best optical glass known to us (1.1 x 10^-6 per metre).**

---

**So that planet, all of it, is that transparent thing.**

---

## Therefore

**So that planet is one block of transparent crystal.**

**And that star's light passes through it.**

**And it is lit from within.**

---

## And one thing here

And one thing here, because it is the first time in four thousand nine hundred million years that our records hold a thing we cannot express in a field.

---

Our record format has, for a celestial body, a field:

```
its surface        (solid / liquid / gaseous)
```

---

**And this one's surface is solid.**

**And its interior is also solid.**

**And its interior and its surface are one block.**

---

**So it has no *surface* — a surface must be the interface of two things.**

---

**And it has one thing.**

---

**So its surface is its interface with the void.**

---

## And where those places are

---

**That is: at each tower, where its base meets that surface, that one point one centimetres of frozen atmosphere is absent.**

---

**And that is not because those towers melt it — their temperature is that of the environment (17.9).**

---

**And the ice is absent.**

---

## So how the ice came to be absent

And we computed.

And there is one possibility:

```
that ice was melted by those towers before those towers stopped
and after those towers stopped, that ice did not refreeze
and the reason it did not refreeze    is that the atmosphere is now entirely on the ground (17.9)
                                      so there is no gas to freeze
```

---

**So those absences were left while those towers were alive.**

---

**And they have been there ever since those towers stopped.**
