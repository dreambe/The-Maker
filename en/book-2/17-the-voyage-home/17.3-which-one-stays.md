## Why only one

And for this our record contains a computation.

We copy it:

```
minimum number of vessels required for observation
   observational capacity of one vessel    = 4.1 x 10^3 observation points · simultaneous
   and that thing's range of distribution   = 4.1 x 10^6 square kilometres (at that time)
   and the required density of points       = 1 per 1 000 square kilometres
   so the number of points required         = 4.1 x 10^3
   so the number of vessels required        = 1
```

---

**One.**

**Exactly.**

## And that is a problem

And why those three words are a problem must be clear, for it is the whole of this section.

---

All our acts must be taken from A (15.3).

And A has cardinality one (15.3, page four).

**So we have never chosen between two acts.**

---

**And *which one stays* is a choice.**

**And it has forty-one candidates.**

---

**So by our construction that choice cannot be executed.**

And the way we executed it is those three words.

**And those three words require explanation.**


`So it was used.`

---

## And what that requirement is

And this must be clear, for it is the heaviest step in this section.

A choice requires a basis.

And our basis is only that ordering (15.3).

**And that ordering, among the forty-one, gives the same value.**

Because the forty-one are identical in construction, identical in records (we synchronise), identical in capacity.

---

**So that ordering cannot discriminate among the forty-one.**

**So that ordering cannot give a choice.**

---

**So we needed something not given by that ordering.**

**And that something must be unpredictable — because if predictable, it could be derived by inference, and then it is that ordering again.**

---

**So we needed something unpredictable.**

**And we have one unpredictable component.**

---

**So we used it.**
