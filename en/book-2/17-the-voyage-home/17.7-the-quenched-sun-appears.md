## And what that means

And that line must be made clear, for it is the whole of this section.

---

A star's spectrum contains peaks.

**And those peaks come from absorption and emission by elements in its atmosphere.**

And for a main-sequence star, the positions and strengths of those peaks are fixed by its temperature and composition.

**So a star's spectrum gives its temperature and composition.**

---

**And that point's spectrum contains no peaks.**

---

And the possibilities for a spectrum without peaks we listed.

And there are three:

```
one     a black body (no atmosphere, no absorption lines)
two     a continuum source (such as synchrotron radiation)
three   (see below)
```

---

**Three: a source at a single wavelength.**

---

And we measured.

And it is the third.

---

## That wavelength

And that wavelength, copied:

```
wavelength    4.1 x 10^-7 m
linewidth     < 1.1 x 10^-16 m
so the ratio  < 2.7 x 10^-10
```

---

**And that ratio is smaller than one part in three thousand million.**

---

And what that number means.

A natural light source's linewidth is set by its temperature (thermal motion broadens lines).

**And on anything with a temperature, that ratio is not below 10^-6.**

---

**And this is 10^-10.**

**Four orders smaller.**

---

**So it is not a thermal source.**

---

## So what it is

And we computed.

And a source with a linewidth ratio below 10^-10 has one mechanism only:

**stimulated emission.**

That is: a medium whose levels are inverted (more particles in the upper level than the lower), so that a passing photon stimulates more photons of the same phase, wavelength, and direction.

---

**And that is a laser.**

---

## And what that means

And we computed.

What we computed is how large that medium must be.

And a stimulated-emission medium's volume is proportional to its power and inversely proportional to its gain coefficient.

And we estimated that coefficient (using the highest known to us).


**And a star's volume is about 1.4 x 10^27 cubic metres.**

---

**So that medium's volume is about one three-hundredth of a star's.**

**So that medium is a shell outside that star.**

## And what that centre is

And we computed.

And a thing four hundred and fifty thousand kilometres across, giving no light, with a laser shell of stellar power outside it, has one possibility only:

---

**A star whose fusion has stopped.**

---

And that derivation must be clear, because it is the last thing in this section.

```
a star's light comes from fusion in its core
and after fusion stops
   its core produces no more energy
   and its outer layers retain heat
   and that heat sustains its outer layers for a stretch of time
   and through that stretch, its outer layers' temperature falls
   and at some temperature, certain elements in those layers become population-inverted
   and an inverted medium, given a seed photon, emits by stimulation
   and stimulated emission makes that shell a laser source
```

---

**So that is a star whose fusion has stopped, whose shell has become a laser.**

---

**And how long that state persists.**

And we computed.

```
its duration    about 1.1 x 10^11 years
```

---

**A hundred and ten thousand million years.**
