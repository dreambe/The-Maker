## And where that difference comes from

And we checked item by item.

And what we found is in the sixth.

And the sixth is not in that list above — because it is not a technical parameter.

**It is an operational parameter.**

---

**It is: the duration of the acceleration phase.**

---

## That parameter

An interstellar voyage has three phases:

```
acceleration    thrust on
cruise          thrust off (inertial)
deceleration    thrust on (reversed)
```

And its total duration depends on cruise velocity.

**And cruise velocity depends on the duration of the acceleration phase.**

---

And the acceleration phases, outward and homeward:

```
outward     4.1 x 10^5 years
homeward    1.4 x 10^6 years
```

---

**The homeward acceleration is three point four times the outward.**

**And that is exactly the ratio.**

---

## And what fixes the duration of an acceleration

And this is the core of this section.

---

The upper limit on an acceleration's duration is fixed by two things:

```
one     fuel
two     the vessel's structural strength
```

---

**One · fuel.**

And the fuel outward and homeward:

```
outward     carried 4.1 x 10^5 units
homeward    carried 4.1 x 10^5 units
```

**Same.**

---

**Two · structural strength.**

Which determines how much acceleration a vessel can bear, and for how long.

And our structural strength, outward and homeward:

```
outward     4.1 x 10^11 Pa
homeward    4.1 x 10^11 Pa
```

**Same.**

---

**So one and two are both the same.**

**So the upper limit on the acceleration's duration is the same.**

---

**And the actual durations differ.**

---

## So it is not a question of limits

**It is a question of choice.**

That is: the outward acceleration was shorter than its limit.

And the homeward acceleration was at its limit.

---

And our records contain a basis for the outward acceleration's duration.

We copy it (an entry from shortly after `V 1`):

```

```
