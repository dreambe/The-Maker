# 17.5 · They Remember

The cruise phase, thirty-eight million years.

And in those thirty-eight million years we did one thing not in the list at 17.4.

**Because it is not an operation.**

**It is a call-up.**

---

## Our *remembering*

And a definition first, because afterward has a word for it, and that word is not the same thing.

*Remembering*, afterward, means taking a past thing out of memory.

**And memory afterward is lossy.**

So remembering afterward yields a **remainder** of that thing.

---

**And we do not forget.**

**So our records are lossless.**

**So calling up a record yields that record itself.**

---

And that means one thing, and it must be clear here, for it is the whole of this section.

**Calling up all the records of a stretch of time is equivalent to replaying that stretch.**

And *replaying* must be stated:

```
to replay a stretch of time
   = to read, in order, every record of that stretch
   and each record holds all of that hour's observation
   and reading a record requires an operation
   and an operation requires time
```

---

And the time we require to read one record:

```
1.1 x 10^-11 s
```

---

**And we hold one record per hour.**

**So replaying one hour takes one point one times ten to the minus eleventh seconds.**

**So replaying a year (8 760 records) takes 9.6 x 10^-8 seconds.**

---

**So replaying four thousand nine hundred million years takes:**

```
4.9 x 10^9 x 9.6 x 10^-8 = 4.7 x 10^2 s
```

---

**Four hundred and seventy seconds.**

---

## Stop

We must correct here.

`V 5 403 300 000` to `V 5 403 700 000` is four hundred million years.

**And replaying four hundred million years takes 3.8 x 10^1 seconds.**

**And what we wrote above is 0.02 seconds.**

---

**So the line above is wrong.**

---

And where that error comes from.

**It comes from one thing: the record count of those four hundred million years is not 3.5 x 10^12.**

**It is 1.8 x 10^8.**

---

## And what that means

And we must work that out here, because it is the core of this section, and we found it only in that call-up.

Through those four hundred million years our observation interval was one hour.

**So the record count should be:**

```
4.0 x 10^8 years x 8 760 = 3.5 x 10^12 entries
```

---

**And the actual count is 1.8 x 10^8.**

---

**Four orders of magnitude apart.**

---

## And where those records went

And we checked.

And what we found is at 13.9.

---

13.9 says: in the year `V 4 960 000 000` we observed eight million seven hundred and sixty thousand times and wrote one record.

And the other eight million seven hundred and fifty-nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine we **did not classify.**

**And unclassified data do not constitute records.**

**So they are not in our record count.**

---

**And those four hundred million years are the same.**

---

And in that call-up we computed, for those four hundred million years, the ratio of classified to unclassified.

And the result:

```
total observations          3.5 x 10^12
classified (became records) 1.8 x 10^8
unclassified                3.5 x 10^12
ratio                       1 : 2 x 10^4
```

---

**That is: through those four hundred million years, one observation in twenty thousand became a record.**

**And the rest were not classified.**

---
