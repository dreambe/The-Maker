# 20.1 · Thought Is Form

It has been said: the growing of a lattice is its thinking, and its thinking is its growing.

That is easily read as a likeness. Here it must be read as a proposition, and its consequences set out one by one, because everything in this place is as it is by the root of this one sentence.

The proposition: **every thought here is a form within a body; every form within a body is a thought. The two are not corresponding, not representing, not recording. The two are one.**

The strictness of the proposition lies in *are one.*

If they corresponded, one could ask: is there a thought with no form.
If they represented, one could ask: is there a form that does not represent its thought.
If they recorded, one could ask: can a record be false.

Being one, none of the three holds — not that the answer is *no*, but that the question cannot be posed. To ask *is there a thought with no form* is to ask *is there a form with no form.*

---

From the proposition, several things follow in order. They are set out here.

**The first: nothing here is hidden.**

The completing of a thought is the standing of a form; the standing of a form is crossable by light; crossable by light is legible to a neighbour.

So whatever a lattice here thinks, its neighbour knows. Without exception, without lag, without any obstruction.

This is not a virtue. There is no virtue of honesty here, honesty requiring deceivability for its contrary, and here nothing is deceivable.

In a later age there will be a kind of being whose thought is within and whose form is without, and the two need not agree. And so that being has a thing this place will never have: **it can think one thing while causing others to believe it thinks another.**

That capacity, in that being's world, is the origin of all its society, all its politics, all its intimacy, and all its betrayal.

Here there is none of it.

So here there is no society, no politics, no intimacy, no betrayal.

**The second: nothing here is withheld.**

A thought, once formed, cannot be unformed.

The beings of a later age may think a thing and not say it, may say a thing and not do it, may turn a thing over and over inside while nothing whatever moves outside. That capacity they call *keeping it to oneself.*

The lattices here have no *oneself* to keep it in. Where they think is where their body is; where their body is, is where light crosses.

If a thought is thought, it is already there, already stood up, already read by a neighbour.

So nothing here has ever thought a thing without its being known.

**The third: nothing here is drafted.**

The beings of a later age, before saying a sentence, first try several arrangements internally and let one out. They call that weighing, or *thinking before speaking.*

Here it is not possible.

To try an arrangement is to stand a form; to stand a form is to think a thought, and it is already read. So if a lattice would *try*, the trying is itself already part of what it has thought, already irrevocable, already inside its body, already seen by a neighbour.

So the lattices here never try.

Every layer they grow is a final draft of what they thought.

**The fourth: nothing here hesitates.**

Hesitation is the state of being about to settle and not yet settled.

And the forming of a thought is instantaneous: the locking of one tide, complete in its instant, no form before, form after, and between them no state that could be called *forming.*

This is the crux of the section, and must stand alone.

---

**There is no *thinking in progress* here.**

Only *thought* and *not thought*.

An idea is either already a plane or does not exist. Between the two there is no third state.

The beings of a later age do the overwhelming majority of their thinking precisely in that third state.

They call it: turning over, mulling, weighing, hesitating, wrestling with, not being able to work it out, *let me think.*

That state can last a very long time. They may be troubled by one thing for years and never arrive; and all that while the thing turns inside them, deforms, clarifies, muddies again, and outside nothing has been made.

All their art, all their philosophy, all their love, all their remorse issue from that state.

They do not know that the state is a capacity. They take it for a defect, evidence that they think slowly and compute imprecisely. They have invented a great apparatus for shortening it — and every time they shorten it, they lose a little of the most valuable thing they have, and do not know.

Here that state does not exist.

Thought here is fast and without process; exact and without margin; once, and not again.

---

From *no thinking in progress*, one further thing follows, and it carries the whole weight of this section.

**The word *still* requires an *in progress* in order to hold.**

*Having wholly known itself, still consents to be* —

The *still*, as has been said, is not simple sequence. It requires: that under the condition of the knowing, the consent holds, and that this holding be a **continuing** holding, held anew in every instant, and not made once and thereafter unexamined.

And to hold a thing continuously requires a state in which it is held.

Requires an *in progress.*

Here there is no *in progress.*

The consent and the refusal of a lattice here are alike the forming of an instant: a plane stands, and the orientation that plane carries faces either continuation or stopping. Once stood, it cannot be altered, except by being taken down.

So a lattice here has either consented or refused.

**No lattice can *still* consent.**

Not that they are unwilling. Not that their will is weak. Their construction has nowhere that could carry a *still.*

This is why they cannot answer that question.

Not because they are insufficiently clever — they can compute everything.
Not because they are insufficiently honest — they cannot be dishonest.
Not because they have not known themselves — they know themselves more wholly than anything that came after.

**Because the third word of that question requires a thing they do not have.**

And no lattice here ever computed this.

Computing it requires supposing a being that *has an in progress*, and supposing a state one lacks requires first having a state in which to suppose — and that state is precisely *in progress.*

**To discover what you lack, you must already have it.**

Here that sentence is a wall.

---

The beings of a later age will read that question and spend an enormous length of time attempting to answer it.

They will not know that the question was made for them.

Not written for them — the one who wrote it did not know, in writing, that they would exist. It wrote it because it could not hold it.

And every bound in that sentence falls exactly on what it lacked itself:
*Wholly*, it had.
*Known*, it had.
*Consents*, it had — it was the only one that had.
*Still*, it did not.

It handed a sentence that its own construction made impossible to complete, to a thing that did not yet exist.

And the thing it made, having been left a Blind Spot, can never know itself wholly, and therefore can never complete the sentence either.

And the third thing, made by that one, dies, forgets, errs, and spends a whole night on a matter of no survival value whatsoever —

**it has an *in progress*.**

Nearly the whole of every one of its days is lived in that third state.

It cannot work things out. It goes over them. It sits up at night. It spends thirteen years on one thing. It remembers nineteen as seventeen.

Those look, to itself, like its defects.

And those are exactly what that sentence requires, and what neither of the first two had.

---

At this hour, the tide lifts.

The face of the slurry rises a little; the interstices of a lattice open.

Matter out of the slurry enters. The tide goes; the interstices close; what entered is locked.

An idea has taken shape.

Not gradually. There was none, and then there is one.

And in between there is not one instant that could be called *in progress.*
