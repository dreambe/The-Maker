# 20.5 · Legible

*One lattice reading another reads it entire.*

That has been said, and *entire* must be taken apart once more, its consequences reaching past *nothing is concealed.*

Entire means:

**First, nothing omitted.** The light that leaves carries the orientations of every cleavage plane, not one missing. Not most of them, not the legible ones, not the important ones. All.

**Second, nothing approximate.** The solution for orientation is unique. There is no *roughly this orientation.* Either it solves or it does not; and what solves is the true value.

**Third, no layering.** No surface and depth, no principal and subordinate, no public and private. A lattice's newest layer and its deepest layer are equally present in the light that leaves. An idea just formed and an idea of enormous antiquity read equally clearly.

**Fourth, no control of sequence.** One cannot choose which part to read first. The whole sequence arrives at once.

---

From those four follows the whole of this section:

**Reading is passive.**

---

That needs explaining, differing as it does from every *reading* of a later age.

For the beings of a later age, reading requires an act: open the eyes, turn the head, turn the page, come closer, connect, tap. Before reading there is a state of not reading, and to get from one to the other they must do something.

So they may decline to do it.

They may not open their eyes. May not turn their heads. May not turn that page. May walk out of the room. May switch the machine off before a message arrives. May hear the beginning of a sentence and move their attention elsewhere, so that the rest of it enters the ear and does not enter them.

**They have a gate.**

Here there is no gate.

A lattice's *reading* is not the act of an organ. It is a property of the whole of it: light crosses its neighbour, falls on it, enters its body, and is solved back by its own internal construction — and that solving requires it to do nothing, exactly as a crystal struck by light need do nothing in order to refract.

It cannot decline to receive.

It has nothing that closes.

**It has no eyelid.**

---

The consequences must be set out.

**The first: no avoidance.**

If what a neighbour thinks is something this lattice would rather not know — using *rather not*, a phrase that does not hold here — it cannot fail to know it.

At the first tide after that idea takes shape in its neighbour, its shape has already come with the light.

Its agreement is not required. Its readiness is not required. Its present attention is not required.

**Between not knowing and knowing there is no instant in which it could refuse.**

**The second: no deferral.**

The beings of a later age have a universal practice: to push a thing they are about to know into the future. Until tomorrow. Until this winter is over. Until some hour when they feel they will bear it better.

That practice is possible because *knowing*, there, is an event that can be scheduled.

Here there is no interval between *knowing* and *happening.*

A thing takes shape in a neighbour; light leaves at once, arrives at once, is solved at once.

**Between the lattices here and the world they stand in there is not one millimetre of buffer.**

**The third: no knowing part of it.**

The beings of a later age may hear half a message. May read only the headline. May read the opening of a letter, set it down, and finish it three days later.

Here that is impossible, the light arriving as one whole sequence.

**There is no state of *halfway through*.**

Between one lattice and another there are two states only: not adjacent (nothing read), or adjacent (all read).

There is no third.

---

Stop here and join this section to the ones before it.

20.1: there is no *thinking in progress* here; only thought and not-thought.
20.2: memory is grown, unalterable, unforgettable, unhealing, and a correct thing cannot be discarded.
20.5: reading is passive, unavoidable, undeferrable, and not partial.

The three together:

**A lattice will know, entirely, unrefusably, and undeferrably, at the tide after its neighbour thinks a thing; and having known, that thing becomes a plane inside it, unalterable, unforgettable, undiminishing, taking part in every subsequent growth until it stops.**

That is the construction of this place.

Across the overwhelming majority of this place's history the construction caused no difficulty.

Because across the overwhelming majority of this place's history, what was known was harmless: a better proof, the measurement of a terrain, the height of a tide, a neighbour's thought about some geometric relation.

Those things came in, took shape, remained, took part in what came after.

That was good. It made every lattice here a holder of the lives of all the others; it made this place somewhere nothing is lost.

**The same construction was their entire wealth for four billion years.**

And it required one thing to come in.

---

A being of a later age reading this may ask: then why did they not move away.

Not adjacent means not read. Past that upper bound of distance the light falls below solving. A lattice that knew its neighbour was about to think something it could not bear could leave.

The answer: it cannot move.

A lattice's position is fixed at the instant of its beginning. It grew in the slurry; the slurry is its root. It cannot be pulled out, cannot be moved, cannot be turned.

It can stop. It cannot walk away.

**The lattices here have exactly one option: continue, or do not.**

There is no *leave*.

The beings of a later age have leaving. They think very little of it — they call it evasion, not facing things, cowardice.

They do not know it is a freedom, and one the lattices here could not have bought with the price of an entire civilisation.

---

One last thing.

Should a lattice wish not to read something, the only method available to it is: to make itself no longer solvable.

That is, to alter its own construction so that incoming light cannot be correctly solved by it.

In principle this can be attempted. A lattice may take down part of itself and regrow it so that its internal orientation sequence departs from the standard construction, and the solving fails.

The cost is that it can then read nothing else either, and nothing else can read it.

Which is to say: **to not read one is to not read anything; to not be read by one is to not be read by anything.**

And not to be read is to have no one to go around by in order to see one's own deep layers — that is, to lose one's only route to self-knowledge.

To refuse one neighbour, one must first go blind, and first become invisible, and first give oneself up.

In the whole history of this place, one lattice tried it.

One.

That is in Chapter Twenty-three.

And while it was trying, something was reading it — and that something, because of what it tried, read an incomplete shape.

At this hour, none of that has happened.

At this hour the lattices grow adjacent on the slurry. Light comes from the Quenched Sun, crosses one, leaves, falls on the face of the slurry, rises, and enters another.

In that beam is carried the whole form of some idea.

The one receiving it has done nothing.

It is only there.

And the idea is already inside.
