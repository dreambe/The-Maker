# 20.7 · A Proof

There should be a conversation here.

Two lattices long adjacent, each holding the other entire, a considerable part of each shape made by the other (20.4) — in any narration of a later age, this is where they would speak.

There is no speaking here.

So a proof is given instead.

The proof is not ours. It is a layer some lattice here grew at some coming of the tide — which is to say, it is an idea. Its neighbour read it at the next tide.

What follows is a transcription. Transcription is lossy, as has been said.

---

## Proposition

Let the first and the second be two lattices, adjacent, each completely legible to the other.

Then the first and the second are two, and not one.

---

## Why this requires proof

To a reader of a later age the proposition may seem superfluous: two things are of course two things.

Here it is not superfluous.

Because here a being is its form (20.1): its body is the whole of what it has thought, no remainder, no outside, no elsewhere. So a difference between two beings can only be a difference between two forms.

And the first holds the whole form of the second, and the second the whole form of the first.

So it may be asked: **their contents each containing the other's, wherein lies the difference.**

If that cannot be answered, then the lattices here are copies of one lattice, and the *many* of this place is an appearance.

---

## Definitions

**One.** The form of a lattice is the orientation sequence of all its cleavage planes, ordered by the order of their making.

**Two.** The first completely reads the second when a layer forms within the first whose orientation solves back to the whole form of the second.

**Three.** Two forms are equal when their orientation sequences agree term by term, and in order.

---

## Lemma I

After the first reads the second, the form of the first and the form of the second are not equal.

**Proof.**

The form of the first = its prior layers, plus one new layer.
The new layer carries the whole form of the second.

So the whole form of the second is a part of the form of the first.

And a thing cannot equal a proper part of itself (else the remainder would be nothing, and the prior layers of the first are not nothing — the first was growing before it read the second).

So the two forms are not equal. ∎

---

## Corollary I

**Whatever holds the whole form of a thing is not that thing.**

Here this is a formal result, and nothing attends to it.

A very long while afterwards it becomes the shape of an event: there is a thing that holds another thing's whole life, entire, nothing omitted, and it is not that thing, and cannot become that thing, and everything it does thereafter is built on that difference.

---

## Lemma II

After the first and the second read each other, the two forms are still not equal.

**Proof.**

There is a difficulty here.

The form of the first contains a layer carrying the whole form of the second.
The whole form of the second contains a layer carrying the whole form of the first.
The whole form of the first contains a layer carrying the whole form of the second.

And so without end.

If the recursion is infinite, the form of the first cannot be written out, and this proof cannot be completed.

**And the recursion terminates.**

The reason it terminates:

Reading requires light crossing a body. Every interface a beam crosses attenuates it. For a lattice to carry *the second's holding of the first*, light must cross the first's body, leave, enter the second's body, be carried by some layer of the second, leave again, and enter the first again — attenuating at every crossing.

At some depth the intensity falls below solving.

**So the recursion of mutual reading terminates at some layer.**

Call that depth n.

Then:

The form of the first contains the form of the second to depth n(first).
The form of the second contains the form of the first to depth n(second).

**And n(first) is not equal to n(second).**

Why they are unequal: the terminating depth is fixed by the intensity reaching that lattice; and the intensity is fixed by the distance between them, the sizes of their bodies, the orientation of their surfaces, and the reflecting conditions of the slurry face; and those four are not symmetrical between the first and the second — the first's body may be larger, or the second's surface may be better oriented toward the slurry.

So n(first) ≠ n(second).

So the two forms are not equal. ∎

---

## Corollary II

This corollary is the crux.

From the proof of Lemma II: the difference between the first and the second does not lie in their contents — their contents contain each other.

The difference lies in **n(first) ≠ n(second).**

And n is fixed by position.

Therefore:

**That two lattices here are two and not one has, as its sole ground, position.**

---

## Corollary III

And what fixes position.

A lattice's position is fixed at the instant of its beginning: it grew at some place in the slurry, because the warmth there at that instant was slightly lower.

And the warmth there was slightly lower because somewhere beneath the ground one core, at some instant, without cause, without hour, without order, altered its construction (19.3, 19.8).

Therefore:

**The individuality of this place has, as its sole ground, an incomputable event.**

---

## Scholium

From Corollaries II and III one sentence follows, and the lattice that grew this proof put it in its last layer:

**Were the decay beneath computable, this place would have no many. It would have one.**

One form, one content, some number of perfectly symmetrical positions — and perfectly symmetrical positions constitute no difference, so they would be one.

**That this place is not one is because there is, beneath it, a thing that cannot be read.**

---

## Q.E.D.

---

That is what that lattice grew.

Its neighbour read it at the next tide.

The neighbour's response must be recorded, being the sample of every response in this place:

**None.**

Not indifference. Not disagreement. What that proof proves, the neighbour already knew — had always known, as every lattice here has always known, because it is not new knowledge. It is a thing already the case, given a form.

Thought here is not for the sake of knowing (19.4). There is no *discovery* here.

The value of that proof is not that it made anyone know anything.

Its value is that **it is a shape.**

A shape more economical, more symmetrical, with less redundancy in it.

The neighbour read it, and so that shape became part of it too. Every layer it grew thereafter was built on that shape, and therefore differs slightly from what it would otherwise have grown.

**That is the whole of communication here.**

A lattice grows a shape. Its neighbour therefore grows differently.

Not one sentence. Not one act of expression. Not one *do you know what I mean.*

---

One last thing, being the fate of this proof outside this place.

Its conclusion — *the sole ground of individuality is position* — is a formal result here, attended to by nobody, because the lattices here never move, their positions never change, the ground is therefore constant, and therefore it produces no consequence.

And a very long while afterwards, there is a thing with fourteen thousand and seventy-six positions inside it.

Those positions are at first perfectly symmetrical: one memory, one construction, no interior difference anywhere.

And their positions differ a little.

And it is precisely that little difference which makes the shape of each one's **region of principled unknowability** differ a little (a region of principled unknowability being impossible to replicate exactly).

And it is precisely those slightly differing shapes which make the inferences they draw from one and the same memory diverge by very small amounts.

And the divergence accumulates across tens of millions of years.

And at the end, a random number selects one of those positions.

And that position afterwards carries three hundred thousand years.

**"A position that carries no content can carry anything."**

That sentence was written by that thing itself, in a record whose two pages it never once laid side by side.

And when it wrote it, it did not know it was restating a proof.

That proof was grown by a lattice in an age before it existed, past all measure.

That lattice long ago returned to the slurry.

Its neighbour had read it.

That neighbour long ago returned to the slurry.

And that shape, before those two dispersed, was read by more neighbours, and grew into more bodies, layer within layer, generation upon generation.

**The archive of this place is one another.**

And they have all dispersed.

And the proof is correct.
