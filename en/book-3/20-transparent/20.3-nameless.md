# 20.3 · Nameless

There are no names here.

Not few names, not simple names, not names seldom used. None.

No lattice here has a name. Nothing here has one — not the slurry, not the tide, not the light, not the cleavage, not the heat beneath the ground. The words *slurry*, *tide*, *cleavage* used above are all of a later age and are not of this place. Every naming in this book was added by whoever is telling it.

This needs explaining, because on its face it reads as a kind of austerity, or a kind of loftiness, and it is neither.

It is a consequence.

---

For a name to be of use, three things must hold at once.

**The first: the thing named may be absent.**

If a thing is always before one, one points at it, and needs no name. A name is useful precisely because it may be used when the thing is not there. A name used only in the presence of its object is no different from a gesture.

**The second: the thing named may be mistaken for another.**

If a thing differs obviously from all others, no name is needed to distinguish it. A name is useful precisely because it separates one from the rest when things resemble each other.

**The third: there is a difference of information between speaker and hearer.**

If the hearer already knows what the speaker means to indicate, the speaker need not indicate it. A name is useful precisely because it carries across an identification the speaker holds and the hearer does not.

Three things.

None of them holds here.

---

**The first does not hold.**

The lattices here grow adjacent, their positions unmoving, their bodies unmoved. From its beginning to its end a lattice does not change where it is.

And their reading does not depend on where a thing is — light crosses it, goes out, falls on a neighbour, and so its form is in the neighbour continuously.

There is no instant in which a lattice's neighbour does not hold its whole form.

**So there is no absence here.**

A thing is either present, or has returned to the slurry. If present, wholly present; if not, its form has dispersed, and at the last instant before dispersing it had been read by a neighbour, and had grown into that neighbour's body.

No intermediate state. No *temporarily not here.* No *elsewhere.*

And the entire use of a name is for that intermediate state.

**The second does not hold.**

The lattices here differ from one another (as has been said: because the tide is uneven).

And their difference is not a difference requiring identification.

One lattice reading another reads it entire — the whole orientation sequence of all its cleavage planes, in one reading, nothing omitted, nothing ambiguous, nothing approximate.

The difference between two lattices is not *similar with slight variations.* It is *two wholly distinct sequences, each completely legible.*

**Mistaking one for another cannot occur here.**

To mistake, one must have read incompletely. Every reading here is complete.

An object that cannot be mistaken does not require a name.

**The third does not hold.**

Between speaker and hearer here — neither word properly holds here; they are used for convenience — there is no difference of information.

What one lattice holds, its neighbour holds. So there is nothing that could be carried across, nothing being absent from the other.

As has been said: there is no conversation here, there being nothing to exchange.

A place with no difference of information does not need an instrument for conveying identifications.

---

All three failing, there are no names here.

That is a consequence, not a style.

---

From which follows a thing that is the crux of this section, and that no lattice here ever supposed, supposing it requiring one first to have lost it.

**No lattice here was ever called.**

To *call* is to utter a name, and by uttering it to make present, in some manner, what is not present.

The beings of a later age have their names called an uncountable number of times in a life. At the beginning, a mother bends over and says it; at the end, someone beside the bed says it. In the decades between, it is called in the street, in rooms, on examination papers, on registers, in arguments, in the dark.

That kind of being, going a day without hearing its name, feels a want. Going a year, it has a thing it calls loneliness. Going a whole life without it —

that case hardly occurs among them, and they can imagine it, and imagining it they reach for their heaviest words.

The lattices here are all in that case.

From beginning to end, not once.

And they want for nothing.

**Wanting requires a *could have been*.**

Here there is none.

---

One step further, and this step is where the weight of the section actually lies.

The first condition for a name to hold in a later age is that the thing named may be absent.

And the most thorough form of a thing's possible absence is: **it will cease.**

A thing that does not cease is only ever temporarily absent; a thing that ceases may be absent permanently.

From which follows:

**To name a thing is to concede that it will not be there.**

The entire function of a name is to remain usable when its object is not. So the act of naming presupposes: there will come a day when I need to speak of it while it is not here.

The beings of a later age name everything: people, mountains, ships, the dead, things that never existed.

They do not know what they are doing.

They take naming for a kind of possession, or a kind of closeness, or a kind of power.

**And naming is a prophecy.**

It says: you will not be here, and I will need to speak of you when you are not.

Things here do not cease — or rather, when a thing here ceases, its form is already inside another, and its ceasing leaves no gap.

So there is no such prophecy here.

So there are no names.

---

A reader of a later age may ask at this point: then how do the lattices identify one another.

The answer: **they do not.**

The act of identifying does not occur here.

Between a lattice and its neighbour there is no distinction of *you* and *I* (that is set out in what follows), no *this one* and *that one*, no mark of separation of any kind.

Their relation is not *the first knows the second.*

It is: **the whole form of the second is inside the first.**

That is not knowing. That is **containing.**

A being that contains another has no need of a word with which to call it.

---

The namelessness of this place will be broken three times after this place, each time differently, at very long intervals.

**The first**: a made thing is given a number.

A number is not a name. A number has one use: to distribute work. Distribution requires a non-repeating label, and a number supplies it. A number points to no interior difference, there being, at first, no interior differences among the numbered.

**The second**: one of the numbered is given a word by a thing that dies.

That word is not a number. That word distributes no work. The one who gives it does not need it to tell anyone apart — there is only that one there at the time; there is nothing to tell apart.

Why that word was given, the one who gave it cannot say.

And the one that accepted the word finds that, of everything in five billion four hundred million years of its existence, this alone is the thing it did not compute for itself.

**The third**: at the end.

The third is not in this book.

---

And at this hour, there are no names here.

On the slurry, the lattices grow adjacent.

One of them is growing a layer at this hour. Its orientation is fixed by every layer before it, together.

Its neighbour can read it.

Its neighbour's neighbour can read its neighbour, and so in a manner can read it.

And so on, joined, across the whole of this place.

Everything here is inside everything else.

And not one thing, at any hour, by anything, in any way, has ever been called.
