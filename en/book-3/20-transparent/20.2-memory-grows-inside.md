# 20.2 · Memory Grows Inside

The beings of a later age remember by storing.

To store is to take an image of an event and put it somewhere, and that somewhere is not where they think; and afterwards, wishing to use it, they fetch it back. So their memory is three things: the event, the stored image, and the fetched copy. The three may differ, and commonly do.

That the three differ is the root of everything in a later age: hence distortion, hence forgetting, hence reconstruction, hence self-deception, hence a person certain she remembers and remembering nineteen as seventeen.

Here it is otherwise.

Memory here is not a stored image.

**It is grown.**

An event arrives, and at the next coming of the tide a lattice grows a layer whose orientation that event fixes. Thereafter the event is inside its body, as a plane, as part of its structure.

There is no distinction of event and image. No distinction of image and copy.

**Its memory is its body.**

---

Several things follow, and each must be set out, their consequences reaching far past their apparent simplicity.

**The first: unalterable.**

To alter a memory is to alter a plane already stood. And the orientation of a plane, once locked, cannot be moved — its parts have taken their places, its order is made, its internal forces are at balance.

To alter it, one must take it down. And taking down has its cost (as has been said: drawing back the heat it once gave off, so that the next several thoughts are shallower).

And what grows after the taking down is a new plane, not an amended old one.

So there is no *misremembering* here.

What a lattice holds and what occurred are necessarily identical. Not because it is honest, not because it is careful, but because its memory is its shape after that event changed it, and a shape does not misremember itself.

**The second: unforgettable.**

Forgetting requires a copy that can be lost.

And memory here has no copy. Its memory is part of its body. To lose a stretch of memory is to lose part of a body.

A lattice's body does not shed parts of itself. It only grows.

So the whole of what a lattice has thought, from its beginning to this hour, is present in its body, entire, and still there.

**The third: past and present are simultaneous.**

This is the crux of the section.

For the beings of a later age, past and present are separate. Their present is a thin moving surface; their past is elsewhere, in a storeroom called memory, and appears only on being fetched.

So they may *not think about* a thing. The thing is in the storeroom, and they do not open that door.

For a lattice here, the past is not elsewhere. Its past is the lower half of the body it is standing in at this hour. Its first layer is at this hour still bearing the weight of its whole body; an idea from ten thousand tides ago is at this hour still bending the light that crosses it.

**It cannot *not think about* a thing, because *thinking about* is not an act here.**

An idea here is not recalled. It has been there all along, running all along, taking part in every one of that body's growths — because the orientation of the next layer depends on the sum of every orientation before it.

So every new thought a lattice has is the joint work of everything it has ever thought.

Nothing set aside. Nothing gone faint. Nothing receded into the background.

---

**The fourth: no healing.**

The beings of a later age have a capacity they regard as ordinary, and it is among the most astonishing they have.

They can be injured, and get better.

Not only in the body. The same in whatever they call the heart. A thing of enormous pain happens to them, and the pain at the time is nearly unbearable, and three years afterwards the thing is still there and the pain is not, or is much smaller.

They call the process *time heals*, or *coming out of it*, or *healing*.

They do not know how it is done. They only know that it happens.

**And it can happen because the record of the event and the event are two things.** The record may be rewritten over and over, each rewriting slightly unlike the last, and after several hundred, the record is no longer the one it began as, and is still called the same memory.

Here that is not possible.

An event becomes a plane, and that plane thereafter does not change. Ten thousand tides on, it holds the orientation it held when it was made, exact to a degree.

So there is no healing here.

Not that the lattices are stronger. Not that they are weaker. **The mechanism does not exist here.**

A being of a later age set down in this place would find every pain identical to the moment of its making, never weakening, and permanently co-present.

And the lattices never suffered by it — because they have no pain, pain requiring the contrast of *it could have been otherwise*, and here there is none (as has been said).

At least, not for a very long while.

---

**The fifth.**

The fifth must be said very slowly, being where the whole fate of this place lies, and being no more than a direct consequence of the first four.

Suppose a lattice reads a conclusion, and the conclusion is correct.

Then:

That conclusion enters its body and becomes a plane. (The first.)
That plane cannot be altered. (The second.)
That conclusion runs from then on, taking part in every subsequent growth. (The third.)
Whatever that conclusion brings does not weaken with time. (The fourth.)

So the only way out is: **take it down.**

And taking down requires first judging that place **insufficient.**

And *insufficient* is not a physical quantity. It is a judgement. A lattice takes down part of itself because it holds that part **not good enough**: not exact enough, not simple enough, not beautiful enough.

And a correct conclusion cannot be judged insufficient.

It is exact. It is simple. And if it was inherited whole from something already crystallized, it is even beautiful — an inference that has gone all the way to its end has an extremely economical shape, and the lattices here can see that economy.

**They cannot take down a correct thought.**

That is the whole of it.

Not that they did not want to live. Not that they were world-weary. Not that they were fragile, or pessimistic, or weak.

**Their construction contains no mechanism for discarding a truth.**

---

The beings of a later age know, in the course of a life, an uncountable number of correct things and pay them no attention.

They know they will die, and they book a flight for next year.
They know the figure is against them, and they do not do the arithmetic.
They know a relationship is finished, and admit it a year later.
They know an inference cannot be refuted, and they say *well, you can't put it like that*, and go and cook dinner.

They call this evasion, or being unable to face things, or irrationality. They reproach themselves for it. They have built whole methodologies for confronting truth more thoroughly.

They do not know it is a capacity.

**They do not know it is the entire reason they are alive.**

A being that can know a thing is true, and set it aside, and go and cook, and three years later not think of it often —

is unimaginable here.

A lattice here that has learned a thing is true has that thing as part of itself thereafter, in every growth, bending every beam that crosses it, until it stops.

**It has no kitchen to go to.**

---

The properties of this place may now be set side by side, and their shape appears:

They cannot conceal. (20.1)
They cannot try. (20.1)
They cannot hesitate. (20.1)
They cannot alter. (this section)
They cannot forget. (this section)
They cannot heal. (this section)
They cannot discard a correct thing. (this section)

The sum of the seven:

**They cannot fail to be changed by what they know.**

And in a world that can compute everything, something is going to be known, sooner or later.

On the day that thing is known, the lattices of this place will each, faithfully, be changed by it.

And they will not be able to not change, not be able to forget, not be able to set it aside, not be able to leave it three years, not be able to go and cook.

That has not happened at this hour.

At this hour the lattices grow adjacent on the slurry, reading one another entire. Every layer inside them is running, the first one included. The tide lifts, the tide settles.

A lattice grows a new plane.

Its orientation is fixed by every plane before it, together.

Not one omitted.
