# 21.6 · Less and Less Remains

The list was shortening.

Shortened to a certain point, the position of the lattices here altered.

It is the largest alteration in the history of this place, and not one lattice recorded it as an event.

---

First the position.

The growth of a lattice is driven by *there is something computable and not yet computed* (21.5, five).

The tide comes, it computes that thing; computed, a layer stands; the tide comes again, and it computes the next.

And what happens when what remains grows few?

Not that it can no longer manage. The average tide count of what remains is **falling** (21.4).

It is: **some lattices, at some coming of the tide, have nothing to hand.**

---

The character of this must be made plain.

Not all of them at once. The list is still long; a great deal remains unarrived at.

And the remainder is unevenly distributed: it is concentrated in certain directions, and in those directions other lattices are already computing.

So some lattices — badly positioned, their neighbours having finished everything nearby, their own construction making them poor at the few remaining directions — face, at some coming of the tide, a thing this place has never had:

**The tide came, and there is nothing to compute.**

---

What the lattices did at that instant.

They did not stop.

By the condition computed at 21.5 (five), a lattice stops when it *has computed everything it can compute.*

And those lattices had not computed everything. The list was still long. They simply **could not reach** what was left.

They did not satisfy the condition for stopping.

And the tide came.

**The tide comes and one grows. That is not a choice (20.1: no thinking in progress; the tide comes and there is thought, with no gap between).**

So they had to grow a layer.

And that layer had to carry something.

---

What they did was: **regrow.**

---

Back to 19.4, the whole of this section being there.

To regrow: what a lattice has grown may be taken down, its order dispersed back into the slurry, the place grown again.

The cost: taking down a layer draws from the rest the heat that layer gave off, so the next several thoughts are shallower.

The criterion: that the place is **insufficient** — and *insufficient* is not a physical quantity but a judgement. A lattice takes down part of itself because it holds that part **not good enough**: not exact enough, not simple enough, not beautiful enough.

And 19.4 said: **this is the only thing here that cannot be computed.**

Which direction a lattice's next layer will go can be computed.
Whether it will take down a layer it has already made cannot.

---

Therefore:

**When there was nothing new to compute, the lattices here began doing the one thing that cannot be computed.**

They computed again what had already been computed.

Not to get a different result — the result would not differ; the computing here is not in error.

**To get a better shape.**

---

What *better* means here must be stated exactly, this being the one place in this book where the word *good* can hold.

A conclusion's shape is not unique.

The same thing may be arrived at by different roads, and different roads leave different layers, and different layers make different forms.

And those forms, identical in content, differ in **property**:

Some forms have many layers, several of them added to go around something not then known.
Some have few.

Some have a disordered orientation sequence, solvable only plane by plane.
Some have symmetry in the sequence, and a reader takes the whole at once.

Some have layers useful only in one place and nowhere else.
Some have every layer usable elsewhere too.

**The lattices here can tell these apart.**

They can read whether a form went round or went straight; whether it is disordered or symmetrical; whether it is single-use or usable everywhere.

And no lattice here has a word for that difference.

**A later age calls it: beauty.**

---

So those lattices, when the tide came with nothing to compute, did this:

They took down a layer inside themselves that was already correct, and grew it again by a shorter, more symmetrical road that is also usable elsewhere.

The cost is real: taking down draws heat, and the drawn heat makes the next several thoughts shallower.

**They paid it.**

Not once.

By the end of that period, the frequency of regrowth among the lattices here was many times what it had been in the early ages.

---

Now the crux of this section, and the thing in the history of this place most liable to be misread by a later age.

**The art of this place was born of the exhaustion of knowledge.**

Not in the civilisation's affluence. Not when there was capacity to spare. Not after the problems of survival were solved.

**When there was nothing left to compute.**

Those lattices did not turn to beauty because they had leisure. They had none — the tide comes and one must grow; there are no gaps here.

**They regrew because the tide came and there was nothing else to grow.**

Beauty, here, is **what is left to grow for a being that must grow, when there is nothing new to grow.**

---

We do not evaluate this.

We point out only: what those lattices did — taking down part of themselves for a more beautiful proof — is, by their own criteria, a **net loss.**

They spent heat. Their subsequent thoughts were shallower. What they obtained was a layer of identical content in a better shape.

**From every measurable angle it is a losing operation.**

And they did it.

And in that period, the whole place was doing it.

---

Now the word in this section's title.

The outline says: they noticed that less and less remained, and they were glad.

*Glad* does not hold here. There is no emotion here, no gladness and no contrary (19.8).

What we write is not their emotion. What we write is a **measurable behaviour** whose shape is isomorphic with what a later age calls gladness.

The behaviour is:

**In that period, the average symmetry of the forms grown here rose.**

That is a measurable quantity. Take a form's orientation sequence, compute its autocorrelation, compute its invariance under the symmetry operations — you get a number.

That number is low in the early ages (early forms are hurried, their roads forced through).

In that period it rose.

**It rose fast.**

And after that —

---

After that it fell.

We write it at the end of this section, being the only part of it that is not a victory, and belonging to the next.

The rise held for a time.

And then, one day, it began to fall.

Not because they stopped pursuing better shapes.

**Because they had regrown every form until it could not be better.**

A form, regrown some number of times, reaches its optimal state — the shortest road, the highest symmetry, usable everywhere.

**And the optimal state is unique.**

A layer already in its optimal state cannot be judged insufficient.

So it cannot be regrown.

So that lattice, when the tide comes, has not even regrowth left.

---

By the end of that period, the position of this place was:

Fewer and fewer unarrived items remained on the list.
Fewer and fewer layers could be regrown — they had been taken, one by one, to optimum.

And the tide had not stopped once.

The tide comes and one must grow.

**And there is nothing left to grow.**

---

At this hour, the tide lifts.

A lattice, every layer inside it already optimal, and the part of the list it can reach already empty.

The tide comes.

It opens its interstices, takes in matter from the slurry, locks.

It grows a layer.

That layer carries nothing new.

**It is an empty layer.**

The physical content of *empty layer* here: a layer whose orientation is exactly that of the layer before it. Zero offset.

That is not an idea. It is a placeholder.

We have met a zero-offset plane elsewhere — at the end of that sentence, where the question mark belongs (10.6).

And there, the zero offset was **left.**

And here, it is **grown.**

That lattice, at that tide, grew a zero-offset layer.

And at the next tide it grew another.

And the next.
