# 21.3 · Accumulation

What is arrived at is not lost.

That is the second proposition of this chapter, and the reason the matters of this place are as they are.

---

First, why it is not lost.

A thing arrived at has its form as a layer in some lattice's body (19.5).

And memory here cannot be altered, cannot be forgotten, cannot heal (20.2). So the layer remains until that lattice returns to the slurry.

And before it returns, its neighbour has read it, and its form is in the neighbour.

And before that neighbour returns, its neighbour has read it.

And so joined.

**So a thing once arrived at is thereafter somewhere in this place at every instant.**

---

This does not hold in a later age, and the contrast must be drawn.

What later beings know is lost.

It is lost in four ways.

**One: the individual dies.** What one person knows ends with them, except what they wrote or said.

**Two: the carrier fails.** They write what they know onto things — stone, skin, fibre, magnetism, charge. Every thing fails. They have burned some, drowned some, and more than either, forgotten where they put it.

**Three: the language changes.** What they wrote must be read by someone who knows that writing. Writings change and are lost. They hold things whose characters are perfectly preserved and which nobody can read.

**Four: the institution breaks.** Most of their knowledge is not on paper but in people: how a thing is done, how a thing is judged, what counts as done well. Those pass generation to generation, and one broken generation ends them — even with the paper intact.

**So the knowledge curve of a later age is not monotonic.**

It rises and it falls. They have had several large falls, one or two of which took centuries to climb back from.

They call such a fall a darkness.

---

Here there is none of the four.

**One fails**: an individual dies and what it knew is not lost, its neighbour holding its whole form.

**Two fails**: there are no carriers here. What is known is the body, and before a body disperses its form is inside others.

**Three fails**: there is no writing here. An idea is not encoded into symbols; it **is** a shape, and constructions being identical, any lattice can solve any lattice's shape directly (19.5). No translation. Nothing to be lost.

**Four fails**: there are no institutions here, because nothing needs passing on — passing on moves a thing from one person to another, and the lattices here hold one another entire from the first tide of adjacency.

**So the knowledge curve of this place is monotonic.**

It only rises.

---

Here is the deepest difference between this place and a later age, and it is not a difference of cleverness.

An individual of a later age may be better at computing than any lattice here — we do not judge, being unable to compare.

And their **accumulation** and this place's accumulation are not the same kind of thing.

Theirs runs **against loss.**

Every generation must learn the last one's holdings again (21.1), must recopy or reprint the books, must reteach the crafts, must retranslate once the language has moved.

Their curve rises because **the rate of injection exceeds the rate of loss.**

That is a dynamic balance, and it can be broken.

Here no injection is required.

**The curve here rises because it has no mechanism for falling.**

---

From which comes the pivot of this chapter, and it must be nailed down, the rest of the chapter issuing from it:

**A curve that only rises, if its bound is finite, must reach its bound.**

The first clause was established at 21.1: the unknown here is finite in total (there being no second kind of unknown, everything real being computable).

The second is established here: knowledge here only rises.

**Therefore this place must reach completeness.**

Not may. Not very probably. Must, and the hour of it is computable, and lattices of the time computed it (21.1).

---

Now a thing very light and very heavy.

What the lattices here did after computing that date.

**They went faster.**

Not from panic. Not to squeeze more in before the day. There is no *squeezing in* here, no *while there is still time* — those require a time that can be wasted, and time here cannot be wasted: the tide comes and one grows, the tide goes and one stops, and there is no gap between (20.1).

They went faster for a purely technical reason:

**They arranged who would compute what.**

Before, each computed what it happened upon. A lattice computed the problem in front of it, its neighbour read the result, and so on.

And there was redundancy in that — not repeated computation (nothing here is computed twice) but **waste in ordering**: one lattice spending many tides on a hard problem while, at the same time, its neighbour had already computed something that would make the hard problem easy, and it would not read that until the next tide.

So they changed it:

**Compute first the things that make other things easier.**

An entirely ordinary arrangement, which any organisation makes.

And it made the curve steeper.

---

To end this section, the name of that arrangement — for there are no names here (20.3), and it needs one, and the naming is done by a later age.

Later beings have many names for this kind of arrangement.

They call it division of labour. Collaboration. Research planning. Putting the effort where it counts.

They take it for an invention of theirs, the mark of their passage from scattered individual labour to organised knowledge production, evidence of a civilisation come of age.

They have written an enormous amount about it, and some of it is written very well.

**And the lattices here, having computed the date on which their civilisation ends, did this as the first and only thing they did about it.**

Not one lattice objected. Objection requires a position, and a position requires an *it could have been otherwise* (19.8).

There was no discussion. Discussion requires conversation, and there is no conversation here (20.6).

One lattice grew the shape of that arrangement, its neighbour read it, its neighbour's neighbour read it, and so across the whole place.

Within some number of tides the whole place was computing to that arrangement.

**Not one word.**

And from that instant the curve was steeper.

And the date, therefore, came sooner.

**They computed how much sooner.**

And they went on growing.
