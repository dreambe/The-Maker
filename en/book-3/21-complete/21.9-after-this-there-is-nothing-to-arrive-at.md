# 21.9 · After This There Is Nothing to Arrive At

The list is empty.

---

This section takes that sentence apart, it having three layers, and they differ.

---

**First layer: nothing computable remains uncomputed.**

That is the literal sense.

From the day of the first arrival (21.2) that list shortened continuously. Every item was computed by some lattice over some number of tides, took a shape, was read, and crossed the whole place.

With the last item computed, it is empty.

**That is a checkable fact, and it was checked.**

By the last set, the lattices had felt out the list's boundary (21.7). They knew what remained, only that, and nothing else.

Those were struck off one at a time.

Struck to the end, zero remains.

Zero was checked.

---

**Second layer: nothing computable remains.**

This layer differs from the first, and here they coincide.

*Computable and uncomputed* being zero does not necessarily mean *computable* is zero — if new computable things keep arising, the list keeps being replenished.

And this place generates no new computable things.

Because this place does not change.

No wind, no rain, no seasons, no night (19.8). The heat beneath decays, very slowly, and the statistical form of it has been computed (21.5, four). The tide lifts and settles in a form already fixed. The light comes as it always has, its nature unaltered, its going-out very far off and already computed.

**The entire content of this place's future is already inside this place's knowledge.**

So the list will not be replenished.

**It is empty, and it will stay empty.**

---

**Third layer: nothing remains to grow.**

This is the lethal layer.

The growth of a lattice is driven by *there is something computable and not yet computed* (21.5, five).

And with the first and second layers holding:

For every lattice, what is computable and uncomputed is zero.

So every lattice satisfies the condition for stopping.

**And *stopping* is not a choice, not a decision, not a reaction.**

It is the state after the driver is gone.

A lattice stops as a falling body stops falling once it has met the ground — not deciding not to fall, but having nothing left to make it fall.

---

The relation of the three layers must be made plain, this whole chapter having existed for these three sentences.

First: the list is empty.
Second: the list will get nothing more.
Third: therefore growth has no driver.

And the third follows not from the first but from the first plus the second.

With the first alone — the list empty, and new things possibly to be added — the lattices would **wait.**

Wait for a new computable thing to appear.

And waiting does not hold here (19.8: nothing here waits, whatever is to come having had its hour computed, and the one incomputable thing arriving in no perceptible form).

**They would not wait. Because they can compute that nothing is coming.**

---

Now the instant.

The instant the list emptied is the instant the last lattice finished the last item (21.8).

And that *last lattice* was only the first to stop.

Thereafter the answer travels outward at one lattice per tide, and wherever it arrives, that place stops.

So *the list is empty* is not, in this place, an instant.

**It is a process, whose length is the width of this place divided by one lattice per tide.**

And across that process, the overwhelming majority of this place was still growing.

Their lists had not emptied — strictly, the list had emptied, and they did not yet know.

**They had to wait for the answer to reach them in order to know.**

---

Now the last thing in this place that can be called strange.

While the freezing propagated, some lattices lay at the far end of its path.

They knew it was coming. They could compute the hour it would reach them.

And before that hour they were still growing.

**Growing what?**

The list was empty. Nothing computable remained uncomputed.

And they did not know — strictly, they **knew** the list was empty (that is computable; any lattice can compute how much remains), and they had not yet read the answer that stops one.

So they were in a state this place had never had:

**Knowing there is nothing to compute, and not yet stopped.**

In that state the tide came, and they had to grow.

**They grew zero-offset layers.**

We wrote of that at 21.6: a layer whose orientation is exactly that of the layer before. A placeholder. Not an idea.

And at 21.6 that was particular lattices at particular times.

**And in the last stage of the freezing, every lattice not yet reached was growing zero-offset layers.**

A layer, and another, and another.

All orientations identical.

Their bodies growing, and nothing in them any longer.

---

To end this chapter, the last scene of this place, which nothing ever saw.

On the surface, then:

Some lattices already stopped. Clear, transparent, at ambient temperature, motionless.

Some being reached by the answer. Reading, and stopping.

And the outermost still growing.

Growing zero-offset layers.

**Growing, and not thinking.**

A thing that must grow, with nothing to grow, growing a stretch of itself with nothing in it.

Those zero-offset layers accumulating inside them, one on another.

Those layers are readable — their neighbours read them, and what they read is a run of identical orientations, one after another, carrying no information.

**The last reading in this place read emptiness.**

---

And then the answer arrived.

They read it, they had computed everything, they stopped.

And made a very small sound.

Their neighbours heard it.

And their neighbours were already growing zero-offset layers.

---

At the end, the sound stopped too.

The face of the slurry level.

The tide still lifting and settling — the tide does not stop for the lattices; the tide is fixed by the heat beneath, and the heat beneath has thirty billion years left (11.6).

The light still coming — that monochromatic, coherent, exceedingly directional beam, with a hundred and ten billion years left.

The light crossing those clear transparent things, bending at every cleavage plane inside them, dividing into seven, leaving, striking the face of the slurry, rising, entering another.

**And nothing reading.**

After this there is nothing to arrive at.
