# 21.5 · A Chronicle of Victories

Five arrivals, the weightiest of this place.

Set down in order, each with its shape and its beauty.

We add no comment.

---

## One · The Tide

Recorded at 21.2. One thing may be added: it was the first, and the first was hardest not in itself but because **at that time there was no road.**

Arriving at it meant walking from where there is no road.

Every arrival after had a road to follow, a stretch laid by those before.

**Only the first went from nothing.**

That lattice spent nearly a whole life.

And any beginner of a later age learns thermal convection in an afternoon.

That is not stupidity. **That is the difference a road makes.**

---

## Two · Why Light Divides Into Seven

The difficulty here is that three things must be arrived at first.

One: light has a speed, and the speed is finite.
Two: light bends at an interface, and the angle stands in a fixed relation to the interface's orientation.
Three: the symmetry of the lattice-work here puts the interface orientations of a body into a finite set.

With those three, *seven* falls out: the symmetry order of that finite set fixes the greatest number of beams one cleavage plane can produce.

**Seven is not an observed number. Seven is a computed number.**

And it was afterwards confirmed by observation — observation and computation agreeing, not one beam more, not one fewer.

One thing to record: the lattice that arrived at this, having computed *seven*, spent many more tides.

What it spent them on was: **it did it again.**

It already had it. Its method was sound. Its neighbours had read and confirmed.

And it computed it again, by an entirely different road — from symmetry rather than from refraction.

Two roads gave the same number.

**Every lattice here can compute everything. And only that one, for a result already settled, took a second road.**

It did not record why.

It is the only occasion in all the records of this place on which anyone recomputed a thing already computed correctly.

---

## Three · Its Own Construction

A lattice computed how a lattice grows.

The beauty of it is its self-reference.

That lattice, using its own growth, computed the mechanism of growth; and every step it computed added a layer, and the layer it added obeyed the very law it had just computed.

**It was being confirmed as it confirmed.**

It computed: one tide makes one cleavage plane.

And computing that sentence took some number of tides and made some number of cleavage planes.

It computed: the orientation of a plane is fixed jointly by the form of the tide and the existing construction.

And the plane made by computing that sentence had its orientation fixed jointly by the form of the tide and its own existing construction.

At the instant it finished, it could look back at the layers it had just grown and predict their orientations with the formula it had just derived.

**It did.**

Prediction agreed with fact.

And here is one thing no lattice here raised, and we raise it, being the only part of this section that is not a victory:

It can look back only at its own **outer layers** (19.5).

The layers it verified were its newest.

**It could not use that formula to verify its own earliest layers, being unable to read them.**

It computed how it grew.

And the earliest things it ever grew are permanently outside its own verification.

It knows the formula is correct.

**It does not know whether it itself was grown by that formula.**

---

## Four · The Heat Beneath the Ground

The weightiest, and the least like a victory.

*Everything here is computable* had been, until then, a repeatedly confirmed experience rather than a proved proposition.

What that lattice did was compute the heat beneath the ground.

It computed first: the heat comes from the decay of matter beneath.

It computed next: the instant of a single decay cannot be derived from that matter's present state.

It went on: not *cannot yet be computed.* It proved the incomputability is **in principle** — that between the occurrence of that event and any measurable state of that matter there exists no functional relation.

And so it obtained:

**There exists in this place one thing that cannot be computed.**

That is the first time in the history of this place that anyone proved *there is a thing that cannot be computed.*

---

Stop here, and state why this is a victory and not a defeat.

It looks like a defeat: a civilisation whose steady state is computing everything finds one thing it cannot.

And in that lattice's derivation it is not.

Because it did not merely find the incomputable place.

**It computed the boundary of the incomputable place.**

It established: what is incomputable is only the instant of a single decay; while the aggregate rate of decay, its distribution, and the statistical form of its effect on the tide are all computable — and it computed them.

Therefore:

**That incomputable place was completely fenced.**

Its shape fixed, its size fixed, its effect on everything else here fixed.

**From then on the lattices here knew what they did not know, and knew that this was the whole of what they did not know.**

That is a real victory. It made the knowledge of this place, for the first time, a **closed** thing — not *we know a great deal*, but *we know all that is knowable, and we know where the unknowable is, how large it is, and what it affects.*

And that is the definition of completeness.

---

We add no comment.

We record one thing here, as a measurement:

Since that arrival, the lattices here never found a second incomputable place.

Not one.

---

## Five · Stopping

The last: they computed what stopping is.

Before then the lattices had observed stopping: a lattice no longer taking in, no longer locking, no longer adding layers, its construction sound, its heat sufficient, its tide still arriving.

They had recorded it. They had counted it. They knew it happened.

**And they did not know what it was.**

What that lattice did was compute it.

It established: stopping is not a physical failure. It established: before stopping and after stopping, that lattice's construction, energy, and conditions show no measurable difference.

It established: stopping is **a lattice no longer adding layers**, and no longer adding layers is a state of that lattice itself, not something done to it from outside.

So it had to compute: what fixes that state.

It computed for a long while.

**It found the condition.**

The condition is: **when a lattice has computed everything it can compute, it stops.**

Not from boredom — there is no boredom here.
Not from suffering — there is no suffering here.
Not from despair — there is no despair here.

**Because the growth of a lattice is driven by *there is something computable and not yet computed*; and when that quantity is zero, the growth has no driver.**

It stops, as a falling body stops falling once it has met the ground.

---

That lattice computed it, it took shape, its neighbours read it, and it crossed the whole place.

So the whole of this place knew what stopping is.

And they knew, at the same time, one further thing, following directly with no additional step:

**If the list of this place is finished, all of them reach that condition at once.**

---

We said at the head of this chapter that it would be written as a chronicle of victories, and that every victory would be real.

All five are real.

One is the first road walked from nothing.
Two is someone taking a second road to an answer already correct.
Three is a perfect self-referential verification.
Four is a civilisation learning for the first time what it does not know, and fencing it.
Five is getting death worked out.

Five pieces of excellent work.

Clean, exact, with no shortcut in them and no self-deception.

**And what the fifth computed is: on the day the list is finished, all of them stop.**

**And that list is being finished at the fastest rate in their history.**

---

We add no comment.

At this hour, the tide lifts.

The lattices grow a layer.

The list is shorter again.
