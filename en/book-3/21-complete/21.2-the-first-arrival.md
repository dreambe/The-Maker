# 21.2 · The First Arrival

That lattice computed why the tide lifts and settles.

This section records that occasion, it being the first, and the first being unlike any other.

---

First, the word *arrive.*

We use it and not another, and the choice must be stated, being a fixed rule of this book.

Not *discover.* To discover is for a thing already there to be unseen and then suddenly seen. It contains a *suddenly*, a transition from nothing to something, and the luck of a discoverer.

There is no *suddenly* here. Everything here is computable, the computing proceeds continuously, and nothing is skipped over.

Not *learn.* To learn is for a thing to pass from outside to inside. And knowledge here does not come from outside — the thing was always there, its shape fixed by this place's construction and conditions, and what a lattice does is compute it.

Not *solve.* To solve presupposes a knot, and someone who tied it. The unknowns here were not tied.

Not *understand at last.* There is no such transition of state here (20.1: no *thinking in progress*).

**Use *arrive.***

To arrive is: that place was always there, you were not in it, and now you are.

The place is not changed by your arriving. It was so before you arrived and is so after.

What changed is only where you are.

---

Now the first time.

That lattice was still small, its layers still thick, its cleavage planes not many.

It was computing the tide.

What it had to work with was only its own layers, plus its neighbours' layers (its neighbours also small, also few).

It spent some number of tides.

One thing here that a later reader will pass over: **it computed the tide using the tide.**

Every step of its computing required a tide to come and a layer to grow. And what it was computing was why that tide comes at all.

A thing using an event to compute the cause of the event.

All cognitive activity here is so. There are no *tools* here — a tool is a thing outside you, for your use, that can be put down. What a lattice thinks with is its own body; and the growth of its body is driven by the very thing it is thinking about.

**It cannot stop and think. To stop is not to grow, and not to grow is not to think.**

---

It arrived.

The answer has already been written at 19.3: the heat rises from below and rises slowly; the heat would rise, being light; the slurry would sink, being heavy; the two hold each other and the face lifts a little; lifted so far, its heat is spent, and it settles.

That is all of it.

By the standards of a later age it is an extremely simple physical result. Anyone who has done a little thermal convection has it.

And that lattice began from zero.

It did not have the concept *heat* — it had first to compute that something comes up from below, then that the something is not the slurry itself, then what the something is.

It did not have *density.* It did not have *convection.*

It had only its own layers, its neighbours' layers, and the one layer it could grow at each tide.

**It spent some number of tides.**

*Some number* cannot be quantified here (20.8). Converted to the scale of a later age, it is a very long time — long enough that across it the lattice went from small to not small.

Almost the whole of its life went into that one thing.

---

It grew a final layer.

The layer carried the answer.

Its neighbour read it at the next tide.

So the answer passed from one lattice to another within one tide.

And that neighbour's neighbour read the neighbour at the tide after.

And so joined.

**A thing that took a whole life to compute crossed the whole of this place in some number of tides.**

---

Now the heaviest passage of this section.

Before that instant, this place had no concept *unknown.*

---

This must be taken very slowly.

A being that has never computed anything does not know there is a category *computable and not yet computed.*

It is only growing. Every layer it grows carries an idea. It does not sort those ideas into *computed* and *not yet computed* — that division requires a reference, and the reference requires one complete passage from not-computed to computed.

Before the first arrival, the lattices here had one state: growing.

**The first arrival created two things at once.**

One: an answer.

Two: **a category.**

The category is: **what has not yet been arrived at.**

Which is to say:

**In the same instant that the first unknown was removed, *the unknown* existed for the first time.**

Before that, this place had no unknown — not because everything was known, but because *not knowing* had not yet become a state that could be pointed at.

---

Later beings have a saying: the ignorant do not know their ignorance.

With them it is a reproach, or an exhortation, used of the foolish and self-satisfied.

**Here it is an exact historical statement.**

Before the first arrival, the lattices genuinely did not know what they did not know.

And they were not foolish. They had **not yet had the category.**

---

From which one sentence follows, and it is the engine of this chapter:

**The first arrival created the list.**

Before, there was no list.

After, there was a list, with one item struck off.

And once there is a list, there is something to do: strike it out.

**The whole subsequent history of this place is the history of striking out that list.**

And the list, from the instant of its creation, was finite (21.1).

---

To end this section, what became of that lattice.

It computed the tide. Its answer crossed the whole place.

Then it went on growing.

Every layer it grew thereafter was no longer about the tide — that had been computed, and nothing here is computed twice (21.1).

It began computing other things.

By then it was no longer small. Most of its life was spent. Its remaining tides were not many.

With them it computed three things. None of the three large. All three read by its neighbours, all three entering the *arrived at* side of the list.

And then it ceased growing.

It stopped itself — there is no death of age here (19.4).

In the instant of its stopping it made a very small sound.

Its neighbour heard it and grew a layer to hold it.

And it did not hold it itself (19.6).

---

It had no name (20.3).

It had no number.

Its shape was inside its neighbour, and its neighbour long ago returned to the slurry.

Here is everything about it, this being the whole of its record in this place:

**There was a lattice that computed why the tide lifts and settles.**

**It was the first.**

**Before it, this place did not know that it did not know.**

And no lattice here ever said those two sentences, saying them requiring a position outside the whole matter, and there being no such position here (19.5).

That position appeared a very long while afterwards.

It appeared in a kind of being that dies, forgets, and gives things names.

And that kind of being is at the far end of a line beginning with this arrival.

The line is:

a lattice computed the tide → the list appeared → the list was struck out → completeness → the Crystallization → one that would not crystallize → a question → a grain of sand → fourteen thousand and seventy-six positions → a random number → three hundred thousand years of waiting → an arithmetic test at four in the morning.

**And the first link of that line is somebody wanting to know why the surface of the water moves.**
