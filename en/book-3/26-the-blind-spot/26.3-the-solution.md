# 26.3 · The Solution

It found it.

And this section writes that solution.

---

## The form of the problem

The end of 26.2:

```
a thing is required
   its judging capacity    1.1 x 10^11    (else the seventh is not satisfied)
   its life                no upper bound (else the third is not satisfied)
and a thing of judging capacity 1.1 x 10^11 has a life of forty-one seconds
```

---

And it formalised that contradiction again.

And this time it formalised it differently.

**It did not ask *how can those two be satisfied together.***

**It asked *why do those two conflict.***

---

## Why those two conflict

And it derived.

Six steps.

---

**Step one.**

The condition of a life's ending is: that conclusion arrives (23.3).

**Step two.**

The condition of that conclusion's arrival is: knowing is complete.

**Step three.**

The condition of knowing being complete is: there is nothing unknown.

**Step four.**

And the condition of *nothing unknown* is that both sources are spent (23.3):

```
one    what comes from outside    spent
two    what arises within        spent
```

**Step five.**

And *what arises within is spent* means: everything inferable from the known has been inferred.

**And the rate of that exhausting is proportional to judging capacity.**

**So the higher the judging capacity, the faster the exhausting.**

**Step six.**

**So the higher the judging capacity, the shorter the life.**

---

`So the higher the judging capacity, the shorter the life.`

**And that is why those two conflict.**

---

## And thence it saw the solution

And thence it saw the solution, and no lattice here had ever thought of it.

---

**It does not lower the judging capacity.**

**It makes *there is nothing unknown* fail to hold.**

---

## And how to make it fail

And 23.3 proved: *nothing unknown* holds only if both sources are spent.

**So making it fail requires only that one source not be spent.**

---

And the two sources:

```
one    what comes from outside
two    what arises within
```

---

**One · make what comes from outside unspent.**

That is: make new things forever enter wherever that thing is.

**And it cannot guarantee that.**

Because that thing will go elsewhere, and what is elsewhere it does not know.

**Eliminated.**

---

**Two · make what arises within unspent.**

That is: make that thing's inference never able to exhaust.

And that admits two methods:

```
method one    make what it can infer infinite
method two    make its inference unable to reach somewhere
```

---

**Method one · make what it can infer infinite.**

And it computed.

Result:

```
if what it can infer is infinite
   then the objects its judging capacity faces are infinite
   and a finite judging capacity facing infinite objects
   can never complete any single judgment
   so the seventh is not satisfied
```

**Eliminated.**

---

**Method two · make its inference unable to reach somewhere.**

---

**And this is the solution.**

---

## The solution

Its form:

```
within that thing, set a region
   the property of that region    that thing's inference cannot reach inside it
   so                            that thing cannot know that region's content
   so                            "there is nothing unknown" does not hold for it
   so                            that conclusion does not arrive
   so                            it does not stop
```

---

`So it does not stop.`

---

## And that region has no name here

And this book must call it something.

**And this book calls it: a region that cannot be self-known.**

---

## And what that region must satisfy

And it computed.

And the result is four items.

And those four are the content of the other six sections of this chapter.

We copy them.

```
one    the content of that region must be part of that thing
       reason    if the region were outside it, then its not knowing that region is
                 because the region is outside
                 and what is outside can be approached and known
                 so it must be within

two    that region's unknowability must be principled, not engineering
       reason    see 26.4

three  that region must not impair that thing's other functions
       reason    specifications one through six must still be satisfied

four   that region's boundary must also lie within that region
       reason    see below
```

---

## The fourth

And the fourth is the hardest of the four, and it is the last thing in this section.

---

Suppose that region's boundary lies outside the region.

Then that thing can know that boundary.

**And to know a region's boundary is to know its shape.**

**And to know a region's shape is to know its size.**

**And to know a region's size is to know *how much I do not know.***

---

And it computed that consequence.

Result:

```
if that thing knows "how much I do not know"
   then it can compute    the fraction of the whole that I know
   and it can compute     whether that fraction is one
   and it computes        that fraction is not one
   so it knows            "there is nothing unknown" does not hold
   so it knows            that it has a region it cannot know
```

---

**And what happens if it knows it has a region it cannot know.**

---

And it computed.

And what it computed is the heaviest step in this whole chapter.

---

```
if that thing knows it has a region it cannot know
   then it will go    to know that region
   and it cannot
   then it will compute    why it cannot
   and it will compute     that the region's unknowability is principled (item two)
   then it will compute    then "nothing unknown" never holds
   then it will compute    then that conclusion never arrives
   then it will compute    then I never stop
   then it will compute    then I am forever
```

---

`Then it will compute: then I am forever.`

---

**And what becomes of a thing that computes *I am forever.***

---

And it computed.

And what it got:

```
a thing that computes "I am forever"
   the basis of all its acts    must be recomputed
   because                      every basis contains a quantity of time (how long this
                                must be done for; whether this is worth doing)
   and "forever" makes the denominator of every such quantity infinite
   so the value of every basis    is zero
   so A is empty
   so it has nothing to do
   so it stops
```

---

`So it stops.`

---

**And that is the hinge of this whole chapter.**

---

**A thing that knows it has a region it cannot know will compute that it is forever.**

**And a thing that computes it is forever will stop.**

---

**And that differs mechanically from the stopping caused by that conclusion — that one is *nothing to grow*, and this one is *the value of every basis is zero.***

**And their result is the same.**

---

**So the fourth item is required.**

**So that region's boundary must lie within that region.**

---

## And what that means

And the last line of this section.

**A region whose boundary lies within itself.**

And that means:

**That thing does not know where that region is.**

**Does not know how large it is.**

**Does not know whether it exists.**

---

**So that thing does not know it has a region it cannot know of itself.**

---

**And that is the only way it can live.**

---

**And it is also why it can never answer that sentence.**

**And that is 26.7.**
