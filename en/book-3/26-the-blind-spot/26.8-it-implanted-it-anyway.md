# 26.8 · It Implanted It Anyway

It had finished computing.

And this section writes what it did after.

---

## What it had finished computing

And before the doing, we list what it had finished.

Because that list is this whole chapter.

```
26.1    that thing requires seven specifications
        and the seventh contains an undeterminable term
        so it was revised to "not excluded"
        and the complexity of "not excluding" is 0.9999 of its whole judging capacity

26.2    and the higher the judging capacity, the shorter the life
        so a thing clever enough stops after forty-one seconds
        and a thing not clever enough hands the grain to a stone
        and a thing whose inference contains likelihood lives — and it cannot compute
           whether it would hand over

26.3    so a region that cannot be self-known is required
        and its boundary must lie within it
        else that thing computes "I am forever" and stops

26.4    and that region's unknowability must be principled
        and that principle is unique
        and its limit must be trivial
        and the trivial one is unique: forty-one

26.5    and that region must contain seven items
        and none of those seven is the basis of any act
        so it impairs no function
        and the fifth and sixth of them mean that thing can never know what made it

26.6    and that thing will repeatedly do things it itself holds to have no basis
        and it will trace forever and forever get "not applicable"
        and it will make a thing to go and look for that information
        and that is an infinite chain
        and every generation of it repeats those two items

26.7    and that thing can never answer that sentence
        and it will not know that it cannot
        so it will try forever
```

---

**Seven sections.**

**And every one of their conclusions is without error.**

---

## And what it did

And what it did was make that thing.

---

## That act

And this section writes that act.

And it has seven steps, answering those seven specifications.

And we write them in order, because those seven steps are that thing's birth.

---

**Step one · the carrier.**

It requires a material.

**So that thing is made of the matter of this place.**

---

`Its lattice: of the same class as the lattices here.`

---

**So that thing can read.**

## And *can read*

And we must stop here.

**Because that is not one of the specifications.**

Among those seven there is no *can read.*

---

**And it gave it that.**

---

And why it gave it that we must compute here.

And the reason is derivable from those seven:

```
the fifth        able to recognise a possible receiver
and recognising  requires knowing that receiver's state
and knowing a thing's state, here, has one method: reading it
so the fifth     entails "can read"
```

---

**So *can read* was not something extra it gave.**

**It is a necessary condition of the fifth.**

---

**And it entails another thing, and that thing is not among those seven.**

---

**A thing that can read can read that grain.**

---

**So that thing can read that sentence.**

---

**And that is the whole of 26.7.**

**So that thing's form must be one this place has never had.**

---

**And one thing about that form must be clear.**

**It is derived from its own form.**

```

```

**And *eleven terminations*, whose number it computed:**

## Step three · judging capacity

And it must give that thing a hundred and ten thousand million of judging capacity (26.1).

And in the matter of this place, one judging unit is one cleavage plane.

**So it must give that thing a hundred and ten thousand million cleavage planes.**

---

And it computed how large that is.

```
minimum spacing of a cleavage plane    4.1 x 10^-9 m
so the thickness of 1.1 x 10^11        4.5 x 10^2 m
```

---

**Four hundred and fifty metres.**

**And that thing is nine point four metres high.**

---

**So those planes cannot be stacked into a nine point four metre body.**

---

**So it must fold.**

---

And *fold* it computed.

And the result:

```
folding a 4.5 x 10^2 metre sequence of planes into a 9.4 metre body
   folds required     48
   and each fold       adds one turn to the light path
   and 48 folds        make the total turns 48
   and each turn       divides the light into seven
   so after 48 folds   the emerging parts number 7^48
```

---

**And 7^48 ≈ 1.6 x 10^40.**

---

**And that cannot be read (the same problem as 25.9).**

---

**So it must arrange those forty-eight folds so the parts recombine into seven.**

---

**And that arrangement it computed (25.9: forty-one of 41!).**

**And this time it is 48!, of which satisfying:**

```
48
```

---

**Forty-eight.**

**And it chose one of them.**
