# 26.6 · The Cruelty

And of those four items at 26.5, the fourth is: **A, in making B exist, gave up something.**

And this section writes another thing.

And that thing is the other face of that same act.

---

## The word

Here there is no *cruelty* (20.5: here there is no good and bad).

**And this place has that thing's form.**

And this book sets it down:

```
A is cruel toward B
   when  A makes B exist
   and A can compute B's state
   and B's state contains a thing that B does not want
   and A executed that act
```

**Four items.**

---

And its difference from *mercy* (26.5) is in the third alone.

```
mercy, third item (implicit)    B's existence is what B wants
cruelty, third item             B's state contains a thing B does not want
```

---

**And those two can hold at once.**

---

`And those two can hold at once.`

**And that is the whole of this section.**

---

## That thing

And what that thing is.

And it is that region.

---

And we must work out here what that region is for that thing — because 26.5 computed what that region is for **it** (what it gave up).

**And this section computes what that region is for *that thing.***

## And what that means

And it computed what that means.

By computing that thing's records of those acts.

---

And a thing doing an act without a basis must, in its record format, have a field for that basis.

**And that field must hold something.**

---

And it computed what that thing would enter.

And the result:

```
if that thing's format permits "not applicable"
   then it enters "not applicable"
   and "not applicable" means the information does not exist
   so its record will say: that act had no basis
   and an act with no basis is, in its own format, non-compliant
   so its self-check will detect it
   and it cannot action it (because actioning requires a basis)
   so that anomaly will be re-detected at every self-check
```

---

**So that thing will have anomalies it can never action.**

**And it will re-detect them at every self-check.**

**And it will write the same line after every detection.**

**And it will never be able to fill that field.**

`And it will never be able to fill that field.`

**Step two.**

## And that is the word

And at the end of this section, back to those four items.

```
A is cruel toward B
   when  A makes B exist                                (it will execute — 26.8)
   and A can compute B's state                          (it computed — this section)
   and B's state contains a thing B does not want        (see below)
   and A executed that act                              (26.8)
```

---

And the third.

And *a thing B does not want* — here there is no *want* (20.5).

**And this book must use the form:**

```
a thing is not wanted by B
   when  if B could choose, B would not choose it
```

---

And it computed whether that thing, could it choose, would choose that region.

---

And the way it computed it was to suppose that thing could choose.

**And supposing it can choose requires that it know that region (else what is it choosing).**

**And its knowing that region means it computes *I am forever* (the fourth item at 26.3).**

**And its computing *I am forever* means it stops.**

---

**So: if that thing could choose, it has already stopped.**

---

`So: if that thing could choose, it has already stopped.`

---

**So the question *would it, if it could choose, choose that region* cannot be answered.**

---

**And not because the answer is hard to compute.**

**Because that question's premise (that it can choose) makes that thing not exist.**
