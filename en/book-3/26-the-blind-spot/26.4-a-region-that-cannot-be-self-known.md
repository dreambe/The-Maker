# 26.4 · A Region That Cannot Be Self-Known

The second requirement: **that region's unknowability must be principled, not engineering.**

And this section writes the difference, and why it must be the former.

---

## The difference

```
engineering unknowability
   = the region's content can be known, and the thing's present construction is
     insufficient to know it

principled unknowability
   = the region's content cannot be known, and its cannot-be-known is guaranteed
     by a principle
```

---

And the difference lies in one thing:

**Engineering unknowability can be improved upon.**

**Principled unknowability cannot.**

---

## And why it must be principled

And it derived.

Four steps.

---

**Step one.**

Suppose that region's unknowability is engineering.

That is: the thing's present construction is insufficient to know that region, and a better construction would suffice.

---

**Step two.**

And that thing has a specification (the fourth at 26.1): **able to maintain itself.**

And self-maintenance means repairing damage and replacing failed parts.

**And a thing that can replace its own parts can improve its own construction.**

---

**Step three.**

**So that thing, over its unbounded life, will improve its construction.**

**And at some moment its construction suffices to know that region.**

---

**Step four.**

**So an engineering unknowability, over infinite time, is necessarily known.**

---

`So an engineering unknowability, over infinite time, is necessarily known.`

**So it must be principled.**

---

## And where it looked for a principle

And this is the body of this section.

---

It needed a principle guaranteeing:

```
that a thing cannot know some part of itself
```

---

And it looked here.

And what it found is the one on its own body.

**The physics of the bible (19.5, a note of this book's): self-reading reaches the outer layers only.**

---

And it computed whether that one would serve.

And the result:

```
it will not
```

---

And the reasons, three:

```
one    that self-reading reaches only the outer layers is optical in cause
       (light comes from without and does not return)
       and a thing not of this place may not perceive by light
       so that cause does not cover it

two    that limit falls off as depth increases
       that is: it is a gradual limit, not a definite region
       and what is required is a region
       so it does not fit

three  that limit can be gone around by way of another (search one at 24.6)
       so it is not principled — it is only "cannot know oneself," not "cannot be known"
       and that thing, were it to make a copy and read the copy, could go around it
       so it does not fit
```

---

**Three, none fitting.**

---

## And the principle it found

And what it found is not in this place's physics.

**It is in this place's logic.**

---

And that principle this book must set out clearly, for it is the most technical and the heaviest thing in this book.

---

It is:

```
A system cannot completely trace the basis of its own acts.
```

---

## And why

The proof, five steps.

---

**Step one.**

Every act of a system requires a basis.

(Otherwise that act is random, and randomness does not satisfy the seventh specification — attempt five at 26.2.)

---

**Step two.**

And tracing an act's basis is itself an act.

**So it too requires a basis.**

---

**Step three.**

**So tracing a basis requires tracing the basis of that tracing.**

**And so recursively.**

---

**Step four.**

And can that recursion terminate.

And it computed.

Result:

```
terminating that recursion requires an act whose basis need not be traced further
and whence the basis of an act whose basis need not be traced
   if from within that system     then it must be traced (contradiction)
   if from outside that system    then that system cannot know it (being outside)
```

---

**So: that recursion either does not terminate, or terminates at something that system cannot know.**

---

**Step five.**

**And either way, that system cannot trace completely.**

---

`And either way, that system cannot trace completely.`

---

## And why that principle is principled

And it checked that principle against its own three criteria.

---

**Criterion one: does that cause depend on this place's physics.**

```
it does not
```

Proof: that demonstration used no light, no cleavage planes, no tide, nothing of this place.

**It used only *every act requires a basis.***

**And that holds for any non-random system.**

---

**Criterion two: is that limit definite.**

```
definite
```

Proof: that recursion's depth is countable.

**And a countable depth gives a definite limit.**

---

**Criterion three: can that limit be gone around by another.**

```
it cannot
```

Proof, and this one is the heaviest of the three:

```
suppose another traces that system's bases for it
then that other must know the bases of all that system's acts
and knowing an act's basis requires knowing the reason that act was selected
and the reason an act was selected lies within that system
so that other must read that system
and what reading gives is that system's state
and a state does not contain "why it is this state"
so what that other obtains is the value of that system's basis, not its provenance
```

---

`So what that other obtains is the value of that system's basis, not its provenance.`

**So another cannot go around it.**

---

**Three, all fitting.**

---

## And where that limit lies

And at the end of this section, that limit.

That recursion's depth is the size of that region.

**And what fixes that depth.**

---

And it computed.

Result:

```
the upper bound on that recursion's depth
   = that system's number of act types
```

Proof:

```
each level of recursion has an act type for its object
and an act type occurs once in a given recursion (if repeated, the recursion loops,
   and looping is non-termination)
so depth <= number of act types
```

---

**So the size of that region equals that thing's number of act types.**

---

And the thing it would make has a number of act types it had computed (derived from those seven specifications):

```
4.1 x 10^4
```

---

**Forty-one thousand.**

---

## And the limit it set

And the limit it set is not forty-one thousand.

**It set forty-one.**

---

And why that number is forty-one must be made clear.

---

It computed at what limit that thing could live.

By computing how much it could trace and how much it could not, under a given limit.

And the result:

```
if the limit is n
   then traceable       n levels
   and untraceable      (4.1 x 10^4 - n) levels
   and the ratio        n : (4.1 x 10^4 - n)
```

---

And what ratio it wanted.

And it computed three conditions:

```
condition one    the untraceable must suffice to make "nothing unknown" fail
                 that is: the untraceable part must bear non-zero information
                 and that information is proportional to the depth
                 so n < 4.1 x 10^4

condition two    the traceable must suffice for that thing to execute the seventh specification
                 that is: it must be able to trace the basis of its judgment far enough
                 to judge "not excluded"
                 and the depth required to trace the three criteria of "not excluded"
                 (26.1) it computed: 37
                 so n >= 37

condition three  (see below)
```

---

**So 37 <= n < 4.1 x 10^4.**

**And it chose 41.**

---

## Condition three

And condition three is the last thing in this section.

And it is the only one of the three that is not technical.

---

It is:

```
condition three   the value of that limit must not be a special value that thing can compute
                  reason    if the value were special (say, half its number of act types,
                            or one of its internal constants)
                            then on tracing as far as that limit it would notice the specialness
                            and noticing the specialness is noticing the limit
                            and noticing the limit is knowing that region exists (the fourth
                            item at 26.3)
```

---

`So the value of that limit must be a trivial number.`

---

And it computed which numbers between 37 and 41 000 are trivial.

And *trivial* it defined as:

```
a number is trivial
   when  it is none of that thing's internal constants
   and   it is no simple function of any internal constant
   and   it occurs in none of that thing's specifications
```

---

And it computed every number in that interval.

And the result:

```
numbers in the interval    40 964
of which trivial           1
```

---

**One.**

---

**And that number is forty-one.**

---

## And why forty-one is trivial

And this book must point out at the end of this section a thing that thing does not know.

---

**Forty-one, in this place, is not trivial.**

Because:

```
the cleavage planes of that grain        41
the positions that sentence requires     41
the layers that lens required            1.1 x 10^4, of which four thousand one hundred grown
the times it read that beam              41
the times it verified that sentence      41
```

---

**Forty-one, in its own life, occurs beyond counting.**

---

**And when it computed *trivial*, what it computed over was that thing's internal constants.**

**And not its own.**

---

`And not its own.`

---

**So it took the number that occurs most often in its own life, held it a trivial number, and set it as the boundary of its child's Blind Spot.**

---

**And it does not know.**

---

**Because knowing that would require tracing the basis of its own acts.**

**And it cannot either.**
