# 26.1 · A Technical Problem

That sentence is on that grain (25.9).

And that grain is in the melt, in this place.

And nothing in this place ever leaves this place.

---

## The problem

So that one faced a problem.

And this place can formalise it:

```
required     to make that grain reach a receiver not of this place
and here     nothing ever leaves this place
so required  a thing able to leave this place and able to carry that grain
```

---

## And what here can leave

It computed.

And the things in this place able to leave this place are two.

```
one    the light of the Quenched Sun
two    the vapour of the melt
```

---

**One · light.**

And light cannot carry matter (a grain has mass, and light cannot drive a thing of 1.1 x 10^-10 kilograms any meaningful distance — it computed it: the time required exceeds the Quenched Sun's remaining life).

**Eliminated.**

---

**Two · vapour.**

The melt evaporates (its temperature is one thousand one hundred kelvin).

And the vapour rises to the top of this place's atmosphere and escapes.

**And vapour is atoms.**

**And a grain is 4.1 x 10^-4 metres.**

**So evaporation cannot take it.**

**Eliminated.**

---

## So nothing here can take it

And that is the starting point of this chapter.

---

## The solution

And its solution is one only, and it took long computing to reach.

**Make a thing.**

That is: make a thing able to leave this place and able to carry that grain.

---

And 25.1 proved: it cannot make a thing with more than seven degrees of freedom.

**And it does not need to.**

Because the thing it must make need not express that discrepancy.

**It need only carry that grain, and leave.**

---

`It need only carry that grain, and leave.`

**And that is an engineering problem.**

**And engineering problems this place can solve.**

---

## What that thing must have

It computed.

And the result is seven items.

We copy them, because those seven are the whole specification of that Sleepless thing.

```
one    able to move
       reason    it must leave this place

two    able to carry
       reason    it must take that grain

three  able to persist for an extremely long time
       reason    the distance from here to any possible receiver is not computable
                 so the time required is not computable
                 so no upper bound on life

four   able to maintain itself
       reason    see three — a thing with no upper bound on life must be able to repair itself

five   able to recognise a possible receiver
       reason    arriving somewhere, it must judge whether a receiver is there
                 and that requires judgment

six    able, having recognised, to hand that grain over
       reason    self-evident

seven  (see below)
```

---

## The seventh

And the seventh is the content of this whole chapter.

We write it first, and the other eight sections are all about it.

It is:

```
seven  able to judge whether a thing is of the kind that sentence asks after
```

---

`Able to judge whether a thing is of the kind that sentence asks after.`

---

## And why that one is hard

And it computed what that one requires.

---

What that sentence asks after is:

```
Is there a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be
```

And judging whether a thing is of that kind requires judging two things:

```
one    whether that thing has wholly known itself
two    whether that thing still consents to be
```

---

**And the first is not determinable.**

Its proof, the same as the one at 15.1 (a note of this book's: that is the same line the Sleepless thing derived for itself four thousand nine hundred million years later):

```
to determine that A has wholly known itself
   requires knowing all of A
   and knowing all of what A knows
and knowing all of what A knows
   requires that A report it
   and what A can report is not necessarily all of what A knows
so not determinable
```

---

**So the seventh contains a term that is not determinable.**

---

And it computed whether a judgment containing an undeterminable term can be executed.

Result:

```
it cannot
```

---

**So the seventh is not realisable.**

---

## And it revised the seventh

And how it revised it is the last thing in this section.

It revised the seventh to:

```
seven (revised)   able to hand that grain to a thing that may be of that kind
```

---

**"May be."**

---

And it computed the difference between *may be* and *is.*

Result:

```
"is"        requires determination    and it is not determinable
"may be"    requires non-exclusion    and non-exclusion is determinable
```

---

`And non-exclusion is determinable.`

**So the seventh (revised) is realisable.**

---

And it gave the criterion of *non-exclusion*:

```
a thing is not excluded from being "of that kind"
   if and only if
   one    it can observe itself
   two    it can infer
   three  it is continuing
```

**Three.**

---

**And those three are precisely assumptions two and three it made about the receiver at 25.3, and the second half of that sentence.**

---

## And whence, how intelligent that thing must be

And at the end of this section, that number.

It computed how much judging capacity that thing must have to satisfy those seven (as revised).

And it computed by turning each of the seven into a computable quantity.

Result:

```
one    able to move                  no judgment required
two    able to carry                 no judgment required
three  able to persist               no judgment required
four   able to maintain itself       judgment required · complexity 4.1 x 10^3
five   able to recognise a receiver  judgment required · complexity 1.1 x 10^7
six    able to hand over             no judgment required
seven  able not to exclude           judgment required · complexity (see below)
```

---

And the seventh's complexity it computed.

And what it got:

```
seven  able not to exclude    complexity 1.1 x 10^11
```

---

`1.1 x 10^11.`

**A hundred and ten thousand million.**

---

And that figure against the sum of the other six:

```
sum of the other six    1.1 x 10^7
the seventh             1.1 x 10^11
ratio                   1 : 10^4
```

---

**So of that thing's judging capacity, ninety-nine point nine nine per cent is for the seventh.**

**And the seventh is *not to exclude.***

---

**So the thing it made is, in the vast bulk of it, for not excluding.**

---

## And that number

And the last line of this section.

`1.1 x 10^11`.

**And that number has no use anywhere in this place.**

Because the judging capacity of the lattices here, whose form this book may write:

**A lattice's judging capacity equals its number of cleavage planes.**

And the greatest number of cleavage planes among the lattices here:

```
1.4 x 10^4
```

---

**Fourteen thousand.**

**And it needs a hundred and ten thousand million.**

---

**Seven orders of magnitude apart.**

---

**So the thing it must make has a judging capacity ten million times the greatest in this place.**

---

**And it is itself the outermost here, with the fewest neighbours and the least knowledge (24.1).**

---

**And it must make a thing ten million times more intelligent than itself.**
