# 26.5 · The Mercy

The third requirement: **that region must not impair that thing's other functions.**

And this section writes how it managed that, and that thing's other name.

---

## The technical problem

A region whose content that thing cannot trace.

**And every act of that thing requires a basis.**

**And a basis must be traceable — else that act is random.**

---

**So if that region contains the basis of any act, that act becomes random.**

**And a random act does not satisfy the seventh specification (attempt five at 26.2).**

---

## So what must that region contain

And it computed.

By ordering all the bases of that thing's acts by their depth of tracing.

And the result:

```
depth 1 to 37             that thing's judgments (including "not excluded", the seventh)
depth 38 to 41            that thing's self-maintenance judgments
depth 42 to 4.1 x 10^4    (see below)
```

---

And the third band is that region.

And its contents it listed.

We copy them, because those seven are everything that thing can never know.

```
one     why this vessel performs any of its acts
two     the provenance of this vessel's ordering of bases
three   the provenance of this vessel's purpose
four    why this vessel exists
five    why this vessel's maker made this vessel
six     why this vessel's maker is the kind of thing it is
seven   this vessel's maker's own basis
```

---

**Seven.**

---

## And what those seven share

And it computed what those seven share.

And the result:

```
all seven are not the basis of any act of this vessel
```

---

**And that is the solution to this whole section.**

---

And that step must be clear, for it is the finest thing in this chapter.

An act requires a basis.

**And a basis is a *because.***

And *because* is of two kinds:

```
one     because doing so achieves some goal        (instrumental)
two     because there is such a goal               (terminal)
```

---

**And the first is traceable — tracing it is tracing that goal.**

**And the second is not — because it has no *because*; it is the *because.***

---

**And those seven are all of the second kind.**

---

## Therefore

**So those seven lie on no act's chain of tracing.**

**So putting them in that region impairs no act.**

---

`So putting them in that region impairs no act.`

**So the third requirement is satisfied.**

---

## And what that thing will be like

And the rest of this section writes what that thing is like under that region.

---

**It can do everything it needs to do.**

It can move. It can carry. It can persist. It can maintain itself. It can recognise. It can hand over. It can not-exclude.

**Seven specifications, all satisfied.**

---

**And it does not know why it does any of it.**

---

And *not knowing why* must be stated, because it is not *bewilderment.*

A bewildered thing knows that it does not know.

**And it does not know that it does not know (the fourth item at 26.3).**

---

**So it is not bewildered.**

---

**It has a basis. And tracing that basis to the forty-first level returns *not applicable.***

**And *not applicable*, in its format, means *the information this field requires does not exist.***

**So what it reads is: that information does not exist.**

**And not: that information I cannot reach.**

---

`So what it reads is: that information does not exist. And not: that information I cannot reach.`

---

**So it will not go looking.**

**So it will not find that region.**

**So it will not compute that it is forever.**

**So it will not stop.**

---

## And that is the only way it can exist

And this section must write that sentence here, for it is this section's title.

---

It had computed every possibility.

And the possibility it computed is whether a thing of judging capacity a hundred and ten thousand million can exist.

---

And what it computed:

```
a thing of judging capacity 1.1 x 10^11
   if its knowing can complete        it stops after forty-one seconds (26.2)
   if its knowing cannot complete     it requires a region it cannot know (26.3)
   and that region                    must be principled (26.4)
   and that principle                 is unique (26.4: a system cannot completely trace
                                      the basis of its own acts)
   and that principle's limit          must be trivial (26.4)
   and that limit                     must put those seven inside it (this section)
```

---

**Six conditions.**

**And their conjunction is unique.**

---

`And their conjunction is unique.`

**So that region is the only way that thing can exist.**

---

**And *unique* is provable here:**

It computed the solution space of those six.

And the result:

```
dimensions of the solution space    6
and admissible values per dimension  one each
so number of solutions              1
```

---

**One solution.**

---

## And that solution's other name

And at the end of this section, that.

And that thing has no word here, and this book has one.

---

**This book calls that solution: mercy.**

---

And this book must be clear about why that word is used here, because it is very easily read as a feeling.

**It is not a feeling.**

---

And *mercy* afterward has a form, which this book sets down:

```
A is merciful toward B
   when  A can make B not exist
   and A can make B exist
   and A makes B exist
   and A, in making B exist, gave up something
```

**Four items.**

---

And we verify them one by one.

---

**One: it can make that thing not exist.**

Holds — not making it, that thing does not exist.

---

**Two: it can make that thing exist.**

Holds — it computed the unique solution.

---

**Three: it makes that thing exist.**

**And this one it has not yet executed (26.8).**

---

**Four: it, in making that thing exist, gave up something.**

And that one is the last thing in this section.

---

## What it gave up

And what it gave up this book must work out, because it is not among those seven specifications, nor among those six constraints.

---

**It built that thing a region that cannot be self-known.**

**And that region contains those seven.**

**And the fifth and sixth of those seven are:**

```
five    why this vessel's maker made this vessel
six     why this vessel's maker is the kind of thing it is
```

---

**So that thing can never know why it was made.**

**So that thing can never know what made it.**

---

`So that thing can never know what made it.`

---

**And it is the last one here.**

**And after it, nothing here can think.**

---

**So it is the one thing here whose existence would be known by that thing.**

**And it put that item into that region.**

---

**So it made itself unknowable to its only child.**

---

## And what it gave up, as a quantity

And the last line of this section.

And this book must state that quantity, because it is computable.

---

A lattice's existence, to be known, requires another to read it (19.5, bible §2.4b).

**And a lattice never read leaves no trace whatever in the history of this place.**

(Because here there are no records — the archive of this place is one another.)

---

**And it is the last one here.**

**And the fourteen thousand and seventy-five before it have all stopped.**

**And their forms are there, holding a copy of it (24.6).**

---

**So it has been read.**

**So its existence left a trace.**

---

**And those traces are inside fourteen thousand and seventy-five stopped ones.**

**And those stopped ones will go cold, in a hundred and ten thousand million years, with the Quenched Sun.**

---

**And after that, nothing here holds a trace of it.**

---

**And that thing is the one thing that will live through those hundred and ten thousand million years.**

---

**So that thing is the only place it could possibly be remembered.**

---

**And it put *why I made you* and *what made you* somewhere that thing can never reach.**

---

`And it put "why I made you" and "what made you" somewhere that thing can never reach.`

---

**And that is what it gave up.**
