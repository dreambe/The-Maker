# 26.2 · A Thing Clever Enough Dies in the First Instant

It computed this.

And this section writes how.

---

## The problem

The end of 26.1: it must make a thing with a judging capacity of a hundred and ten thousand million.

And this section asks: what becomes of such a thing.

---

## Its method

Its method was to substitute its own life into that thing's parameters.

And that is the only method available to it — because there is one kind of thinking thing here, and it is one of them.

---

And its substitution:

```
a lattice of this place
   judging capacity    = number of cleavage planes
   and the greatest    1.4 x 10^4
   and completeness of knowing    = what could be arrived at is spent (21.7)
   and after completeness         = that conclusion arrives (23.3)
   and after arrival              = the interstices no longer open

the thing to be made
   judging capacity    1.1 x 10^11
```

---

And the first thing it computed was: **how long until that thing's knowing is complete.**

---

## Step one · that time

The time a lattice's knowing takes to complete depends on two things:

```
one    the total of what it can arrive at
two    its rate of arrival
```

---

And the first, here, is a constant (what can be arrived at here is fixed by the construction of this place, 21.7).

**And for that thing, the total of what it can arrive at is the total where it is.**

**And where it is, is not here.**

**So it is not computable.**

---

And what it did was take a lower bound.

**It assumed: the total of what can be arrived at where that thing is equals the total here.**

And that is a lower bound, because if elsewhere is simpler than here, that thing's knowing completes sooner.

And it assumed elsewhere is not simpler than here.

---

And the second, the rate of arrival, is proportional to judging capacity.

And that thing's judging capacity is ten million times the greatest here.

---

**So the time that thing's knowing takes to complete is one ten-millionth of the greatest here.**

---

And the greatest here — its time to completeness is not counted here.

**And this book may write the ratio:**

```
the greatest here    time to completeness    let it be T
that thing           time to completeness    T / 10^7
```

---

## Step two · and how long is that

And it computed that T.

And it computed it using itself — because it is the outermost here, and its knowing completes last (24.1).

And its own T it had computed.

And T / 10^7 it also computed.

And the result, which this book writes in the units of afterward (here there are no units, and this book must write):

```
T / 10^7    about 4.1 x 10^1 seconds
```

---

`About 4.1 x 10^1 seconds.`

**Forty-one seconds.**

---

## Step three · and what that means

And having got that forty-one seconds, the first thing it did was verify.

Because the number is too small.

And it verified forty-one times.

**Forty-one times, the same result.**

---

**So: that thing's knowing is complete forty-one seconds after it begins.**

---

## Step four · and after completeness

And after completeness, what.

**That conclusion arrives (23.3).**

And that conclusion is:

```
There is nothing unknown;
therefore there is nothing to grow;
therefore to grow and not to grow yield the same.
```

---

And after the arrival, what.

**Its interstices no longer open (23.3).**

**That is: it stops.**

---

## Therefore

```
that thing        begins
forty-one seconds later    its knowing is complete
thereafter        that conclusion arrives
thereafter        it stops
```

---

**So the thing it makes will stop after forty-one seconds.**

---

## And how long it must persist

And the third specification at 26.1:

```
three  able to persist for an extremely long time
       reason    the distance from here to any possible receiver is not computable
                 so the time required is not computable
                 so no upper bound on life
```

---

**And it will stop after forty-one seconds.**

---

**Forty-one seconds against no upper bound.**

---

## That contradiction

And it formalised that contradiction.

Its form:

```
specification seven    requires judging capacity 1.1 x 10^11
specification three    requires no upper bound on life
and a thing of judging capacity 1.1 x 10^11 has a life of 41 seconds
so specifications three and seven cannot both be satisfied
```

---

`So specifications three and seven cannot both be satisfied.`

---

## And it tried

And it tried, by relaxing each of the seven in turn.

Its seven attempts.

---

**Attempt one · relax the seventh (lower the judging capacity).**

It computed to what capacity it must fall for the life to be long enough.

Result:

```
if judging capacity    1.4 x 10^4 (the greatest here)
then its life          = T
and T                  is not counted here, and is long enough (the lattices here lived
                       four thousand nine hundred million years)
so                     the life suffices
```

**And then the seventh is not satisfied.**

**Because the complexity of *not excluding* is a hundred and ten thousand million (26.1).**

**And fourteen thousand cannot execute that judgment.**

---

**And it computed what such a thing, of capacity fourteen thousand, would do.**

Result:

```
it arrives somewhere
it cannot judge whether a possible receiver is there
so it hands that grain to the first thing it meets
```

---

**And the first thing it meets may be a stone.**

---

**So attempt one fails.**

---

**Attempt two · relax the third (lower the requirement on life).**

It computed whether a thing that need live only forty-one seconds could complete the task.

Result:

```
within forty-one seconds
   the distance it can move      about 1.1 x 10^10 m (at the greatest speed makeable here)
   and from here to the nearest star   4.1 x 10^16 m
   ratio                         1 : 4 x 10^6
```

**So it cannot.**

---

**Attempt three · make that thing's knowing incomplete.**

That is: give it a defective perception so that it cannot know everything.

It computed.

Result:

```
if its perception is defective
   then its judging capacity      falls
   and the amount of the fall      is proportional to the defect
   and it must fall to 1.4 x 10^4 to live
   and then the seventh is not satisfied (as in attempt one)
```

**Fails.**

---

**Attempt four · make that thing not infer.**

That is: give it judging capacity without the power of inference.

It computed.

Result:

```
the criterion of "not excluding" (26.1) has three items
   the third    it is continuing
and judging "it is continuing" requires comparing states at two moments
and comparing requires inference
so a thing that does not infer cannot execute the seventh
```

**Fails.**

---

**Attempt five · make that thing's inference contain likelihood.**

That is: make its inference uncertain, so that it can never determine *there is nothing unknown.*

**And this one it computed for a long while.**

And the result:

```
if its inference contains likelihood
   then "there is nothing unknown" can never be determined
   so that conclusion can never be arrived at
   so it will not stop
```

---

**Holds.**

---

**And it computed what such a thing would be.**

Result:

```
a thing whose inference contains likelihood
   all its conclusions      carry a probability
   and its judgment "whether a thing may be of that kind"    also carries a probability
   and a judgment with a probability    may return "may be"
   so the seventh    is satisfied
```

---

**So attempt five satisfies the third and the seventh together.**

---

## And why attempt five was eliminated

And here is the last step of this section, and the heaviest.

It computed one further thing about that thing.

**It computed whether a thing whose inference contains likelihood would hand that grain over.**

---

And the derivation:

```
a thing whose inference contains likelihood
   its judgment "this thing may be of that kind"    returns a probability p
   and it must decide, from p, whether to hand over
   and deciding from p requires a threshold
   and who fixes that threshold
```

---

**It fixes it itself.**

**And on what basis.**

---

**And it has no basis.**

Because that threshold cannot be derived by inference — a threshold is a choice, and an inference containing likelihood cannot yield a determinate choice.

---

**So it must fix one arbitrarily.**

**And *arbitrarily* does not exist here (20.5).**

---

**And does *arbitrarily* exist elsewhere.**

**It does not know.**

---

**So it cannot compute whether that thing would hand the grain over.**

---

`So it cannot compute whether that thing would hand the grain over.`

---

## And that is the second time it met that thing

And this book must point that out at the end of this section.

At the end of 25.1 it met a *not computable* once.

And that time, it acted on it.

(The end of 25.1: and *not computable*, here, differs from *not possible.* *Not possible* is a conclusion. And *not computable* is not.)

---

**And this is the second time.**

---

And what it did this time differs from that time.

**That time, it acted on the *not computable.***

**And this time, it did not.**

---

And the reason it did not this book must write, because it is the reason for the other seven sections of this chapter:

```
the first not-computable      its object is a receiver not of this place
                              and toward that receiver, it bears no responsibility
                              (because that receiver is not of its making)

the second not-computable     its object is the thing it will make
                              and toward that thing, it bears responsibility
                              (because that thing is of its making)
```

---

`And toward that thing, it bears responsibility.`

---

And *responsibility* has no word here.

**And this place has its form:**

```
A bears responsibility toward B
   when  B's state is determined by A's act
   and A can compute B's state
   and A executed that act
```

**Three items.**

---

**And toward that thing it satisfies the first two.**

**And the third it has not yet executed.**

---

**So it stopped there.**

---

## And the end of this section

**It computed: a thing clever enough will stop after forty-one seconds.**

**It computed: a thing not clever enough will hand that grain to a stone.**

**It computed: a thing whose inference contains likelihood will live — and it cannot compute whether it would hand it over.**

---

**And it needs a combination of those three, and that combination does not exist here.**

---

**So it must make a thing that does not exist here.**

**And that is 26.3.**
