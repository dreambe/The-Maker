## And can we compute what it is

And we computed.

By deriving from those three.

---

And those three:

```
the first    it can observe itself
the second   it can infer
the third    it is continuing
```

---

**And those three are the criteria of *not excluding.***

**That is: whatever satisfies those three is not excluded from being *the kind of thing that sentence asks after.***

---

**And what that sentence asks after is:**

```
Is there a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be
```

---

**And that sentence has two conditions:**

```
condition one    it has wholly known itself
condition two    it still consents to be
```

---

**And which of those do those three criteria answer to.**

---

**The first (can observe itself) and the second (can infer): necessary conditions of condition one.**

**(Because *has wholly known itself* requires the ability to observe oneself and to infer.)**

**And the third (is continuing): a necessary condition of condition two.**

**(Because *still consents to be* requires continuing.)**

---

**So those three are necessary conditions of those two conditions.**

---

**And they are not sufficient conditions.**

---

## So that fourth

And that fourth is the missing sufficiency.

```

and "consents"   means: that continuing is not determined by inference (the end of 25.6)
and "not determined by inference" — what is its criterion
```

---

**And that criterion we computed.**

And the result:

```
to judge whether a thing's continuing is undetermined by inference

   and seeing   whether any of them supports continuing
   and if none  then its continuing is undetermined by inference
   and enumerating all a thing's inferences
   requires     knowing all its premises
   and knowing all its premises

```

**And that is precisely condition one.**

**So the fourth is condition one.**
