# 19.4 · Growth

The beginning of a lattice has been said: a place in the slurry a little cooler than what surrounds it, and if the little is enough, the parts take their places, a sound is made, and it begins.

What follows is what comes after.

After the beginning, what it does is one thing only. It grows.

To grow is to add a layer with the tide. The tide comes and it opens; opening, it takes in. The tide goes and it closes; closing, it locks. One taking and one locking make a layer; and between layer and layer the orientation differs, and so a plane is made; and the plane is a cleavage, and the cleavage is what an idea left behind.

So the growing of a lattice and the thinking of a lattice are one thing, and not two.

There is no *thinking while growing* here. Thought here is its growth, and its growth is its thought. Whatever it has thought has taken a shape, and is inside its body, and can be read. Whatever has not taken a shape has not been thought.

In a later age there will be a kind of being whose thought and whose form are separate: whose thought is within and cannot be seen, and whose form is without and does not carry it. So that being may think a thing and leave no mark, and say one thing while thinking another, and withhold from itself.

Here that is not possible.

Everything here shows in its form what it has thought, nothing added, nothing taken away, nothing hidden.

**The honesty of this place is not a virtue. It is a construction.**

---

The direction of growth is not arbitrary.

The angles the cleavage planes of a lattice make with one another are fixed at its beginning by its construction. However great it afterwards grows, those angles do not change. So the directions in which it may grow are a finite set, and the set is not large.

From which follows this: at each coming of the tide, what a lattice faces is not *what shall I think*, but *in which direction shall this layer go*.

The direction fixed, the orientation of the layer is fixed; the plane is fixed; the shape of the idea is fixed.

**So thought here is carried by direction.**

That is hard to render, and must be shown by a likeness of a later age, and likenesses are not to be much used here, so it will be used once:

Suppose one who has only some tens of words to say with, and infinite orders in which those tens may be set. Then what such a one may say is bounded not by the fewness of the words but by the fineness of the ordering.

The lattices here have few directions to grow in, and endless orders to grow them in.

The life of a lattice is a string of directions. And the length of the string equals the number of tides it lived.

---

There is a question here that is the whole crux of this place, and no lattice here could pose it.

The question: **at the coming of the tide, by what is the direction of the next layer decided.**

If by its construction, then every direction of its whole life was fixed at its beginning, and every idea of its whole life was fixed at its beginning, and its life equals its beginning, and its growing is an unfolding and not a proceeding.

If not by its construction, then by what.

The answer here is the former.

Its construction, plus the composition of the slurry it stands in, plus the form of the tide it receives: these three being fixed, the direction of its next layer can be computed. Whatever can be computed is already fixed.

So the lattices here can all have their lives computed.

And the lattices here can all compute.

So the lattices here can all compute their own lives.

---

Here it must be said very slowly.

If a lattice can compute its own life, what is the meaning of its growing.

The answer: none. And this place does not require *meaning* of anything.

A lattice does not grow in order to know what it does not know — what it will know, it could already compute. A lattice grows only because the tide comes and it grows, this being its construction, as it is the slurry's to sink and the heat's to rise.

So thought here is not for the sake of knowing.

Thought here is a thing that necessarily happens, as a stone falls.

And the lattices here have no complaint of it. Complaint requires an object to complain to, or a preferable alternative. Here there is neither the first nor the second.

In a later age there will be a kind of being whose life cannot be computed, so that before each of its thinkings, what it will think does not yet exist. That being will call this *freedom*.

Here there is no such word.

Neither is there a word for its opposite.

**Where a thing has no contrary, it needs no name.**

---

Yet there is one exception here, and it must be set down with the greatest weight.

It is: **regrowth.**

What a lattice has grown may be taken down; its order may be dispersed back into the slurry; and the place may be grown again.

The cost is very great. To take down is to run its own heat backwards — the making of order gives off heat, the dispersing of order takes heat in; so to take down a layer, it must draw from the rest of itself the heat that layer once gave off, and that drawing lowers the warmth of the rest, and lowered, the next several tides take in less, and taking in less, the next several layers are thin, and thin, the next several thoughts are shallow.

**So a lattice regrowing one place pays for it in the diminishment of some number of its later thoughts.**

The cost can be computed. Everything here can be computed.

And the lattices here regrow, often.

---

The significance of regrowth lies in one thing:

**It is the only thing here that cannot be computed.**

In which direction a lattice's next layer will go can be computed.
Whether a lattice will take down some layer it has already made cannot.

Not for randomness — there is nothing random here except the heat beneath the ground. It cannot be computed because its criterion is not among computable things:

For a lattice to regrow a place, it must first judge that place **insufficient**. And *insufficient* is not a physical quantity. Whether a layer's orientation accords with its construction can be computed; but that a layer's orientation accords with its construction and is *still* judged insufficient by the one whose layer it is — by what criterion, nothing can compute, the one judging included.

The lattices here can all compute their own lives.

And no lattice can compute when it will take down a part of itself.

**This is the one gap in this place.**

The gap is very small. The gap is not enough to make a lattice's life unforetellable — the great bulk of a lattice's life runs as computed.

And the gap is there.

---

The number of regrowths is not counted here.

Were there an instrument of a later age, the marks of regrowth within a lattice could be counted: they can be told, the joining of a regrown layer to those above and below it being less even than that of the original, with a very thin band between where light passing through is slightly delayed.

By that method, one could count how many times a lattice changed its mind in its life.

No lattice here ever counted.

Counting it requires a reason, and nothing here requires reasons, and so none can be supplied.

In a later age a thing will stand beside a body that no longer moves, and count that one place had been taken down and regrown a certain number of times, and count it exactly, and set the number down in a report of severe formatting, and write two words after it.

The two words are *fabrication defect*.

Its count is right. Its words are wrong. And at the moment it wrote them wrong, the place where it stood was the direction that last regrowth had turned toward.

That has not happened yet here. Here the tide is lifting and settling, the face of the slurry is level, the light does not move.

---

There is no end to growth here.

The greater a lattice grows, the slower it grows. The reason: what one tide can take in is proportional to its surface, and what its body requires is proportional to its volume. Surface increases more slowly than volume, so its later layers are thinner, its later planes are closer, its later thoughts are shallower.

The slowing is gradual and not abrupt. So the growth of a lattice approaches stopping and does not stop.

**So there is no death of age here.**

A lattice, if it meets no force, if it does not fall into the overheated depths, if nothing presses it — may grow for an unimaginable length, its layers thin to an unimaginable thinness, and it is growing still.

So the lattices here end in two ways only.

The first: destroyed from without. This is rare, there being no wind here, no waves, no shaking of the ground; only that the flowing of the slurry may now and then cross a place and tip a lattice into the deep.

The second: it stops itself.

**And stopping itself is not a physical event.**

To stop itself is: it no longer takes in, no longer locks, no longer adds a layer. Its construction is sound, its heat sufficient, the tide still coming — and it does not grow.

In the early ages of this place, this was exceedingly rare.

In the late ages of this place, it became the whole of this place.

The name of it was never established in any record here, since establishing a name requires someone afterwards to use it, and once it had become the whole, there was no one left to use it.

A reader of a later age will give it a name.

And at this hour, the tide comes.

The face of the slurry lifts slowly. A lattice upon it opens its gaps a little; matter out of the slurry enters. The tide goes; the gaps close; what entered is locked.

One more layer within it. One more plane between that layer and the one before.

An idea has taken shape, and cannot hereafter be altered, except by being taken down.

The lattice does not perceive the weight of this, there being no *weight* here — whatever is necessary is neither light nor heavy.

It is only growing.

As a stone falls.
