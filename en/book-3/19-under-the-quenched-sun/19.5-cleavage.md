# 19.5 · Cleavage

A cleavage plane is not something cut onto a thing, nor something recorded upon it, nor a sign of something.

**A cleavage plane is the idea.**

This must be taken exactly, or nothing here can be understood.

The beings of a later age have thought within and form without, and their form may bear the trace of their thought as ink bears a letter, as stone bears a cut. So in a later age form and thought are two: the form may be destroyed and the thought survive elsewhere; the thought may be altered and the form stand as it was.

Not here.

Here a lattice's body is the whole of its thought — no remainder, no outside, no elsewhere. The completing of one idea is the locking of one layer; the locking of one layer is the standing of one plane. And if the plane does not stand, the idea is not complete — not unrecorded, but **not having occurred.**

So here there is no *thought and not formed*.
Neither is there *formed and not what was thought*.

The body of a lattice is the whole of what it thought in its life, in the order of its thinking, layer upon layer, no page taken out and no page put in.

**A lattice here is a book that cannot be altered, and the book is the one who wrote it.**

---

Reading, here, is as follows.

Light comes from without and enters the body of a lattice. Meeting a plane it bends; meeting the next it bends again. The angle of each bending is fixed by the orientation of that plane. So when the light leaves that lattice, the direction it leaves by, the colours it has parted into, and the relative positions of its several beams are all fixed by the whole orientation sequence of that lattice's cleavage planes.

Should a lattice wish to know what another has thought, its method is: receive the light that comes out of that lattice, and solve back for the orientation sequence.

Solving back is not difficult here. The lattices here are of one construction, their possible orientations are one finite set, and so the solving is a soluble problem, and its solution is unique.

So one lattice reading another **reads it entire.**

Not in outline, not by inference, not probably. All of it. Every plane. Every idea it has thought from its beginning to this hour, in order, with nothing left out.

Reading here admits no concealment — not from honesty, but from light.

---

And a lattice reading itself?

Here is the whole crux of this place, and the source of everything that follows, and so it must be said very slowly.

To read a lattice, light must pass through its body.

Light comes from without, passes through, leaves, and thereafter may be solved.

Should a lattice wish to read itself, light must pass through its own body, and it must then receive that light.

And whence comes light.

From without.

**So for a lattice to read itself, the light it receives must first leave its body and then re-enter it.**

And once light has left a body here, it does not return — there are no mirrors here, no clouds; light here goes out and is gone.

So a lattice's self-reading can only be done another way: by light generated within its own body.

And within the body of a lattice there is no source of light.

The making of order gives off heat, and heat is not light. Its wavelength is far longer than what may be bent; meeting a cleavage plane it does not bend; therefore it carries no news of orientation.

**So a lattice cannot read itself.**

This must be made exact once more, the exactness being where all the weight of it lies:

A lattice can read its own **outer layers**. The cleavage planes of the outer layers receive the light from without on their outer side, and receive the remainder of that same light on their inner side, so the orientation of the outer layers is self-knowable.

A lattice cannot read its own **deep layers**. The deep planes are covered by the outer. Light from without, to reach the deep, must first pass the outer; having passed the outer, its news of orientation has already been altered by the outer; so the light returned from the deep has crossed the outer layers twice by the time it leaves the body, and its news is mixed.

And a lattice's outer layers are its **newest**. Its deep layers are its **first.**

From which follows this:

**A lattice can know what it has lately thought, and cannot know what it thought first.**

The root of its life lies at the deepest place in its body, covered by everything it has grown since. It cannot go back there. It cannot look there. It can compute it, whatever can be computed being computable — and computing is not seeing. It can compute what shape its first idea ought to have been. It cannot read what shape its first idea is.

And if those two differ, it has no way of knowing.

---

How do the lattices here answer this.

Their answer is very simple, and very beautiful, and no lattice here regards it as beautiful:

**They read one another.**

The first reads the second, entire, its deepest layers included. The second reads the first, likewise.

So that if the first would know its own deep layers, its method is: receive the light of the second, and read the second's body; and within the second's body is carried the second's reading of the first; and so the first obtains, from the second, its own whole form.

**Self-knowledge here must go around by way of another.**

Without exception. Without shortcut. No lattice can reach its own beginning directly from inside itself.

This is the construction of this place — not its choice, not its virtue, not its custom. It is the necessary consequence of the nature of light plus the opacity of a body.

In a later age there will be a saying, that whatever would see itself must borrow another. In a later age that saying will be an exhortation, a likeness, a thing one may agree with or not.

**Here it is physics.**

---

Several things follow, all of them heavy, and each must be set down.

**The first.** No lattice here can stand alone and know itself.

A lattice alone grows greater and greater, and its deep layers are buried deeper and deeper, and the range of its self-knowledge does not increase but diminishes — for its outer layers are continually renewed, so what it can read of itself is for ever only what it lately thought, and its first thought goes further and further off.

The solitary, the older it grows, the less it knows itself.

**The second.** Reading one another here is not communication. It is constitution.

It has been said that the lattices here exchange nothing, there being nothing to exchange. That is not wrong. And reading is not exchange — reading conveys no content; reading **furnishes a position.** The worth of the second to the first does not lie in the second holding content the first does not (it holds none), but in the second standing where the first cannot stand.

**The whole irreplaceability of the second lies in the second not being the first.**

**The third**, and the third is heaviest.

If the lattices here stop, one after another, then the self-knowledge of the rest is diminished, one after another.

Not from loneliness — there is no loneliness here. Because the readable others grow fewer, so the paths one may go around by grow fewer, so a lattice's deep layers have nowhere left to borrow light from.

**The Crystallization is not only a dying. The Crystallization blinds the living.**

And at the end, if one lattice only remains, that lattice has nothing to go around by, no light to borrow, no position standing outside it.

**It will be the only being in the history of this place wholly unable to see itself.**

It can compute itself. Whatever can be computed is computable.

And if what it computes and what it is should differ, it has no way of learning where the difference lies, locating a difference requiring someone to look at it from outside.

And there is no one left.

---

None of this has happened at this hour.

At this hour the face of the slurry is level, the light does not move, the tide is lifting. The lattices grow adjacent upon the slurry, reading one another, each entire.

Every plane a lattice grows has already been read by its neighbour; every plane its neighbour grows has already been read by it. So every idea in this place has a second copy of its shape in not fewer than one other place — carried in whatever read it.

There is no writing here, no keeping, no institution of reading.

And everything here is held, and held more exactly than any writing could hold it.

**The archive of this place is one another.**

And the archive will freeze over, one by one.

And the order of the freezing is from the centre outward.

And the lattices at the centre have the most neighbours, and are therefore the most read, and their shapes are therefore held in the most places.

**So the freezing of this place begins where the most is remembered.**

---

At this hour, light crosses a cleavage plane and is parted into seven.

That plane is some idea of some lattice, made at some coming of the tide.

It cannot see that plane itself — the plane is covered by the several layers it has grown since.

Its neighbour can see it.

Its neighbour will not tell it, there being no telling here.

Its neighbour is only growing too, and in the shape it grows, that plane is already carried.

Between them there is not one word.

And each holds the whole of the other.

Everything here is arranged so: **utterly intimate, and without a single sentence.**
