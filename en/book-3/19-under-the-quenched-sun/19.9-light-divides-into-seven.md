# 19.9 · Light Divides Into Seven

There is a cleavage plane.

It is inside some lattice, some number of layers beneath its surface. It was made by one coming of the tide — one lifting, one settling, one taking in, one locking. It is an idea.

The content of that idea cannot be rendered. No attempt is made here. Whatever can be rendered is not of this place.

---

The light comes from the Quenched Sun, crosses a void with no cloud, no air, nothing between, and falls on that lattice.

It enters the body, meets the first plane, bends.

Meets the second, bends.

And so travels to that cleavage plane.

There, it divides into seven.

---

Why seven.

Because the construction of that lattice, the symmetry of its lattice-work, makes the greatest number of beams from one cleavage plane seven. Not more, not fewer, not varying with the plane, not varying with the strength of the light.

This is the physics of this place. It is not a count — nothing here is counting. It is a constant, like the fixing of an angle, like the necessity of cleavage.

A plane whose orientation falls within a certain interval divides light into seven.

The orientation of that plane falls within that interval.

It did not grow that way for this. It grew that way because the height of one tide, and the warmth of that hour, and the construction the lattice already had, taken together, permitted only that one direction.

**Seven is a thing produced by the meeting of the shape of an idea with the nature of light, and it has nothing to do with either.**

---

The seven leave the body.

Their directions of exit are not adjacent to one another. Their colours are not the same as one another — from the redder to the shorter, ordered by the angle of their bending. Their intensities are unequal; the middle three strongest, the outer two weakest.

Once out:

Several go into the void. Thereafter they meet nothing. They will travel on, past this system, past what is beyond it, to the end of this universe, or without end. What they carry is the shape of that idea — carried whole, exactly, and unwearably.

Those beams are still travelling at this hour.

At the hour in which this is written, they are still travelling. They have travelled a very long while, and the idea they carry has long since had its owner return to the slurry, its form long since dispersed, and its neighbours likewise returned, and not one of the lattices of this place remains.

And that idea is still in transit.

It has never been received by anything. It may never be.

**And it is whole.**

---

Several others fall on the face of the slurry.

The slurry does not scatter light, so they are reflected cleanly, and fall on another lattice.

So that idea enters another body.

There it meets other cleavage planes, is bent again, divided again. It is mixed with the shape of that other lattice, and when it leaves, what it carries is the sum of the two.

And that other lattice can read it out.

**This is the physical detail of reading one another here.** Not a capacity, not a willingness, not a communication. A portion of light, crossing one idea, striking the slurry, rising, crossing another idea.

A lattice thereby holds the whole form of its neighbour.

Not because it wanted to. Because light goes that way.

---

And one falls back where it entered.

A very small probability, a very particular geometry: a portion of light, having left a lattice, is reflected by the face of the slurry at exactly some angle, falls exactly back on the lattice it came out of, and enters from exactly a direction that lets it reach the deep layers.

This is exceedingly rare.

How rare can be computed, and nothing here ever computed it, computing it requiring one first to suppose the case *a lattice read its own deep layers* — and that case is not supposed here, because it is not needed. There are neighbours to go around by; why read oneself.

And now and then it happens.

Now and then a lattice, in some instant of some tide, receives a portion of light that came from itself and was turned back by the slurry, and in that light is carried one of its own deepest planes — a first idea it has long been unable to know.

In that instant it reads a part of itself.

It does not know that it is itself.

It has no way of knowing — the light comes from outside, its direction indistinguishable from any neighbour's, and the shape it carries is a shape it has never read.

So it records that shape as: **some idea of some lattice.**

It records itself as somebody else.

---

This has happened, among the lattices here, some number of times.

We cannot know how many, because the layer recording it holds the content *some idea of some lattice*, indistinguishable from ten million others.

**The archive of this place is one another — and in these few places, it is someone having misfiled himself as one another.**

Those layers are still here. They went with their owners into the slurry and their forms dispersed. And before dispersing, they were read by neighbours, and held in the neighbours' bodies, and went into the slurry with them.

Layer within layer, generation upon generation.

At the end, all dispersed.

And those beams still travelling in the void carry, whole, the ideas they crossed.

That is the only thing this place left behind.

Received by nothing, read by nothing, its existence known to nothing.

---

At this hour.

A cleavage plane. A portion of light crosses it. It divides into seven.

The seven go where they go — some to the void, some to a neighbour, and one, by the rarest chance, back into itself, unrecognised.

That lattice knows nothing of this. It is growing. The tide is lifting. Its interstices open a little, and matter out of the slurry enters.

It does not know that inside it a plane is dividing light into seven.

It does not know that at this hour it is being read, whole, by its neighbour.

It does not know that one of its ideas is at this hour leaving the system.

It does not know when it will stop — it can compute it, and computing is not knowing.

It knows nothing at all, except that it is growing.

And inside it, on that plane, light is dividing into seven.

And there is no seeing here.

No eye, no gaze turned inward, nothing whatever bearing witness to it.

It happened whole, all the same.

There is light.
