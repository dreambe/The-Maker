# 19.7 · Heat

The heat of this place comes from below.

Beneath the ground there is matter whose decay is very slow. To decay is for the core of a thing suddenly to alter its construction, put out a part of itself, and let the remainder settle lower, the difference being heat. The event has no cause, no hour, no order; it cannot be foretold when it will occur at any one place, and can only be foretold in its total across very many places over very long times.

So the power of this place has for its source a thing **unknowable one by one and computable in the aggregate.**

That must be held onto. Of the matters of this place, all that can be computed stands upon this one thing that cannot.

---

The heat rises from below, and its rising is very slow.

Its slowness is not of its nature but of its road. The matter beneath the ground lies pressed layer upon layer, the interstices exceedingly small, so the heat must be passed on by the striking of part against part, one striking and one passing, and to travel one metre takes a very long while.

So a portion of heat, from where it came out to the face of the slurry, must travel a span that is long by comparison with everything else in this place.

It has been said: the tide lifting a lattice now was made by heat from before that lattice existed. One step further may be taken:

**The heat lifting everything in this place now came out when this place had no lattices at all.**

At the first of this place there were no lattices, no thought, no cleavages, nothing that could be read. And already the matter beneath was decaying, and its heat was already rising. That heat travelled a very long while, and by the hour it reached the face of the slurry, lattices were growing adjacent upon it.

What holds those lattices up comes out of an age entirely before them.

Nothing in that age remembers it, that age having had nothing in it.

---

The heat is such that metal runs like honey.

*Like honey* is one of the few likenesses in this book, and why it is used must be stated.

The metal here does not run as water runs. Water's parts hold one another very weakly, so it may be quick, may splash, may scatter into spray. The parts of molten metal hold one another strongly, so its going must be whole, its surface must be thick, and its breaking must draw out a thread and not break at once.

Among the things of this place there is nothing whose flowing resembles it.

So if a likeness is to be taken from this place, there is no likeness to take.

And in a later age there is a thing whose flowing is exactly so, and it is called honey. Honey does not exist here. Honey comes of a creature that feeds on flowers, lives in a crowd, and tells its fellows things by dancing; and that creature appears a very long while after this place, and of the conditions its appearance requires this place has not one.

Which is to say: **to make plain how the metal of this place flows, one must borrow a thing this place could never possibly have.**

The whole difficulty of this book is here. Everything of this place, said only with the things of this place, cannot be said; said with the things of a later age, loses its own colour.

We choose to borrow.

Losing the colour is better than not being said.

And one thing must be set down here. The honey that flower-feeding creature makes is among the first things that the beings made out of clay ever called *sweet*. And there is no sweet here — no sweet meaning not that it is bitter, but that there is no mouth, and without a mouth no taste, and without taste neither word may be used.

So there is this scene, strange and true:

**To describe the flowing of metal in a world with no mouth, one must use the thing that made the first taste ever remembered in a world that has one.**

That the beings have a mouth, and taste, and can remember sweetness — all of it because something happened after this place.

And that something happened because the last one here, at the last, did a thing.

At this hour that has not happened. At this hour there is only metal running, like a thing this place will never have.

---

Heat cannot be read.

This is the crux of this section, and a great joint of this book.

It has been said: light can be read, because light bends at cleavage and the angle of bending carries the orientation of the plane, so that the light going out carries the form of the whole.

Heat is otherwise.

The wavelength of heat is far longer than the spacing of the cleavage planes here. Whatever has a wavelength far longer than the spacing of an obstacle does not bend at it, does not scatter from it, does not disclose its structure — it merely passes through, or is absorbed, and both the passing and the absorbing carry no news whatever of what was passed through.

So no lattice can read another by heat.

So no lattice can read itself by heat.

So all reading here must borrow light; and the light is very slight, its power not a small fraction of what this place puts out of itself.

From which follows a thing that is the whole predicament of this place in miniature:

**The lattices here can read everything except the thing that holds them up.**

They can read one another, entire, nothing omitted. They can read the slurry, and the heights of the tide, and the layer a dead one left upon its neighbour.

And that which comes up from below, sustaining their growth, determining their tide, and thereby determining every one of their thoughts — that they cannot read.

Not that they did not try. They tried.

---

They tried.

The trace of it remains in the record of this place, and the trace is in the deep layers of several bodies: for a time, certain lattices grew within themselves a layer of a particular kind, its orientations packed extremely close, the spacing approaching the wavelength of heat.

It was an instrument. The instrument had one use: to make heat refract within it.

If it worked, heat could be read. If heat could be read, the source of this place's power could be traced, and the incomputable thing could be computed.

Those layers are still inside those bodies.

The layers grown after them do not accord with those above and below — that is the mark of regrowth.

They took it down.

The reason is not recorded. And what is not recorded here is often what needed no recording — of a thing that failed, the failing is itself the finding, and nothing further need be written.

We computed that instrument afterwards.

It could not have worked. The wavelength of heat and the spacing of cleavage differ by more than three orders of magnitude, and under the physical conditions of this place the planes cannot be packed closer — packed closer, the structure is unstable, and unstable, it collapses back into slurry of itself.

The thing they attempted was impossible in principle.

**And they attempted it.**

And it is the only occasion in the whole record of this place on which anyone tried to read a thing that is unreadable in principle.

Those few lattices — their names cannot be known, there being no names here. Their bodies afterwards returned to the slurry. That stretch of extremely close layers they had grown dispersed into the slurry and left no mark.

Only the mark of their regrowth remained, in the shapes of their neighbours — a lattice's regrowth being visible to its neighbour.

So there are lattices here in one of whose layers is held: **so-and-so tried to read the thing that cannot be read, and took it down.**

Those neighbours died too.

And when they died, their shapes were read by their neighbours in turn.

Layer within layer, generation upon generation. The archive of this place is one another.

So the thing is, at the end, still here.

And not one of those who knew it is.

---

Another name for heat.

Here we must say a thing that could not be said in this place, saying it requiring a concept this place does not have.

Heat is **energy that has forgotten where it came from.**

A portion of power, if its form is ordered, has a traceable origin: look at its form and you know what it came out of. And the form of heat has no order — heat is precisely what is left of ordered power once its order has gone out of it. It does not remember that it was once the reconstruction of a core; does not remember where it was; does not remember how long it rose. It has only a magnitude left.

Because it remembers nothing, it cannot be read.

**Unreadable and already forgotten are the same thing.**

And everything here stands upon it.

The lattices here: their thought all takes form, their forms are all readable, their whole lives are held in themselves and in their neighbours, not one page lost — a civilisation that never forgets.

And what holds that never-forgetting civilisation up is forgetting itself.

No lattice here said this. In a later age another kind of being will come upon it by another road — a being that will be given a gift, and the gift will be forgetting, and the giver will consider it its finest work.

That the gift is its finest work, and that the heat here holds everything up, are the same reason.

And at this hour, nobody knows.

At this hour the heat rises from beneath, having risen a very long while. It reaches the face of the slurry, and the face lifts a little. The lattices upon it open their interstices and take in what they take in.

They grow a layer. In that layer an idea is held.

That idea can be read.

The heat that made it possible cannot.
