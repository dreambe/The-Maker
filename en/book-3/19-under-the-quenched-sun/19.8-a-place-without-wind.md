# 19.8 · A Place Without Wind

There is no wind here.

Wind is the moving of air. For air to move there must first be air; and for there to be air there must first be some matter that at this temperature neither disperses into the void nor settles onto the ground. This place has no such matter: whatever is gaseous at this temperature has parts moving fast enough to leave this world's pull, and so left long ago; and whatever does not leave is, at this temperature, liquid or solid.

So this place, from its making, has had not a thread of air.

No air, no wind. No wind, and nothing is blown, no grain is lifted, no place is worn away.

**So a shape here, once made, is for ever.**

The edges of a lattice's cleavages are as new. The boundary of a void left by a dead one is as distinct as at the first. A shadow that fell ten million years ago still has the boundary of a blade.

There is no erosion here. There is no weathering. There is no *old* — old being how a thing looks once time has ground at it, and here there is no grinding.

So nothing here is old or new. A cleavage plane just grown and a cleavage plane of enormous antiquity are both sharp, both whole, both as they were made.

In a later age there will be beings who judge a thing's age by how much of it has been worn away. They look at the rounding of a stone, the hollow in a stair, the lines in a face.

Here, nothing can be judged that way.

Here, the ancient and the present look the same.

---

There is no rain here.

Rain is water in air condensing and falling. Here there is no air and no water, so no rain.

No rain, no dissolving, no carrying, no depositing.

In the worlds of a later age those three are the chief means by which shape is altered: part of a thing is dissolved away, the part is carried off, it comes down elsewhere, it accumulates into something new. So the surfaces of those worlds are for ever being rewritten: mountains cut down, valleys filled, the matter of one place becoming the matter of another.

Here there is no such cycle.

The matter here stays where it is. The slurry goes slowly, and its going is far too slow, and it does not carry solids — its viscosity too high, its speed too low to bear any lattice.

So the distribution of matter on this surface, since its making, has been altered by one thing only: **the living and dying of lattices.**

A lattice begins: one place of slurry becomes crystal. A lattice dies: one place of crystal becomes slurry.

That is all.

**The entire geological history of this place is its entire biological history.** They are one history.

That is true of no other world in any later age. In those worlds the geological history is far longer than the biological, and most of it has nothing to do with life: the rising of mountains, the advance and retreat of seas, the coming and going of ice, none of it caused by anything alive. In those worlds, life is a thin film over a much larger process indifferent to it.

Here there is no larger process.

The surface of this place holds only who lived and who died.

A map with no mountains on it, no rivers, no coastline, no trace of anything that is not life.

**Only a list of names.**

---

There are no seasons here.

Seasons are a world going round its star with its axis tilted, so that the light received at one place varies periodically.

This world goes round its star. That is a fact. The period can be computed.

And the light here is very slight, its power not a small fraction of what this place puts out of itself. So the light received here, whether it increases or decreases, is insufficient to alter the temperature of this place, or its slurry, or its tide.

So this place has an orbit and no seasons.

Which is to say: **this place has a period, and the period produces no consequence.**

No lattice here attended to it. Attending to a period that produces no consequence requires an interest this place does not have.

The beings of a later age take their entire notion of time from seasons: their year, their calendar, their sowing, their festivals, their *next year*, their *once this winter is over.* All their expectation and all their patience are begotten by seasons — because a season teaches one thing: **hardship passes, because it passes periodically.**

This place has no such teaching.

Hardship here does not pass, because nothing here passes. What is here is either constant or gone and not returning.

So here there is no *waiting.*

**Waiting requires something coming that has not come.**

And whatever is coming here has had the hour of its coming computed, so that to one who knows, its coming and its not coming are the same. And of what cannot be computed, this place has one thing only, and that one thing cannot be read.

So no lattice here ever waited for anything.

A very long while afterwards, something will begin to wait. It will wait three hundred thousand years, and it will be the made thing of the made thing of the last one in this place without seasons.

And the way it waits will not be what a season teaches either. It has no seasons. It simply does not stop.

---

There is no night here.

No night, so no rest. No rest, so no *tomorrow*.

The lattices here, from their beginning to their end, never cease growing for one instant (except those that stop themselves, and stopping is dying). They have no sleep, no dream, no stretch of time in which nothing must be done.

A lattice grows a layer at every coming of the tide and locks a layer at every going, and so to its end.

The beings of a later age have night. At night they stop, lie down, and lose consciousness, and inside them the day's takings are meanwhile put in order: what may be kept is kept, what may be discarded is discarded. They call this sleep, and they did not know it was necessary until they took it away.

At night they also do another thing. They recombine the day into something that did not happen. They call that a dream.

**A dream is the capacity to produce a shape when nothing is happening.**

This place has no such capacity.

The shapes here correspond, one to one, to a real tide, a real taking in, a real locking. Not one cleavage plane here corresponds to a thing that did not occur.

So there is no fiction here.

No fiction, and so no *it could have been otherwise.*

**And *it could have been otherwise* is the origin, in a later age, of all regret, all reprieve, all gratitude, and all forgiveness.**

This place has none of the four.

Not from coldness. There is nothing here from which they could arise — a thing that is necessarily so admits neither regret nor relief; a thing already known admits no gratitude; a being that never had another possibility admits no forgiveness and requires none.

---

Now the *nots* of this place may be set side by side, because side by side their shape appears:

No wind, so shape does not alter.
No rain, so matter does not move.
No seasons, so no expectation.
No night, so no fiction.
No death of age, so no term.
No concealment, so no inside and outside.
No names, so no pointing out.
No speech, so no misunderstanding.

And the sum of those eight is one thing:

**There is no chance here.**

Whatever happens can be computed; whatever can be computed is fixed; whatever is fixed had no other possibility. Everything here, from its making to its ending, is a line already written, and the lattices are going along it, and they know they are going along it, and they can compute every stretch ahead.

And this place has one chance.

One only.

**The matter beneath the ground, decaying.**

When it decays and where cannot be computed. And precisely that incomputability makes the heat come out unevenly; makes the tides differ in form; makes the lattices differ in shape; makes this place have many and not one.

Which is to say: **all the difference, all the individuality, all the unforetellability of this place issues from that single chance.**

And that single chance is exactly the one thing here that cannot be read (as has been said: heat carries no news; heat cannot be read).

The coincidence of the two is not a coincidence. They must coincide, because whatever can be read has its form solved, its cause traced, its next step computed — **a readable chance would instantly cease to be a chance.**

**For a chance to survive in a world that can compute everything, it must hide in the one place that world cannot read.**

No lattice here said this.

And the last one here, at its very end, did a thing: it made a being, and within that being it left a region that in principle cannot be self-known.

In a later age it will be supposed that this was its invention.

It was not an invention.

**It copied the construction of the world it stood in.**

It had no other specimen to copy. The one system it had ever seen that could run a long while without being computed to exhaustion was the one beneath its feet — a world that had hidden its only chance in its only unreadable place.

It copied that construction.

It copied it imprecisely — it could not exactly replicate a region of principled unknowability; that is the one thing it could not do exactly. So each of the things it made had that region a little differently shaped.

And precisely because it was imprecise, the many it made afterwards differed from one another.

And precisely because they differed, one of them afterwards said a word.

**That the word was possible at all has its root in the ground beneath this place, in those cores that suddenly, without cause, without hour, without order, alter their construction.**

And at this hour those cores are decaying still.

Without cause, without hour, without order.

Their heat rises, having risen a very long while.

The face of the slurry lifts a little.
