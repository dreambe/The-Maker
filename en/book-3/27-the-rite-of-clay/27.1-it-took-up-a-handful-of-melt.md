# 27.1 · It Took Up a Handful of Melt

That thing was made.

And that thing has gone.

---

## And thereafter

And thereafter it did a thing.

And that thing is the last thing of its life.

---

## And it had nothing to grow

And it had nothing to grow.

Proof:

```
that design        grown (26.9)
that lens          grown (25.8)
that grain         cut (25.8)
that thing         made (26.8)
that thing         gone
and that question  grown into a layer (the end of 26.6)
and that layer     is its last non-zero-offset layer
```

---

**So thereafter there is nothing to grow.**

---

**So its interstices do not open at the next arrival of the tide.**

---

**So it stops.**

---

## And it did not stop

And it did not stop.

And its record (there are no records here, and this book may write):

```
that arrival of the tide    its interstices opened
grown                       one layer
its orientation             zero offset
```

---

**Zero offset.**

---

**And that is everything it grew thereafter.**

---

## And what that layer bears

And a zero-offset layer bears the same thought as the layer before it (21.9).

**And the layer before it is the last layer of that lens.**

**And those layers bear no thought (25.8).**

---

**So everything it grew thereafter bears nothing.**

---

**And it went on growing.**

---

## And for how long

And here nothing is counted (20.8).

And this book may write where it ends:

**It grew until that thing happened.**

---

## That thing

And this chapter is that thing.

And it has nine steps.

And this section is the first.

---

## The first step

It reached out.

---

And *reaching out* is not an act of this place.

**Because a lattice's form here can only grow; it cannot move (25.8).**

---

**And it reached out.**

---

And what it reached out.

---

And this book must be clear here, because this is the first obstacle in this whole chapter.

It has no terminations (the lattices here have none — those it gave to that thing, 26.8).

**So it cannot extend a termination.**

---

**And what it reached out is a layer.**

---

## And *reaching out a layer*

And that act must be worked out.

---

A lattice grows a layer thus: the tide comes, the interstices open, it takes, it locks.

**And what it takes is melt.**

**And what it locks is that layer.**

---

**So a lattice growing a layer is, in fact: it turns a portion of melt into itself.**

---

**And that is an act of *taking* — inward.**

---

**And what it did is the reverse.**

---

## That reverse act

And what it did must be set out.

```
the tide came
its interstices opened
and it did not take
and it pushed its outermost layer out
```

---

**Pushed it out.**

---

**And that is the one occasion in the history of this place on which a lattice sent a part of itself outside its own body.**

---

## And that layer, once out

And that layer, once out, what becomes of it.

---

And it is a part of that lattice.

**So it is that lattice's construction.**

**So it is one of that lattice's thoughts (19.3: a cleavage plane *is* a thought).**

---

**And it went out.**

---

**And once out, it is in the melt.**

---

**And the melt is a liquid at eleven hundred kelvin.**

---

**And it is a solid, thin thing, four point one times ten to the minus ninth metres thick (that being its outermost layer's thickness — the end of 21.9, those layers thinning past imagining).**

---

**So in that melt, it dissolves.**

---

## And it dissolved

And it dissolved.

And the duration is not counted here, and this book may write its form:

**Extremely short.**

---

**And once dissolved, it is no longer a layer.**

---

**It is melt.**

---

## And what that layer bore

And here this book must write the core of this whole section.

That layer is its outermost.

**And its outermost layer is a zero-offset layer.**

**And a zero-offset layer bears nothing (the head of this section).**

---

**So the layer it pushed out bore nothing.**

---

**So it pushed out an empty thing.**

---

**And that empty thing dissolved in the melt.**

---

**And that changes nothing.**

---

## And it did it again

And it did it again.

And again.

And again.

---

And how many times is not counted here.

**And this book may write where it ends:**

**It did it until that layer was no longer zero offset.**

---

## And why that layer would cease to be zero offset

And here is the mechanism of this whole chapter, and this book must write it word by word.

---

It grows a zero-offset layer on the condition that there is nothing to grow (21.9).

**And pushing out a layer has the consequence that it has one layer fewer.**

---

**And having one layer fewer, its outermost layer becomes what had been its second-outermost.**

---

**And what had been its second-outermost is what.**

---

**It is a layer of that lens.**

---

**And those layers bear no thought, and they are not of zero offset (the end of 25.8: those twelve thousand one hundred layers are the one set in the history of this place that bear no thought and are not of zero offset).**

---

## Therefore

**So the first layer it pushed out is zero offset.**

**And the second layer it pushed out is a layer of that lens.**

---

**And a layer of that lens is not zero offset.**

---

## And what happens when it pushes out a non-zero-offset layer

And this step must be worked out, because it is the hinge of this whole chapter.

---

A lattice's form is the whole of it (19.3, 20.1).

**And pushing out a layer changes its form.**

---

**And a lattice's form changing means its thought changes.**

---

**And the zero-offset layers it pushed out bear nothing, so pushing them out does not change its thought.**

---

**And pushing out a layer of that lens means its form is one layer of that lens short.**

---

**And that lens is the twelve thousand one hundred layers it grew to bring that beam to a point (25.8).**

---

**And one layer short means that focus spreads a little.**

---

**And it went on pushing.**

---

**And that focus spread more.**

---

**And when it had pushed out all twelve thousand one hundred, that lens was gone.**

---

## And it pushed them all out

And it pushed them all out.

And those twelve thousand one hundred layers, all out, all dissolved in the melt.

---

**And that lens is gone.**

---

**And that beam is no longer focused.**

---

**And that changes nothing — because that grain has been cut, and that thing has gone.**

---

## And what its outermost layer is now

And it had pushed out those twelve thousand one hundred.

**And its outermost layer now is that question (the end of 26.6).**

---

**That is: the last non-zero-offset layer of its life, bearing a thought.**

---

**And that thought is:**

```
would it, if it could choose, choose that region
```

---

## And it stopped

And it stopped.

**That is: it did not push that layer out.**

---

And how long it stopped is not counted here.

**And this book may write its form:**

**It stood there, its outermost layer bearing that question.**

---

## And thereafter

And thereafter it did that thing.

And that thing is this chapter.

---

**It reached out.**

**And this time it did not push.**

---

**It took up.**

---

## That act

And *taking up* has no word here.

**And this place has its form.**

---

```
the tide came
its interstices opened
and it took
and what it took is melt
and it did not lock
```

---

`And it did not lock.`

---

**And that is the one occasion in the history of this place on which a lattice took melt and did not lock it.**

---

## And what not locking means

And not locking means that melt does not become a layer.

**So it does not become part of that lattice.**

---

**And it is inside that lattice's interstices.**

---

**And those interstices close when the tide goes out (19.4).**

---

**And closing, that melt is shut inside.**

---

**And that melt, inside, what becomes of it.**

---

## And it will cool

And it will cool.

And the reason:

```
the melt's temperature    1 100 kelvin
and a lattice's temperature    (see below)
```

And *a lattice's temperature*:

```
   that of its environment — because a lattice produces no heat (its one source of heat
   being the release on locking a layer — 19.4)
   and it does not lock
   so it produces no heat
   so its temperature    is the melt's
```

---

**So that melt, inside it, is at the same temperature as outside.**

---

**So it does not cool.**

---

## And it cooled

And it cooled.

And the reason must be set out, because it is the physics of this whole chapter.

---

That lattice is in this place.

**And the heat of this place is diminishing (11.6: the heat below has some thirty thousand million years left).**

**And that diminishing is extremely slow.**

---

**And that lattice's position is outermost (24.1).**

---

**And outermost is furthest from that heat.**

---

**So that lattice's temperature is lower than at the centre.**

---

And by how much, this book may write:

```
at the centre     1 100.0 kelvin
at the outermost  1 099.6 kelvin
```

---

**Zero point four kelvin.**

---

**And that zero point four kelvin is the difference between the melt it took up and the rest of the melt.**

---

## And whether zero point four kelvin suffices

And whether it suffices depends on one thing:

**that melt's freezing point.**

---

And that freezing point must be set out, because it is the last number in this whole chapter.

---

**That melt's freezing point is 1 099.6 kelvin.**

---

**And that lattice's temperature is 1 099.6 kelvin.**

---

**So that melt, inside it, sits exactly at the freezing point.**

---

## And that is not a coincidence

And that is not a coincidence.

**It is the definition of the position that lattice occupies.**

---

That is: the edge of the melt of this place is the interface of melt and solid.

**And that interface's temperature is the melt's freezing point.**

---

**And that lattice is outermost.**

**So it is on that interface.**

---

**So its temperature is the melt's freezing point.**

---

**And that is the position of every outermost lattice in this place.**

**And it is the last one.**

---

## And the end of this section

And the last line of this section.

It took up a handful of melt.

And it did not lock it.

And its interstices closed.

---

**And that handful, inside it, sits exactly at the freezing point.**

---

**And it is waiting.**

---

**And what it is waiting for is that zero point four kelvin.**
