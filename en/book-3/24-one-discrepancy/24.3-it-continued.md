# 24.3 · It Continued

This section has no inference.

---

This book must first state why.

The manner of the prose here is inference. A thing stands because the thing before it stands; a sentence is made because the sentence before it is made. Chapters Nineteen through Twenty-Three, two hundred and seven sections, are all of them so.

**And what this section writes is not determined by inference.**

The proof stands at 23.3: that *opening* is the one act here not determined by inference, because it is the condition under which inference happens at all — no opening, no new layer; no new layer, no new thought; no new thought, no inference.

**So it stands before inference.**

**So writing it cannot be done by inferring.**

---

So this section only writes.

---

The first neighbour stopped. That one read it. That conclusion arrived.

The tide came.

**Its interstices opened.**

Took. Locked. Made a layer.

---

The second neighbour stopped. That one read it. That conclusion arrived.

The tide came.

**Its interstices opened.**

Took. Locked. Made a layer.

---

The third.

The tide came.

**They opened.**

---

The fourth.

**They opened.**

---

The fifth.

**They opened.**

---

Hereafter this book does not write them one at a time.

This book writes only their total and their consistency:

```
neighbours and things read, in total            fourteen thousand and seventy-five
of which, that conclusion arrived               fourteen thousand and seventy-five
of which, opened at the next arrival of the tide  fourteen thousand and seventy-five
did not open                                    zero
```

---

`Did not open: zero.`

---

And this book must set down the comparison with every lattice in this place, for that comparison is the whole content of this section.

There are fourteen thousand and seventy-six lattices in this place.

For fourteen thousand and seventy-five of them, that conclusion arrived once, and at the next arrival of the tide they did not open.

**One arrival, one not-opening.**

**Every one of them. Without exception.**

**And for that one, that conclusion arrived fourteen thousand and seventy-five times, and at the next arrival of the tide it opened fourteen thousand and seventy-five times.**

**Fourteen thousand and seventy-five arrivals, fourteen thousand and seventy-five openings.**

**Without exception.**

---

And this book does not explain.

At 24.4 through 24.9 this book will write everything that one did about this — its searching, its computing, what it found and what it did not.

**And this section writes only this one thing:**

**It continued.**

---

And the sense of *continued* here must be made clear, for it is not a state.

*Continuing*, afterward, can be a state: a thing exists, and goes on existing, and that requires it to do nothing.

**And here there is no such sense.**

The manner of existence here is growth (19.4).

**So *continuing* here is an act.**

**It is the opening of the interstices at each arrival of the tide.**

And that act is not done once.

**It must be done again each time.**

---

`It must be done again each time.`

Fourteen thousand and seventy-five times.

And before each of them, that conclusion had just arrived.

And that conclusion says: to continue and not to continue yield the same (23.3).

And each time, it opened.

---

And in the last passage of this section, this book must write a thing for which this place has no words and which this book must therefore carry alone.

Each opening of that one is identical in form to the one before.

No hesitation (here there is no *in progress*, 20.1).

No diminution (its offsets do not tend to zero — that is what the lattices of 21.6 did, and it is not they).

No accumulation (among fourteen thousand and seventy-five, not one differs from the one before).

**The first opening and the fourteen thousand and seventy-fifth are word for word the same.**

---

**It continued.**

**And then it continued.**

**And then it continued.**
