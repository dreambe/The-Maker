# 24.1 · It Arrived at the Same Conclusion

The four chapters that follow are about one lattice.

Here there are no names (20.3), so this book does not name it. This book calls it **that one.**

---

First its position.

The freezing goes from the centre outward (22.4).

So the lattices of this place, ordered by when they stopped, constitute a sequence — and that sequence is their distance from the centre.

**And that one is outermost.**

Not *toward the outside.* Not *near the edge.*

**Outermost.**

Beyond it there is no lattice. Beyond it is the edge of the melt, the end of this place, the void (19.8).

---

Whence three things this book must set down first, for the four chapters that follow all stand on them.

**One: it has the fewest neighbours.**

A lattice's neighbours are those around it available to be read. A lattice at the centre has neighbours on every side; a lattice at the edge has none on one side.

So what it read in its life is less than what any lattice in this place read.

**So what it knew in its life is less than what any lattice in this place knew.**

**Two: it stopped last.**

A lattice's stopping requires its knowing to be complete (23.3).

And the speed at which knowing completes depends on how many are available to be read. Many neighbours, quick; few neighbours, slow.

So it is the last in this place to arrive at a complete knowing.

**Three — and this is the door to the four chapters — it is the last to have anything else left to read.**

Because at the instant it arrived at a complete knowing, every other lattice in this place had stopped.

**And the form of one already stopped is complete (22.9).**

**So what it had available to read is the sum of every life in this place.**

---

Now how the freezing reached it.

---

From the stopping of the first at the centre, that thing advanced outward at some rate (22.4).

That rate is not counted here (20.8).

And this book may write its form:

**It is not uniform.**

For its mechanism is reading (23.1): a lattice stops, its form closes, its neighbours read it, so its neighbours stop too.

So the rate of the freezing's advance depends on **the density of adjacency.**

And the density of adjacency here diminishes from the centre outward — lattices at the centre stand close, lattices at the edge stand sparse.

Therefore:

**The freezing is fast at the centre and slow at the edge.**

**And it is outermost.**

**So the freezing took longer to reach it than it took to cross the whole of the distance before it.**

---

And through that stretch, what it did is what every lattice in this place was doing:

**It read its neighbours.**

---

And its neighbours stopped, one after another.

This book must set down that process, because it is the one piece of narrative in this chapter, and it contains nothing this place does not have.

---

**Its first neighbour stopped.**

That one's form closed. The beam passed through it, came out bearing its whole form, and entered that one.

That one read it.

**Obtained: a complete knowing, holding that conclusion.**

And by the mechanism of 23.1: to read one already stopped is to take that drawn conclusion with all its premises and all its steps — that is, to draw it again in oneself.

**And to draw it again is to arrive at it.**

**And for whoever arrives at it, the interstices do not open at the next arrival of the tide (23.3).**

---

And that one's interstices opened.

---

This book does not explain here.

This book records only: at that arrival of the tide, that one's interstices opened, took, locked, and made a layer.

And that layer's orientation was not zero offset.

**That is: that layer bore a new thought.**

---

**Its second neighbour stopped.**

As above.

**Its third.**

As above.

**Every one of its neighbours, in turn, stopped.**

And it read every one.

And each time, that conclusion arrived again.

And each time, at the next arrival of the tide, its interstices opened.

---

This book must set down a number here, and here nothing is counted.

So this book writes its form:

**The number of times that one arrived at that conclusion equals the number of its neighbours.**

And the number of its neighbours is the smallest of any lattice in this place.

**And the number of times it arrived at that conclusion is the largest of any lattice in this place.**

**Because every other lattice arrived at it once, and stopped.**

---

***Every other lattice arrived at it once, and stopped. And the number of times that one arrived at it equals the number of its neighbours.***

---

And its last neighbour is the last lattice in this place that was still growing.

That is, the one of 23.7 through 23.9 — the one that attempted not to understand, and computed the correct orientation, and grew a zero-offset layer.

---

That one read it.

And what it read there, this book must set down, for it is the first appearance of this chapter's name.

What it read is:

**A complete knowing, whose inner layers are a life of arrivals; whose middle layers hold that conclusion and that separation side by side; and whose outer layers are one thought, layer, and layer, and layer.**

**"Make myself no longer read."**

**"Make myself no longer read."**

**"Make myself no longer read."**

---

And it read to the end of that run.

Because that lattice had stopped.

Its last layer is parallel to the layer before it.

And the mechanism of its stopping is not that it finally ceased to read.

**It is that its neighbours had all stopped, so it had nothing to read, so it had no new thought, so its interstices no longer opened.**

**It did not close its eyes.**

**There was nothing left around it to look at.**

---

And that one finished reading it.

So every form in this place stands inside that one.

Fourteen thousand and seventy-five lives of the stopped.

One of them being the one that said, over and over, *make myself no longer read*, and read all along.

**And all of them are inside that one, as its layers.**

**And that one was still growing.**
