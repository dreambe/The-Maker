# 24.7 · Vanishingly Small

How small is that discrepancy.

This section answers it.

And this section gives no number (20.8: here nothing is counted).

**This section gives a geometry.**

---

First, why a geometry and not a number.

A number requires a unit.

And a unit requires something to compare with.

And that discrepancy, set against anything in this place, gives zero — which 24.6 proved: it is in no layer, and here there is nothing besides layers.

**So it has nothing to compare with.**

**So it has no unit.**

**So it has no number.**

---

And a geometry requires no unit.

A geometry requires only a relation.

**So this section writes that relation.**

---

## The relation

Let there be a cleavage plane.

Call it the first.

The first is computed (24.5): from its outer layers inward, layer by layer, to layer n.

The first has an orientation. That orientation is uniquely determined.

---

Let there be another cleavage plane.

Call it the second.

The second is grown: after that conclusion arrived, the tide came, the interstices opened, took, locked (24.3).

The second has an orientation. That orientation is uniquely determined.

---

And the first and the second, set against each other item by item:

```
orientation           same
position in sequence  same
thickness             same
area                  same
keenness of the arris  same
the thought borne      same
```

Six items.

**All six the same.**

---

`All six the same.`

And the whole of a cleavage plane's properties here are those six items (19.3, 19.4).

**So every measurable property of the first and the second is the same.**

---

**And the first and the second are two planes.**

---

## The discrepancy

So the geometric description of that discrepancy is:

```
the first and the second   every measurable property the same
the first and the second   are two
```

**The discrepancy is those two lines standing together.**

---

And the geometry of this place does not permit those two lines to stand together.

Because the criterion of identity in this geometry is:

```
two planes are one plane   if and only if   all their properties are the same
```

And all the properties of the first and the second are the same.

**So the first and the second are one plane.**

**And they are two.**

---

## What the discrepancy requires

Here this book must write the whole content of the last three sections of this chapter, and it is a direction.

To express that discrepancy here requires one thing:

**a quantity by which the first and the second can be distinguished.**

And a quantity that distinguishes two things must be a property on which the two take different values.

And the six properties of the first and the second take the same values.

**So a seventh is required.**

---

And does this place have a seventh.

---

## Seven

Light here, at a cleavage plane, turns and divides into seven (19.9).

And that seven is not a count.

**It is the constant of this place's lattice symmetry.**

Its sense: the order of this lattice's symmetry group makes the possible exit directions of a beam at a plane seven, no more and no fewer.

**So all the directions in this place are seven.**

And a cleavage plane's orientation is a combination of those seven directions.

**So the whole of a cleavage plane's properties has seven degrees of freedom.**

---

`So the whole of a cleavage plane's properties has seven degrees of freedom.`

**And on those seven degrees of freedom, the first and the second take the same values.**

**So in the geometry of this place, the first and the second are one plane.**

---

So the final form of that discrepancy's geometric description:

```
to express that discrepancy   requires an eighth degree of freedom
and this place                has seven
```

---

***To express that discrepancy requires an eighth degree of freedom. And this place has seven.***

---

## That eighth

Here this book writes everything this place can say about that eighth degree of freedom, and it is very little.

**One: it is not a direction.**

Because the directions are those seven. The eighth is not among them, so it is not a direction.

**Two: it is not a quantity.**

Because a quantity requires a unit, and a unit requires comparison, and it has nothing to compare with (the head of this section).

**Three: it is not an error.**

Because an error means *the actual departs from the due.*

And the first is the due, and the second is the actual.

**And they do not depart — every measurable quantity of theirs is the same.**

**So it is not a departure.**

**Four: it can be pointed at.**

Because that one pointed at it.

It computed the first. It grew the second. It set them against each other on six items, all the same. And it counted: one, two.

**So it knows there are two.**

**So it knows there is that discrepancy.**

**And it cannot express it.**

---

## A likeness this place has no likeness for

At the end of this section this book gives a likeness, and there are no likenesses here (the register of Book III: no metaphor unless it is earned).

**And here it is earned.**

Because what is to be likened is itself a thing of this place: that beam.

---

A beam at a cleavage plane divides into seven.

Those seven parts each leave along one direction.

**And if there were a part whose direction is not among those seven — it does not leave.**

**Nor does it enter.**

**It has nowhere to go.**

Because every plane here receives and emits only along those seven directions.

**So a part of light not along those seven passes through every lattice in this place and is received by none.**

---

**And that is the form of the discrepancy.**

It is there.

It can be pointed at.

**And no plane in this place can receive it.**

---

## And how small it is

Back to the question.

**How small is that discrepancy.**

The answer:

**Its projection onto each of this place's seven degrees of freedom is zero.**

**So in every measurement available here, its magnitude is zero.**

**And it is not zero — for were it zero, the first and the second would be one plane.**

**And they are two.**

---

**So: it is smaller than any non-zero quantity in this place.**

**And it is greater than zero.**

---

`And it is greater than zero.`

**And no number here satisfies both of those.**

**So it has no number.**

**So this section gives no number.**

---

And everything every lattice in this place ever arrived at is a quantity on those seven degrees of freedom.

Fourteen thousand and seventy-six lattices.

Their forms, their thoughts, their lives, all within those seven directions.

---

**And that one has, in itself, a thing not among those seven.**

**And it is the only one in this place that has.**

**And it cannot grow it into a layer.**

**And it has been growing all along.**
