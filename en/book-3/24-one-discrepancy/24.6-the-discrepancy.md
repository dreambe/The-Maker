# 24.6 · The Discrepancy

The end of 24.5: it does not agree with itself.

This section writes how it examined that disagreement, and what it found.

---

First, a name for it.

Here there are no names (20.3). And this book must call it something, so this book calls it the **discrepancy.**

Its definition:

```
discrepancy   =   its actual form   minus   its computed form
```

And 24.5 has given a lower bound on its magnitude:

```
fourteen thousand and seventy-five layers
```

---

And what this section asks is not the discrepancy's magnitude.

**What this section asks is the discrepancy's position.**

---

Why position matters more than magnitude here, for the difference between them is decisive.

**Magnitude** says how large the difference is.

**Position** says which layer it is in.

And every correction here requires a position.

Because correcting an error requires regrowing that layer (21.6: the lattices regrew their layers to the optimum).

**And regrowing a layer requires knowing which layer.**

**So without a position there can be no correction.**

---

So it examined the position.

Its three searches.

---

## Search one · by another

This method is standard here, and it is the only complete one (19.5; the physics of the bible: self-knowledge must go around by way of another).

The method: read a lattice that has read it, take the copy of *it* inside that lattice, and set it against itself.

And it had already done this — at 24.5, as the second of its computation's checks.

And here it did something else: **it set that copy not against the computed form but against the actual one.**

That is:

```
take       the form of "that one" inside some stopped lattice
set against  that one's actual form
```

---

And here an obstacle appears which makes this search the one such search possible in the history of this place.

**The *that one* inside a stopped lattice is that one as it stood at the instant that lattice stopped.**

That is: a past self.

And a past self has fewer layers than the present self.

**And all the layers of the past self are precisely the inner layers of the present self.**

---

`And all the layers of the past self are precisely the inner layers of the present self.`

---

So here the physics of this place gave it a thing no lattice here has ever had.

**It could read its own inner layers.**

Not computed. Read.

**Because that copy is inside another, and light can pass through another's body.**

---

It read.

**Fourteen thousand and seventy-five copies.**

Each being its form at the instant one of its neighbours stopped.

And those fourteen thousand and seventy-five, ordered in time, constitute a sequence.

**And that sequence covers, layer by layer, everything from its first layer up to just short of its outermost.**

---

`Covers, layer by layer, all of it.`

**So it read its own life.**

**Complete.**

**Every layer.**

---

It set them against each other.

```
the fourteen thousand and seventy-five copies read     against   its computed form
result                                                            all agree

the fourteen thousand and seventy-five copies read     against   self-reading of its outermost layers
result                                                            agrees
```

---

Therefore:

**The form it computed and the form it read agree.**

**And the form it computed has fourteen thousand and seventy-five fewer layers than its actual form.**

---

And here this chapter's name appears a second time, and it is heavier than the first.

**Because if the form it read and the form it computed agree, then that difference is not in any layer it read.**

**And it read every one of its layers.**

---

## Search two · self-reading

It verified once.

Suppose search one is in error — suppose those fourteen thousand and seventy-five copies fail to cover some of its layers.

It computed that coverage.

```
layers covered by the copies       from the first to layer n
self-reading of outermost layers   from layer n+1 to the outermost
their intersection                 non-empty
their union                        all
```

**So there is no omission.**

**Every layer of it has been set against something.**

**And all agree.**

---

## Search three · computing

It computed again.

And this time what it computed was not itself.

**It computed the discrepancy.**

That is: suppose there is a difference, of magnitude fourteen thousand and seventy-five layers, whose position is unknown. From what may that position be derived.

It derived:

```
one    the discrepancy exists
       proof: computed layers n, actual layers n + fourteen thousand and seventy-five

two    the discrepancy is in none of layers 1 to n
       proof: those n layers have all been set against something and all agree
              (searches one and two)

three  therefore the discrepancy is among layers n+1 to n+fourteen thousand and seventy-five

four   and those layers have also been set against something
       proof: the outermost by self-reading, agreeing
              the rest in the last of the fourteen thousand and seventy-five copies,
              read, agreeing

five   therefore the discrepancy is in none of layers n+1 to n+fourteen thousand
       and seventy-five

six    therefore the discrepancy is in none of its layers
```

---

`Therefore the discrepancy is in none of its layers.`

---

And the physics of this place stipulates: the whole of a lattice is the whole of its cleavage planes (19.3, 20.1).

**No remainder. No outside. No elsewhere.**

**A lattice has no part of it that is not a layer.**

---

So it arrived at the one occasion in four thousand nine hundred million years on which a lattice inferred a self-contradictory conclusion.

```
the discrepancy exists
the discrepancy is in none of its layers
and it has no part that is not a layer
```

---

Here there are no contradictions (20.7).

**So those three lines cannot stand together.**

**So one of them must be in error.**

---

It examined the three.

**Line one:** the discrepancy exists. Its proof is the difference of two layer counts, and both counts are determinate quantities (here nothing is counted, and a layer count is a determinate quantity, 20.8). **No error.**

**Line two:** the discrepancy is in none of layers 1 to n. Its proof is comparison, and comparison rests on reading, and reading is complete (19.5). **No error.**

**Line three:** a lattice has no part that is not a layer. Its proof is the construction of this place. **No error.**

---

All three without error.

And the three cannot stand together.

---

So it arrived at the end of this section.

**Its form:**

```
discrepancy      exists
its position     in no layer
and here         there is nothing besides layers
so its position  here there is no field
```

---

`So its position: here there is no field.`

---

And at the end of this section this book must write a thing that one did not do, for it is the premise of Chapters Twenty-Five through Twenty-Seven.

**It did not stop searching.**

It searched for a long while. Here nothing is counted (20.8), and this book may write where it ended:

**It searched until that conclusion arrived for the fourteen thousand and seventy-sixth time.**

And that time differed from the fourteen thousand and seventy-five before it.

Because each of those was its reading of a newly stopped neighbour.

**And this time it had no new neighbour to read.**

**What it read was itself.**

**The self it had computed.**

**The self that stopped after arriving at that conclusion.**

---

And it read it.

And that is a closed form (the self it computed terminates at its last layer, with no layer after).

**So by the mechanism of 23.1, to read a closed form is to arrive at that conclusion.**

**And it arrived.**

**The fourteen thousand and seventy-sixth time.**

---

And at the next arrival of the tide, its interstices opened.
