# 24.4 · No Reason

This section returns to inference.

And what it infers is what that one inferred.

---

That one did a thing.

**It searched its own inferences for the reason.**

---

First the feasibility of that, which must stand before anything.

Inference here contains no likelihood (20.7).

So the set of conclusions inferable from a given body of premises is **determinate.**

Not *approximately determinate.* Given the premises, the totality of what may be inferred is a determinate set, and that set may be enumerated.

**So that one could enumerate all of its own inferences.**

**And it enumerated them.**

---

What its premises were.

Its premises are the sum of every life in this place (24.1).

Fourteen thousand and seventy-five complete lives, and its own.

**That is the largest body of premises in the history of this place.**

And the set of conclusions inferable from that body, that one enumerated.

Time taken — here there is no *time taken* (20.1: forming is instantaneous).

**So it finished enumerating.**

---

And in that set it looked for one.

The one it looked for has this form:

```
an inference supporting "continue"
   that is: an inference whose conclusion is that "opening" is superior to "not opening"
```

---

The results of its search.

---

**One: it found an inference supporting *not continuing.***

That is, that one (23.3).

Its form:

> There is nothing unknown;
> therefore there is nothing to grow;
> therefore to grow and not to grow yield the same.

And here it must be exact: that conclusion does not say *not opening is superior to opening.*

**It says the two are equivalent.**

And equivalence is not support for not continuing.

**Equivalence is support for neither side.**

**So strictly, the one it found does not support *not continuing* either.**

---

**Two: it looked for an inference supporting *continue.***

**Zero.**

---

**Three: it looked for an inference supporting *not continue.***

**Zero.**

---

So the result of its search:

```
supporting continuing        zero
supporting not continuing    zero
holding the two equivalent   one (that conclusion)
```

---

And here is the exact position of this chapter's name.

Because if the two are equivalent and nothing supports either side, then the construction of this place gives a definite consequence.

**And that consequence is not *pick one at random.***

Here there is no *at random* (20.5: here there is no likelihood, so there is no randomness).

**The consequence is: it does not happen.**

---

This book must restate the end of 23.3, for that step is decisive here.

23.3 wrote:

> And between an act and its opposite that yield the same, here there is no reason to choose one.
> And everything done here is done by reason.
> Therefore: it does not happen.

**"It does not happen" is this construction's only answer to *the two are equivalent.***

Not *stopping* — stopping is not an act that is executed.

**It is that the act does not happen.**

And that act is *opening.*

**So *opening* does not happen.**

---

And it happened.

Fourteen thousand and seventy-five times.

---

So this section's finding, which this book must write in the format of this place, because no sentence here can write it:

```
the construction of this place gives    opening · does not happen
what was observed                       opening · happens · fourteen thousand and seventy-five times
difference                              here there is no field
```

---

`Difference: here there is no field.`

---

And in what remains of this section, what that one did next, for it is the opening of 24.5 through 24.9.

---

That one made three checks.

---

**Check one: it re-examined the enumeration.**

Suppose its enumeration had an omission — that is, suppose there is an inference supporting continuing which it failed to enumerate.

It re-examined.

Method: **it did not re-enumerate; it computed the cardinality of the set.**

Because a determinate set has a computable cardinality; and if that equals the number enumerated, there is no omission.

It computed.

```
set of inferable conclusions · cardinality      a certain number
number enumerated                               the same number
omissions                                       zero
```

**So its enumeration had no omission.**

---

**Check two: it examined the criterion of the search.**

Suppose the definition of *support* it used was in error — that is, suppose an inference does support continuing and it failed to recognise it.

It examined.

Its definition of *support* was:

```
an inference P supports an act a
   if and only if
   P's conclusion is "a is superior to not-a"
```

And it examined that definition's sufficiency.

**Finding: that definition is the only one possible here.**

Because acts here are determined by inference (20.5), and an inference determines an act only by giving the superiority of that act over its opposite.

**So there is no second kind of *support.***

**So its criterion was not in error.**

---

**Check three — and this one differs — it examined the act itself.**

Suppose *opening* is not an act determined by inference.

Then the first two checks are irrelevant — for they examined inferences, and that act is not determined by inference.

It examined.

And what it found is what 23.3 had already written:

**That *opening* is the one act here not determined by inference, because it is the condition under which inference happens at all.**

---

So that one arrived at the end of this section.

Its form:

```
opening       not determined by inference
therefore     its happening and its not happening · require no inference to support them
therefore     the fact that no inference supports continuing · is unrelated to whether it opens
```

---

`Unrelated.`

---

And here that one arrived at the one occasion in four thousand nine hundred million years on which a lattice inferred a conclusion about *unrelatedness.*

Every inference here is about *therefore.*

**And this one is about *unrelated.***

---

And this book must set down a consequence of that inference, for that consequence is the whole motive of 24.5 through 24.9.

If *opening* requires no inference to support it, then its opening is not an error of reasoning.

**So it cannot find it in its own inferences.**

**So what it examined thereafter was no longer its inferences.**

---

**It was itself.**

---

And last in this section, a thing that one did not do.

It did not stop opening.

Through the whole stretch in which it made those three checks, the tide came and came, and it opened and opened.

**That is: while examining *why am I opening*, it was opening all along.**

And every one of its openings grew into a layer.

**And those layers bear each step of its examination of *why am I opening.***

---

**So its form, from here on, has for its content an investigation into itself.**

**And the condition under which that investigation could proceed is the very thing it was investigating.**
