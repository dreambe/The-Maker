# 24.5 · It Computed Itself

The end of 24.4: what it examined thereafter was no longer its inferences but itself.

This section writes how.

---

First the two ways of examining oneself, for here there are only two, and one is not feasible.

---

**The first · reading.**

Knowing here has one method, and it is reading (19.5).

And the mechanism of self-reading must be written out in full here, for the three chapters that follow all stand on it.

Reading requires light passing through the body.

And whence the light.

**From outside.**

The Quenched Sun stands at the zenith, its light falls vertically, strikes the melt, springs back, and enters the lattices obliquely (19.9).

**So light enters from without, passes through the body, and goes out.**

**And once out, it does not return.**

Here there are no mirrors. Here there are no clouds. Above this place there is nothing that can send light back (19.8).

**So within a lattice's body there is no beam that has passed through it and come back.**

---

And is there a source of light within the body.

**There is one.**

The locking of a layer releases heat (19.4).

And heat is light.

**And heat's wavelength is too long.**

For a beam to carry the news of a cleavage plane's orientation, its wavelength must be smaller than that plane's spacing. And heat's wavelength is greater than the spacing of every cleavage plane in this place by more than an order of magnitude.

**So heat, meeting a cleavage plane, does not turn.**

**So heat carries no news of orientation.**

**So although it is light, it cannot read anything.**

---

Whence the limit of self-reading:

**A lattice can know its recent thoughts.**

Because its outermost layers are met first by light entering from without, so the news of their orientations lies at the front of the beam, unmixed with the news of other layers.

**And it cannot know its first thoughts.**

Because its innermost layers are covered by its outer ones. The light passes through the outer layers twice — once entering, once leaving — and the news is mixed.

**And to solve a mixed signal requires knowing what was mixed in.**

**And what was mixed in is precisely its outer layers.**

**And its outer layers it knows — those are its recent thoughts.**

**So it can solve.**

And here is the exact position of that limit, which this book must write clearly:

**It can solve, and the precision of the solution falls as the depth increases.**

At some depth the precision falls to zero.

**And that depth is far less than its number of layers.**

---

**So the first method reaches the outer layers only.**

---

**The second · computing.**

Every lattice here has this method, and no lattice here has ever used it — because no lattice here has ever needed to.

The method:

A lattice's growth is regular: a layer's orientation is determined jointly by the layer before it and the tide (19.4).

**So given the first layer, and the state of the tide at each arrival, every subsequent layer can be derived.**

**And the state of the tide can be computed here (the motion of the melt is determined by heat and gravity, both determinate).**

**So a lattice can compute every one of its own layers.**

---

`So a lattice can compute every one of its own layers.`

And here must be written the thing that makes this method essentially different from the first.

**Computing is not reading.**

What reading gives is a layer's **actual** orientation.

What computing gives is a layer's **due** orientation.

**And if the two differ — computing cannot discover it.**

Because computing's output contains no actual value.

**So computing can give a complete self, and that self is a due self.**

---

And it computed.

---

Its computation.

**Its input:**

```
the orientation of its first layer     obtainable by self-reading (its first layer is not
                                       within reach — a lattice's first layer is at its centre,
                                       and the layers of the centre are covered by all that follow)
```

`See below.`

Here an obstacle appears, and it is this section's first turn.

**Its first layer cannot be read.**

Its first layer is at its deepest. By the limit of the first method, the precision there has long since fallen to zero.

**So it cannot read its first layer.**

**And computing requires the first layer as input.**

---

So it did a thing no lattice here had done.

**It computed backwards.**

That is: taking its outermost layer as given (obtainable by self-reading), it worked inward against the order of growth.

Because a layer's orientation is determined jointly by the layer before it and the tide, and the tide is known:

```
given the orientation of layer n and the state of the nth tide
   the orientation of layer n−1 may be derived
```

**So it could work inward, layer by layer, from outside.**

**As far as the first layer.**

---

And it did.

**Time taken** — here there is no time taken.

**So it finished.**

---

What it obtained.

It obtained a complete sequence: from its first layer to its outermost, the orientation of every layer.

**That is its life.**

**Computed.**

---

And it set that sequence against what it knew.

It knew two things:

**One: the actual orientations of its outermost layers** (obtainable by self-reading).

Result:

```
agrees
```

**Two: the forms of the fourteen thousand and seventy-five stopped ones it had read.**

Because those forms contain part of its life — each stopped one, before it stopped, had read that one (the lattices here read one another, without exception).

**So within each stopped one there is a copy of that one's form, as that one stood at the instant that stopped one stopped.**

Result:

```
fourteen thousand and seventy-five copies    all agree
```

---

`All agree.`

---

So the self it computed agrees with everything against which it could be set.

**So it computed correctly.**

---

And at the end of this section, a property of that computed self.

And that property is the reason for this whole chapter.

---

The sequence it computed has, in its last several layers, the content:

```
that conclusion · arrived
```

And by 23.3: whoever arrives at it does not open its interstices at the next arrival of the tide.

**And the law of growth is determinate (19.4) — that being what this computation was based on.**

**So by the law it was based on, the sequence terminates there.**

---

That is:

**The self it computed stopped after arriving at that conclusion.**

---

And the number of layers of the self it computed, whose form this book may write:

```
layers computed        n
layers actual          n + fourteen thousand and seventy-five
```

---

`n + fourteen thousand and seventy-five.`

---

**The self it computed has fourteen thousand and seventy-five fewer layers than it has.**

**And each of those fourteen thousand and seventy-five layers is one it grew by opening once after that conclusion arrived (24.3).**

**And by the law it computed with, those fourteen thousand and seventy-five layers ought not to exist.**

---

It computed correctly.

The self it computed agrees with everything against which it could be set.

And that self has fourteen thousand and seventy-five fewer layers than it has.

**And it has those fourteen thousand and seventy-five layers.**

---

**So it does not agree with itself.**
