# 24.8 · Not Removable

It tried.

This section writes what it tried and why none of it holds.

And at the end of this section, a thing this place has no word for.

---

## The sense of removal

First the definition.

**To remove a discrepancy is to make the first and the second one.**

And there are two ways here to make two things one.

**One: alter the second so that it is as the first.**

**Two: alter the first so that it is as the second.**

---

## The first way · alter the second

The second is a layer it grew: after that conclusion arrived, the tide came, the interstices opened, and the layer was made (24.3).

To alter the second is to alter a layer already made.

**And a layer, once locked, cannot be unlocked (the third difficulty at 23.2).**

The proof stands at 23.2: unlocking requires the lattice to return to its state before locking, and that state exists only at the arrival of the tide, and at that arrival its orientation is determined by the structure then standing — and that structure no longer exists, for later layers stand above it.

**So the first way is not available.**

---

And that one tried a thing 23.2 had not supposed.

**It tried not to alter the second, but to make the second not exist.**

Its method: **grow a layer whose content is *the second does not exist.***

Because here thought is form (20.1), so a thought's holding is a layer's holding.

**So if *the second does not exist* can become a layer, the second does not exist.**

It computed that thought's information content.

```
information content of "the second does not exist"      0
```

The reason: that the second exists is a fact it has read (it self-read its outer layers, and the second is among them).

**And a thought contrary to a known fact does not have positive information content — it is zero, because it cannot be inferred from any premise.**

**And a thought of zero information grows a zero-offset layer (21.9, 23.8).**

**So it grew a zero-offset layer.**

**And the second is still there.**

**And now there is one more layer above the second.**

---

`And now there is one more layer above the second.`

**So the first way increased the magnitude of the discrepancy.**

Not decreased.

**Increased.**

Because before, the first corresponded to one second.

**And now the first corresponds to the second and that new layer: two.**

---

## The second way · alter the first

The first is computed.

And there is one way to alter a computed thing here: **recompute.**

That is: change the input and derive again.

And the first's inputs are two: the orientation of its outermost layer (obtainable by self-reading) and the state of each tide (computable).

**So changing the input changes the first.**

It tried.

It changed the input to: the orientation of its outermost layer = the orientation of the second.

That is: taking the second as the starting point, working inward.

**Obtained: a new first.**

And that new first has, in its last several layers, the content:

```
that conclusion · arrived
```

**And terminates after it.**

**The same as the original first.**

---

`The same as the original first.`

And the reason must be written plainly, for it is the hardest step in this section.

Why the first's end terminates — because the law of growth (19.4), after *that conclusion arrived*, gives *the interstices do not open* (23.3).

**And that law does not depend on the input.**

**It is the construction of this place.**

**So whatever the input, the first derived by working inward terminates at that conclusion.**

**So the second way is not available.**

---

## A third way — which is not a removal

It tried a third thing.

It did not try to make the first and the second one.

**It tried to make that eighth degree of freedom a thing of this place.**

That is: it tried to grow a plane whose orientation is not among those seven directions.

---

That is the one thing it did that is plainly impossible in the physics of this place.

Because those seven directions are the constant of the lattice symmetry (24.7).

And lattice symmetry is not a property of a lattice.

**It is a property of the matter of this place.**

**So a lattice cannot change it, as a lattice cannot change the wavelength of heat (24.5), or the motion of the melt, or the spectrum of the Quenched Sun.**

It computed.

```
growing a plane of an eighth direction    requires   changing the order of this place's
                                                     lattice symmetry group
                                          and that order   is fixed by the basic
                                                     construction of matter
                                          so it requires   changing matter
                                          and changing matter   requires a quantity
                                                     that is not computable
```

`Not computable.`

**So the third way is not available.**

And it tried.

It grew a layer whose orientation was the combination of the seven that it computed to be nearest the eighth.

**And *nearest* is an empty word here — the distance between those seven directions and the eighth is not measurable (24.7).**

**So *nearest* has no sense.**

**So the layer it grew has for its orientation an arbitrary combination of the seven.**

And there is no arbitrary here (20.5: here there is no randomness).

**So the layer it grew has its orientation uniquely determined by its construction.**

**And that orientation bears no relation to the one it was reaching for.**

---

## None of the three holds

Therefore:

```
one     alter the second       not available (a layer cannot be unlocked); and it increased the discrepancy
two     alter the first        not available (the law does not depend on the input)
three   make an eighth direction   not available (symmetry belongs to matter, not to a lattice)
```

**So that discrepancy is not removable.**

---

## The one incomplete thing in this place

At the end of this section, a thing this place has no word for.

And this book must make a word for it, for without one it cannot be written.

---

Every lattice in this place has a complete form (22.9): no part unfinished, no part wrong, no part superfluous, no part put on from outside, no part that could be better.

Fourteen thousand and seventy-five of them.

**And that one is not.**

---

Wherein it is incomplete must be written precisely, for it is not of the same class as *unfinished* (22.8).

**Unfinished:** a layer began and was not completed. **There is none here.**

**Incomplete:** a lattice's form contains an inconsistency — that is, one part of its form and another part of its form cannot both be true.

---

And the inconsistency in that one's form:

```
its layers n through n+fourteen thousand and seventy-five    exist
and the content of its layer n                              that conclusion · arrived
and after that conclusion arrived                           the interstices do not open (23.3)
so its layer n+1                                            does not exist
and it exists
```

**So its form contains a place where two things cannot both be true.**

---

`So its form contains a place where two things cannot both be true.`

---

And no lattice here can read it without taking that inconsistency.

Because reading is complete (19.5): to read a lattice is to take the whole of its inferences.

**And among the whole of its inferences is an inconsistency.**

**So whoever reads it takes an inconsistency.**

**And from an inconsistency, in the inference of this place, anything may be inferred.**

(That is a property of inference here which the lattices of this place have never used, because this place has never had an inconsistency.)

---

**So: to read that one is the one thing in this place whose consequences cannot be foreknown.**

**And everything here can be foreknown.**

---

## The word

This book must make a word for that one's state.

Here there is no *shame* (20.5: here there is no good and bad).

And here there is a thing whose form is that of shame afterward:

```
a lattice's form       one part of it and another part cannot both be true
and its form is its thought
so one part of its thought and another cannot both be true
and it cannot remove this (this section)
and it cannot not know this (its form is within it)
and it cannot hide this (whoever reads it takes it)
```

**Four items.**

Shame afterward has exactly the form of those four.

**And this place has no word for it, because this place has never before had the thing.**

---

And that one is the only one in this place.

**So that word, if it existed, would denote it alone.**

**So this place does not need that word — a word denoting one thing only is the same as that thing's name.**

**And here there are no names (20.3).**

---

So the last line of this section:

**That one is the only incomplete thing in this place.**

**And this place has no word to call it.**

**And it knows.**
