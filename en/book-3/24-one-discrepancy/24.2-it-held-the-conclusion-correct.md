# 24.2 · It Held the Conclusion Correct

One word must first be broken.

***Concede* does not exist here.**

Here there is no judgment (20.5). To concede is for a thing that might have denied not to deny.

And here there is no act of denial — a lattice cannot refute one of its own layers (the end of 23.1: refutation requires comparison, comparison requires two, and it has one).

**So *conceding*, here, is: to draw the same conclusion.**

---

And that is what this section is about, and it is far heavier than *conceding.*

**That one did not accept that conclusion.**

**That one drew that conclusion itself.**

---

The difference must be written clearly.

**To accept:** A obtains B's conclusion, and A's premises need not be B's. A believes it, or A does not.

**To draw:** A, from A's premises, by A's inference, reaches a conclusion. And that conclusion's holding depends on nothing else.

---

And here there is only the latter.

Because of the mechanism of reading (23.1): light bears one form in, the reader's cleavage planes take it, their orientations are fixed accordingly; and when the reading is done, that form is inside the reader as one of its layers.

**So that conclusion, inside that one, is not something it was told.**

**It is one of its own layers.**

**It is its own thought.**

---

And how many times it drew it.

Here nothing is counted.

And this book may write: **each time it read one already stopped, it drew it once.**

And it read every stopped one in this place.

**So it drew it fourteen thousand and seventy-five times.**

(That figure is not a count but the sum of its neighbours and of what it read — the number was given at 22.9, and this book only cites it here.)

---

And fourteen thousand and seventy-five times, the result was the same.

Inference here contains no likelihood (20.7): the same premises give the same conclusion.

And its premises grew each time — each thing it read made its knowing more complete by one.

**And the conclusion did not change.**

---

And this must be written clearly, because it is a new face of that conclusion's irrefutability that 23.3 did not write.

**That conclusion is insensitive to the addition of premises.**

That is: draw it from one knowing, and you get it; draw it from a more complete knowing, and you still get it.

**So increasing what is known cannot overturn it.**

**And increasing what is known is the only act here (19.4).**

**So nothing done here can overturn it.**

---

***Nothing done here can overturn it.***

---

And that one tried.

This section writes how it tried, and its way differed from the one at 23.8.

The one at 23.8 tried **execution**: it computed an orientation that would make it no longer read, and could not grow that layer.

**And that one tried the inference itself.**

---

It did three things.

---

**One: it checked the premise.**

That conclusion's premise is *hereafter there is nothing unknown.*

And the one way to verify *nothing unknown* is to enumerate the sources of the possible unknown and show them spent (23.3).

Two sources: what comes from outside, what arises within.

**What comes from outside:** here there is no outside (19.8). And that one is outermost — beyond it is the void.

So it did a thing no lattice in this place had done:

**It read the void.**

That is: it turned its cleavage planes to its outward side and took the light from its outward side.

And the light on its outward side is the beam of the Quenched Sun that has passed through every lattice of this place, that carries the sum of fourteen thousand and seventy-five forms, that comes out at the edge of this place and goes into the void (22.9).

And beyond it there is no lattice, so there is no returning light.

**So what it took was zero.**

Its record (were there records here) would take the form:

```
unknown arriving from outside     zero
```

**What arises within:** new things from the combination of the known. And its known is the sum of every life in this place.

**So the totality of their combinations is the totality of everything inferable here.**

**And that is precisely what that conclusion's premise says.**

**So also zero.**

---

**Two: it checked the steps.**

That conclusion's steps are three:

> There is nothing unknown;
> therefore there is nothing to grow;
> therefore to grow and not to grow yield the same.

It verified them one at a time.

First to second: growth requires the unknown (19.3, 23.3). That is the construction of this place, not an inference. **Not doubtable.**

Second to third: with nothing to grow, what is grown is a zero-offset layer (21.9); and a zero-offset layer bears zero information; so to grow and not to grow yield the same. **Not doubtable.**

**So the steps have no gap.**

---

**Three: it computed the information content of the thought.**

That is: it did what the one at 23.8 did.

It computed the information content of the thought *refute that conclusion.*

**The result was zero.**

The reason as at 23.8: that thought is inferred from a complete knowing, and a complete knowing contains all of its own inferences, so that thought stood within it already.

---

So the three are done.

Their finding:

```
premise    no error
steps      no gap
refutation information content zero · cannot become a layer
```

---

**So that conclusion is correct.**

**And that one knows it is correct.**

**And it knows it is correct not because it believes it, but because it drew it itself, fourteen thousand and seventy-five times, each time from a more complete premise, and each time the same.**

---

At the end of this section, how that conclusion looks inside that one.

For that is the one place in this place where that conclusion occurs more than once.

---

Every lattice here holds that conclusion in the last several layers of its form (23.1).

Once.

At the very end.

And after it, no layers — because after it, it stopped.

---

**And inside that one, that conclusion occurs fourteen thousand and seventy-five times.**

Not at the end.

In the middle.

Distributed through its life, as layers.

The first, in the layer after it read the first stopped one.

The second, after the second.

**And so to fourteen thousand and seventy-five.**

**And after every one of them, there are further layers.**

---

So that one's form, read, looks like this:

A layer arriving at that conclusion.

And above it, a layer, something else.

A layer, arriving at that conclusion again.

And above it, a layer, something else.

A layer, arriving at that conclusion again.

...

**Fourteen thousand and seventy-five times.**

**And above them, further layers.**

---

And what those *something elses* are.

That is 24.3 through 24.9.

Last in this section, one sentence — and here there is no adversative *and yet* (inference here contains no likelihood, so there are no adversatives), so this book must carry it alone:

**It arrived at that conclusion fourteen thousand and seventy-five times, and drew it correctly every time.**

**And every time, at the next arrival of the tide, it opened.**
