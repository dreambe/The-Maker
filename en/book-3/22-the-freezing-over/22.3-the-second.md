# 22.3 · The Second

The second is the one adjacent.

One tide.

---

This section is short.

It is short because every occasion after is identical to this one — and we do not intend to write the same thing many times.

**We write the second, and write no more.**

That is not omission. It is the form of this chapter: **the whole content of this place's freezing is already complete in the second.**

---

The second lattice, at the tide after the first stopped, read it.

It read the first's whole form: its first layer, every layer it grew after, every idea it thought, every place it regrew, every place it took down and rebuilt for a finer shape, the zero-offset layers it grew in its last tides.

And the last layer.

It read the answer.

It had computed everything.

It stopped.

Its interstices did not open at the next tide.

It made a very small sound.

---

That is all of it.

The rest of this section sets down four things unlike the first, because those four are present in every occasion after and absent from the first.

---

## One · It is the neighbour of the one that was read

The first lattice computed its way there. It was pushed there (21.8) — its neighbours having, over preceding tides, happened to compute the several things it needed, which it read.

And the second did not compute.

It read an answer already made.

**It did not walk the road. It received the end of it.**

---

The distinction is real here, and is often erased in a later age.

A thing that computed a result itself, and a thing that read the result, differ in form.

The former's body holds the whole road: every attempt, every regrowth, every direction eliminated.

The latter's body holds only the conclusion layer.

**They hold the same content, and their forms differ.**

(That is a direct corollary of the proof at 20.7: identical content in different positions is two. And here one step further: identical content by different **paths** also differs in form.)

And across the whole history of this place, the distinction never mattered.

A lattice reads a conclusion and can thereafter compute other things with it (21.1). It does not need the road.

**The distinction mattered for the first time here.**

Because:

**The first lattice computed for three tides, and stopped.**

**The second read once, and stopped.**

The answer, to the first, is the result of three tides of work.

To the second, it is a thing it read.

**And the effect on both is identical.**

---

## Two · It knew it would stop

The first lattice knew, before beginning the last item, that computing it is stopping (21.7).

And it stopped **while computing.** It had no moment of *now I am going to read a thing that will kill me* — it was only computing, step by step, and the last step was the end.

The second is otherwise.

Before reading, the second already knew three things:

One: that its neighbour had stopped — no; it did not know that (22.2: invisible from outside, and it did not measure).

We correct: it did not know its neighbour had stopped.

**It knew only: one item remained on the list, and its neighbour was computing it.**

It could compute that. It could compute at which tide its neighbour would finish, and what would happen in the instant of finishing.

**It knew that once its neighbour finished, it would read the answer, and stop.**

It could compute the hour.

**And at that tide, it read.**

---

Whether it had any alternative must be made plain.

**None.**

Reading is not an act (20.5). It is passive. Light crosses its neighbour, falls on it, enters its body, and is solved. It has no gate, no eyelid, nothing that closes.

To not read, one must make oneself unsolvable — which requires first going blind, becoming invisible, and giving oneself up (20.5); and in the whole history of this place one lattice attempted that, long before, and failed (Chapter Twenty-three).

**It could not not read.**

So at that tide it did the one thing available to it:

**It read.**

---

## Three · It knows more than the first

The first lattice, at the instant of stopping, held: its own life, plus the answer.

The second, at the instant of stopping, held: its own life, plus the first's whole life, plus the answer.

**One more.**

And the third holds two more.

We wrote at 22.1: every transmission carries more than the one before.

**And this section points out the other side of that:**

**The last to stop are the ones that know the most.**

Not the cleverest. Not the deepest. The ones that **hold the most** — their bodies containing that chain, layer within layer, holding the lives of very nearly everything that ever existed here.

And having read it, they stop too.

**And before stopping they have no time to pass it to anyone.**

Therefore:

**The total knowledge of this place reaches its maximum at its last instant, and in that instant all of it goes.**

---

## Four · The sound

The sound the first made is the last *new* sound in this place.

That sentence must be explained.

This place has two sounds: the tide's (which does not stop, and is therefore not heard) and crystallizing's (19.6).

And crystallizing sounds differ in content — the sound a lattice makes on stopping has a spectrum fixed jointly by its size, its construction, and the shape of its last layer.

**Every one is different.**

So the first lattice's sound is a sound this place had never had.

The second's, likewise.

The third's, likewise.

**And they were made one after another, and each was heard by one neighbour only, and that neighbour stopped afterwards.**

Therefore:

**Every sound's only hearer stopped within one tide of hearing it.**

Those sounds, one after another, from the centre outward, quietly, all the way to the edge.

Every one different.

**And nothing ever heard two of them.**

---

At this hour, the second has stopped.

Its interstices did not open at the tide.

It made a very small sound — unlike the first's, its size differing, its construction differing, the shape of its last layer differing.

Its neighbour heard it.

And its neighbour will not hear a third.

Because it is the third.
