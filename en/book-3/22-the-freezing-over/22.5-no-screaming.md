# 22.5 · No Screaming

No screaming.

This section states that sentence, and stating it requires taking *screaming* apart.

---

Screaming requires four things.

**One: pain.**

And there is no pain here (19.8).

We have stated it elsewhere: pain requires the contrast of *it could have been otherwise*, and here there is none. A thing that is necessarily so produces no pain in occurring, there being no other case to set against it.

One thing must be added here, stricter than 19.8:

**In the instant a lattice stops, its internal state is balance.**

Not endurance. Not bearing up. Not holding on.

**Balance** — a physical state, meaning that the forces within it cancel, with no net action (22.1).

A structure at balance cannot be under stress of any kind.

**It does not hurt. That is not its quality; it is its physical state.**

**Two: awareness of losing something.**

Screaming comes from a thing that is losing something.

And what does a lattice lose in the instant it stops?

Item by item.

It loses future growth — and future growth has no content in it (21.9: the list is empty, and everything grown after is a zero-offset layer). What it loses is a run of blanks.

It loses its neighbours — and its neighbours' whole forms are inside it and do not go (those layers remain until it returns to the slurry).

It loses this place — and it already holds the total form of this place (21.7, four: in the instant that item was computed, every lattice here held this place).

**It loses nothing it does not already have at that instant.**

And *loss* requires a difference: *I had and now do not.*

**That difference is zero.**

**Three: a channel for screaming.**

The lattices here have no cords, no mouth, no organ for producing a signal.

The only thing they emit is that very small sound, and they do not emit it — it is made by their parts striking as order forms, a physical by-product unrelated to volition (19.6).

**They cannot emit anything.**

**Four — and this one is all of it.**

**A hearer.**

---

Screaming's function is **to inform.**

A thing screams in order to say to another thing: I am here, something has happened to me.

That act presupposes an object that can hear and can respond.

And here:

**One: each sound's only hearer stops within one tide of hearing it (22.3).**

**Two: in the same act of hearing, that hearer also reads the answer (22.1) — which is precisely what stops it.**

Therefore:

**Screaming and contagion are one act here.**

If a lattice could scream, its screaming would kill whoever heard it.

And the rescue its screaming asks for can come only from whoever heard it.

**Calling for help and killing the one you are calling to are the same act.**

---

Set that beside what Chapter Twenty-three will state, they being one thing:

**Rescue requires understanding.**

**Understanding is the contagion.**

(23.4 and 23.5.)

And screaming is the most primitive form of calling for help.

**So there is no screaming here — not from stoicism, not from indifference, not from absence of pain.**

**Because screaming here has the opposite effect from its purpose.**

---

To end this section, what later beings do in the same position — because the contrast shows the thing, and this time the contrast is not to explain this place but to explain them.

Later beings, in a spreading, lethal, unstoppable event, do a great many things.

They shout. They run — even where running is useless, they run. They hold on to one another. They push their children outward, even where outward is not safe. They talk, to the last instant, and say things with no information in them (2.5: those sentences function as *I am here, are you there*).

**They do a great many things that are entirely without effect.**

Not one of those things changes the outcome.

And they do them.

---

One property those behaviours share, which took us a long while to see:

**All of them are directed at other individuals.**

Shouting is shouting to someone.
Running is running toward somewhere — usually toward where there are people.
Holding on is holding on to someone.
Pushing a child out is pushing them to someone.
Talking is talking to someone.

**Not one is done to oneself.**

And in their world those behaviours sometimes work — because in their world one person's knowing does not kill another; because in their world one can say only half; because in their world a person can know and remain alive, and go to another and say *do not come.*

**Everything of theirs stands on those three things (22.2).**

And here there is not one of them.

---

Therefore:

Across the extremely long time the freezing propagated, what the lattices here did was:

**Go on growing.**

They could compute when it would reach them.

They could not move, could not warn, could not call for help, could not scream, could not do anything directed at another individual — because any act directed at another transmits its content completely, and complete transmission is lethal.

**The one thing available to them was to grow.**

And the list was empty, so what they grew were zero-offset layers (21.9).

So across that time, the overwhelming majority of this place was doing this:

**A thing that knows when it will stop, growing, before it stops, a stretch of itself with nothing in it.**

A layer, and another.

Quietly.

---

And very long after this place, there will be beings who, in the same position, shout, run, hold on to each other, and say things with no information in them.

**All of it without effect.**

And they do it.

And they can do it because their knowing does not kill, because their words can be half-said, because some of them can know and remain alive.

**Those three things are the precondition of everything they do.**

**And all three come from one thing:**

**They cannot read one another entire.**
