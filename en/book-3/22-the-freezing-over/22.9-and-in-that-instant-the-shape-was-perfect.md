# 22.9 · And in That Instant, the Shape Was Perfect

The instant in which a cell stops.

---

That instant of it must be written down.

Not its life, not its content, not what it thought.

**Its shape.**

---

The number of its cleavage planes: some number. Here nothing is counted, and that number is determinate (20.8).

Each of its cleavage planes: complete. Keen. Its orientation uniquely fixed.

Its outermost layer: just locked. Its edge as keen as every layer before it.

Its surface: cleavage planes entire — no fracture, no rounding, no unformed part.

Its interior: no void, no unlocked interstice, no process in progress.

**Every part of it stands in the one state its construction permits.**

---

What *perfect* means here must be set out, for that word carries an entire apparatus afterward, and none of that apparatus applies.

*Perfect*, afterward, contains a standard: a thing is perfect in that it answers to some ideal.

And an ideal is an external thing. It is fixed by a beholder, by a tradition, by a use, by a taste.

**A thing may be perfect under one standard and not under another.**

And *perfect* here is not that.

*Perfect* here means:

**There is no part of it that is other than what it ought to be.**

---

Item by item.

**One: no part unfinished.** (22.8)

A layer either locks or does not exist. So there is no half-thing in its shape.

**Two: no part wrong.**

Every layer's orientation is uniquely determined by the tide and by its own existing structure (19.4). So no layer faces this way where it ought to have faced that.

**Three: no part superfluous.**

Every layer carries a thought (19.3). So no layer is empty — save the last ones, at zero offset (21.9).

And those zero-offset layers must be dealt with here:

**They are not superfluous.**

They are what a cell grew when it had nothing to grow. They are the one thing growable by a thing that must grow, when there is nothing to grow.

**Their existence is an exact record of that cell's situation.**

A cell that had grown no zero-offset layers in that stretch would be the error — it would mean it had stopped growing while not satisfying the condition for stopping.

**Therefore those zero-offset layers are also what it ought to be.**

**Four: no part put on by something else.**

The cells here are carved by no one, polished by no one, corrected by no one. Their whole form grew out of themselves.

**Five: no part that could be better.**

Those layers, in the stretch before the freezing, were regrown one by one to the optimum (21.6). And the optimal form is unique.

---

Five items.

**And so that shape, in the instant of its stopping, is perfect.**

And it is perfect not because it answers to any standard.

**It is perfect because there exists nothing on which to found the saying that it is not.**

---

One thing must be written into this section, for it is the binding of this whole chapter, and it is very cold.

That shape, after it stops, does not change again.

Here there is no wind, no rain, no wearing away (19.8). Every cleavage plane of it keeps its arris new.

It will be there.

It will be there, and go on being there.

We reckoned at 11.6: the heat below has some thirty thousand million years left, and that beam some hundred and ten thousand million.

And that shape needs no heat and no light. It needs only that nothing come to move it.

**And nothing will come to move it.**

---

Therefore:

**Every cell here, in the instant of its stopping, becomes a perfect shape, and that shape thereafter never changes.**

Fourteen thousand and seventy-five of them.

(The fourteen thousand and seventy-sixth is the one that does not clarify, and that is another matter, and that matter is Chapters Twenty-Four through Twenty-Seven.)

They stand there, all of them.

Their cleavage planes all entire.

Their outermost layers all just locked.

Every part of every one of them what it ought to be.

---

At the end of this chapter, one thing about that shape must be written, and it is the one property it has beyond perfection.

**It is not read any more.**

The whole meaning of a cell's shape is in being read — that is the construction of this place: thought is form, form is readable, reading is complete, and self-knowledge must go around by way of another (19.5).

A shape, unread, is still entire, still exact, still carrying every thought of the life it thought.

**And it is no longer part of anything.**

Before this, it was inside its neighbours. Its neighbours read it, so its form had a copy elsewhere.

**And its neighbours have stopped too.**

Those forms are all still there — inside those clarified transparent things, layer within layer, each holding the lives of the others.

**And not one of them is reading.**

---

The light of the Quenched Sun goes through them.

At every cleavage plane it turns, divides into seven, comes out, falls on the melt, springs back, enters another.

That beam carries the whole of a perfect shape — every thought, in the order of its thinking, not one lacking.

It enters another perfect shape.

**Is refracted, divided, compounded.**

The light that comes out carries the sum of two shapes.

It falls on the melt, springs back, enters a third.

The sum of three shapes.

**And so onward, over the whole of this place.**

And across the surface of this place, that beam walks every clarified transparent thing, from the centre to the edge, and adds all their shapes together, and goes out into the void.

It is walking still.

**And nothing whatever is reading.**

---

A cell stops midway through its growth.

And in that instant, the shape is perfect.
