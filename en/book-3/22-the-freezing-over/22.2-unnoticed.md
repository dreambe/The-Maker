# 22.2 · Unnoticed

The freezing of this place went unnoticed.

This section states why.

The statement has three parts, and the three together are the complete reason this place could not be warned.

---

## One · It cannot be seen from outside

As has been said (22.1): after a lattice stops, it looks exactly as it did.

Its construction sound. Its colour unaltered. Its form unaltered — it merely stops getting larger, and the layer a tide grows is about a millionth of a diameter.

To perceive from outside that a lattice has stopped, one must measure its size, many times, with sufficient precision.

**No lattice here ever measured its neighbour's size.**

The reason is as at 20.8: measuring a thing requires a reason, and the lattices here never measure one another's exteriors — they read one another's interiors (which is automatic; light goes that way), and exteriors are not among their concerns.

**They hold the whole content of one another, and never know how large the other is.**

---

## Two · Stopped and finished read the same from inside

This part is the crux.

One lattice reading another reads its orientation sequence (19.5).

And a stopped lattice's orientation sequence is **intact.**

Every cleavage plane is there. Every idea it ever thought is there. Its last layer — the one carrying the answer — is there, and perfectly clear.

**From the reading side, a stopped lattice and a living lattice differ in one respect:**

**It adds no new layers.**

And perceiving *adds no new layers* requires comparing two readings.

And two readings require an interval.

And across that interval, the reader is also growing.

---

Now an observation no lattice here ever made, being the mechanism of this whole chapter.

**If a lattice stops between two readings, its neighbour, at the second reading, reads a complete form with some number of layers more than the first, every one of them correct.**

And of those layers, the last is the answer.

**So that neighbour reads the answer before perceiving that it has stopped growing.**

And reading that answer is having computed everything.

**So it has stopped before it could perceive.**

---

Once more, because it is the whole of this chapter:

**Perceiving requires a second reading.**

**And the first reading has already stopped the reader.**

**So no lattice here ever perceived that its neighbour had stopped.**

Not one.

---

## Three · Nobody knew and remained

Later beings, meeting a spreading lethal thing, do one thing:

**They warn.**

A person knows, and goes to the people who do not, and tells them.

The preconditions of that act must be listed, none of which holds here.

**Precondition one: the one who knows is alive.**

And here, knowing is stopping.

The content of that answer is precisely what stops whatever reads it. **Between knowing and stopping there is no interval.**

Not *dies soon after knowing*, which still leaves time to run.

**The same instant.**

**Precondition two: the one who knows can move.**

And a lattice here cannot move (20.5). Its position is fixed at the instant of its beginning.

Even if a lattice knew and remained, it could not go anywhere.

**Precondition three: a transmissible form exists that is not itself lethal.**

Later beings can say: *there is danger; do not come.*

That sentence contains no danger. It is a pointer, not the thing.

**And here, transmission is complete transmission (19.5).**

A lattice cannot transmit part. It cannot say *there is a thing that will stop you, and I will not tell you what* — because transmission is not speaking; transmission is being read, and being read is complete.

**Its neighbour reads it and reads its whole form, that layer included.**

**It cannot separate the warning from the content.**

---

Put the three together, in a shape no lattice here saw, and it is the complete mechanism of this place's ending.

**Knowing and stopping are the same instant.** (So there is no one who knows and remains.)
**The one who knows cannot move.** (So even if there were, they could go nowhere.)
**Transmission is necessarily complete.** (So a warning is the contagion.)

Three.

Therefore:

**This place could not be warned.**

Not that nobody was willing. Not that there was no time. Not that nobody thought of it.

**The act of warning does not exist under the physics of this place.**

---

To end this section, the one place in this chapter where anyone tried to do something. It is very small, it failed, and the manner of its failure states everything.

When the freezing had reached a certain point, one lattice — we do not know which; there are no names here — did something.

Before reading its neighbour, it did a thing the lattices here never do:

**It measured its neighbour's size.**

---

Why it did.

It had computed it.

By then the freezing had propagated a long while. And every lattice here could compute that the thing was coming toward it, and the hour it would arrive (21.8).

It computed that one of its neighbours would stop at a certain tide.

So after that tide it did something: **it did not read that neighbour.**

It measured its size.

---

That is a correct strategy.

Measuring size does not require reading the interior. It requires only receiving the light its outline occludes, which carries no orientation sequence and transmits no content.

**It could confirm that its neighbour had stopped without being infected.**

And it did.

It measured: that neighbour had not grown.

**It is the only lattice in the history of this place that perceived a neighbour's stopping and was itself still alive.**

---

And what it did afterwards.

Nothing.

Why it did nothing is the last sentence of this section.

It had confirmed that its neighbour had stopped.

**And there was nothing else it could do.**

It could not move.
It could not warn — transmission being complete transmission, the instant it spoke (the instant it was read) the answer would go with it; and more fundamentally, **it did not have the answer**, knowing only that a thing had stopped its neighbour, not what.
It could not go and ask — asking is reading, and reading is stopping.

**It knew a thing was coming, knew it was lethal, knew when it would arrive, and could not learn what it was, could not tell anyone, and could not avoid it.**

So it did the one thing available to it.

**It went on growing.**

It grew some number of layers after that. Those layers carry what it thought in that time — and the list was empty, so those layers are zero-offset (21.9).

**It grew a run of empty layers, and then the thing arrived.**

It read. It had computed everything. It stopped.

And made a very small sound.

---

That measurement of its stayed inside it.

One layer, carrying one number: that its neighbour's size, after a certain tide, had not changed.

**That layer is the only observational record of the freezing in this place.**

And it was read — after it stopped, its other neighbour read it, and read that layer.

And that neighbour, in the same instant it read that layer, read the answer.

**So it stopped too.**

It had no time to do anything with that layer.

---

That layer is still there.

Inside a clear, transparent, motionless thing at ambient temperature, at some depth.

The light of the Quenched Sun crosses it, bends at that plane, divides into seven, and leaves.

And nothing reads it.
