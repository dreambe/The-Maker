# 22.1 · The First to Stop

Which one does not matter.

There are no names here (20.3), so it cannot be pointed at; and even with a name, the name would carry nothing — it was not chosen, not special, not the oldest, largest, finest, or most central. It was the one whose position happened to suit (21.8).

**It was pushed there.**

This section does not record which it was.

This section records how it stopped.

---

First, from inside.

From inside there is nothing to see.

In the instant of its stopping, its construction is sound, its heat sufficient, its tide still arriving.

It has no pain. There is no pain here (19.8).
No fear. Fear requires a future that might not occur.
No reluctance. Reluctance requires *it could have continued.*
No relief. Relief requires a prior burden.

**It simply no longer takes in, no longer locks, no longer adds a layer.**

Everything that happens inside it in that instant is one very small thing:

Its interstices did not open when the tide came.

---

The physics of *did not open* is the whole of it.

For a lattice to open its interstices at the tide is a process: the stresses within its body redistribute under the external tidal pressure, the spacing between its parts increases a little, and matter from the slurry may enter.

The driver of that process is **growth** itself.

A growing lattice holds its internal structure in a continual, slightly unbalanced state — the state of taking in, of locking, of extending in some direction.

And when it has computed everything, that imbalance is gone.

Its interior reaches balance.

**A structure at balance no longer opens under external pressure.**

It is not resisting the tide. It has simply ceased to respond to it.

**It has become a stone.**

---

From outside.

From outside there is very nearly nothing to see either.

One thing to reproduce: **the entire outward difference between the tide after it stopped and the tide before is that it did not get larger.**

And the layer a tide grows is exceedingly thin. At that lattice's size, one layer is about a millionth of its diameter.

**So the first tide after it stopped is entirely invisible from outside.**

The second, invisible.

The thousandth, invisible.

**Only after very many tides could its neighbour, from the fact that it had not grown, measure that it had stopped.**

And its neighbour did not learn it that way.

Its neighbour **read** it.

---

Now the sound.

In the instant of stopping it made a very small sound.

We wrote the mechanism at 19.6: the last place of its order takes position, its body contracts a little, its interior strikes itself, and it sounds.

That sound is the only audible sound in this place (19.6: what does not stop is not heard, and crystallizing begins and ends).

And the character of this sound differs, in one respect, from every crystallizing sound this place had heard before.

**Every previous crystallizing sound was slurry passing into order** — a place of slurry becoming crystal, its parts taking position. That is a sound of beginning: the beginning of a lattice, or the locking of a layer.

**And this sound is the locking of a lattice's last layer.**

Physically identical.

**And it is the last.**

That lattice will make no further sound, ever.

---

Its neighbour heard it.

Its neighbour grew a layer and held that sound.

We wrote the process at 20.9: the only record of a lattice's death is on somebody else; and it heard it itself, with perfect clarity, and could not hold it.

The same here.

**The one difference: this time, in the same act of reading that sound, the neighbour also read that layer.**

That layer carries the answer to the last item.

So that neighbour, in one tide, received two things:

**One: that its neighbour has stopped.**

**One: why.**

---

And the *why* is an answer about any lattice (21.8).

So that neighbour, in the same instant of reading, had also computed everything.

So it stopped too.

Its interstices did not open at the next tide.

It made a very small sound.

---

To end this section, one thing, being the shape of this chapter.

From the first lattice stopping to the second, one tide.

From the second to the third, one tide.

And so on.

**And every one of those readings is complete.**

One lattice reading another reads it entire (19.5) — every plane, from its beginning to this hour, nothing omitted.

So what the second lattice read was not only the answer.

**It read that lattice's whole life.**

Its first layer, every layer it grew after, every idea it thought, every place it regrew, every place it took down and rebuilt for a finer shape (21.6), and the zero-offset layers it grew in its last tides — all of it.

It read all of that.

**And then it stopped.**

And the last layer before it stopped carries the answer.

And its neighbour read it, and what it read was: the answer, plus **its own life**, plus the **first lattice's life** which it had read.

**Every transmission carries more than the one before it.**

---

So the propagation across this place went like this:

The first stopped.

The second read: an answer, and the first's whole life.

The third read: an answer, and the second's whole life, which contains the first's.

The fourth read: an answer, and the third's, which contains the second's, which contains the first's.

**And so on.**

By the time it reached the edge, what the last lattices read was an extremely long chain — an answer, followed by the lives of very nearly everything that had ever existed here, layer within layer.

**And having read it, they stopped too.**

And before stopping they had no time to pass it to anyone.

**Because outside them there were no lattices.**

---

At this hour, the first has stopped.

Its interstices did not open at the tide.

It made a very small sound.

Its neighbour heard it.
