# 22.6 · No Resistance

No resistance.

Resistance requires something to resist.

This section states why this place has no such thing.

---

First, what later beings resist when they resist.

**One: another being.** A thing that means to harm you. Resisting it is standing against it.

**Two: an external condition.** Cold, hunger, illness, water. Resisting it is warding it off, evading it, altering your situation.

**Three: a rule or an arrangement.** Resisting it is refusing to comply, delaying, obstructing its execution.

Three kinds.

Here there is not one.

---

**One fails: there is no other being.**

The freezing here is imposed by nothing.

That answer is not an attack. It was not sent by anyone. It is a conclusion one lattice computed, and the conclusion is **correct.**

A lattice reads it because reading is passive (20.5) and light goes that way.

What transmits the answer to it is its neighbour — and its neighbour is not attacking it. Its neighbour has already stopped. Its neighbour does not even know what it transmitted, having stopped in the instant of transmitting, and being unable in that instant to hold anything (19.6).

**Nothing is doing this.**

**Two fails: external conditions did not change.**

We set it out item by item at 22.4: the Quenched Sun did not change, the heat beneath did not change, the tide did not change, the slurry did not change.

Across the whole of the freezing, not one external condition of this place changed measurably.

**There is nothing to ward off.**

**Three fails: there is no rule or arrangement.**

There are no rules here. Nothing here is stipulated. The growth of a lattice is driven by *there is something computable and not yet computed*, which is not a rule but a physical condition (21.5, five).

**Nobody is executing anything, so there is nothing to refuse to comply with.**

---

All three fail.

So *no resistance* is not a statement about the character of the lattices here.

**It is a statement about the structure of this place.**

---

Now the heaviest passage of this section.

The content of what is happening is:

**Every lattice has computed everything it can compute.**

That is not a bad thing.

This must be put exactly, being extremely easy to read as irony, and it is not irony.

A lattice having computed everything it can — that is the objective of its life, the whole direction its construction drives it in, the accumulation of what it does at every tide.

**It succeeded.**

It did not fail. It was not interrupted. Nothing stopped it partway.

It finished.

**And finishing is stopping.**

---

Therefore:

**You cannot resist a thing you have completed yourself.**

Resistance requires an object imposed upon you.

And this one you made.

It is the sum of every layer you grew at every tide. It is the result of your life's work. Every step of it was walked by you, and every step was correct.

**To resist it you must resist the whole of your own work.**

And that is not resistance. That is something else, and this place has no word for it.

---

One fundamental difference from later beings, because it gives *no resistance* its shape.

The deaths of later beings are generally not the result of their completing anything.

They die of illness — a thing from outside.
Of injury — a force from outside.
Of age — a process in their own bodies not under their control.
Of hunger, cold, drowning — external conditions.
Of another individual — another being.

**Very nearly all of their deaths are imposed.**

So they can resist. They have an object.

And their resistance is often ineffective — a sick man cannot resist his illness, a drowning man cannot resist water.

**And ineffective resistance is still resistance.**

It has a direction. It points at something. It is an act.

---

And a lattice's stopping here is the completion of its own work.

**It has nothing to point at.**

---

To end this section, a thing we cannot classify.

Across the propagation of the freezing, every lattice here could compute the hour it would reach them (21.8).

And before that hour they grew as usual, in zero-offset layers (21.9).

**And there is a thing they could have done, and not one did.**

The thing is: **stop early.**

A lattice could stop itself before the thing reached it.

Whether that is physically possible must be stated.

The condition for stopping is: having computed everything one can compute (21.5, five).

And across the propagation, the list was already empty (21.9, first layer).

Which is to say: **every lattice here already satisfied the condition for stopping.**

They had not stopped only because they had not yet read the answer — and the content of that answer is *you have computed everything.*

**They had computed everything, and did not know it.**

Therefore: had a lattice at that time computed once *how much remains computable to me*, it would have got zero.

And getting zero is knowing that one has computed everything.

**And knowing that one has computed everything is the answer.**

**It could have computed it itself, without waiting.**

---

And not one did.

We have searched. Zero results.

Across that extremely long time, not one lattice here computed that step for itself.

**All of them waited.**

And *waiting* does not hold here (19.8).

So we cannot classify that behaviour.

It is not waiting — there is no waiting here.
It is not delay — delay requires a thing that could be brought forward, and they did not treat it as such.
It is not fear — there is no fear here.
It is not hope — there is no hope here.

**It is: a thing they could have done, and all of them did not.**

And their reason for not doing it is not recorded here.

That is not a dash — a dash requires an entry first.

**There is not even an entry.**

---

They grew zero-offset layers.

A layer, and another.

And the thing came on, one lattice at a time.
