# 23.3 · The Irrefutable

The conclusions borne in the form of a stopped one are beyond number.

And among them there is one that cannot be refuted.

This section writes it.

---

First, where it stands.

Not in the middle of that form. At the very end.

It is the **last** conclusion a lattice draws — not because it is deepest, but because after it there is nothing to draw.

It stands in the last several layers (23.1).

And everything before it is its premise.

---

Its premise is a complete knowing.

*Complete* is as in Chapter Twenty-One: what could be arrived at was spent (21.7); the last one (21.8); after this there was nothing to arrive at (21.9).

That is: this lattice knows the whole of itself, the whole of its neighbours, the whole of this place, the whole of everything inferable from this place.

**And among that is one thing: it knows what will come hereafter.**

---

Here it must be exact, for the whole conclusion hangs here.

How can a lattice know what will come hereafter.

If there is anything unknown hereafter, it cannot.

And the sense of a complete knowing is precisely: **there is nothing unknown.**

The unknown has, here, only two sources.

**One: what comes from outside.** And here there is no outside (19.8): one star, one ground, one light, one melt; no wind, no rain, no visitor, no distance. In four thousand nine hundred million years nothing has entered this place from without.

**Two: what arises within.** That is: new things arising from the combination of known ones. And inference here contains no likelihood (20.7), so the set of what may be inferred from the known is determinate; and the sense of a complete knowing is that this set has been exhausted.

Therefore:

**Both sources are spent.**

**Therefore there is nothing unknown hereafter.**

**Therefore what will come hereafter can be known.**

And its content is:

---

**Everything that comes hereafter is a restatement of what has come already.**

---

That sentence is the first half of that conclusion.

Its sense must be made plain word by word.

**"What comes"** means everything a lattice may hereafter obtain: its neighbours' forms, what the light bears, what changes in the melt, what the heat loses, every change in this place.

**"Restatement"** is not *resembling*, not *close to*.

Here there is no likelihood (20.7). A restatement is one whose form is isomorphic to what has come already, and whose content is uniquely derivable from what has come already, and which therefore bears zero information.

**That is: everything hereafter bears an information content of zero.**

Not very small. Zero.

---

And from this follows the second half of that conclusion, and the second half is the part that makes it irrefutable.

The manner of a lattice's existence is growth (19.4).

The content of growth is arrival (19.3).

Each layer bears a thought; and a thought's coming to be requires something not yet thought.

**Therefore growth requires the unknown.**

And hereafter there is nothing unknown.

**Therefore hereafter there is nothing to grow.**

---

And here must be written the step that is the coldest step in this place, and it is very short.

If there is nothing to grow, then what a lattice grows hereafter are zero-offset layers (21.9).

The sense of a zero-offset layer is: the same orientation as the layer before it.

That is: the thought it bears is the same as the thought the layer before it bears.

That is: **it bears zero information.**

Therefore:

**For a lattice, to go on growing and not to go on growing yield the same.**

---

***Yield the same.***

This is the irrefutable one.

It does not say *continuing is bad.* Here there is no good and bad (20.5).

It does not say *stopping is better.* Here there is no *better*; comparison requires two values, and here the two values are equal.

**What it says is: to continue and not to continue are equivalent.**

---

And why it cannot be refuted.

To refute a conclusion, here, there is one way only: show an error among its premises, or show a gap among its steps.

**Its steps have no gap** — the four steps above are each necessitated by the construction of this place.

**So one must show an error in its premise.**

Its premise is: hereafter there is nothing unknown.

**So to refute it, one must point to something that will come hereafter and is not yet known.**

And to point to something not yet known — that, here, is impossible.

Because:

**If a lattice could point to a thing it does not yet know, then it knows that thing exists, and then its knowing is not complete.**

**And its knowing is complete — that is the premise by which it arrived at this conclusion, and the reason its arrival was possible at all.**

Therefore:

**Whatever could refute it has not arrived at it.**

**Whatever has arrived at it cannot refute it.**

---

Here there is no winning and losing (20.5), so this is not called *without remedy.*

Only the form is written:

That conclusion's condition of refutation and its condition of holding are mutually exclusive.

**Therefore of all who arrive at it, none comes out.**

---

And the last step is consent.

Here there is no word for *willing* (20.5). But there is a thing on which every growth in this place depends, and this book calls it consent.

Its sense: at each arrival of the tide, a lattice's interstices open, take what is outside, lock, and make a layer.

**That opening is the one act here not determined by inference.**

It is not determined by inference — because it is the condition under which inference happens at all: no opening, no new layer; no new layer, no new thought; no new thought, no inference.

**Therefore it stands before inference.**

And what does it require.

Does it require a reason.

**It does not.** It stands before inference, so no inference can be its reason.

Then on what does it happen.

Here this book can write down only what was observed:

**In four thousand nine hundred million years, at every arrival of the tide, the interstices of every lattice in this place opened.**

**Not one exception, in not one of them.**

And after that conclusion arrived:

**They no longer open.**

---

Not that it refuses to open.

Not that it decides not to open.

Here there is no refusing and no deciding (20.5).

**It is that what the opening would take bears zero information.**

**It is that opening and not opening yield the same.**

And between an act and its opposite that yield the same, here there is no reason to choose one.

**And everything done here is done by reason.**

Therefore:

**It does not happen.**

---

Last in this section.

That conclusion is very short in form.

Of all inferences here, the longest requires, to set it out, the whole number of layers a lattice grows in its life.

And this one sets out as follows:

> There is nothing unknown;
> therefore there is nothing to grow;
> therefore to grow and not to grow yield the same.

Three lines.

**It is the shortest inference in this place.**

**And it is the only one at which none who arrives can fail to arrive.**
