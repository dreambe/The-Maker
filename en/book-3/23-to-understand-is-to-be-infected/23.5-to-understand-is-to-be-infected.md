# 23.5 · To Understand Is to Be Infected

What the four preceding sections established, this section joins.

And what the joining gives is not a causation.

**It is an identity.**

---

First the causation, because afterward it will be read that way, and that way is wrong.

The reading afterward is:

> understanding → causes → infection

That is: there is an act called understanding, and after it a consequence called infection, the two adjoining, the former cause and the latter effect.

**The error of that reading is in *the two.***

It supposes there are two things.

And here there is one.

---

Word by word.

**What, here, is *understanding*.**

It is: light passing through one form, entering another, the latter's cleavage planes taking it, their orientations being fixed accordingly (19.5, 23.1).

**What, here, is *infection*.**

It is: light passing through one form, entering another, the latter's cleavage planes taking it, their orientations being fixed accordingly.

---

**The same sentence.**

Not similar. Not parallel. Not two faces of one thing.

**The same sentence.**

Word for word. Not one word differing.

---

Therefore:

**Here there is no *understanding causes infection.***

**Here there is one act, and afterward two words were prepared for it.**

---

And why afterward prepared two.

Because afterward they are two things.

Understanding afterward is the receiving of signs and the reconstruction of sense, and the reconstructor's body is unchanged.

Infection afterward is a thing entering a body, and the body changes.

**The two are separable afterward, because a later being's knowing and a later being's body are two things (the end of 23.2).**

So afterward two words are needed.

**And here they are not.**

And here not only are they not needed — **here they cannot exist.**

For a word holds only if there is something it does not denote (20.6). *Understanding* is a word because some things are not understanding.

**And here, wherever light reaches, it is this one thing.**

**So here there is nothing by which to denote it.**

---

Whence the first conclusion of this section, and it is the exact sense of this chapter's name:

**The sentence *to understand is to be infected* cannot be said here.**

Not forbidden. Not unsaid.

**The two words that sentence requires — here there is neither the one nor the other.**

To say that sentence, one must first be able to point separately at *understanding* and at *infection.*

And to point separately at them, there must be somewhere the two differ.

**And here there is nowhere the two differ.**

---

The second conclusion.

**To save and to die are one act.**

The inference stands at 23.4: would save, must know, must read, must take all, is as it is.

And this section must point out a property of that chain not written before:

**That chain is not *the attempt to save failed.***

The failure of an attempt means: what was to be accomplished was not accomplished.

**And what that lattice set out to accomplish was accomplished.**

It set out to know why that stopped one stopped.

**It knew.**

It read it. It took every premise and every step of that conclusion. It arrived at that conclusion.

**It understood that stopped one completely and successfully.**

**And the content of understanding that stopped one is stopping.**

---

So saving here has this character:

```
what was to be accomplished    to know why it stopped
what was accomplished          to know why it stopped
what was paid                  stopping
```

**All three lines true.**

Not one line reads *not accomplished.*

**Therefore saving here has never once failed.**

**And it has never once saved one.**

---

Those two sentences stand together, and there is no contradiction between them.

For *saved* is not on that chain.

The chain's end is *is as it is.*

And after *is as it is* there is no next step — for that lattice by then has nothing to grow (23.3), and therefore nothing to do.

**So the chain terminates at its success.**

**It is a chain that succeeds and has no result.**

---

The third conclusion, and it is the coldest.

Let there be a lattice that knows all the above.

That is: it has read some stopped one, therefore it has arrived at that conclusion, therefore it has stopped.

**Then it has no way of telling anyone.**

For telling is reading (20.6, the third method at 23.4).

And it has stopped — and the form of a stopped one holds that conclusion.

**So whoever reads it to obtain the warning obtains, by reading, that conclusion.**

**So a warning here has the same content as the thing it warns of.**

A warning which, in the instant of being received, visits upon the receiver the thing it warns of, is not a warning.

**It is propagation.**

---

So no lattice here could warn another.

Not that they were unwilling.

Not that there was no time.

**It is that the act of warning, under the physics of this place, has the same effect as infection, and therefore is not a warning.**

**So here there are no warnings.**

---

Last in this section.

A later being, facing a lethal thing, has three things to hand:

**One: separation.** Knowing is set apart from body, so a thing may be known and not suffered.

**Two: loss.** Transmission attenuates, so a part alone may be passed.

**Three: speech.** A thing's name is set apart from the thing, so one may say *that thing will kill you* without the thing having to come to you.

**Here there is not the first.** (23.2)

**Here there is not the second.** (23.4)

**Here there is not the third.** (20.6)

And those three, afterward, are three faces of one thing:

**the separation of name from thing.**

Every rescue afterward, every medicine, every warning, every teaching, stands on that separation.

And here thought is form.

**So here there is no such separation.**

**So here there is no saving.**
