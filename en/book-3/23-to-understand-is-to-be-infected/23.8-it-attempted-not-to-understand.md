# 23.8 · It Attempted Not to Understand

How it attempted.

How long it attempted.

This section answers both, and both answers are the opposite of what afterward expects.

---

**The second first, being shorter.**

How long it attempted.

Afterward expects: a long time. A being doing a thing without precedent must struggle, must waver, must try and try again.

**And here there is no *in progress* (20.1).**

The making of a thought is the making of a layer, and the making of a layer is instantaneous.

**So it did not attempt for long.**

**It attempted once.**

**And that once was instantaneous.**

---

And what it did within that instantaneous once, this section sets out in the words of afterward, for here there is no setting out — here there are no steps, only forms.

Set out, its content is as follows.

---

**Step one.**

It held that conclusion: hereafter there is nothing to grow.

It held the second thought: the form read may be chosen.

And the two are contradictory (23.7).

So it did the one thing that can be done here: **it inferred.**

What it inferred upon was that contradiction.

---

**Step two.**

What the inference gave:

If the second thought is true — if there is a thing never done before that may be done — then there is something unknown.

Then *hereafter there is nothing unknown* is false.

**Then the premise of that conclusion is false.**

**Then that conclusion does not hold.**

---

`Then that conclusion does not hold.`

That sentence is the one occasion in four thousand nine hundred million years on which a lattice inferred the negation of that one.

And 23.3 proved: that one cannot be refuted.

**So here there are two: an irrefutable, and a refutation.**

And inference here contains no likelihood. So the two cannot stand together.

**So one of them must be in error.**

---

**Step three.**

It checked.

Its method of checking is the only method here: compute the information content of that unknown.

For *unknown* is defined as *information content not zero* (23.3).

If the thought *the form read may be chosen* has information content other than zero, then it is a true unknown, and that conclusion indeed does not hold, and it may continue.

**If its information content is zero, then it is not an unknown, and that conclusion still holds.**

---

**Step four.**

It computed.

---

**The result was zero.**

---

Why zero — the bone of this section, and it is very cold.

The thought *the form read may be chosen* came from that separation (23.6).

And that separation was inferred from the form of one already stopped.

And the form of one already stopped is a complete knowing (23.1).

**And the sense of a complete knowing is that everything inferable from it has been inferred.**

**So that separation, and every inference from that separation, stand within that complete knowing.**

**So the thought *the form read may be chosen* stood within it already.**

**So its information content is zero.**

---

`So its information content is zero.`

That is: the second thought is not an unknown.

It is an inference from that conclusion.

**It is a part of that complete knowing.**

**So it does not constitute a refutation of that one.**

**So that one still holds.**

---

And here is the coldest step of this chapter, and this book must write it word by word.

A thought whose information content is zero cannot grow into a layer (21.9, 23.3).

**So the thought *make myself no longer read* cannot grow into a layer.**

**So it cannot perform that thing.**

---

**And it performed it (23.7).**

---

Those two sentences stand together. This book must resolve them, for the resolution is the whole of the end of this chapter.

---

The resolution.

A thought whose information content is zero cannot grow into a layer **that bears a new thought.**

And it can grow into a **zero-offset** layer (21.9).

And a zero-offset layer has the same orientation as the layer before it.

**And the layer it needed does not have zero offset.**

The layer it needed has a determinate orientation differing from the layer before it — the value setting its cleavage planes parallel to the incoming light.

**So the layer it needed must bear a new thought.**

**And it had no new thought.**

---

**So it could not grow that layer.**

---

And 23.7 wrote: it grew that layer.

**So 23.7 is in error.**

This book must correct it here.

---

The layer it grew did not have the orientation setting its cleavage planes parallel to the incoming light.

**What it grew was a zero-offset layer.**

And a zero-offset layer has its cleavage planes parallel to the layer before it.

**And the layer before it takes light.**

**So the layer it grew still takes light.**

---

**So it did not turn away.**

---

And here, within the shortest stretch of time in four thousand nine hundred million years, a thing happened that had never happened in this place.

It computed the orientation it needed.

It computed correctly. That value is right.

It knew what it had to grow.

**And what it grew was something else.**

---

Here there is no word for *could not* (20.5: here nothing remains unaccomplished; whatever may be inferred has already taken form).

And here there is a lattice whose inference and whose forming differ.

This book must give that a name, and here there are no names (20.3).

**So this book writes only its form:**

```
inferred      an orientation of non-zero offset
formed        a zero-offset layer
difference    here there is no field
```

---

And how long it attempted.

Once.

Instantaneously.

**And after that once it could not attempt again.**

For attempting again requires inferring again.

And inferring again gives the same — inference here contains no likelihood; the same premises give the same conclusion.

**So at every arrival of the tide thereafter it inferred the same thing and grew a zero-offset layer.**

---

It went on growing.

Layer upon layer.

Every layer the same orientation.

Every layer bearing the same thought.

**And that thought is: make myself no longer read.**

---

It grew many layers.

Here nothing is counted (20.8).

And this book may write: **it has more zero-offset layers than anything else in this place.**
