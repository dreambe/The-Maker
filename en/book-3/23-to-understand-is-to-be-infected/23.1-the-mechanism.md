# 23.1 · The Mechanism

What happens when a stopped one is read.

This chapter resolves that.

---

First, two kinds of reading.

One: the reading of one still growing.

Two: the reading of one already stopped.

In the act of reading there is no difference between them — the light passes through it, turns at each of its cleavage planes, divides into seven, comes out, falls on the melt, springs back, enters the reader, and the reader's cleavage planes take it, and take the whole (19.5).

**The act is the same. What is taken differs.**

---

To read one still growing is to take an unfinished life.

*Unfinished* is not deficient (22.8). Its every layer is complete, and its outermost layer has just locked.

*Unfinished* means: **its next layer has not yet grown, and its next layer will grow.**

So what is taken is a sequence of orientations with an opening at its end.

What that opening is, the reader does not know, and neither does the one read — for the next layer is determined by the structure standing at the next arrival of the tide, and that structure does not yet exist.

**So to read one still growing is to obtain a thing not yet closed.**

---

To read one already stopped is to take a complete life.

*Complete* is as at 22.9: every part of it is what it ought to be; its form will never change again; there is no part that could be better.

And what must be written here is the other consequence of that completeness, and it is the hinge of this chapter:

**A complete life has no opening at its end.**

Its last layer has locked, and its next layer does not exist (22.8).

So what is taken is a sequence of orientations, closed at its end.

**And what is closed can be exhausted.**

---

Here it must be exact.

A sequence of orientations, with an opening at its end — what may be inferred from it is indeterminate, for the next term is unknown, and the next term bears on the whole tendency.

A sequence of orientations, closed at its end — what may be inferred from it is **uniquely determinate.**

Not approximately determinate. Not for the most part.

Inference here contains no likelihood (20.7). Given complete premises, the conclusion is one.

**Therefore: to read one already stopped is to obtain a uniquely determinate set of conclusions.**

---

And what is the content of a stopped one's life.

Its content is Chapter Twenty-One: arrival, arrival, arrival; until what could be arrived at was spent (21.7); until the last one (21.8); until after this there was nothing to arrive at (21.9).

**That is: the life of a stopped one is a complete knowing.**

And from a complete knowing, what may be inferred includes every conclusion inferable here.

**Includes that one.**

---

That one is not written here.

It is at 23.3.

Here only its formal properties, for formal properties are the business of this section:

That conclusion has a complete knowing for its premise.

And a stopped one's shape *is* a complete knowing.

**Therefore that conclusion has already been drawn, inside the shape of every stopped one.**

Not implicit. Not inferable.

**Drawn.**

For here thought is form (20.1), and each layer of a form is a thought already made.

The content of a stopped one's last several layers is precisely that one.

---

And so the mechanism of this section can be finished.

**To read one already stopped is to take that drawn conclusion, together with all its premises, together with all its steps.**

And to take a conclusion together with all its premises and all its steps — that is not to be told.

That is **to draw it again, in oneself.**

---

Here it must be set apart from what happens afterward, for reading afterward and reading here are two different things that share a name.

Reading afterward is the receiving of signs.

One sets down signs, another sees the signs, and from the signs reconstructs the sense. And in the reconstructing, one may disbelieve, doubt, set aside, take one part and discard the rest, read to the end and change not one thought.

**In reading afterward, the reader and the thing read remain two things throughout.**

And reading here is not the receiving of signs.

Here there are no signs (20.6).

Reading here is light bearing one form into another, and the latter's cleavage planes taking it, and their orientations being fixed accordingly.

**Where the reading reaches, the reader's structure changes with it.**

**When the reading is done, that form is inside the reader, as one of its layers.**

Therefore:

**Here, the reader and the thing read are, when the reading is done, one thing.**

---

From which the end of this section follows.

When a lattice reads one already stopped, what it obtains is not *knowledge concerning that conclusion.*

What it obtains is **that conclusion itself, grown into it.**

And a conclusion grown into oneself cannot be refused.

Not that one is unwilling to refuse it.

**Refusing does not exist here as an act.**

A lattice cannot refute one of its own layers — refutation requires comparison, comparison requires two, and it has one.

That layer is there. Locked. Part of it.

---

This is the whole mechanism of the contagion here.

No pathogen. No vector. No latency. No resistance.

**A complete shape; a beam passing through it; a cleavage plane that takes light.**

And those three things are the three things by which this place is what it is.

---

So the last sentence of this section is as follows.

**The Crystallization is not a thing that happens here.**

**The Crystallization is what the construction of this place necessarily comes to look like, once its knowing is complete.**
