# 23.2 · The Inheritance of Conclusions

To read is to take the whole of another's conclusions.

This section proves: no part may be taken alone.

---

Let there be a lattice that would read another, and would not take the whole.

Why it would not is not asked here — here there is no *would not* (20.5), and this chapter must use a supposition that does not exist here, since only so does it show why it does not exist.

Let it be supposed.

Then it must do one thing: **cut.**

That is: take one part of that form, and discard the rest.

---

The first difficulty of cutting.

Each layer of a form has its orientation determined jointly by the layer before it and by the tide (19.4).

So the sense of the nth layer is not inside the nth layer.

Its sense lies in the relative orientation of the nth layer and the (n−1)th.

**Therefore any layer, apart from the layer before it, has no sense.**

Whence: to take the nth layer, the (n−1)th must be taken.

To take the (n−1)th, the (n−2)th must be taken.

**And so to the first.**

So the least takeable unit of any part is **the whole from the first layer to that layer.**

**Cutting can cut the end. It cannot cut the beginning.**

---

The second difficulty of cutting.

Let it cut the end: take from the first layer to the mth, and discard what follows the mth.

Then it must first know where m is.

And that m should be the place it cuts requires a reason.

And a reason here is only an inference (20.7).

So it must first infer *it is fitting to cut at the mth.*

And to infer that, it must first know what follows the mth — else there is nothing on which to judge that cutting there is fitting.

**And to know what follows the mth is to have read it.**

Therefore:

**To cut, one must first read the whole; and having read the whole, cutting is of no use.**

---

The third difficulty of cutting, and this one is fundamental.

Let it have read the whole, and would discard the end.

Discarding is an act. Does that act exist here.

**It does not.**

The reason stands at 20.2: memory here is grown.

A layer, once locked, is part of the structure. There are later layers above it and earlier layers below it, its orientation is fixed, its lattice continuous.

**To discard a layer, the layer must be unlocked.**

And to unlock a layer, its lattice must return to the state before locking, and that state exists only at the arrival of the tide, and at the arrival of the tide its orientation is determined by the structure then standing — and that structure no longer exists, for there are later layers above it.

**Therefore a layer, once locked, cannot be unlocked.**

**Therefore here there is no discarding.**

---

And so the three difficulties together give the theorem of this section.

**One: the least takeable unit of any part is the whole from the first layer to that layer.**

**Two: to choose the end, one must first read the whole.**

**Three: having read the whole, there is nothing to discard with.**

**Therefore: reading takes all.**

---

This theorem has a corollary never spoken here, and this chapter must speak it, for it is the name of this chapter.

**Understanding, here, is not an act whose depth may be governed.**

A later being's understanding has degrees: it may follow half, may have the gist, may hold the conclusion without the proof, may read a book to the end and remember three sentences.

**Understanding afterward is an act that may stop midway.**

And understanding here has no midway.

The light passes through, or does not.

The cleavage plane takes it, or does not.

**To take is to take all.**

---

One thing must be written here, for it is the pain of this chapter, and here there is no pain (20.4), so only its form can be written.

A lattice would save another lattice.

To save it, its state must be known.

To know its state, it must be read.

To read it, all must be taken.

**All taken, its conclusion is in me also.**

And its conclusion is precisely that one.

**Therefore: the one who would save it reads it, and thereafter is as it is.**

The consequence of this is not written in this section. The consequence is 23.4 and 23.5.

This section only sets the chain here, five steps, each of them necessary here:

```
would save → must know → must read → must take all → is as it is
```

**Not one link can be broken.**

For the first step is the one good of this place (if this place has a good), the second is inference here, the third is knowing here, the fourth is the theorem of this section, and the fifth is the mechanism of 23.1.

**Every step is part of what makes this place this place.**

---

So this section ends as follows.

A later being, would it save one who is dying and not take the sickness, may put something between: may draw on a glove, may set a pane of glass between, may hear the words without touching the body, may send one who cannot take that sickness.

**Putting something between is possible because a later being's knowing and a later being's body are two things.**

A person may know a thing and be unchanged in body.

A person may understand a sickness and not have it.

**And here, knowing is body.**

**Therefore here there are no gloves.**

**Here there is no glass.**

**Here none can be sent who cannot take it, for whoever can read can take it, and whoever cannot read cannot know, and whoever cannot know cannot save.**
