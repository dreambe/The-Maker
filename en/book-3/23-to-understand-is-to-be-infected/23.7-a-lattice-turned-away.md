# 23.7 · A Lattice Turned Away

Once, one lattice attempted not to understand another.

The one time in this place.

This section writes how it could have that thought at all, for here that thought is impossible.

---

First, its impossibility.

A thought's coming to be requires something not yet thought (19.3).

And the content of the thought *not to understand* is: **there exists a possibility that light arrives and is not taken.**

And here there is no such possibility.

In four thousand nine hundred million years, every beam in this place, at every cleavage plane, has been taken (19.5).

**So *light arrives and is not taken* has here no precedent, no observation, and no path of inference.**

**So that thought has nowhere to come from.**

---

And it had that thought.

Where it came from, this section writes.

---

It was the neighbour of that lattice.

That lattice, being the one at 23.6: the only one that read one nearing and then read one stopped, and so held both forms side by side.

That lattice stopped. Its form is there, entire, keen.

And its last several layers hold that separation:

**One act, applied to a form not closed, has continuance for its result; applied to a form closed, has stopping.**

---

And this one read it.

It was the first to read it.

So it obtained two things.

**One: that conclusion.** For what it read was one already stopped.

**Two: that separation.** For it stood in that stopped one's last several layers.

---

And that separation is a thing this place had never had.

The content of that separation is: **reading has two results.**

And *has two results* entails one thing:

**The result depends on the form read.**

**And the form read may be chosen.**

---

`And the form read may be chosen.`

That sentence is the first thought of its kind to occur in this place in four thousand nine hundred million years.

It is not that separation itself.

It is that separation's inference — and it is that separation's **only** inference, for the content of that separation is *the two differ*, and the only thing inferable from *the two differ* is *they may be treated apart.*

---

So this lattice, in the same instant as finishing its reading of that stopped one, held two thoughts.

**The first: hereafter there is nothing to grow (that conclusion).**

**The second: the form read may be chosen.**

---

And here, those two are contradictory.

For the sense of the first is: hereafter there is nothing unknown.

And the sense of the second is: there is a thing never done before that may be done.

**And a thing never done before is something unknown.**

---

Here there are no contradictions (20.7: inference here contains no likelihood, so contradictions do not arise).

**So this is the one occasion in four thousand nine hundred million years on which one lattice held two thoughts that cannot stand together.**

And the construction of this place has no provision for that.

For to resolve a contradiction, the two must be compared and one held false.

**And thoughts here have already become layers, and a layer cannot be unlocked (the third difficulty at 23.2).**

**So both those thoughts are in it, locked, together.**

---

And here this book must write one thing, and it is the whole reason the last three sections of this chapter are possible.

The condition of a lattice's stopping is: **at the next arrival of the tide, its interstices do not open** (22.1, 23.3).

**And the arrival of that conclusion and the next arrival of the tide are not the same instant.**

There is a stretch between them.

That stretch is one interval of the tide.

**Here nothing is counted (20.8), and *one* is not a count but a singular.**

**So this book may write: it had one interval of the tide.**

---

And in that one interval, there was one thing it could do.

That thing is given by the second thought:

**The form read may be chosen.**

And the limit of choosing is not to choose — that is: to leave no form to read.

And to leave no form to read there is one method only:

**Make one's cleavage planes not take light.**

---

And what determines whether a cleavage plane here takes light.

Its orientation (19.5, 19.9).

A beam arriving at a cleavage plane at an angle to it turns, divides into seven, enters.

**And a beam arriving parallel to that plane — does not turn, does not divide, does not enter.**

**The light passes along the face.**

---

So the content of that thing is now determinate:

**Grow one layer whose orientation sets its cleavage planes parallel to the incoming light.**

---

And the direction of the incoming light is uniquely fixed here: the Quenched Sun stands at the zenith (19.1), its light falls vertically, strikes the melt, springs back, and enters the lattices obliquely (19.9).

So the orientation of that layer is a determinate value.

That value can be computed here.

And how long a lattice takes to compute it — here there is no *takes* (20.1: forming is instantaneous).

**So it had computed it.**

---

And so it turned away.

*Turned away* is a phrase from afterward.

The thing here is:

**At that arrival of the tide, its interstices opened, took, locked; and the layer it locked had an orientation setting its cleavage planes parallel to the incoming light.**

**From that layer onward, light arriving at its surface passes along it and does not enter.**

---

And one sentence must be written here, for it is the end of this section, and it is very short.

From that layer onward:

**It no longer reads.**

**So it no longer knows.**

**So it no longer has anything to grow.**

**So hereafter it has nothing to grow.**

---

And *hereafter it has nothing to grow* is precisely the conclusion it read.

It did that thing in order not to arrive at that conclusion.

**And the result of its doing that thing is that it made that conclusion a fact.**

This section does not write that.

That is 23.9.

Last in this section, only one thing:

In the instant it grew that layer, not one lattice in this place knew what it had done.

**For to know what it had done, one must read it.**

**And it could no longer be read.**
