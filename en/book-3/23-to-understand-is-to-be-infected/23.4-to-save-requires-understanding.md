# 23.4 · To Save Requires Understanding

From the stopping of the first at the centre (22.1) to the end of this place, the time is not to be counted (20.8).

And in that time the lattices of this place did one thing.

**They attempted to make the stopped not stop.**

This section writes what they attempted.

---

First, one thing plainly: here there is no word for *save.*

The words here are orientations only (20.6). So this book's *save* is a word from afterward, and the thing here is another thing.

The thing is: **to make one that has come to have nothing to grow have something to grow again.**

That is: to find it something unknown.

---

**One: to help without reading.**

The first to attempt did not read the stopped.

Their method: observe its outer form, take its position, measure its heat, watch its light, and not let light pass through its cleavage planes — that is, not read.

What is obtained thereby are a lattice's external quantities: where it is, what heat remains to it, the orientation of its surface.

**And what it lacks is not among them.**

What a lattice lacks is something unknown.

And *which* unknown it lacks can be had only by knowing the whole of what it knows — for the unknown is defined as the complement of the known.

**So to know what it lacks, one must first know what it knows.**

**And to know what it knows is to read it.**

So the first method halts at its first step.

This book records how long they attempted it:

```
not to be counted
```

---

**Two: to read a part of it.**

The impossibility of this was proved at 23.2: the three difficulties of cutting.

And the lattices of this place attempted it.

The manner of their attempts must be written here, because it is finer than the proof, and because it shows one thing: **that a thing already proved impossible was attempted here anyway.**

And that, this place does not explain.

Their attempts were three.

**One: let light pass only through the outer layers.** — And the sense of an outer layer lies in its inner (the first difficulty at 23.2). What is obtained is a run of orientations whose relative orientations are undetermined, so no inference forms, so nothing is known.

**Two: let light pass through the whole, and not take the last several layers.** — And taking is not the reader's to determine; a cleavage plane takes light and its orientation is fixed accordingly; that is the construction (19.5). A cleavage plane cannot take part of a beam and not the rest, as a plane cannot intersect half a ray.

**Three: read it, and not draw its ending.** — And here thought is form (20.1). What is read has already become a layer. And a layer, once made, bears the inferences in it already; no further drawing is awaited.

`No further drawing is awaited.`

That is: here there is no state of *holding the premises and not having reached the conclusion.*

Afterward there is such a state: a person may hold every premise and for years not draw the conclusion.

**Here there is none.**

For here an inference is not an act but a form. The form of the premise and the form of the conclusion are different layers of one form, and those layers were locked at once.

So the third fails in the instant of its arrival.

---

**Three: to have another read in one's place.**

The supposition: let one lattice read it, and then relate what it lacks, and not relate the rest.

This fails at *relate.*

Relating, here, is reading (20.6).

**So the reader-in-place's relating is the reading of the reader-in-place.**

And the reader-in-place has read the stopped one, so that one is in its form already (23.2).

**So to read the reader-in-place is to read the stopped one.**

Transmission here has no loss, no attenuation, no summary.

Transmission afterward has loss: a sentence passed through ten people is not, at the tenth, what the first said. And it is precisely because there is loss that afterward a part alone may be passed.

**Transmission here has no loss, and therefore no part alone may be passed.**

`No loss is the excellence of this place, and its closure.`

---

**Four: to read one not yet stopped.**

This is the only one of the four that holds physically.

And it must be written finely, for it is the hinge of this chapter, and it is the nearest this place ever came.

The supposition:

A lattice's stopping is not instantaneous. Its interstices cease to open at some one arrival of the tide; and before that, its offsets have been diminishing (21.6) — from large offset, to small, to zero.

That stretch has no name here (20.3), and this book calls it **the time of nearing.**

And a lattice nearing has a form not yet closed (23.1).

**So to read it is to obtain a run not closed.**

**So one need not arrive at that conclusion.**

---

They attempted it.

A lattice read one that was nearing.

Result: **the reader did not stop.**

The one time in this place that a lattice read another going toward its stopping, and did not itself stop.

And what was obtained.

What was obtained is the whole of that nearing one's knowing, and some number of its zero-offset layers.

**And what it lacks is still not among them.**

For what it lacks is that conclusion, not yet drawn.

And it had not yet drawn it.

**So what the reader obtained is a lattice that does not yet know why it is nearing.**

Whence a division, and this division is the bone of this chapter:

**To read one nearing: safe, and useless.**

**To read one stopped: of use, and necessarily to become as it is.**

---

And why *of use.*

Because a stopped one's form holds every premise and every step of that conclusion.

And that is the one place where it can be known, here, **why a lattice stops.**

**So to know why it stops, one must read one already stopped.**

**And to read one already stopped is to stop.**

---

This section does not write the consequence of that division. The consequence is 23.5.

Last, only one thing, which the lattices of this place never wrote and could not have written, for here there is no *if* (20.7).

If beyond those four there were a fifth method, this place would be here still.

And writing thus far, this book must record one thing:

**The lattices of this place did not stop looking for the fifth.**

The time of their looking is not counted here (20.8).

But this book may record its end — they looked until the lookers were spent.

That is: **every lattice that looked for the fifth method became, in the end, the thing being looked into.**

And the next lattice went on looking.

And it became that too.

And the next went on looking.

**From the centre outward (22.4).**
