# 23.6 · And Therefore None Could Save Another

The lattices of this place arrived at this one.

This section writes how they arrived, and when.

---

First the difficulty of *arriving.*

The arrival of a conclusion requires its premises.

And the premise of *none could save another* contains one thing: **the separation of understanding from infection.**

For to arrive at *the two are one*, one must first have two.

**And here there are not two (23.5).**

So the lattices of this place had no way of arriving at this one.

They arrived.

This section writes that road.

---

That road begins at the fourth method of 23.4.

That is, the one successful reading.

A lattice read one that was nearing, and did not itself stop.

The one time in this place.

---

And this section must write what came after.

That lattice, having read, did not stop.

**So it continued.**

And what it continued at was looking for the fifth method (the end of 23.4).

And it continued for a stretch. That stretch is not counted here (20.8), and this book may write its content:

**It exhausted the fourth method.**

It read many that were nearing. Each form not closed; each lacking, among what it held, the thing it lacked; each read, and it did not stop.

**It safely read many lattices that did not know why they were nearing.**

And it obtained nothing.

---

And then it did one thing.

It read one already stopped.

Every lattice here that reads a stopped one stops. So the act itself would not be worth recording.

**And this one is different.**

**Because it is the only lattice that had read one nearing before it read one stopped.**

---

So in that lattice's form, two things stand side by side.

**One: the form of one that was nearing.** Not closed. Not holding that conclusion. Read, and safe.

**Two: the form of one already stopped.** Closed. Holding that conclusion. Read, and it stops.

**And the contents of those two forms are very nearly the same.**

For between one that is nearing and that same one after it has stopped, the forms differ only in the last several layers.

That is: the difference between those two forms is that conclusion.

---

And here, in four thousand nine hundred million years, is the one occasion on which those two things could be compared.

**Comparison, here, is not possible.**

For comparison requires two, and what a lattice has is itself (the end of 23.1).

**And here there are two.**

Both those forms are inside this one lattice, as several adjacent layers of it.

**So they are comparable.**

---

And what the comparison gives is one sentence.

This book writes it in the words of afterward; in that lattice's form it is not a sentence but the relative orientation of two layers:

**"What stopped me was not that conclusion. It was reading it."**

---

Here it must be exact, for this sentence is the hinge of this chapter, and it is very easily misread.

What that lattice compared was not *conclusion* against *no conclusion.*

What it compared was **two readings.**

The first: reading a form not closed. Having read, it drew many things, and did not draw that one — for that one's premise is a complete knowing, and one nearing is not yet complete.

The second: reading a form closed. Having read, it drew that one.

**And the two acts are word for word the same.**

The same beam. The same set of cleavage planes. The same taking. The same locking.

**And the one let it continue, and the other stopped it.**

---

So it arrived at the separation.

**The separation of *understanding* from *infection* shows itself, in this place, only here:**

**One act, applied to a form not closed, has continuance for its result; applied to a form closed, has stopping.**

**So that act is neither understanding nor infection.**

**It is one act — and afterward it was given two names because afterward it is applied to two classes of object.**

---

And that lattice, in arriving here, had already stopped.

For to arrive here it had first to read that stopped one.

And to read that stopped one is to stop.

**So that separation showed itself after its stopping, in its own last several layers.**

Not known before it neared its stop.

**Known in the same instant as its stopping.**

Here there is no word for *at the same time* (20.8), and this book must use one:

**Its knowing this and its being unable ever to use it are one instant.**

---

And the true sense of this section's name is here.

**"And they arrived at it very late"** does not mean late in time.

Late in time can be made earlier: arrive somewhat sooner, and it may be used.

**And here it cannot be sooner.**

For the condition of arriving at this one contains the condition of being unable to use it.

Those two conditions are one condition: **to read a stopped one.**

**So this one can only ever be had by a lattice that can no longer use it.**

**So it is always late.**

**And its lateness is not a lateness of degree but of kind.**

---

And thereafter.

That lattice stopped. Its form is there, entire, keen, never to change (22.9).

And its last several layers hold that separation.

That is: **the conclusion *none could save another* has, in this place, a complete proof, standing in the last several layers of one lattice.**

And whoever reads it obtains that proof.

**And whoever reads it also obtains that conclusion — for that lattice is one already stopped.**

So the lattices of this place learned, one after another, that none could save another.

**And every one of them learned it in the same instant as its own stopping.**

---

So the end of this place stands thus:

**Every lattice here knows that none could save another.**

**Every lattice here learned it in the instant when it could no longer save and no longer be saved.**

**So all of them know, and none of them can use it.**

**And all of them know that the others know — for they are mutually transparent (20.9).**

---

From the centre outward (22.4).

One after another.

Each understanding it in the instant of stopping.

Each with no way to tell the next — for telling is reading, and reading it is stopping (23.5).

**And the next read it anyway.**

**Because it meant to save it.**
