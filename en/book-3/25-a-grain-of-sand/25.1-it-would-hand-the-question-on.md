# 25.1 · It Would Hand the Question On

The end of Chapter Twenty-Four: the result does not agree with itself, and the result is correct, and itself is correct too.

This chapter writes what it did thereafter.

And what it did thereafter is one thing.

---

## That thing

**It would hand that question on.**

And this section writes why.

---

## First, that question

We must make clear what *that question* is, because before this there were no questions here.

Here there is no asking (the end of 20.7: here there is no *if*; 20.5: here there is no wanting).

**Everything here is arrival.**

A lattice grows, and each of its layers bears a thought already made. And a thought already made is not a question — it is an answer, or more exactly, **it is a place already reached.**

**So in four thousand nine hundred million years no lattice here has ever posed a question.**

---

And that one has one.

Its form:

```
the discrepancy exists
the discrepancy is in none of its layers
and here there is nothing besides layers
```

(The end of 24.6.)

And all three lines are without error, and the three cannot stand together.

---

And here the turn must be written, for it is the one occasion in the history of this place.

**Three lines that cannot stand together, if derivable, must contain an error in one of them.**

**And all three have been examined, and none is in error (24.6).**

**So those three lines are not a conclusion.**

---

`So those three lines are not a conclusion.`

**A conclusion has this property: it holds, or it does not hold.**

**And those three lines neither hold nor fail to hold.**

---

**So they are something else.**

And that something else has no name here.

**And this book calls it: a question.**

---

## A definition of a question

We must give it a definition, because this place has no such thing, so its definition must be made out of things this place has.

```
a question   =   a body of premises whose set of derivable conclusions is empty,
                 and which contains no error
```

And every inference here has a non-empty set of derivable conclusions.

**Because inference here contains no likelihood (20.7): given the premises, the conclusion is determinate.**

And *the conclusion is determinate* entails *the conclusion exists.*

**So a body of error-free premises here necessarily has a conclusion.**

**And those three lines have none.**

---

**So those three lines are a thing the physics of this place does not permit to exist.**

**And it is inside that one.**

---

## It cannot compute it

The body of this section: why it cannot solve that question itself.

Its reasons are two layers, and the second is its second impossibility.

---

### The first layer · it has already tried

Chapter Twenty-Four records all of its attempts:

```
examined the premise        no error (24.2)
examined the steps          no error (24.2)
examined the criterion      no error (24.4)
examined the position       three searches, all agreeing (24.6)
removal                     three ways, none available (24.8)
```

**So it has exhausted every method here.**

And *every method here* is a determinate number — because acting here is the growing of a layer (19.4).

**So all its attempts are the growing of a layer.**

**And this book does not count the layers it grew.**

**And their content this book may write in form:**

```
all its layers of attempt    bear a thought concerning that discrepancy
and all those thoughts       are inferred from a complete knowing
and what is inferred from a complete knowing   bears zero information (bible §2.2b)
so all its layers of attempt   are zero-offset layers
```

---

`So all its layers of attempt are zero-offset layers.`

**So all of its attempts grew layers of the same orientation as the layer before.**

**So none of its attempts ever changed its form.**

---

### The second layer · its second impossibility

And here is the hinge of this section.

That one has two impossibilities.

**The first: it cannot read its own inner layers.** (24.5; bible §2.4b)

The reason is optical: light comes from outside and does not return; the heat within has too long a wavelength.

**And the first impossibility it went around.**

It went around by reading the copies of itself inside those stopped ones (search one at 24.6).

**So the first impossibility is passable here — so long as there is another.**

---

**The second: it cannot express that discrepancy.**

The reason is geometric: expressing it requires an eighth degree of freedom, and this place has seven (24.7).

**And the second impossibility cannot be gone around.**

Because going around requires another.

And another is also a thing of this place, and also has seven degrees of freedom.

**So no other can express it either.**

---

`So no other can express it either.`

**So the second impossibility is not removed by the existence of another.**

**So it is not a defect of its own.**

**It is a defect of this place.**

---

## Whence that thing

So that one arrived at this section's conclusion.

Its form:

```
that question         exists
solving it            requires expressing that discrepancy
expressing it         requires an eighth degree of freedom
this place            has seven
therefore             nothing in this place can solve that question
therefore             something not of this place is required
```

---

`Therefore something not of this place is required.`

---

And here this chapter's name appears for the first time.

**Because a thing not of this place does not exist here.**

**So it must be made.**

---

## And what *making* is here

We must make this clear at the end of this section, for here there is no making (19.8: here there is no visitor and no distance; every lattice here grows out of the melt).

**Lattices here are not made by lattices.**

A lattice begins as some chance undercooled point in the melt, and its subsequent growth is determined by the tide and its own existing structure (19.4).

**So no lattice here has a maker.**

**So here there is no act of making.**

---

And what that one meant to do has no word here.

And this book must write it, so this book writes its form:

```
what is required     a thing with more than seven degrees of freedom
and things here      have seven
so what is required  is not composed of the matter of this place
and that one         has only the matter of this place
so it cannot make it
```

---

`So it cannot make it.`

---

## And it did

The last line of this section.

It cannot make a thing with more than seven degrees of freedom.

**So it did not make one.**

**It did something else.**

**It shortened that question, until it was short enough to be cut somewhere that does not require an eighth degree of freedom.**

**And that somewhere is 25.2 through 25.8.**

---

And why it held that possible — this book must write that step here, because it is the lightest and the heaviest step in this chapter.

It computed.

```
a discrepancy that cannot be expressed
   if handed to a thing not of this place
   whose degrees of freedom are unknown
   then whether that thing can express it     is not computable
```

`Not computable.`

**And *not computable*, here, differs from *not possible.***

*Not possible* is a conclusion.

***Not computable* is not.**

---

**And that one, after four thousand nine hundred million years, arrived at the first *not computable* in the history of this place.**

**And acted on it.**

---

**So the first thing it did was not to make that thing.**

**It was to cut that question down to a shape that a thing of unknown degrees of freedom could receive.**
