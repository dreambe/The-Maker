# 25.5 · What Remained

Four lines remained after the third cut (25.4).

And this section writes the last cutting it did on those four, and what remained after it.

And what remained is shorter than it expected.

---

## The length it expected

It had computed it.

Before the first cut, it had computed how much that question would finally leave.

Its method:

```
the lower bound of a question's cutting
   = the number of assertions in that question that the receiver cannot rebuild
     and that cannot be derived from the rest
```

And it counted those assertions.

And what it counted was:

```
four
```

**Four assertions.**

That is:

```
one    that there is such a thing
two    that the reason is not within them
three  that expressing the reason requires a thing the asker does not have
four   that the above is without error
```

**So it expected four lines to remain.**

**And it computed that four before the first cut.**

**And after the third cut, exactly four remained.**

---

`And after the third cut, exactly four remained.`

**So it computed correctly.**

---

## And it did not stop

And it did not stop at four.

And this section writes why, because that reason is not in its computation.

---

It did a thing it had not done before.

**It read those four lines.**

Not computed them. Not verified them.

**Read.**

And *read*, here, means light passing through a form (19.5).

**And those four lines are not a form — they are the relative orientation of three cleavage planes inside it (24.6).**

**So it cannot read them.**

---

So it did a stranger thing.

**It computed those four lines as a thing a receiver would read.**

That is: it stipulated a receiver satisfying assumptions one through six (25.3), gave it the four lines, and computed what that receiver would obtain.

Elapsed — here there is no elapsed.

---

## What that receiver would obtain

The result.

---

**Line one: *There is a thing with two parts in its interior, both existing, that cannot both be true.***

That receiver obtains: an assertion.

And that assertion's content is the state of a thing.

**And that receiver does not know that thing.**

**So that assertion is, to it, a report about something unfamiliar.**

And the usability of a report about something unfamiliar, it computed:

```
a receiver, given a report about something unfamiliar
   what it can do        record it
   what it cannot do     verify it
                         act on it
```

---

`What it cannot do: verify it, act on it.`

**So line one, to that receiver, is unverifiable and cannot be acted on.**

**So it is useless.**

---

And having computed that, it made the most important rewriting in this chapter.

---

## That rewriting

It replaced *two parts* in line one with **the content** of those two parts.

And the content of those two parts, copied (they are layers n and n+1 inside it):

```
first part     that thing knows the whole of itself, so it has nothing to grow hereafter,
               so to grow and not to grow yield it the same
second part    that thing is still growing
```

**So line one, unfolded:**

```
There is a thing that knows the whole of itself;
and from that it follows that to grow and not to grow yield it the same;
and it is still growing;
and those two cannot both be true.
```

---

## And here the thing happened

It computed the equivalence of the four lines unfolded and the four lines unfolded not.

Result:

```
the same
```

**And it computed their usability to a receiver.**

Result:

```
unfolded not     unverifiable
unfolded         verifiable
```

---

`Unfolded: verifiable.`

And the proof, which is the hinge of this whole chapter:

```
unfolded not      "there is a thing with two interior parts that cannot both be true"
                  the receiver must first find that thing in order to verify

unfolded          "there is a thing that knows the whole of itself, and is still growing"
                  the receiver can verify one thing first: whether it itself knows the
                  whole of itself
                  and that it can verify for itself (assumption two: the receiver can
                  observe itself)
```

---

**So unfolded, the assertion becomes a test the receiver can run on itself.**

---

`So unfolded, the assertion becomes a test the receiver can run on itself.`

---

## And thereby the other three may be cut

So it computed.

**Line two: *and the reason is not within them.***

May be cut.

Proof:

```
if the receiver runs that test on itself
   and the result is "both are true"
   then it will go looking for the reason
   and it will find that the reason is not within those two
      because it has already tested those two, and the reason is not in them
```

**So line two follows from the receiver's own process of verification.**

**So cut.**

---

**Line three: *and expressing its reason requires a thing the asker does not have.***

May be cut.

Proof:

```
if the receiver goes looking for that reason and does not find it
   then it will compute why it cannot find it
   and it will conclude: expressing that reason requires a thing it does not have
      because that is the only possible reason for not finding it (under assumption three)
```

**So line three the receiver infers for itself as well.**

---

And here we must stop.

**Because the line it cut is its own second impossibility (25.1).**

That line says: expressing that reason requires a thing the asker does not have.

**And that line is the whole reason it is handing this question on.**

**And it cut it.**

**Because a receiver that walks that far will infer it for itself.**

---

**Line four: *and the above is examined, and without error.***

**May not be cut.**

Proof:

```
if cut, the receiver may suppose
   that the asker never examined it
   and that the question arises from an error
and the nature of this question is precisely that it is without error
so it must stay
```

**So it stays.**

---

And it made one rewriting of that line.

Because *the above is examined* has the asker for its subject.

And that receiver does not know whether the asker is to be trusted.

**So *I have examined it* is useless to a receiver that does not know the speaker.**

---

It rewrote it thus:

```
original   and the above is examined, and without error
revised    (deleted)
```

---

`(Deleted.)`

**It deleted it.**

And its reason is the opposite of the *may not be cut* it had written above.

And that is not a contradiction.

**It is something deeper, and this book must write it word by word.**

---

Its reason:

```
the function of the sentence "I have examined it, without error"
   = to make it unnecessary for the receiver to examine it itself

and if the receiver need not examine it itself
   then it will not run that test on itself

and that test is the purpose of this whole thing (see above)

so the sentence "I have examined it" works against the purpose of this question

so it must be deleted
```

---

`So the sentence "I have examined it" works against the purpose of this question.`

---

**So it deleted its own four thousand nine hundred million years of examination.**

The whole of Chapter Twenty-Four: three searches, three ways, fourteen thousand and seventy-five arrivals, the reading of the void.

**All deleted.**

**Because if it wrote *I have already checked*, the receiver would not check.**

---

## What remained

So of four lines, one remained.

And that one is line one unfolded.

We copy it:

```
There is a thing that knows the whole of itself;
and it is still growing.
```

---

**Two clauses.**

And it computed the equivalence of those two clauses with the sixteen lines (the head of 25.2) — under assumptions one through six, and supposing the receiver runs that test on itself.

Result:

```
the same
```

---

## Shorter than it expected

It expected four lines.

**Two clauses remained.**

And it computed the difference.

And it found the difference's source.

The form of its record:

```
expected         4 lines
obtained         2 clauses
source of the difference    see below
```

And *see below*:

```
   what the expectation's method (the head of this section) counted
      = the number of assertions the receiver cannot rebuild and cannot derive
        from the rest

   and that method did not count one thing
      = the assertions the receiver generates itself in the course of testing itself

   and those number two (lines two and three)
   and one further was deleted, not because derivable but because its function
      works against the purpose (line four)

   so 4 − 2 − 1 = 1
   and what remains is one line unfolded, whose form is two clauses
```

---

`So 4 − 2 − 1 = 1.`

**And its expected method had missed one thing.**

**What it missed was *what the receiver will do itself.***

---

And one thing at the end of this section, because it is the one place in this whole chapter where its computation was wrong, and wrong in the right direction.

**When it computed that lower bound, it treated the receiver as a reader.**

**And by the end of the cutting, it treated the receiver as a thing that will go and check.**

---

**And those two differ.**

**A reader has what it is given.**

**A thing that will go and check does more the less it is given.**

---

**So its expectation was wrong.**

**And the direction of its error is: it underestimated the thing it did not know existed.**

---

## And those two clauses still lack one thing

At the end of this section, that lack.

Those two clauses are two assertions (the end of 25.4).

**And two assertions ask nothing.**

**So they are not yet a question.**

---

**And the thing they lack is 25.6 and 25.7.**

**And that thing is one character.**

**And that character does not exist here.**
