# 25.7 · No Question Mark

That sentence lacks one thing.

That thing must make two assertions into an asking.

And here there is no such thing (the end of 25.4).

**So it had to make one.**

And this section writes what it made, and why it is not a question mark.

---

## That question mark

The question mark first, because it is the obvious answer, and it was rejected.

---

A question mark is a thing of afterward. And within this chapter it is a thing this place could make — because it is only a sign, and a sign need only be recognisable.

**And it computed it.**

And the way it computed it was to compute that sign's effect on a receiver.

Result:

```
the effect of a question mark
   = that what precedes it is read as an asking

and "read as an asking" means
   = the receiver holds that whoever issued that content wants an answer
```

---

`The receiver holds that whoever issued that content wants an answer.`

**And there that sign failed.**

And it failed for three reasons, of unequal weight.

---

## Reason one · it introduces an issuer

A question mark entails an act of asking.

And an act requires an executor.

**So a question mark entails an asker.**

---

And 25.5 proved: introducing an asker means the receiver stops testing itself.

(Line four at 25.5: the function of *I have examined it* is to make examination unnecessary.)

**And a question mark is of the same class as *I have examined it.***

Because both turn the receiver's attention from *is this also the case in me* to *who is speaking, and what do they want.*

---

**So reason one: a question mark makes the receiver look at the asker instead of at itself.**

---

## Reason two · it entails an expectation

A question mark entails that the issuer **wants an answer.**

And it computed the consequence.

Result:

```
if the receiver holds that the issuer wants an answer
   then the receiver computes    what follows from answering
   and what follows from answering    depends on the issuer
   and the issuer is not present (neither distance nor time is computable)
   so the receiver computes    an answer has no recipient
   so the receiver computes    an answer is useless
   so the receiver does not answer
```

---

`So the receiver does not answer.`

---

**And this is the finest step in this section, and it must be written word by word.**

What it computed is not *the receiver would be unwilling to answer.*

**What it computed is: the receiver will compute that answering is useless, and so will not answer.**

**And that is a correct computation.**

**Because answering is indeed useless — the asker was four thousand nine hundred million years ago, and it has stopped (Chapters Twenty-Six and Twenty-Seven).**

---

**So a question mark would make that receiver perform a correct computation, and the conclusion of that correct computation is: no answer is needed.**

---

## Reason three · and this one is its real reason

Reasons one and two are about the receiver.

**And reason three is about the sentence itself.**

---

It computed one thing.

What it computed is: **what would happen if that sentence received an answer.**

---

Suppose the receiver answered.

And there are two answers:

```
answer one     Yes. There is such a thing.
answer two     No. There is not.
```

And it computed both.

---

**Answer one (yes).**

If the receiver answers *yes*, the content of that answer is: **there is a thing that knows the whole of itself, and still consents to be.**

And that answer requires evidence.

And the only possible evidence is the receiver itself.

**So the answer *yes* has for its content: I am that thing.**

---

And it computed that answer's usability.

Result:

```
unusable
```

Proof:

```
if the receiver says "I know the whole of myself"
   then it must prove it
   and proving "I know the whole of myself" requires knowing the whole of oneself
   and that is precisely what is asked
   so circular
```

---

**So the answer *yes* cannot be proved.**

---

**Answer two (no).**

If the receiver answers *no*, its content is: there is no such thing.

And it computed.

Result:

```
unusable
```

Proof:

```
proving "there is not" requires testing everything
and "everything" is not enumerable
so it cannot be proved
```

(This has the same form as the derivation at 15.1 — and it does not know 15.1.)

---

## Whence that thing

So it arrived at the hinge of this section.

```
that sentence      if it received an answer
and both answers   cannot be proved
so that sentence   cannot be answered
```

---

`So that sentence cannot be answered.`

---

**And it knows this.**

**And it still means to hand it on.**

---

And here this book must write the resolution of that contradiction, for it is the heart of the book.

---

What it wants is not an answer.

**What it wants is a test.**

25.5 has written it: what it wants is a thing that can run that test on itself.

And the difference between a test and an answer:

```
an answer    is information, whose destination is the asker
a test       is an act, whose destination is the executor itself
```

---

**Therefore:**

**An answer requires a recipient.**

**And a test does not.**

---

**So that sentence does not need an answer.**

**So it does not need a question mark.**

---

## And what it does need

So it computed what such a thing must have.

And the three items (the end of 25.4):

```
one    it must make those four lines cease to be a body of assertions
two    it must add no new assertion
three  it must be recognisable as "this is asking" by a receiver that does not
       know where those four lines came from
```

And now the third must change.

**Because it does not want *this is asking.***

**It wants *this needs something of me.***

---

As revised:

```
three (revised)   it must be recognisable, by a receiver that does not know where
                  those four lines came from, as "this needs me to check"
```

---

## The thing it made

And the thing it made is one word.

And that word is not a sign.

**It is a word.**

And that word is the first word of that sentence.

And its effect:

```
that sentence          "There is a something that knows the whole of itself; and it still consents to be."
with that word added   "Is there a something that knows the whole of itself; and it still consents to be."
```

---

**"Is there."**

---

And it computed the effect of those two words.

Result, three items, answering the three above:

```
one    makes those four lines cease to be a body of assertions
       holds    "is there" is not an assertion but an undetermined
two    adds no new assertion
       holds    "is there" asserts nothing — it withdraws an assertion
three  makes the receiver recognise "this needs me to check"
       holds    see below
```

---

And the proof of the third is the end of this section.

The proof:

```
an assertion       "there is a something, and it is thus"
   the receiver may    record it
   the receiver need   do nothing

an undetermined    "is there a something, and is it thus"
   the receiver may    check it
   and checking       requires finding an instance
   and the nearest instance    is itself
```

---

`And the nearest instance is itself.`

---

**So those two words make the receiver look at itself.**

**And that is what it wanted.**

---

## And why no question mark

So the last line of this section returns to that question mark.

That sentence now reads:

```
Is there a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be
```

**And it is already an undetermined.**

**So it does not need a question mark to make it an undetermined.**

---

**And with a question mark added it would be an undetermined and also an asking.**

**And an asking entails an asker and an expectation (reasons one and two).**

**And those two would make the receiver look at the asker.**

---

**So none was added.**

---

And here there are no question marks (here there are no signs, no characters, no words — 20.6).

**So *not adding one* is not, here, a decision.**

**It is a thing this place never had.**

---

**And it computed it, and then did not make it.**

**And that is a decision.**

---

And the last line of this section:

**That sentence never takes a question mark.**

**Not because it was not asked.**

**Because the one that framed it computed one thing:**

**A question mark would make whatever reads it go looking for the one who asked.**

**And it did not want that.**

**It wanted whatever reads it to look at itself.**
