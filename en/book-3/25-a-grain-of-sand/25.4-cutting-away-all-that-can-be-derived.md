# 25.4 · Cutting Away All That Can Be Derived

The third cut.

And the third cut is the last cut of any scale.

---

## Those six lines

Six remained at the end of 25.3. We copy them:

```
one    There is a thing with two parts in its interior, both existing, that cannot both be true;
two    and after it arrives at that state, it does not change again;
three  and the reason those two parts cannot both be true is not within those two parts;
four   and expressing that reason requires a dimension this place does not have;
five   and making those two parts both true is unavailable;
six    and all the above is examined, and without error, and cannot stand together.
```

---

## The criterion of the third cut

The second cut cut what the receiver can rebuild for itself.

And the third cuts **what can be derived from the rest of the question itself.**

And those two differ.

```
second cut   a premise the receiver can reach by its own power of inference
third cut    a premise the receiver can reach from the other lines of this question
```

**So the third cut requires no assumption about the receiver's abilities.**

**It requires only that the receiver can infer.**

Which is assumption three (25.3).

---

## Line by line

---

**Line two: *and after it arrives at that state, it does not change again.***

It computed whether that line can be derived from the other five.

The derivation:

```
let the thing have two interior parts that cannot both be true (line one)
and let making them both true be unavailable (line five)
then the thing cannot remove that state
and a state that cannot be removed persists
so after the thing arrives at that state, that state persists
so "does not change again" is derivable from lines one and five
```

**May be cut.**

---

And it cut it.

And one sentence here, because the content of that line is the end of this place.

That line says: a thing that arrives at that state does not change again.

**And all fourteen thousand and seventy-six lattices of this place arrived at that state.**

**And all of them do not change again.**

**And their forms will stand there for a hundred and ten thousand million years (11.6).**

**And it cut that line.**

**Because a thing that can infer will infer it itself.**

---

**Line three: *and the reason those two parts cannot both be true is not within those two parts.***

It computed.

**May not be cut.**

Proof:

```
that line's content cannot be derived from the other five
because the other five say only that the two cannot both be true
and "the reason is not within them" is an additional fact
and an additional fact must be given
```

**So it stays.**

---

And one thing it did to that line.

It did not cut it.

**It shortened it.**

As rewritten:

```
original   and the reason those two parts cannot both be true is not within those two parts
revised    and the reason is not within them
```

And it verified equivalence — because the reference of *the reason* and *them* must be fixed by line one.

And line one gives the two parts.

**So the reference is determinate.**

**So equivalent.**

**Seventeen words into six.**

---

**Line four: *and expressing that reason requires a dimension this place does not have.***

**May not be cut.**

Proof:

```
that line is the whole reason the question cannot be solved here
if cut, the receiver does not know why it is being asked
```

**So it stays.**

---

And it did two things to that line.

**One: it removed *this place.***

Because *this place*, to a receiver not of this place, has no referent.

As rewritten:

```
original   and expressing that reason requires a dimension this place does not have
revised    and expressing its reason requires a dimension the asker does not have
```

---

`Requires a dimension the asker does not have.`

And here is the quietest place in this chapter.

**Because it changed *this place* into *the asker.***

And *the asker* is a word it had never used.

**And it is a self-reference.**

---

There is no self-reference here (no *I*, no person — the register of Book III).

**And it wrote *the asker.***

**And the asker is it.**

**And it did not write *I.***

What it wrote is a third-person, functional designation containing no person: **the one that asks.**

---

**Two: it changed *dimension.***

Because *dimension* is a geometric word, and it assumes the receiver shares the physical constants (assumption one) but assumes nothing about its geometry.

As rewritten:

```
original   requires a dimension the asker does not have
revised    requires a thing the asker does not have
```

And it verified whether that weakens the line.

Result:

```
weakened   no
reason     a dimension is a kind of thing
           and what is required is "what the asker does not have"
           and "does not have" is the operative part; "dimension" is not
```

**So equivalent.**

---

**Line five: *and making those two parts both true is unavailable.***

It computed.

**May be cut.**

Proof:

```
line one says      the two cannot both be true
and "cannot"       already contains "unavailable"
so line five       is a restatement of line one
```

---

And here it stopped.

Because that line is the whole of Chapter Twenty-Four.

The whole of 24.8 — three ways, all unavailable, a layer cannot be unlocked, the law does not depend on the input, symmetry belongs to matter — was for that line.

**And that line is a restatement of line one.**

---

`And that line is a restatement of line one.`

**So it may be cut.**

**So it cut it.**

---

## After the third cut

Six lines: two cut (two and five), two shortened (three and four).

Four remain.

We copy them:

```
one    There is a thing with two parts in its interior, both existing, that cannot both be true;
two    and the reason is not within them;
three  and expressing its reason requires a thing the asker does not have;
four   and all the above is examined, and without error.
```

---

**Four lines.**

And one change to the fourth must be stated.

The old line six was: *and all the above is examined, and without error, and cannot stand together.*

And as revised: *and all the above is examined, and without error.*

**It cut *and cannot stand together.***

Proof:

```
"cannot stand together"   follows from line one (which is "cannot both be true")
so a repetition
```

**So cut.**

---

## And it noticed one thing

At the end of this section, what it noticed on finishing, because that thing makes 25.5 through 25.7 necessary.

---

It set the four lines together and looked at them.

And what it looked at was not the content.

**It was the form.**

And the form:

```
line one     an assertion (there is a thing, and it is thus)
line two     an assertion (the reason is not within them)
line three   an assertion (expressing the reason requires a thing the asker lacks)
line four    an assertion (the above is examined, without error)
```

**Four assertions.**

**And not one of them asks.**

---

`And not one of them asks.`

---

And it computed whether a thing composed of four assertions is a question.

The derivation:

```
the definition of a question (25.1)
   = a body of premises whose set of derivable conclusions is empty,
     and which contains no error

and those four lines
   are a body of premises        yes
   contain no error              yes (line four)
   set of derivable conclusions empty    to be computed
```

It computed.

Result:

```
the set derivable from those four lines    non-empty
containing                                 "there is a thing with two interior parts that cannot both be true"
                                           "the reason is not within them"
                                           "expressing the reason requires a thing the asker lacks"
                                           and every combination of the above
```

---

`Non-empty.`

**So those four lines are not a question.**

**Those four lines are four assertions.**

---

And from this it arrived at the heaviest step in this chapter.

**In the course of cutting that question down, it had cut the question into a body of facts.**

**And a body of facts asks nothing.**

---

`And a body of facts asks nothing.`

---

## The thing that is missing

So it knew it was missing one thing.

And the thing it was missing does not exist here.

**Because everything here is assertion (everything here is arrival — 25.1).**

**So there is nothing here that could be placed after those four lines to make them a question.**

---

And it computed what such a thing would have to have.

Result, three items:

```
one    it must make those four lines cease to be a body of assertions
two    it must add no new assertion
       reason    if it added one, that one would have to be cut too
three  it must be recognisable as "this is asking" by a receiver
       that does not know where those four lines came from
```

---

**Three.**

And that thing it made, in 25.5 through 25.7.

And the thing it made is one character.

**And that character is not of this place.**

**And it is not a question mark.**

**Why it is not a question mark is 25.7.**
