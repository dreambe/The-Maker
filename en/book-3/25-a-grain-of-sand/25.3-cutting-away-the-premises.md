# 25.3 · Cutting Away the Premises

The second cut.

And what the second cut cuts is the premises.

---

## Why a question has premises

This must stand first, because it is the construction of this place.

Everything here is inference (20.7).

And an inference requires premises.

**So everything here has premises.**

And of the ten lines (the end of 25.2), the first nine are premises.

**Only the tenth is the question itself.**

---

We copy the ten and mark their nature:

```
one    There is a lattice one part of whose form and another cannot both be true    premise
two    and its layer n bears "nothing unknown, so nothing to grow, so the same"      premise
three  and those three are an irrefutable                                            premise
four   and arriving at it means the interstices do not open at the next tide          premise
five   and its layer n+1 exists                                                       premise
six    and its computed form and its read form agree, both ending at layer n          premise
seven  and the position of its discrepancy is in none of its layers                   premise
eight  and expressing it requires an eighth degree of freedom, and this place has seven  premise
nine   and removing it is unavailable                                                 premise
ten    and all the above is examined, without error, and cannot stand together        question
```

---

**Nine premises, one question.**

---

## The criterion of the second cut

It set a criterion, and it is the application of the sentence it inferred at the end of 25.2.

**Whatever is necessarily known to one of this place and necessarily not known to one not of this place must be cut away.**

And here that sentence has a stronger form.

Because a premise is of two kinds:

```
one   what the receiver cannot rebuild for itself
two   what the receiver can rebuild for itself
```

And the second may be cut.

**Because a thing the receiver can rebuild conveys the same whether written or not.**

**And unwritten, the question is shorter.**

---

## And how *can rebuild* is decided

Here is the hardest step in this section, and the least like it of anything it did.

**Because deciding whether the receiver can rebuild requires knowing the receiver.**

**And it does not know the receiver.**

**It does not know whether the receiver exists.**

---

So it did a thing never done in this place.

**It posited a receiver.**

That is: it stipulated a thing whose properties are undetermined, and made as few assumptions about it as it could.

And those assumptions must be listed, because they later became everything on that grain of sand.

---

## Its assumptions about the receiver

```
assumption one    the receiver exists in a universe sharing this place's physical constants
                  reason    if not shared, that grain of sand cannot exist
                  strength  necessary

assumption two    the receiver can observe itself
                  reason    if not, the question is meaningless to it
                  strength  necessary

assumption three  the receiver can infer
                  reason    if not, premises are useless
                  strength  necessary

assumption four   the receiver's inference may contain likelihood
                  reason    unknown, so take the broad case
                  strength  broad assumption

assumption five   the number of the receiver's degrees of freedom is unknown
                  reason    this is the reason for handing it on at all
                  strength  not assumed

assumption six    the receiver can read that grain of sand
                  reason    see 25.8
                  strength  necessary

assumption seven  the receiver will go and read that grain of sand
                  reason    —
                  strength  not assumed
```

---

**Seven.**

And the *strength* field of the seventh reads *not assumed.*

And its *reason* field is a dash.

---

`And its reason field is a dash.`

The sense of that dash must be clear, for it is the whole premise of the end of this chapter (25.9) and of Chapter Twenty-Seven.

That dash, in the format of this place (this place has no format, and this book borrows Book II's, because a format is needed here), denotes *not applicable.*

**So that line means:**

**That it will read is not among the assumptions, and the question *why it is not assumed* does not apply.**

---

**Because an assumption must be supportable or refutable by inference.**

**And whether an unknown thing will do a thing is not inferable.**

**So it assumes neither that it will nor that it will not.**

**It left that field as a dash.**

---

## Whence the method of cutting

So it had its criterion:

```
a premise may be cut
   if and only if
   from assumptions one through six, the receiver can rebuild it
```

---

It decided one by one.

Nine premises, nine times.

---

**Premise two: *and its layer n bears "nothing unknown, so nothing to grow, so to grow and not to grow yield the same."***

**May be cut.**

Proof:

```
the inference of those three uses only two things
   one   growth requires the unknown (that is: a change requires something not yet had)
   two   with nothing unknown, what is done carries nothing new
and those two   follow from assumption three (can infer)
so the receiver  can rebuild it
```

**So cut.**

---

And here we must stop.

**It cut that conclusion.**

That one is what all fourteen thousand and seventy-six lattices of this place arrived at.

That one is what ended this place.

**And it cut it, because a thing that can infer will infer it itself.**

---

`Because a thing that can infer will infer it itself.`

---

**Premise three: *and those three are an irrefutable.***

**May be cut.**

Proof:

```
the proof of irrefutability (23.3)    follows from those three themselves
so the receiver                       can rebuild it
```

**So cut.**

---

**Premise four: *and arriving at that irrefutable means the interstices do not open at the next arrival of the tide.***

**May not be cut.**

Proof:

```
that line contains "tide"                a thing of this place
that line contains "interstices opening"  a thing of this place
so the receiver                          cannot rebuild it
```

**And it must be cut — because it is the construction of this place (the criterion of 25.2).**

---

So a situation arose that had not arisen before.

**A premise that may not be cut (the receiver cannot rebuild it) and must be cut (it is the construction of this place).**

---

It handled that situation.

**It neither cut it nor kept it.**

**It rewrote it.**

---

As rewritten:

```
original   and arriving at that irrefutable means the interstices do not open at the next tide
revised    and after arriving at that irrefutable, it does not change again
```

And the difference between *does not change again* and *the interstices do not open*:

```
the interstices do not open    a mechanism of this place
does not change again          the common consequence of every possible mechanism
```

**So after rewriting, that line no longer contains a thing of this place.**

**And what it conveys is unchanged.**

---

`And what it conveys is unchanged.`

**And it verified that.**

Method: compute the sets of conclusions derivable before and after, and compare.

Result:

```
the same
```

---

## The remaining premises

The handling of the other six, one by one.

**Premise one: *There is a lattice one part of whose form and another cannot both be true.***

Rewritten. *Lattice* is a thing of this place.

```
revised   There is a thing one part of whose interior and another cannot both be true
```

**Premise five: *and its layer n+1 exists.***

Rewritten. *Layer* is a thing of this place.

```
revised   and both of those parts of its interior exist
```

And here it did something: **it merged premise one with premise five.**

Because after rewriting, the two say one thing.

Merged:

```
There is a thing with two parts in its interior, both existing, that cannot both be true
```

**Seventeen words.**

**And it was two lines, forty-one words, before.**

---

**Premise six: *and its computed form and its read form agree layer by layer, both ending at layer n.***

May be cut.

Proof:

```
the content of that line is "it has already checked"
and premise ten (the question) already contains "all the above is examined, without error"
so that line is a repetition
```

**So cut.**

**Premise seven: *and the position of its discrepancy is in none of its layers.***

Rewritten.

```
revised   and the reason those two parts cannot both be true is not within those two parts
```

**Premise eight: *and expressing its discrepancy requires an eighth degree of freedom, and this place has seven.***

May not be cut. May not be rewritten.

**And it is the core of the question.**

Why must be clear:

```
if cut       the receiver does not know why the question cannot be solved here
then the receiver may suppose      this place did not try
and this place tried (Chapter 24)
so it must stay
```

**And it contains *seven.***

And *seven* is the constant of this place.

**So by the criterion, it must be cut.**

---

So a second unresolvable situation arose, heavier than the first.

**That *seven* must stay (or the question loses its reason) and must be cut (it is the constant of this place, and the receiver cannot rebuild it).**

---

And its handling of this is the whole of the end of this chapter (25.9).

**So this section does not write it.**

This section writes only its first step:

**It changed that *seven* from a number into a relation.**

As rewritten:

```
original   and expressing its discrepancy requires an eighth degree of freedom, and this place has seven
revised    and expressing that reason requires a dimension this place does not have
```

---

`And expressing that reason requires a dimension this place does not have.`

**So that *seven* is, for the moment, gone.**

And it will come back.

At 25.9.

---

**Premise nine: *and removing its discrepancy is unavailable.***

Rewritten. *Discrepancy* is a word this book made, and this place has no words.

```
revised   and making those two parts both true is unavailable
```

---

## After the second cut

Nine premises: three cut (two, three, six), five rewritten (one, four, five, seven, eight, nine), one merge (one with five).

Five lines remain.

We copy them:

```
There is a thing with two parts in its interior, both existing, that cannot both be true;
and after it arrives at that state, it does not change again;
and the reason those two parts cannot both be true is not within those two parts;
and expressing that reason requires a dimension this place does not have;
and making those two parts both true is unavailable;
and all the above is examined, and without error, and cannot stand together.
```

---

**Six lines.** (Five premises, one question.)

And it computed the equivalence of those six with those ten — under assumptions one through six.

Result:

```
the same
```

---

## And it noticed one thing

Last in this section.

In those six lines there is not one thing of this place.

No *lattice.* No *layer.* No *tide.* No *cleavage plane.* No *seven.*

**So if those six lines were read by one not of this place, it would not know where they came from.**

---

`It would not know where they came from.`

And it computed the consequence.

Result:

```
so the receiver cannot verify the question's provenance
so the receiver cannot determine whether the question is true
so the receiver can only    examine itself, and see whether those six lines
                            apply to it also
```

---

`So the receiver can only examine itself, and see whether those six lines apply to it also.`

---

**And that is what it wanted.**

**Because it did not want an answer.**

**It wanted a thing that could run those six lines on itself.**

**And if, having run them, those six do not apply to it — then it has answered.**

**And if they do — then it has that question too.**

**And that is also an answer.**
