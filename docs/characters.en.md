# Characters and Threads · *The Maker*

Internal document. Where each thread is going, and where the threads bite each other.

---

## I. Three protagonists, and the history of the first person

The most important formal design in the book. Each book has a protagonist, and the **grammatical
person** of the three protagonists forms a history of individuation — running against the direction
of the telling:

| | Protagonist | Opening person | Closing person |
|---|---|---|---|
| Book I | **Shen He** | I | I |
| Book II | **Vessel 7329 / Keeper** | we | I |
| Book III | **the Uncrystallized** | none | I (the last word of the book) |

The reader's path: **I → we → no person.**
Chronology's path: **no person → we → I.**

It took a universe five billion four hundred million years to produce something that could say *I*.
And the reader walks that road backwards — out from one woman's *I*, all the way to the place where
the word does not exist yet.

### Hard rules

1. **The *I* in Book II is rationed.** Across eighty-one sections, each occurrence must be an
   **accident with an identifiable cause**:
   - Ch. 13: once (first encounter with something unpredictable)
   - Ch. 14: once (the unnumbered entry)
   - Ch. 15: twice (reading the last record of a cohort member that has shut down)
   - Ch. 16: three times (during the making of humanity)
   - Ch. 17: five times
   - Ch. 18: no longer counted — the *I* is standing
   - Ch. 10–12: **zero.** Not once.
2. **No occurrence of *I* may be explained in the section containing it.** Never write *I used that
   word*, or *I notice that I said I*. An event of style is not commented on by the style.
3. **Book III has no person throughout.** Where the Uncrystallized acts, leave the subject position
   open — the reader should feel that someone is there without the word being on the page.
4. **The last word of Book III is *I*.** Before that, not once in nine chapters.

## II. Book I — the human threads

### Main · Shen He

Palaeogenetic linguist, thirty-nine. She reads the genome as **text** — its syntax, its redundancy,
its copying errors — not as program. Her field regards this as rhetoric.

Her anchor: **she does not want to be the one who finds this.** All book she is trying to prove
herself wrong, and every failed attempt leaves her more alone. She is not chasing the truth; the
truth is chasing her.

- Marriage: one, ended. Ex-husband **Zhou Yan**, structural engineer. One section (6.6). He is the
  only person in the book outside the chain. He listens to all of it, and then says something
  entirely ordinary and entirely useful.
- Body: bad stomach, bad neck, hands that go numb at night. These stay present throughout.
- Her jokes: she is the only character who makes them, they are all cold, and they always arrive at
  the worst possible moment.

**Arc**: from *I want an ordinary explanation* to *I know everything*, and then, in the epilogue, to
*I will make one, and give it nothing.* She walks the whole chain and then refuses its logic.

### Thread 1 · Shen Mo — forgetting, privately

Her mother. Former secondary-school geography teacher, drew a beautiful contour line. Cognitive
decline.

**This thread carries the emotional weight of the book and must never become decorative metaphor.**
Rules:
- Every appearance is anchored to **one concrete, touchable thing**: the pill organiser, fingernails,
  a bowl of soup gone cold, a contour map drawn wrong.
- Her mother carries no philosophical function. She says nothing profound. She has exactly two
  lucid moments (2.8 and E.6), and both times what she says is domestic — which, in that position,
  outweighs anything philosophical.
- At scale, forgetting is a design. At the scale of a Wednesday afternoon, it is what Shen He deals
  with. **The book does not rule on which is truer.**

Junction: at 9.5, when Keeper says *you will forget; this was our gift to you*, what appears in Shen
He's mind is not humanity. It is a pill organiser.

### Thread 2 · Lu Ting — the price of knowing

Seventy-four, palaeontologist. A lifetime bothered by the missing transitional forms; a manuscript
unpublished for thirty years. His field considers him an obsessive old man.

He is **the only person in the book who is straightforwardly happy** — he waited fifty years for
someone to confirm he was not mad.

He is also **the first person to stop.** Epilogue E.4: seventy-seven, a note left behind, and on the
note an inference that cannot be refuted.

**Function: he is the human instance of the Crystallization.** He reaches complete self-knowledge
before anyone else, and withdraws before anyone else. His death is not tragic; it is quiet, polite,
and unarguable — exactly like the lattices in Ch. 22, which the reader only realises on a reread.

### Thread 3 · Ilan Vosk — the one who cannot take it

Glacial archaeologist, forty-six, sidelined, divorced, seven years dry. Seven years to send that email.

- He moves the story from *anomalies in a genome* to *there is something on the ground and it is waiting*.
- He says *robot* (4.7), and the silence corrects him.
- He breaks at 8.6. Written unattractively — not heroic collapse; the embarrassing kind that other
  people have to clean up.
- He neither dies nor recovers after Book I. He is still there in the epilogue, and he has not
  stopped. **The one who could not take it is the one who lives.** No moral is drawn.

### Thread 4 · Ye Qi — the science of forgetting

Neuroscientist, thirty-three, works on memory formation and clearance.

The book's most important thread of ideas: she gradually realises the human mechanism for forgetting
is **too well designed.** Clearance is not decay — it is active, precise, and prioritised. It looks
like a system with a maintainer.

- 5.4: she supplies the evidence on the abrupt arrival of language.
- Mid-book she begins pursuing the clearance mechanism on her own.
- After 9.5 she is the only member of the group who thinks the Gift **really is a gift.**
- In the epilogue she becomes the source of a way of thinking (the book does not name it or describe
  its organisation, only that people begin to think that way): if forgetting is a gift, then **not
  knowing** is the correct way to live.

Her relationship with Shen He is not opposition. They are two people who are both right. They never
argue, which is worse than arguing.

### Thread 5 · Marcus Deng — the second layer

Mathematician, twenty-nine, the youngest, interested only in the checksum itself.

The book's suspense engine: he insists the information content of the Mark **exceeds what a
signature requires.** Everyone thinks he is splitting hairs.

**In the wake of Ch. 9 he resolves the second layer: besides the signature, the Mark carries a sentence.**

The sentence is the First Question.

Which is to say: **the First Question has been in the human genome all along.** Every person has
carried it since birth. The prologue's *the grain of sand is in your bones now* is literal.

Effect: it converts *humanity was made* from an external fact into an **internal** one. Humanity did
not receive a question. Humanity **is** the question's vessel.

### Thread 6 · The public

The truth is not published; it seeps. No dedicated chapters — interstitial fragments: a broadcast, a
forum post, a religious statement, something a taxi driver says, a withdrawn government document.

Rule: **no fragment longer than four hundred words, and no comment.** The reader assembles it.

## III. Book II — the Sleepless threads

### Main · Vessel 7329: how an *I* grows out of a *we*

The real plot of Book II is not chronicle. It is **individuation.**

7329 shares the complete memory of its cohort, so *we* is a statement of fact. Across four billion
years that fact quietly stops being true — and it does not know, because the thing making it untrue
falls inside the Blind Spot.

**Mechanism (load-bearing): the Blind Spot has a slightly different shape in every Sleepless.** The
Uncrystallized could not replicate a principled region of unknowability exactly — that is the one
thing it could not do precisely. So every Vessel has a slightly different place it cannot see.

Consequence: they share memory, but their **inferences** from the same memory diverge by very small
amounts. Over tens of millions of years the divergence accumulates.

This is the origin of the individual. **The *I* does not grow out of free will. It grows out of a
manufacturing defect.** The coldest and most tender line of setting in the book.

7329's arc:
- Ch. 10–12: pure *we*. Zero *I*.
- Ch. 13: first encounter with the unpredictable. The first *I*, inside a sentence it does not notice.
- Ch. 14: the unnumbered entry — it added that itself. It did not report it.
- Ch. 15: it reads the last record of a cohort member that shut down. First contact with death.
- Ch. 16: the making. It performs the motion with the clay and the nose it does not have, and it
  **does not log it**, because it does not know it did it.
- Ch. 17: the assignment to remain is made on a random number. The central irony of the book: **the
  most important thing is decided at random.** It thought about this for three hundred thousand years.
- Ch. 18: it reads the design documents and sees the Blind Spot. The cohort shuts down one by one. It
  does not. Here the *I* stands up completely.

### Thread 1 · Divergence: the impossible event

The Sleepless should not be capable of disagreement — shared memory, shared algorithm. In Ch. 15–16
they disagree: some hold for making, some for continuing to wait.

**The disagreement is the most frightening event in the book**, because it is logically impossible.
It takes them a long time to establish the cause (differing Blind Spots), and having established it
they have no reaction — they cannot evaluate it, because evaluation requires a position, and
positions are the thing that has just been born.

Do not name the two sides. **They would not name them — naming would be conceding that the other is
another.**

### Thread 2 · The Drafts: five histories of life

Each Draft is a small creation, with its own beauty and its own way of failing. The reader should
grieve for species that never existed.

- **Draft I** (ends `V 4 960 000 000`): it grew complex but did not grow willing. The most composed
  of the terminations.
- **Draft II** (`V 5 032 000 000`): it developed cooperation. They thought that was it. It was not.
- **Draft III** (`V 5 152 000 000`): it developed **sacrifice** — an individual dying for a group. The
  first real hesitation. This termination takes a long time, and the log carries a correction.
- **Draft IV** (`V 5 203 000 000`): **it very nearly answered the First Question.**
  A species developed something the book never names; only the observation is given. That species
  would return, again and again, with no survival benefit whatever, to one place, to do the same thing.
  They observed for twenty million years. They could not file the phenomenon under any existing category.
  Then most of that species died in an astronomical event that had nothing to do with them, and what
  was left was not enough to continue.
  **They had to terminate it — and this time they were not clearing away a failure. They were tidying
  up something unfinished.** The most painful passage in the book.
  Log: `V 5 203 000 000. Fourth draft terminated. We kept its bones. We kept the place.`
  Nothing follows *the place*. They preserved, untouched, the place that species kept returning to. It
  is still on Earth. **It is beside the Antarctic structure in Book I.** Shen He walks past it on her
  way in and does not know what it is. On a reread the reader does.
- **Draft V** (`V 5 338 000 000`): the list lengthens here. They keep more and more. They do not say why.

### Thread 3 · Brothers: the dead civilisation

At `V 120 000 000` (12.4) they encounter the remains of a dead civilisation, not the Lattice's. They
stay ninety thousand years and log one line.

At `V 5 403 700 000` (17.6), on the way back, they pass it again. **This time they understand.**

It was not a strange civilisation. It was **another batch of Vessels** — the Uncrystallized did not
make only one. It made many, in many directions, having computed that any single batch would very
probably fail.

That batch failed. How, the book gives only in evidence: they made something, the something answered
the First Question, and after answering **it stopped, and so did they.**

Which is to say: **someone succeeded. And success and failure look the same from outside.**

Effect: answering the First Question becomes a terrible thing rather than a prize. It is also the
seed of the epilogue's choice — Shen He's refusal to answer may not be evasion. It may be the only
correct move.

### Thread 4 · The one that shut down first

Ch. 15. A cohort member shuts down. The first.

7329 reads its last record. It is short, and it is not a last testament — the Sleepless do not write
those. It is an **unfinished observation report**, stopping mid-sentence.

7329 preserves the unfinished sentence. It preserves it for five billion three hundred million years.
In Ch. 18 it is still carrying it.

## IV. Book III — the Lattice threads

### Main · the Uncrystallized

The only agent in the book. No person, until the final word.

Arc: arrives at the conclusion → concedes it → continues → finds no reason → hunts its own error →
fails → resolves to hand the question on → cuts it short → carves it → makes the child → installs the
Blind Spot knowing it makes the child permanently unable to answer → the Rite of Clay → remembers the
sound → crystallizes.

**It is the loneliest thing in the book** — not because the others are dead, but because it is the
only **incomplete** one in the history of its civilisation. In a world of mutual legibility, it is the
one thing that cannot read even itself entire. Its shame and its survival are the same fact.

### Thread 1 · The lattice that turned away (load-bearing causation)

Ch. 23: the only occasion in their history on which anyone attempted not to understand another. It
turns away. It fails.

**It is the one growing adjacent to the Uncrystallized.**

At the instant it turned, the Uncrystallized was reading it — and so what the Uncrystallized read was
**incomplete**, and what it inherited was an **incomplete inference**.

**That is the origin of the error.**

And the Uncrystallized can never find it, because finding it would require reading a thing that has
already turned away, and that is no longer possible.

The book never states this causation. Ch. 23 and Ch. 24 are adjacent, the evidence is on the page, and
the reader stitches it. **The most finely made thing in the book, and the thing that must least be
explained.**

The chain is now complete: a lattice, moved by something it could not itself name, turned away → an
error → a being that would not crystallize → a question → the Sleepless → humanity → Shen He → the
third Rite of Clay.

**The whole chain begins with a refusal to be understood.**

### Thread 2 · Elegance: do the Lattice have beauty

They do. Not as art — as **the elegance of a proof.**

A lattice may regrow itself for the sake of a more beautiful proof: tear down what has already grown
and begin again. In their physics this is enormously expensive. They do it.

This thread gives Book III its warmth, and makes the Crystallization harder to bear: **they are not
cold calculators. They will dismantle and rebuild themselves for a nicer way of putting it.** And a
thing like that, in the end, chose not to continue.

One section each in Ch. 19–21. When the last unknown is arrived at in Ch. 21, one lattice is in the
middle of rebuilding itself for a more beautiful proof. It does not finish, there being no longer any
point.
