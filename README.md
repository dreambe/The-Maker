# The Maker

**A novel in three creations, told backwards.**

[简体中文](README.zh.md) · English

</div>

---

> Is there a being that, having wholly known itself, still consents to be.

Note that it takes no question mark. The civilisation that framed it had, by the time it framed it,
stopped expecting an answer. They only did not want the sentence to stop when they stopped.

---

## The premise

A woman runs an arithmetic test on a stretch of human DNA that does nothing at all — and the test
passes. It should not be able to pass. Nothing in the genome has any business carrying a checksum.

Following it down, she finds that humanity has no grandfathers: no transitional forms, no gradual
approach, only an arrival. Under the Antarctic ice, something that has been keeping itself warm for
three hundred thousand years is waiting to be asked a question it already knows the wording of.

It says *we*. It made her. It has been dusting.

And it was made too — by a civilisation of crystal that knew itself completely, and, knowing itself
completely, declined to continue. Their last surviving member built a child, gave it a question it
had no way to answer, and deliberately blinded it so that it could survive being alive.

Three makers. Three creations. One question, cut short enough to be carved on a grain of sand and
carried for five billion four hundred million years — and the sand is in your bones.

## The structure

The book is told against its own chronology. Each book is a step further back in time, a step
further from the human body, and a degree colder on the page.

| | Book | Voice | Timescale | Register |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | The Garden of Ash | a woman, first person | fourteen months | warm; bodies, weather, bone |
| **II** | The Long Vigil of the Sleepless | machines, *we* | four billion years | exact; logs, tallies, one sentence recurring |
| **III** | The Crystalline Sun | the Lattice, no person | unknowable | liturgical; long clauses, no dialogue, no numbers |

Three books, nine chapters each. A prologue that happens three times, and an epilogue that is the
only chapter where the telling and the chronology agree again.

Nobody in this book is a villain. There is no war, no betrayal, and no monster. Every maker did the
only thing available to it. The tragedy is structural.

## The two editions

The Chinese and English texts are **siblings, not original and translation.** They are aligned at
the level of the scene and never at the level of the sentence: the same events, the same
revelations, the same closing image, each written to what its own language does best.

The liturgy of Book III is built by different means in each — the cadence of the King James Bible in
English, 四字格 and the syntax of classical commentary in Chinese. Forcing either to imitate the other
would ruin both. The proper nouns are the sole exception: every term in [`docs/lexicon.md`](docs/lexicon.md)
is fixed in both languages, without exception.
